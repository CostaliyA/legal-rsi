## 1. Purpose, Scope and Method

## 2. Executive Summary

The Loan is a well-secured construction facility on a strong site with a capable sponsor, and the Credit Memorandum's credit conclusions are broadly supported by the appraisal. **However, the record does not currently support a closing.** Five findings are, in our view, closing-critical, and each requires a business decision by the Lender rather than a drafting fix:

1. **The $3,400,000 prior deed of trust has no source of funds and no line item in the Sources and Uses.** The Prior Deed of Trust in favor of Triangle Commercial Bank (TC Sched. B-II ¶10; Book 6215, Page 892) must be released at closing for the Lender to obtain a first-priority lien. It appears nowhere in the $56,700,000 budget, the sources table, the draw schedule or the interest reserve. The record does not even fix the payoff amount: the Title Commitment states that no release is of record and that Borrower's counsel advised that the debt was "partially repaid," with the balance unverified.
2. **There is no true equity in the capital structure, and the "land equity" narrative is contradicted by the Borrower's own budget.** The Borrower's Detailed Budget allocates 100% of the $8,200,000 land cost and $1,000,000 of the Developer Fee to *mezzanine loan proceeds* and shows Borrower equity of **$0**. That is irreconcilable with TS §5.1 (equity of $0 but land shown as a use), CM §§2.2 and 5.1 (land as equity, funded by member capital calls), and Org §1.3 (land contributed personally by Ms. Vasquez-Torres at formation). On the Borrower's own numbers, senior and mezzanine debt equal 100% of Total Project Costs.
3. **The environmental land-use restriction has not been resolved, and two of the four NFA conditions are missing from every bank document.** The NFA Letter conditions the determination on four items. The Term Sheet (§17), the Credit Memorandum (§§8.2, 10.1.7) and the closing conditions address only two of them (the vapor mitigation system and the residential use restriction). The **Soil Management Plan** and the **Deed Notice** — which was required to be recorded on or before **November 29, 2024** and which the Phase I ESA states had *not* been recorded — appear nowhere in the Term Sheet, the Credit Memorandum, the budget or the Title Commitment.
4. **Mechanics' lien priority is impaired by pre-closing work.** Demolition (August 2024), environmental remediation (September 2024) and rough grading were performed before the Deed of Trust will be recorded. Under N.C.G.S. §44A-10 a claim of lien relates back to first furnishing, so those claimants could prime the Lender's lien. The Title Commitment's own Note 4 recommends an owner's affidavit that no labor or materials were furnished before recording — an affidavit that cannot truthfully be given on this site.
5. **The capital stack cannot service both tranches from property cash flow.** Stabilized NOI of $4,876,800 covers senior interest-only debt service of $3,728,750 (1.31x), but senior plus mezzanine interest of $1,150,000 to $1,509,000 produces combined coverage of **0.93x to 1.00x**. Neither the Term Sheet nor the Credit Memorandum tests combined coverage. The take-out therefore depends on refinancing, and the mezzanine balance grows to approximately $12.08 million (simple interest) to $12.55 million (monthly compounding) by the Construction Maturity Date.

Each of these is addressable, but each changes the economics or the closing timetable. We recommend the Lender commission revised deliverables before the February 28, 2025 target closing date and treat that date as soft.

## 4. Detailed Issue Analysis

### A. Capital Structure, Equity and Collateral

#### A-1. The Prior Deed of Trust is unfunded and the payoff amount is unknown

**Finding.** TC Sched. B-I ¶4 requires satisfaction and release of the deed of trust from Foster Street Properties LLC (as predecessor to the Borrower) to the trustee for Triangle Commercial Bank, dated September 14, 2018, recorded in Book 6215, Page 892, securing an original indebtedness of **$3,400,000**. TC Sched. B-II ¶10 confirms that no cancellation, release, satisfaction or subordination is of record, and notes that Borrower's counsel advised that the debt "has been partially repaid" and that the remaining balance "will be satisfied in full at or prior to closing from the Borrower's own funds or from closing proceeds," with the Company having "not independently verified the outstanding balance." The CM (§9.7) treats the obligation as $3,400,000 to be "satisfied at closing from the proceeds of the senior construction loan or from borrower funds."

**Why it matters.** Three independent problems: (i) **No source of funds.** The $56,700,000 Uses of Funds (TS §5.2; CM §5.2; WB Detailed Budget) contain no line item for the payoff. Adding it produces Total Project Costs of $60,100,000 against sources of $56,700,000 — a $3,400,000 gap. (ii) **No verified amount.** A payoff may exceed the original principal if interest, default interest, fees or prepayment premiums have accrued on a 2018 loan partially repaid. (iii) **Priority.** Without a recorded release (or an escrow arrangement with the title company), the Lender cannot obtain the first-priority lien that TC Sched. A and TS §18 require, and the Loan cannot close.

**Recommended resolution.** Require (a) a payoff letter dated within 30 days of closing, stating principal, accrued and per diem interest, fees and total payoff; (b) either a recordable cancellation/certificate of satisfaction under N.C.G.S. §45-37 or the title company's undertaking to record upon disbursement through escrow; (c) inclusion of the Refinancing Payoff as a line item in Total Project Costs; and (d) funding from **new third-party equity**, not from the Mezzanine Loan or any Chen/Brightleaf-affiliated source. Note the arithmetic consequence, which is favorable on one test and unfavorable on another: with the payoff included, Total Project Costs become $60,100,000, LTC becomes 79.03% ($47,500,000 ÷ $60,100,000) and the 80% LTC problem disappears — but the required equity rises from $2,140,000 to **$3,400,000** (and to $3,580,000 if the Loan is also reduced to cure the LTV overage in A-4). If the Borrower cannot produce $3.4 million of verified third-party equity, the only alternatives are to reduce the Loan or the project scope. **This is the single largest open item in the transaction.**

#### A-2. There is no equity in the capital structure; the "land contribution" is described three inconsistent ways

**Finding.** The record offers three incompatible accounts of how the land was acquired and funded:

- **Org §1.3**: the land "was originally acquired by Ms. Vasquez-Torres in a transaction funded from her personal resources and was subsequently contributed to the Borrower upon formation of the project entity," at a cost basis of $8,200,000; Brightleaf Investors Group LLC and Mr. Osei contributed **$0 cash to date**; total member equity to date is $8,200,000.
- **CM §2.2**: the land "was acquired by Ms. Vasquez-Torres on behalf of the Borrower through capital calls from all three members, including Brightleaf Investors Group LLC."
- **WB Detailed Budget, row 1.01**: the land is "funded by mezzanine loan proceeds and member capital calls," with **$8,200,000 allocated to the Mezzanine Loan** and $0 to equity.
- **TS §5.1**: "Borrower Equity (land previously acquired; see Uses below) **$0**."

**Why it matters.** The Sources and Uses in the Term Sheet balances only because the land appears as a $8,200,000 *use* while borrower equity appears as *$0* — i.e., the identity of the source that funded the land is omitted. If the land was in fact funded with mezzanine proceeds (as the Borrower's own budget states), then (i) there is no equity in the transaction before the equity escrow, (ii) the "embedded equity" mitigation in CM §9.1 ("the land has an appraised value of $8,900,000, which is $700,000 above acquisition cost") is unavailable because the same asset is pledged to, or was paid for by, the mezzanine lender, and (iii) the circular-funding concern that CM §§5.1 and 9.1 raise is not a risk to be evaluated — it is the documented structure. The Credit Memorandum's LTC analysis (83.77%) and its HVCRE contributed-capital analysis (14.2%) both *credit* $8,200,000 of land as equity; that assumption is not supported by the budget the Borrower submitted.

**Recommended resolution.** Require the Borrower to reconcile the three accounts in writing, with documentary support (the 2023 closing statement, wire confirmations, member capital account ledgers, and the mezzanine loan disbursement schedule), and to present a conformed Sources and Uses in which the land appears consistently either (a) as an in-kind equity contribution at $8,200,000, in which case the mezzanine proceeds are *not* the source of the land and must be redeployed to other costs, or (b) as debt-funded, in which case neither the LTC nor the HVCRE analysis may credit it. Draft Agreement §11.2(b) requires a Lender-approved Closing Statement that resolves this; §11.3 requires source-of-funds verification; §11.7 records the HVCRE analysis on the assumption that the land may be credited, expressly subject to confirmation.

#### A-3. Loan-to-Cost: the 80% covenant, the $2,140,000 escrow, and why the escrow as described does not work

**Finding.** LTC is 83.77% ($47,500,000 ÷ $56,700,000) against an 80% maximum (TS §6.1; CM §5.3). The maximum loan at 80% is $45,360,000, a shortfall of $2,140,000. The CM (§5.3, §9.1, Condition 10.1.2) conditions approval on the Borrower depositing $2,140,000 in verified cash "into a Bank-controlled equity escrow account prior to or at closing," to be "applied to project costs first, ahead of loan advances, to bring the effective LTC into compliance."

**Why it matters.** As described, the mechanism is arithmetically ineffective. LTC is a ratio of *loan* to *costs*; funding $2,140,000 of costs from equity does not change the numerator or the denominator unless the Loan is correspondingly reduced or availability is permanently withheld. If the Loan still funds $47,500,000 and equity adds $2,140,000, total sources ($58,840,000) exceed Total Project Costs ($56,700,000) by $2,140,000 — money that has nowhere to go unless it is applied to the unfunded Triangle payoff (A-1) or to a reduced final advance.

**Recommended resolution.** Express the requirement as a borrowing base limitation, not as an escrow alone: aggregate Advances may not exceed the least of (a) the Commitment, (b) 80% of Total Project Costs, and (c) 65% of as-completed value (Draft Agreement §2.4(a)), with the Commitment permanently reduced by the amount of equity applied (§2.4(c)). Combined with the A-1 fix, the recommended end-state is: Total Project Costs $60,100,000; Commitment $47,320,000 (see A-4); LTC 78.74%; Required Equity Contribution $3,580,000, verified as to source.

#### A-4. Loan-to-Value: the Term Sheet's own 65% limit is exceeded, and the Credit Memorandum tests 70%

**Finding.** TS §4 and §6.2 state that the maximum LTV is **65%** on both an as-completed and an as-stabilized basis; TS §4 also computes and discloses an as-completed LTV of **65.25%** — i.e., the Term Sheet is internally inconsistent on its face. CM §6.1 instead lists the Bank policy maximum as **70%** and records the LTV as "Pass." The Credit Memorandum does not note the 65% Term Sheet limit at all.

**Why it matters.** At 65%, the maximum Loan is $47,320,000 (0.65 × $72,800,000); the Loan exceeds that by **$180,000**. An executed term sheet that the Loan violates on its face is a poor foundation for a closing condition and, more practically, creates a documentary gap that will surface in credit review and any subsequent exam or sale.

**Recommended resolution.** Elect one: (a) reduce the Commitment to $47,320,000; (b) obtain an updated appraisal supporting an as-completed value of at least $73,076,923; or (c) document a policy exception and amend TS §4/§6.2 to 70%. We recommend (a) — it is the smallest change and eliminates the inconsistency. Draft Agreement §16.1 currently requires compliance "at or before Closing" by one of these three routes and discloses the 65.25% result.

#### A-5. Competing pledges of 100% of the membership interests, and a member veto over the cure

**Finding.** TS §8, TS §1.4, CM §4.2 and CM §9.4 all record that both the senior Loan and the Mezzanine Loan are "secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests in the Borrower." Org §2.5 confirms that the Operating Agreement contains no mechanism to resolve competing pledges and does not distinguish a senior from a mezzanine pledge. The Intercreditor Agreement has been "negotiated but is not yet executed" (TS §1.4; CM §4.4; Org §1.2).

Separately, and more consequentially: under Org §2.2, "Major Decisions" — including "any financing or refinancing of the Property in a principal amount in excess of $1,000,000" and "any amendment to this Operating Agreement" — require the affirmative vote of members holding **at least 75%** of the membership interests. Ms. Vasquez-Torres holds 62% and Mr. Osei 10% (72% together). **Brightleaf Investors Group LLC (28%) — whose managing member, Thomas R. Chen, also controls the Mezzanine Lender — therefore holds an effective veto over (i) the senior Loan itself, (ii) the Operating Agreement amendments that would add the SPE covenants and the independent manager, and (iii) the pledge of its own 28% interest to the senior Lender as first-priority collateral.**

**Why it matters.** The party whose affiliate is being subordinated is the party whose consent is a condition to the protections the Lender requires. If Chen declines, the Lender can neither obtain a first-priority membership pledge nor deliver the SPE/independent-manager package that CM §9.3 describes as "the single most important structural safeguard against a bad-faith bankruptcy filing." Additionally, Org §2.2 records a $500,000 single-expenditure approval threshold that would, on its face, require member approval of nearly every construction draw.

**Recommended resolution.** (i) Make the fully executed Intercreditor Agreement — with first-priority pledge to the Lender and second-priority to the mezzanine — a hard closing condition (Draft Agreement §§22.1–22.9; Sched. 9.2 ¶4). (ii) Obtain executed member consents from all three members, together with a specific consent to the pledge and the irrevocable proxy (Draft Agreement §§12.2, 18.5 and the Acknowledgment and Consent of Members signature page). (iii) Obtain an amendment to Org §2.2 either eliminating the $500,000 expenditure threshold for expenditures within the Approved Budget or confirming that Approved Budget expenditures are pre-authorized. (iv) If the Intercreditor Agreement cannot be obtained, adopt the CM §9.4 alternative — drop the membership pledge and rely on the real property collateral — but note that this also eliminates the Lender's ability to take control of the Borrower in a default, which is precisely the tool the CM identifies as valuable in a construction context.

#### A-6. HVCRE: the contributed-capital analysis depends on the same unverified land premise

**Finding.** CM §12 computes contributed capital of $10,340,000 ($2,140,000 equity escrow + $8,200,000 land) as 14.2% of the $72,800,000 as-completed value, below the 15% threshold, and requires $580,000 more to avoid HVCRE classification. The 15% test is met at $10,920,000.

**Why it matters.** The computation credits the land at $8,200,000. If the land was funded with mezzanine proceeds (A-2) or remains subject to the released-at-closing Prior Deed of Trust (A-1), the credit is questionable under the contributed-capital definition, and the HVCRE conclusion may be worse than stated. Conversely, if the Refinancing Payoff is added to costs and funded with $3,400,000 of new cash equity, contributed capital becomes $11,600,000, or 15.93% — which would allow the Bank to avoid HVCRE classification entirely (subject to confirming the rule's conditions, including that loan proceeds are not used to reimburse the borrower for contributed property).

**Recommended resolution.** Do not rely on the 14.2% figure until A-1 and A-2 are resolved. Direct credit administration to re-run the classification after closing documentation is conformed. Draft Agreement §11.7 records the analysis and requires Borrower cooperation, but does not warrant a particular classification.

### B. Environmental

#### B-1. Two of the four NFA conditions are absent from every bank document

**Finding.** ESA §7 identifies four conditions to the NFA determination: (1) the Vapor Mitigation System; (2) the non-residential land use restriction on the 0.38-acre Affected Area; (3) a NCDEQ-approved **Soil Management Plan** before any subsurface disturbance in the Affected Area; and (4) a **Deed Notice** recorded in the Durham County Registry "within 60 days of the date of the NFA Letter — that is, by **November 29, 2024**." The ESA states that as of October 28, 2024 the deed notice had not been recorded.

TS §17 refers only to the vapor mitigation system ("The NFA letter authorizes commercial use of the affected area ... conditioned on the installation and maintenance of a soil vapor mitigation system"). It does not mention the use restriction, the Soil Management Plan or the Deed Notice. CM §8.2 discloses the use restriction and the vapor system but not the Soil Management Plan or the Deed Notice; Condition 10.1.7 likewise requires only (a)–(b) reports, (c) vapor system design and (d) resolution of the use restriction. The Title Commitment does not list any environmental instrument in Schedule B-II; Note 5 excludes environmental matters from coverage entirely.

**Why it matters.** (i) The Deed Notice deadline has passed (or will have passed by closing) and the record contains no evidence of recording. Failure to record is a breach of the NFA conditions that could, at NCDEQ's option, reopen Site ID IHSB-24-1203; it also leaves a latent instrument that will appear in the title chain after closing. (ii) The Soil Management Plan must be *approved by NCDEQ before* excavation in the Affected Area. Construction beginning without it is both an environmental violation and a construction-stoppage risk. (iii) The Late Notice also affects priority: when recorded, the notice and the accompanying Declaration of Land Use Restrictions will appear in the title chain; the Lender needs it to be a controlled, approved exception.

**Recommended resolution.** Add all four conditions to the closing conditions and to the covenants (Draft Agreement §19.2 and Exhibit E; Sched. 9.2 ¶13): (i) evidence the Soil Management Plan has been submitted (and approved if NCDEQ so requires); (ii) the Vapor Mitigation System designed, engineered and incorporated into the plans and the GMP Contract (budget line 2.08 carries a note that it includes the system); (iii) the Deed Notice recorded — at or before closing if not previously recorded — together with a written statement from NCDEQ or environmental counsel that the late recording does not impair the NFA determination; and (iv) Lender approval of the form of the recorded instrument and title coverage that does not subordinate the Deed of Trust. Draft Agreement Exhibit D ¶8 admits the instrument as a Permitted Exception only on those terms.

#### B-2. The land use restriction versus 14 residential units — an unresolved, unquantified valuation issue

**Finding.** CM §8.2 and §9.5 state that the plans "position 14 residential units ... directly above" the Affected Area. The ESA does not verify this: §8.5 states that the consultant "did not independently verify whether any residential units would be positioned directly above the affected 0.38-acre area," and Recommendation 1 directs the client and architect to perform the overlay. The Appraiser likewise disclaims: App. §8 states it "expresses no opinion as to the legal permissibility of the planned residential use," and Extraordinary Assumption 7 assumes compliance with the restriction or a modification. App. §8 also states that the appraiser "has not adjusted the valuation conclusions to account for any potential impact of the NFA use restriction."

**Why it matters.** The CM quantifies the income effect of losing 14 units ($377,160 of annual revenue; DSCR falls to 1.21x) but not the value effect, and its computation ignores the vacancy and credit-loss allowance. Our recomputation (Appendix B, Table B-4) gives an NOI reduction of $358,302, DSCR of **1.21x**, and — capitalizing the lost income at the appraiser's 6.40% rate — an as-completed value reduction of approximately **$5,598,000**, taking as-completed value to $67,202,000 and as-completed LTV to **70.68%**. That would breach both the 65% Term Sheet limit and the 70% policy guideline, and would put the Loan in default of the LTV covenant on day one if the restriction is enforced.

**Recommended resolution.** Require, as a closing condition (or at the Lender's election as a condition to advances above the podium), one of: (a) a written NCDEQ modification permitting residential use above the restricted area with the vapor mitigation system in place; (b) redesign of the affected space as non-residential and revised income projections demonstrating covenant compliance; or (c) written NCDEQ confirmation that residential use above the grade-level restricted area is permitted with engineering controls (CM §9.5 alternatives). In all events, require a **revised appraisal** reflecting the resolved program before the first advance above the podium level, and consider reducing the Loan or requiring additional equity in the interim. Draft Agreement §19.2(b) prohibits residential occupancy in the Affected Area absent an approved modification and requires revised projections.

#### D-3. The draw schedule does not foot, and the "retainage release" is not retainage

**Finding.** TS §9.2 and CM §7.1 both show Draws 2 through 8 totalling **$34,750,000** — one hundred percent of the GMP — notwithstanding a stated **10% retainage**, and a Draw 9 "Retainage Release" of **$3,275,000**, which is **9.42%** of hard costs rather than 10%. The Borrower's workbook compounds the problem: its hard-cost column sums to **$38,225,000** (110% of the GMP); its "Developer Fee" column sums to **$923,750** against a $2,835,000 budget; its "Contingency" column sums to **$3,351,250** against a $1,440,000 budget; and its component columns sum to **$48,500,000** against $47,500,000 of draws, even though the "Total Draw" column itself foots to $47,500,000 (the totals row is entered, not calculated).

**Why it matters.** The document that will drive the Bank's disbursements is internally inconsistent by $1,000,000 to $3,475,000 depending on which column one reads. The practical consequences are (i) over-funding of hard costs by up to $3,475,000, (ii) a retainage holdback that is never actually withheld, and (iii) no mechanism for the Developer Fee and contingency to be disbursed at all, despite both being identified as senior-loan-funded in the budget.

**Recommended resolution.** Adopt **Exhibit C** to the Draft Agreement, which we rebuilt from the budget line items and which foots: hard costs of $34,750,000 (drawn net of $3,475,000 retainage as $31,275,000, then released), soft costs $4,150,000, interest reserve $4,850,000, origination fee $475,000, Developer Fee $1,835,000 (senior share) and contingency $1,440,000 — total **$47,500,000**, with retainage of exactly 10% and cumulative advances that reconcile at every stage (Appendix B, Table B-3). Require the Borrower to reproduce this schedule in its requisition software and confirm the 10% holdback is actually withheld by the disbursement agent.

#### D-4. The DSCR covenant as drafted cannot be satisfied at the time it is tested

**Finding.** TS §6.3 requires the DSCR to be tested "at the commencement of the Mini-Perm Period and annually thereafter based on actual trailing twelve-month net operating income," with a 1.25x floor. At the commencement of the Mini-Perm Period the building will have just received its certificate of occupancy (construction maturity August 28, 2027; projected substantial completion August 31, 2027; stabilization projected May 2028). Trailing twelve-month actual NOI at that date will be near zero.

**Why it matters.** A covenant that is breached on the day it first applies is worse than no covenant: it either forces a waiver at the worst moment, or it converts a technical failure into a default at the outset of the mini-perm.

**Recommended resolution.** Test the 1.25x DSCR at the commencement of the Mini-Perm Period **on a pro forma basis** using stabilized NOI, then on actual trailing twelve-month NOI from the first anniversary of substantial completion, and annually thereafter (Draft Agreement §16.3(a)). This preserves the credit intent while making the covenant operable. The Credit Memorandum's extension conditions already adopt a sensible variant (1.15x on trailing three-month annualized income, CM §11(d)); we have carried that into Exhibit F.

#### D-5. The partial repayment guaranty: 25% aggregate or 25% per guarantor?

**Finding.** TS §7.3 provides for a Partial Repayment Guaranty "in the amount of 25% of the Loan Amount (25% × $47,500,000 = $11,875,000)" that is "joint and several, meaning that each Guarantor shall be individually liable for the full guaranteed amount" — i.e., an aggregate cap of $11,875,000. CM §4.3 and §§2.3.1–2.3.2 instead describe "$11,875,000 **per guarantor**," which implies aggregate exposure of **$23,750,000 (50% of the Loan)**. CM §2.3.3 then analyzes each guaranty as if it were $11,875,000 (39.8% of Ms. Vasquez-Torres's net worth; 103.3% of Mr. Osei's).

**Why it matters.** A $11.9 million difference in guarantor exposure in a document the Guarantors will be asked to sign. The Term Sheet controls (the Credit Memorandum is internal), but the ambiguity should be resolved before the Guaranties are drafted.

**Recommended resolution.** Adopt the Term Sheet's aggregate cap: $11,875,000 in the aggregate, joint and several, each Guarantor individually liable for the full amount, with the Lender's total recovery limited to $11,875,000 (Draft Agreement §13.3). Correct the Credit Memorandum's "per guarantor" language. The Guarantors should also be advised that the Completion Guaranty and the Carry Guaranty are **not** subject to that cap and are effectively unlimited, and that the Environmental Indemnity is unlimited and survives.

#### D-6. Guarantor capacity and the missing guarantor net worth covenant

**Finding.** The Term Sheet contains no guarantor net worth covenant. TS §15 nonetheless makes it an Event of Default that "**Any Guarantor's net worth falls below the minimum threshold set forth in Section 6.4**" — but §6.4 is the *Borrower's* $12,000,000 net worth covenant. The cross-reference is to the wrong covenant and to a covenant that does not exist. Meanwhile Mr. Osei's partial guaranty equals 103.3% of his stated net worth, his net worth is 59% illiquid real estate, and the Completion and Carry Guaranties are unlimited.

**Why it matters.** A guarantor whose guaranty exceeds his net worth and who must fund unlimited completion and carry obligations is a credit exposure that should be monitored by covenant, not only by annual financial statements.

**Recommended resolution.** Add a guarantor net worth covenant of $30,000,000 combined and $8,000,000 per Guarantor (Draft Agreement §13.8 and §16.7) — which requires Borrower consent because it is not in the Term Sheet — or, alternatively, delete the defective cross-reference in §15 and rely on the liquidity covenant. We recommend the former and have flagged it in Section 6 below as a provision beyond the Term Sheet.
