# 5. VALUATION, COVENANTS AND LOAN QUANTUM

## A-1 | The as-completed LTV exceeds the 65% covenant at closing

**Record.** Term Sheet §4 states that "the Lender's maximum loan-to-value ratio shall not exceed 65% on an as-completed basis", and §6.2 sets a 65% maximum on both an as-completed and an as-stabilised basis. Term Sheet §4 and Credit Memorandum §6.1 both compute the as-completed LTV as $47,500,000 ÷ $72,800,000 = 65.25%. Credit Memorandum §6.1 applies a "Bank Policy" column of "Max 70%" and reports the metric as "Pass"; §12 repeats "maximum LTV (70% for construction loans)".

**Analysis.** Measured against the executed Term Sheet, 65.25% exceeds 65.00% by 0.25 percentage points, or **$180,000** (72,800,000 × 0.65 = 47,320,000). The Credit Memorandum never states this. There is no evidence that the Senior Loan Committee was asked to approve a departure from the agreed 65% covenant; the table presents the metric as compliant because the comparator column was changed to the 70% policy ceiling. Two distinct limits are being conflated: a policy maximum (70%) and a negotiated term (65%). A covenant that is breached the moment it is made is unenforceable in practice and creates an immediate default if the Lender later wishes to rely on it.

**Open.** Whether the Senior Loan Committee approved 65% or 70% for this transaction, and why the Term Sheet (the executed document) records 65%.

**Recommendation.** Either (a) reduce the Commitment to $47,320,000, which satisfies the 65% covenant but is not the binding constraint (see A-2), or (b) amend the covenant to 66% (or to 70% if that is genuinely the credit approval) and record that decision in the Committee minutes and in any amendment to the Term Sheet. Do not leave the documentation stating 65% while underwriting to 70%. Draft §2.6(b) and (c) apply the 65% covenant and provide for automatic reduction of the Commitment; Drafting Note 2 records the conflict.

## A-2 | LTC exceeds 80% and the proposed equity escrow does not cure it

**Record.** Term Sheet §6.1: maximum LTC of 80% of Total Project Costs, with Total Project Costs stated at $56,700,000. Credit Memorandum §5.3 and §9.1: LTC of 83.77%, an excess of $2,140,000, to be cured by a condition requiring the Borrower to deposit $2,140,000 in cash into a Bank-controlled equity escrow, "disbursed ahead of loan advances to fund project costs, thereby reducing the effective LTC to 80% or below" (condition 10.1.2).

**Analysis.** LTC is senior debt divided by Total Project Costs. Depositing $2,140,000 of equity changes neither numerator (the Commitment remains $47,500,000) nor denominator (Total Project Costs remain $56,700,000, because every cost is already funded by the $47,500,000 senior loan and the $9,200,000 mezzanine loan). The deposit therefore produces a $2,140,000 surplus, not a cure, and the reported ratio remains 83.77%. Two genuine cures exist: reduce the Commitment dollar-for-dollar to $45,360,000 (with the equity replacing the cancelled commitment), or enlarge the denominator by adding costs that are presently omitted — the $3,400,000 Existing Mortgage Payoff Amount (B-2) and approximately $680,000 of pre-closing demolition and remediation costs (C-8) — and fund them with equity, which produces 47,500,000 ÷ 60,100,000 = **79.03%** if the payoff is equity-funded, or 50,900,000 ÷ 60,100,000 = 84.69% if it is loan-funded (in which case the breach is worse).

**Open.** Whether the Lender's credit approval is for $47,500,000 or for a maximum LTC of 80%, and whether any reduction is acceptable to the Borrower and to the Mezzanine Lender (whose $9,200,000 is sized to fill the gap in a $56,700,000 budget).

**Recommendation.** Adopt the second cure, because it also solves B-2 and C-8 and increases rather than reduces the Borrower's equity at risk: require verified equity of **$4,080,000** — being the $3,400,000 payoff and approximately $680,000 of pre-closing costs — and add those amounts to the uses. On those figures Total Project Costs become $60,780,000 and LTC is 47,500,000 ÷ 60,780,000 = **78.15%**, so the separate $2,140,000 escrow is not required at all; the equity cure and the payoff are the same money. If the Borrower cannot fund $4,080,000 of genuinely new equity, reduce the Commitment to $45,360,000. If the Borrower cannot fund it, reduce the Commitment to $45,360,000. Draft §2.6, §7.6 and Schedule 3 implement this.

## A-3 | The Borrower net-worth covenant is arithmetically unachievable

**Record.** Term Sheet §6.4 and §13: minimum Borrower net worth of **$12,000,000**, tested annually; the Borrower's current net worth is stated at $14,200,000 (Credit Memorandum §2.1; Org chart §1.1). Term Sheet §20 and Article 12 of the Draft Loan Agreement require the Borrower to hold no assets other than the Property and Project assets and to incur no liabilities other than the Loan, the Mezzanine Loan and trade payables.

**Analysis.** On the transaction's own figures, Total Project Costs of $56,700,000 are funded by $47,500,000 of senior debt and $9,200,000 of mezzanine debt, with Borrower cash equity of zero (Term Sheet §5.1). At completion the Borrower's assets (the Project, at cost) approximate its liabilities, and book equity approximates zero; once capitalised interest, the Origination Fee and the Existing Mortgage are taken into account it is negative. The $14,200,000 figure is a pre-financing measurement of an entity whose principal asset is land now pledged as collateral for the senior loan and pledged as collateral for the mezzanine loan. As drafted, the covenant will be breached at closing and on every subsequent test date, which converts a credit protection into a permanent default and, worse, a reporting obligation that must be answered falsely or not at all.

**Open.** The basis of the $14,200,000 net worth figure (historical cost, appraised value, or including the land at $8,900,000 rather than $8,200,000) and whether the Borrower's statements include the Existing Mortgage as a liability.

**Recommendation.** Replace the Borrower net-worth covenant with (a) a Borrower liquidity covenant (unrestricted cash of not less than $250,000 at all times, rising to a $500,000 working capital reserve after substantial completion) and (b) guarantor net-worth and liquidity covenants (A-4). If a net-worth covenant is retained for conformity with policy, measure it at historical cost, exclude subordinated debt, and set the threshold at a figure the Borrower can actually meet (for example $1,000,000). Draft §11.1(e)–(g) contains both formulations with the Term Sheet figure bracketed; Drafting Note 10 explains the point.

## A-4 | There is no guarantor net-worth covenant, and the Term Sheet cross-reference is wrong

**Record.** Term Sheet §15 (Events of Default) includes "[a]ny Guarantor's net worth falls below the minimum threshold set forth in Section 6.4". Term Sheet §6.4 sets the *Borrower's* net worth at $12,000,000. The only guarantor covenant in the record is the $5,000,000 combined liquidity covenant in §6.5. Credit Memorandum §2.3.3 analyses guarantor net worth ($41,300,000) and liquidity ($9,300,000) but recommends no net-worth covenant.

**Analysis.** Section 15 refers to a threshold that does not exist for the person it names. Either the cross-reference is a drafting error or a guarantor net-worth covenant was intended and omitted. A liquidity covenant alone permits the Guarantors to hold their wealth in illiquid real estate — which is precisely what they do (Ms. Vasquez-Torres: $19,400,000 of $29,800,000 in real estate; Mr. Osei: $6,800,000 of $11,500,000).

**Open.** Whether the credit approval included a guarantor net-worth covenant at a specific level.

**Recommendation.** Add a combined guarantor net-worth covenant of not less than $30,000,000 and an individual covenant of not less than $8,000,000, tested annually, and correct the §15 cross-reference to point to the guarantor covenant rather than §6.4. Draft §11.1(g) and §16.1(n)(ii) do this.

## A-5 | The partial repayment guaranty is stated in two irreconcilable amounts

**Record.** Term Sheet §7.3: the Partial Repayment Guaranty is "in the amount of 25% of the Loan Amount (25% × $47,500,000 = $11,875,000)", joint and several, with "each Guarantor … individually liable for the full guaranteed amount". Credit Memorandum §2.3.1, §2.3.2 and §4.3: "each guarantor will provide a 25% partial repayment guaranty — 25% of the loan amount of $47,500,000 = $11,875,000 per guarantor".

**Analysis.** The Term Sheet describes one obligation of $11,875,000 on which each Guarantor is jointly and severally liable (so the Lender recovers $11,875,000 in total). The Credit Memorandum describes two obligations of $11,875,000 each ($23,750,000 in aggregate). The difference is $11,875,000 of recourse and would take Mr. Osei's exposure from 103% to 207% of his net worth — which the Credit Memorandum's own analysis implies was not intended.

**Open.** Which formulation the Senior Loan Committee approved.

**Recommendation.** Adopt the Term Sheet formulation (aggregate cap of $11,875,000, joint and several). If the credit approval intended $23,750,000, that is a change to an executed document requiring the Borrower's and the Guarantors' agreement, and it should be raised now rather than at signing. Draft §15.2 states the aggregate cap; Drafting Note 18 records the conflict.

## A-6 | The contingency is mischaracterised and may be inadequate

**Record.** Term Sheet §5.2 and §2 Exhibit C, Credit Memorandum §5.2 and the Appraisal §3 all show a single line of **$1,440,000** for "Contingency". Credit Memorandum §5.2 states that this "represents approximately 4.1% of hard construction costs ($1,440,000 ÷ $34,750,000 = 4.14%), which is adequate". The Borrower's Detailed Budget (lines 6.01 and 6.02) shows the $1,440,000 split into a **hard cost contingency of $1,140,000** and a **soft cost contingency of $300,000**.

**Analysis.** The hard cost contingency is $1,140,000, or **3.28%** of the $34,750,000 guaranteed maximum price — at the bottom of the 3% to 5% range that the Credit Memorandum itself describes as the policy norm, for a project with (i) a wood-frame superstructure over a concrete podium, (ii) an environmental land use restriction with a mandated vapour mitigation system, (iii) historic-district architectural review that can require design changes, and (iv) a 30-month programme. The 4.14% figure is arithmetically correct only because it counts the $300,000 soft cost contingency as hard cost contingency.

**Open.** Whether the Contractor's guaranteed maximum price includes a contractor-held contingency (which would be additional to the Borrower's line).

**Recommendation.** Require the hard cost contingency to be increased to 5% of hard costs ($1,737,500), or obtain the Contractor's confirmation of its own contingency and of the completeness of the guaranteed maximum price, and correct the characterisation in the Credit Memorandum and in any approval minute. Draft Schedule 3 records both the correct percentages and the recommended increase, and Draft §6.5(a) prevents the contingency from being drawn except against approved change orders.

## A-7 | The interest reserve has been modelled three ways and the results differ by up to $1.28 million

**Record.** The Borrower's Interest Reserve Analysis models a **surplus of $221,325** at an assumed SOFR of 3.50% (all-in 6.75%) over 30 months and concludes "APPEARS ADEQUATE". The same sheet's sensitivity table shows a **shortfall of $365,830** at a SOFR of 4.35% — which the table itself annotates as "Current market SOFR as of Jan 2025". Credit Memorandum §6.2, using an average outstanding balance of $28,500,000, computes a **shortfall of approximately $565,000** at 4.35% and **$1,278,000** at 5.35%, and concludes that the reserve "is tight under current rate assumptions".

**Analysis.** The three figures are not variants of one model; they use different rate assumptions and different draw curves, and the Borrower's own sensitivity contradicts its own conclusion. The Credit Memorandum's conclusion ("approves the $4,850,000 interest reserve as adequate based on current rate assumptions") is therefore not supported by its own arithmetic, which shows a shortfall at current rates. Two further points compound the exposure: the reserve is drawn in full at closing, so it bears interest itself ("interest on interest"), and the draw schedule on which the Borrower's model rests is itself out of balance (E-4). On the Credit Memorandum's own method at 4.35%, the reserve should be approximately **$5,215,830**; at 5.35%, approximately $6,128,000. For comparison, interest on a full draw for the whole 30 months at 7.60% would be $9,025,000.

**Open.** The rate at which the Lender's credit approver tested the reserve; whether a larger reserve is acceptable given its effect on Total Project Costs and therefore on LTC (a larger reserve increases Total Project Costs and improves LTC — a point in favour of increasing it).

**Recommendation.** Re-model the reserve on the corrected Draw Schedule at the then-current SOFR; increase the Line 4.01 amount to the modelled requirement (approximately $5,215,830 at 4.35%); and, in any event, retain the three contractual protections that are the real mitigants — the per-draw adequacy test, the five-business-day replenishment obligation and the monthly reconciliation (Draft §7.4, §7.5 and Schedule 7). The Carry Guaranty is the backstop and should be confirmed to extend to reserve shortfalls, not merely to post-reserve interest (Draft §15.1(b)).

## A-8 | The Appraiser's operating expenses omit the Brightleaf Historic District assessment

**Record.** Title Commitment Special Exception 11(b) records an assessment obligation of **$0.12 per square foot of gross building area per annum**, adjustable annually by the Association. Appraisal §7 lists nine operating expense categories totalling $1,262,100 and contains no line for any owners' association or district assessment.

**Analysis.** On 287,500 square feet of gross building area the assessment is **$34,500 per annum**. Adjusted NOI is $4,842,300, the DSCR is 1.299x and the as-stabilised value at the Appraiser's 6.40% cap rate is $75,660,938. The conclusion is unaffected in kind (the transaction still passes 1.25x), but three figures used throughout the record — NOI, DSCR and as-stabilised value — are all slightly overstated, and the omission will recur in every annual DSCR test.

**Open.** Whether the declaration's assessment is currently levied and at what rate, and whether any special assessment is contemplated for district-wide improvements.

**Recommendation.** Require the Appraiser to add the assessment to operating expenses by addendum and re-run the as-stabilised value and the DSCR; require the Borrower to pay the assessment when due and to covenant to do so (Draft §9.1(f) and §20.3); and reflect the assessment in the annual DSCR computation in Schedule 7.

## A-9 | The Appraisal contains a dating impossibility and expressly makes no adjustment for the use restriction

**Record.** The Appraisal is dated November 15, 2024, with a date of value of November 15, 2024. Section 2 records that "the appraiser reviewed the preliminary title commitment … (Commitment Number NC-2024-88712, dated December 5, 2024)" and that "the title commitment is dated subsequent to the effective date of the appraisal". Section 8 states that the project plans "include residential units on the upper floors of the building positioned above this area", that the appraiser "expresses no opinion as to the legal permissibility of the planned residential use", and that the appraiser "has not adjusted the valuation conclusions to account for any potential impact of the NFA use restriction". Section 2 also states that the land use restriction "is memorialized in a Declaration of Land Use Restrictions recorded at the Durham County Register of Deeds".

**Analysis.** A report dated November 15, 2024 cannot have reviewed an instrument that did not exist until December 5, 2024; the report has either been amended without a revised date or date of value, or the reference has been inserted. Either way, the version in the file is not a stable record, and FIRREA/USPAP compliance for a February 28, 2025 closing depends on the report being the final, dated report. More materially, the Appraisal's as-completed and as-stabilised values of $72,800,000 and $76,200,000 rest on the full 212-unit programme while expressly assuming (Extraordinary Assumption 7) that the Borrower will comply with the restricted use "or will obtain any necessary modifications". If the 14 units above the restricted area cannot be delivered (C-3), the values, the LTV and the DSCR are all overstated and the Lender has no valuation that addresses the risk it is actually taking.

**Open.** Whether an amended appraisal exists; the appraiser's view of value with the restricted-area units removed; the identity and recording information of the land use restriction instrument.

**Recommendation.** Obtain from Ridgeline (a) a clean, dated recertification identifying its final version, (b) a correction of the December 5, 2024 reference or an explanation, (c) an addendum stating the value conclusion if residential occupancy above the restricted area is not permitted, and (d) identification of the recorded land use restriction by book and page. Draft §4.1(d) makes delivery of the addendum a closing condition; Drafting Note 6 records the point.

## A-10 | The combined senior and mezzanine debt service has never been tested and does not work

**Record.** Credit Memorandum §6.1 tests DSCR against senior debt service only ($3,728,750 at 7.85% on $47,500,000), producing 1.31x and a "Pass". Credit Memorandum §4.4 describes the Mezzanine Loan as bearing a fixed rate of 12.50% per annum, with interest "paid-in-kind during construction; cash pay during mini-perm". Term Sheet §1.4 states that the mezzanine maturity is coterminous with the senior loan. No document computes the Project's ability to service both loans.

**Analysis.** Mezzanine interest at 12.50% on $9,200,000 is **$1,150,000** per annum. Combined annual cash debt service is $3,728,750 + $1,150,000 = **$4,878,750**, against stabilised NOI of $4,876,800 — a combined coverage ratio of **0.9996x**. If the coupon is paid in kind through the 30-month construction period, the balance accretes by approximately $2,875,000 to approximately $12,075,000, annual mezzanine interest becomes $1,509,375, combined service becomes $5,238,125 and combined coverage falls to **0.931x**. On either basis the Project cannot pay both loans from operations at stabilisation. Because the mezzanine loan is structurally subordinated, this is not a senior payment default — the senior debt is paid first and remains covered at 1.31x — but it has three consequences that the record does not address: (i) the Borrower will have no cash flow with which to pay the mezzanine lender, (ii) the mezzanine lender is an affiliate of a 28% member (D-3) and will have both the motive and the standing to act, and (iii) a sale or refinancing at maturity, or an extension, becomes close to inevitable, which places the entire credit on the extension conditions in Article 21.

**Open.** The actual mezzanine coupon and payment mechanics, the PIK terms and the intercreditor covenants; and whether the Junior Lender has agreed to any cash trap or coverage test.

**Recommendation.** (a) Require a **combined** debt service coverage test of not less than 1.00x at stabilisation and not less than 1.00x as a condition to any extension (Draft §21.2(d)); (b) require the mezzanine loan's payment terms to be subject to a cash trap on the same DSCR trigger as the senior loan, implemented through the Intercreditor Agreement; (c) consider requiring an interest reserve for the mezzanine loan or a coverage reserve at the Borrower level; and (d) reflect the point in the Credit Memorandum, which presently understates the Project's leverage. Drafting Note 17(b) sets out the arithmetic.

## A-11 | The HVCRE analysis counts borrowed money as contributed capital

**Record.** Credit Memorandum §12 computes contributed capital of $10,340,000 ($2,140,000 of proposed equity escrow plus $8,200,000 of land at acquisition cost) as 14.20% of the as-completed appraised value of $72,800,000, below the 15% threshold ($10,920,000), and concludes that the loan will be classified as HVCRE with a 150% risk weight, accepting a $580,000 shortfall.

**Analysis.** The 12 C.F.R. Part 3 definition of "contributed capital" measures capital contributed by the borrower; borrowed funds, including mezzanine debt, are not contributed capital. If, as the Borrower's Detailed Budget (line 1.01) indicates, the land was funded by mezzanine loan proceeds, then genuine contributed capital is at most $2,140,000 — **2.94%** of as-completed value — and the shortfall to the 15% threshold is approximately $8,780,000, not $580,000. Even on the Credit Memorandum's own figures the loan is an HVCRE exposure, so the classification conclusion is unchanged; what changes is the credibility of the analysis and the feasibility of any future attempt to cure it.

**Open.** The actual source of the land funding (D-4) and the Lender's regulatory reporting treatment.

**Recommendation.** Correct the analysis once the source-of-funds work in D-4 is complete, and do not present the $580,000 figure to the Committee as the size of the HVCRE problem. If avoiding HVCRE classification matters to the Lender, the route is genuine equity, which is the same route that cures A-2 and B-2.
