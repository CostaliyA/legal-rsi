# 6. TITLE, SURVEY AND SITE DESCRIPTION

## B-1 | The record describes the site's geometry in three incompatible ways

**Record.** (a) **Street orientation and parcel adjacency.** The two metes-and-bounds descriptions in Schedule A, Item 6 of the Title Commitment each begin at an iron pipe in the "eastern right-of-way margin of Foster Street" and run from there easterly, so both parcels front Foster Street on their **western** boundary; Parcel 2 begins at the "southwestern corner of Parcel 1" and runs south, so Parcel 2 lies **south** of Parcel 1. The Appraisal §2 agrees ("approximately 420 feet of frontage along Foster Street on the western boundary"). The Phase I ESA §2.1 and §1, by contrast, state that the site "fronts Foster Street to the **north**", describe Parcel 2 as "the eastern parcel", and locate the affected area in the northeast corner of Parcel 2 "bounded by Foster Street to the north". (b) **Frontage.** The Appraisal reports approximately 420 feet; the metes and bounds produce 298.62 + 201.88 = **500.50 feet**. (c) **Acreage.** The Title Commitment allocates **2.09** and **1.38** acres (confirmed by the metes and bounds: 91,202 sq ft = 2.094 acres and 60,086 sq ft = 1.379 acres); the Phase I ESA allocates **1.89** and **1.58** acres. The combined 3.47 acres agrees; the parcel-by-parcel split does not. (d) **Restricted Area dimensions.** The Phase I ESA §4 describes the remediated area as approximately 100 feet by 165 feet (0.379 acres); its own Figure 3 describes the affected area as extending approximately 125 feet by 130 feet (0.373 acres).

**Analysis.** The restricted area is defined by reference to a corner of a parcel, and the record cannot agree which corner or which parcel. Under the title geometry the "northeast corner of Parcel 0821-04-72-4102" is an interior point on the eastern boundary line roughly 300 feet east of Foster Street, not a street corner; under the Phase I ESA's orientation it abuts Foster Street. That difference determines where the vapour mitigation system must be installed, which units sit above it, what the recorded deed notice must describe, and whether the groundwater flow and neighbouring-use assumptions in the Phase II work are right. Because the ALTA/NSPS survey is still outstanding (Credit Memorandum §13: "To be obtained — Pending"), nothing in the record resolves it.

**Open.** The survey; the Phase II delineation figure; the site plan drawing set dated September 12, 2024; the parcel mapping in the Durham County GIS.

**Recommendation.** Do not permit the deed notice to be recorded, the vapour mitigation design to be finalised or the Deed of Trust to be recorded until the Survey has been delivered and the environmental delineation has been tied to metes and bounds on it. Require the Architect and Sentinel to confirm jointly, in writing, the location of the restricted area relative to the building footprint. Draft §4.1(i), Schedule 1 and Drafting Note 15 implement this.

## B-2 | The $3,400,000 Existing Mortgage has no source of funds in the record

**Record.** Title Commitment Schedule B-I, Requirement 4, requires satisfaction and release of the deed of trust recorded in Book 6215, Page 892, Durham County Registry, dated September 14, 2018, securing an original indebtedness of **$3,400,000** in favour of Triangle Commercial Bank, on pain of the policy being issued subject to Special Exception 10. The title officer notes that Borrower's counsel advised on November 22, 2024 that the indebtedness "has been partially repaid" and that the "remaining outstanding balance will be satisfied in full at or prior to closing from the Borrower's own funds or from closing proceeds", and that the Company "has not independently verified the outstanding balance". Credit Memorandum §9.7 records the amount as $3,400,000 and states that it "will be satisfied at closing from the proceeds of the senior construction loan or from borrower funds". Term Sheet §5.1 and §5.2 and Credit Memorandum Appendix C contain no line item for it in either sources or uses.

**Analysis.** Three separate problems. First, the amount is unverified: the Title Commitment records a partial repayment, so $3,400,000 is likely to overstate the payoff. Second, there is no source: the senior Loan is fully committed to the Approved Budget, the mezzanine loan is fully committed to land and $1,000,000 of developer fee, and the Borrower has zero cash equity. Third, the structure is unclear: the deed of trust was granted by Foster Street Properties LLC in 2018 — before the Borrower acquired title by Special Warranty Deed dated June 12, 2023 — so the Lender needs to know whether the Borrower assumed the debt or took subject to it, and whether the $8,200,000 "land cost" is inclusive or exclusive of the assumed liability. If it is an assumed debt, the Borrower's net land equity is approximately $5,500,000 ($8,900,000 as-is value less $3,400,000), not $8,200,000, and the capital stack is worse than every document states.

**Open.** A current payoff letter (with per diem); the 2023 acquisition documents; confirmation of assumption versus purchase subject to.

**Recommendation.** Obtain the payoff letter before the Loan Amount is fixed; add the payoff as a use in the Approved Budget; fund it from verified equity (which also assists A-2); require simultaneous recording of the cancellation and the Deed of Trust; and confirm that the title policy will be issued without Special Exception 10. Draft §4.1(h), §14.3, Schedule 2 (Exception 10) and Drafting Note 16 do this.

## B-3 | The Title Commitment is internally inconsistent about who granted the Existing Mortgage, and expires before any likely closing extension

**Record.** Schedule B-I, Requirement 4 describes the Existing Mortgage as "from Harborview Capital Partners LLC (as successor-in-title to Foster Street Properties LLC, the original grantor)". Schedule B-II, Exception 10 describes it as "from Foster Street Properties LLC (predecessor-in-title to the current vested owner, Harborview Capital Partners LLC)". The commitment expires on **June 5, 2025** (Note 1) unless extended by endorsement.

**Analysis.** The first description implies the Borrower is the grantor; the second says the seller was. The distinction matters for B-2 (assumption versus subject-to) and for any affidavit of no liens. Separately, a closing on February 28, 2025 is within the commitment period, but any extension of the closing date beyond June 5, 2025 requires a written extension, which is not provided for in the Term Sheet.

**Open.** Confirmation from the Title Company of the grantor and of the recording particulars of any assumption agreement.

**Recommendation.** Require the Title Company to correct the inconsistency in writing before closing; require a date-down at closing and again after the first advance (B-9); and add a covenant requiring the Borrower to obtain any extension of the commitment at its own cost if closing is delayed. Draft §4.1(g), §4.2 and §14.2.

## B-4 | The NCDEQ land use restriction is not disclosed in the Title Commitment at all

**Record.** The Phase I ESA §7, Condition 4, requires a deed notice "within 60 days of the date of the NFA Letter — that is, by November 29, 2024", describing the restricted area, the contamination and the non-residential use restriction; the consultant states that it "was not provided evidence that the required deed notice has been recorded". The Appraisal §8 states that "[t]his land use restriction is memorialized in a Declaration of Land Use Restrictions recorded at the Durham County Register of Deeds". Schedule B-II of the Title Commitment (effective December 5, 2024) lists thirteen exceptions and contains **no** reference to any deed notice or declaration of land use restrictions; Note 5 expressly excludes environmental matters from coverage.

**Analysis.** One of two things is true, and they have opposite consequences. If the instrument was recorded, the Title Commitment failed to disclose a recorded encumbrance that materially restricts use of part of the Property — a title examination failure that must be corrected, the instrument added to the Permitted Exceptions, and an endorsement obtained. If it was not recorded, the Borrower is in breach of a condition of the NFA Letter, the NFA determination is at risk, and the Borrower has been in breach since November 29, 2024 — a pre-existing Event of Default at closing. The Credit Memorandum does not mention the deed notice at all.

**Open.** A title rundown for the period from November 29, 2024; the Borrower's recording evidence; NCDEQ's position if the deadline has passed.

**Recommendation.** Require, as a condition to closing: the recorded instrument (or a NCDEQ letter confirming that the NFA remains in effect notwithstanding the delay), its addition to Schedule 2 as a Permitted Exception, and an ALTA 8.1 environmental protection lien endorsement together with (if available) a land use restriction endorsement. Add a covenant to record the notice immediately if it has not been recorded and to deliver proof of recording. Draft §4.1(e)(iii), §13.2(d), Schedule 2 (Exception 14) and Drafting Note 12.

## B-5 | Architectural review approval is assumed by the Appraisal, missing according to the Title Officer, and required by the Credit Memorandum

**Record.** The Appraisal §2 states that "the architectural design has been reviewed and approved by the Brightleaf Historic District Association in accordance with the CC&Rs". The Title Officer's note to Special Exception 11 states that "the Company has not received evidence that the current project plans … have been submitted to or approved by the Brightleaf Historic District Association Architectural Review Committee", and describes the Association's right to seek injunctive relief to halt unapproved construction. Credit Memorandum §9.8 and condition 10.1.14 require evidence of approval or waiver as a closing condition. The Declaration (Exception 11(d)) requires submission not fewer than **90 days** before the anticipated commencement of construction and conditions the building permit on approval or the expiry of a 60-day deemed-approval period.

**Analysis.** This is not only a covenant question: if the City will not issue a building permit without approval (or deemed approval), construction cannot lawfully begin at closing, and the 90-day submission period means that a submission made now would not support a February 28, 2025 start. The record does not establish that any submission has been made. The Appraisal's assumption that approval has been obtained is unsupported and is repeated in the Lender's own file.

**Open.** The ARC submission date, the ARC's response, and the City's permit sequencing.

**Recommendation.** Make evidence of ARC submission and approval (or expiry of the deemed-approval period, or a written waiver) a hard closing condition, and require the Borrower to deliver copies of all ARC correspondence. Confirm with the City of Durham that no permit will issue without it and re-base the construction start date accordingly. Draft §4.1(k), §20.3, Schedule 2 (Exception 11) and Drafting Note 22.

## B-6 | The CC&R assessment and the Association's injunctive rights are not reflected in the underwriting or the covenants

**Record.** Title Commitment Exception 11(b): assessments of $0.12 per square foot of gross building area per annum, subject to annual adjustment. Exception 11(e): the Association and any affected owner may seek injunctive relief, including to halt construction that has not received ARC approval. Neither Term Sheet §13 nor Credit Memorandum §10.3 contains any covenant to pay the assessment or to comply with the declaration.

**Analysis.** The assessment is an operating expense omitted from the Appraisal (A-8) and a lien risk if unpaid (the declaration's enforcement mechanism is not in the record). The injunctive right is a construction-completion risk that no Loan Document addresses: an unapproved change of exterior finish could stop work, with a Completion Guaranty already engaged.

**Open.** The full text of the declaration (including whether unpaid assessments become a lien, and whether the Association's consent is required for any collateral assignment).

**Recommendation.** Add express covenants to comply with the declaration, to pay assessments when due, and to obtain ARC approval before any exterior modification (Draft §9.1(q) and §20.3); add the declaration to the Permitted Exceptions in the qualified form in Draft Schedule 2; require the Borrower to deliver the full recorded declaration and the current assessment notice; and require the Lender's consent to any plan change that would require ARC approval (Draft §6.4(c)).

## B-7 | The Appraisal relies on a 20-foot alley that does not appear in title

**Record.** Appraisal §2: "Secondary vehicular access is available from a 20-foot public alley along the eastern boundary of the site … The proposed site plan incorporates a primary vehicular entrance from Foster Street and a secondary entrance and loading/service access from the public alley to the east." Neither legal description in the Title Commitment refers to an alley; Schedule B-II discloses no access easement other than the Duke Energy and City stormwater easements.

**Analysis.** Loading and service access is a code requirement for a mixed-use building with 18,400 square feet of retail. If the alley is a dedicated public right-of-way it is not an exception to title and the absence of a title reference is not itself a problem — but the survey must confirm it, and the record contains no confirmation. If it is a private easement, it must appear in title and be insured. If it does not exist as a legal right of access, the site plan cannot be built as appraised and the retail leasing assumptions are affected.

**Open.** The Survey; the City's right-of-way map; the site plan.

**Recommendation.** Require the Survey to depict the alley and to state whether it is a public right-of-way or an easement of record; require an access endorsement to the Title Policy covering both Foster Street and the alley; and add any recorded access easement to Schedule 2. Draft §4.1(i)(iv), §4.2(ii) and Schedule 2 (Exception 15).

## B-8 | Work performed before closing creates a mechanic's lien priority risk

**Record.** Demolition was completed in August 2024 and environmental remediation in September 2024 (Phase I ESA §2.2, §6); the site is "graded and secured with temporary construction fencing" and utilities have been "stubbed to the property boundaries" (§2.1). The Borrower's Detailed Budget line 2.01 includes $3,180,000 for "Site work — demolition, grading, utilities, paving". Title Commitment Note 4 records that under N.C.G.S. §44A-10 a claim of lien relates back to the first furnishing of labour or materials, and recommends an owner's and contractor's affidavit of no prior work and appropriate waivers. Standard Exception 4 (mechanics' liens not shown by the public records, including under N.C.G.S. Chapter 44A) cannot be deleted.

**Analysis.** Because work was performed on the site months before the Deed of Trust will be recorded, a claimant unpaid for that earlier work could assert a lien that relates back and primes the Lender. The Approved Budget also appears to charge the same demolition work again within the guaranteed maximum price, which raises both a double-funding question and a question about whether the Contractor has already performed work.

**Open.** Who performed the demolition, the site clearing and the utility stubbing; whether they have been paid; whether the Contractor's GMP contract includes work already performed; and the status of the lien agent designation.

**Recommendation.** Obtain (a) an owner's affidavit and a contractor's affidavit that no work has been performed under the Construction Contract before recording, (b) unconditional statutory waivers and evidence of payment from the demolition, site work and remediation contractors (including Sentinel Environmental Consulting LLC), (c) a lien agent designation under N.C.G.S. §44A-11.3 with the lien agent information on the permit and, where required, the Deed of Trust, and (d) a date-down endorsement immediately after the first advance. Draft §4.1(m), §9.1(n) and Drafting Note 15(f).

## B-9 | The survey and several endorsements required by the transaction are still outstanding

**Record.** Term Sheet §11 and §18 require an ALTA/NSPS survey satisfactory to the Lender and the title company; Title Commitment Schedule B-I, Requirement 5 requires a 2021 ALTA/NSPS survey (with Table A items) dated or re-certified within 90 days of closing. # 7. ENVIRONMENTAL MATTERS

## C-1 | The NFA Letter has four conditions; the Term Sheet describes one and the Credit Memorandum two

**Record.** The Phase I ESA §7 records the NFA Letter dated September 30, 2024 as conditional upon (1) a soil vapour mitigation system beneath any enclosed structure over the affected area, with as-built drawings and a performance verification report to NCDEQ within 90 days of completion of the building foundation; (2) restriction of the affected approximately 0.38 acres to non-residential use; (3) a NCDEQ-approved **soil management plan** before any subsurface disturbance in the affected area; and (4) a **deed notice** recorded within 60 days. Term Sheet §17 refers only to the vapour system and states that the NFA letter "authorizes commercial use of the affected area … conditioned on the installation and maintenance of a soil vapor mitigation system". Credit Memorandum §8.2 and §9.5 describe two conditions (vapour system and land use restriction).

**Analysis.** Both the Term Sheet and the Credit Memorandum omit the soil management plan and the deed notice from the conditions the Borrower is required to satisfy and the Lender is required to monitor. The Borrower's obligations in the Loan Agreement would therefore be narrower than its obligations to NCDEQ, which means the Lender could have no contractual remedy for the two most likely breaches. This is a straightforward gap between the regulatory instrument and the finance documents and should be closed.

**Open.** The NFA Letter itself (only summarised in the record) and any subsequent NCDEQ correspondence.

**Recommendation.** Reproduce all four conditions in the Loan Agreement as affirmative covenants with compliance as an Event of Default, add a compliance schedule to the reporting requirements, and make satisfaction of each a condition to closing (or, for the vapour system, to the relevant advance and the retainage release). Draft §13.2, §13.5, §16.1(k), Schedule 7 and Drafting Note 12.

## C-2 | The deed notice appears to be overdue

See **B-4**, which analyses the same facts from the title perspective. From the environmental perspective the point is narrower: the deadline was **November 29, 2024**, the Phase I ESA recorded non-recording as of October 28, 2024, and nothing in the record shows that it was later recorded or that NCDEQ has been asked to extend or forgive the deadline. An unrecorded deed notice is a present breach of a condition of the Borrower's NFA determination, and the Lender should not accept a representation that it has been recorded without a certified copy of the recorded instrument.

**Recommendation.** Obtain the recorded instrument or a written NCDEQ response before closing; if the deadline has been missed, require an application to NCDEQ for confirmation that the NFA remains effective, and consider whether the Borrower's failure should be treated as an Event of Default waived only by the closing. Draft §13.2(d) and §4.1(e)(iii).

## C-3 | The claim that 14 residential units sit above the restricted area is unsupported, and the exposure is material

**Record.** Credit Memorandum §8.2 and §9.5 assert as fact that the plans "position 14 residential units in the building above this area". The Phase I ESA §8.5 states that the consultant "did not independently verify whether any residential units would be positioned directly above the affected 0.38-acre area … as this determination requires a detailed overlay of the proposed building floor plans with the environmental delineation map". The Appraisal §8 states that the plans "include residential units on the upper floors … positioned above this area", expresses "no opinion as to the legal permissibility", and made no valuation adjustment. Term Sheet §17 makes no reference to any unit conflict at all. The figure "14" does not appear in any document other than the Credit Memorandum, and corresponds to the number of three-bedroom units in the unit mix.

**Analysis.** The Credit Memorandum's own sensitivity is that losing 14 units reduces NOI by $377,160 (14 × $2,245 × 12) to $4,499,640 and reduces the DSCR to 1.21x, below the 1.25x covenant. That analysis is arithmetically correct and establishes that the issue is material if it exists. But the record does not establish that it exists, and the Lender is at risk of two errors simultaneously: assuming a conflict that does not exist (and demanding a redesign that is unnecessary), or accepting a representation that there is no conflict on the basis of documents that expressly decline to confirm it. The NFA Letter's restriction is on *land use of the affected area*; whether enclosed residential space above grade-level restricted soil is a prohibited "residential use" of the affected area is a question for NCDEQ and North Carolina environmental counsel, not for the Lender or the appraiser.

**Open.** The Architect's overlay of the approved plans on Figure 3 of the Phase I ESA; NCDEQ's interpretation; the number and type of units actually affected.

**Recommendation.** Require the Architect's overlay before closing, and require one of three resolutions as a closing condition: (a) a written NCDEQ modification permitting residential use of the affected area (which the Phase I ESA warns "would likely require additional environmental investigation, risk assessment, and potentially additional remediation"); (b) written NCDEQ confirmation that residential occupancy of space above the restricted area is permissible with the vapour system installed and operating; or (c) an approved redesign. Require an addendum to the Appraisal addressing value without the affected units, and withhold advances for work in or above the affected area until the point is resolved. Draft §4.1(e)(vi), §13.4, §13.4(d)–(e) and Drafting Note 13.

## C-4 | The soil management plan is a pre-construction condition that no document requires

**Record.** Phase I ESA §7, Condition 3: any excavation, grading or soil disturbance in the affected area must follow a NCDEQ-approved soil management plan, which "must be submitted to and approved by NCDEQ prior to commencement of any construction activities involving subsurface disturbance within the affected area". Recommendation 4 of the Phase I ESA advises that the plan be incorporated into the construction specifications and made available to the Contractor and subcontractors. Neither the Term Sheet nor the Credit Memorandum requires it.

**Analysis.** Foundation and site work will begin at or immediately after closing. If the plan is not approved by then, work in the affected area cannot proceed lawfully, and the Borrower will be in breach of the NFA Letter on the first day of construction. The Phase I ESA notes that residual concentrations above residential screening levels remain along the eastern boundary at 10 to 14 feet below grade, so the plan is not a formality — it governs screening, stockpiling, disposal and worker protection in the area where the Contractor will be excavating.

**Open.** Whether a plan has been drafted, and NCDEQ's approval timeline.

**Recommendation.** Make NCDEQ approval of the soil management plan a condition to closing (or, at the latest, to the first advance in respect of site work), incorporate it into the Construction Contract by change order, and make compliance a condition to each advance in the affected area and an Event of Default if breached. Draft §4.1(e)(iv), §13.2(c), §13.5 and §9.1(l).

## C-5 | The vapour mitigation system is more prescriptive than the documents describe and its cost is not separately identified

**Record.** Phase I ESA §7, Condition 1: the system must be a **passive sub-slab depressurization system with the capability of conversion to an active depressurization system**, designed by a professional engineer licensed in North Carolina, installed in accordance with NCDEQ guidelines, with as-built drawings and a performance verification report submitted within 90 days of completion of the building foundation. The soil gas results show benzene at 185 µg/m³ against a residential vapour intrusion screening level of 31 µg/m³ — approximately six times the screening level. Term Sheet §17 refers only to "a soil vapor mitigation system in the parking garage structure". Detailed Budget line 2.08 ($3,590,000, parking structure) notes only that it "Includes soil vapor mitigation system per NCDEQ NFA requirements"; there is no separate cost.

**Analysis.** The system must be designed and durably maintained, with a conversion capability that implies ongoing monitoring and a possible future capital cost. Because it sits inside a single budget line, the Lender cannot test the adequacy of the amount allowed, and it cannot identify any reduction if the scope is later value-engineered. The 90-day reporting period also creates a compliance milestone shortly after foundation completion that no document tracks.

**Open.** The engineer's design and budget for the system; whether the Contractor's GMP price includes the conversion capability and the verification report.

**Recommendation.** Require the design to be included in the Approved Plans and the GMP contract, require a separate breakdown of the system's cost in the Approved Budget, prohibit any change order affecting it without the Lender's consent and NCDEQ approval, require annual inspection and testing with results delivered to the Lender, and add the 90-day NCDEQ submission to the reporting schedule and to the Release Conditions. Draft §13.3, §6.4(a)(iii), §6.7(f) and Schedule 7.

## C-6 | The record gives three different accounts of the underground storage tank

Draft §4.1(f) and Drafting Note 14.

## C-10 | Consider whether pollution legal liability insurance should replace the consultant's liability cap

## C-11 | Residual contamination remains along the eastern boundary, constrained by a sanitary sewer main

**Record.** Phase I ESA §6.1: confirmation sampling showed residual contamination remains in situ below non-residential screening levels "throughout the excavation area", but above residential screening levels "in certain areas at depths of 10 to 14 feet below grade, particularly along the eastern boundary where excavation was limited by the proximity of the adjoining property and critical underground utility infrastructure (a 12-inch sanitary sewer main and associated service laterals)". §5.3: dissolved benzene was detected in MW-02 at 0.8 µg/L against a 2L groundwater standard of 1.0 µg/L; post-remediation monitoring showed all dissolved constituents below 2L standards.

**Analysis.** Three points follow. The remaining contamination is where future utility work, landscaping or foundation activities are most likely to occur, which is why the soil management plan matters (C-4). A 12-inch sanitary sewer main along the eastern boundary does not appear in the Title Commitment's Schedule B-II; if it is a public main in an unrecorded easement or in the public right-of-way, the Lender should confirm both its location and its maintenance obligations, because the Survey will show it and a title exception may be required. And the groundwater results, while compliant, are one monitoring round away from a different conclusion, which supports voluntary monitoring.

**Open.** The utility location from the Survey; the City's sewer easement documentation.

**Recommendation.** Require the Survey to depict the sewer main and any easement; require a title exception or an endorsement covering it; require compliance with the soil management plan for any work within the affected area; and consider requiring voluntary annual groundwater monitoring as a covenant (Draft §13.1 and §13.3). Draft Note to Schedule 1 records the geometry point.

# 8. STRUCTURE, SINGLE-PURPOSE ENTITY AND INTERCREDITOR

## D-1 | Both loans are secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests and no intercreditor agreement exists

**Record.** Term Sheet §1.4: the mezzanine loan "shall be secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests in the Borrower"; an intercreditor agreement "is currently under negotiation and must be executed prior to or simultaneously with the closing". Term Sheet §8 lists a senior pledge of 100% by all members. Org chart §1.2 and §2.5 state that the mezzanine pledge is in place and that the intercreditor agreement has been "negotiated but is not yet executed". Credit Memorandum §4.2, §9.4 and condition 10.1.3 recommend "option (b)": senior first, mezzanine second, with a 90-day standstill, cure rights, a purchase option and bankruptcy protections.

**Analysis.** Two first-priority pledges of the same collateral cannot both exist. Until the intercreditor agreement is executed there is no mechanism for determining priority, and in an insolvency the dispute itself would delay the Lender's remedies. The record is also silent on perfection: a pledge of LLC interests is perfected by control (delivery of certificates with transfer powers, or a control agreement for uncertificated interests) or by filing, and the operating agreement does not address certifications.

**Open.** Whether certificates have been issued; whether the mezzanine pledge has already been perfected; the draft intercreditor agreement.

**Recommendation.** Make execution of the intercreditor agreement on the Credit Memorandum's terms a hard closing condition (not waivable); require the senior pledge to be perfected by delivery of certificates, an undated transfer power and an irrevocable proxy, with a UCC-1 in any event; and require the operating agreement amendment described in D-2. Draft §14.4, §14.5 and §4.1(q).

## D-2 | The operating agreement requires a 75% member vote for the Loan itself and permits pledges only with the managing member's approval

**Record.** Org chart §2.2 (operating agreement §3.2): the prior written approval of members holding at least **75%** of the membership interests is required for "any financing or refinancing of the Property in a principal amount in excess of $1,000,000", "any amendment to this Operating Agreement" and "any voluntary dissolution of the Company or the filing of any petition for relief under any federal or state bankruptcy or insolvency law". §9.1: no member may pledge its interest without the prior written consent of the managing member and members holding at least 75%.

**Analysis.** The senior Loan itself requires a 75% member approval, and so do the amending and restating of the operating agreement (needed for the SPE covenants and the independent manager) and the express authorisation of the senior pledge. On the ownership percentages (62% / 28% / 10%), Ms. Vasquez-Torres and Mr. Osei together hold only 72% — so **Brightleaf Investors Group LLC's vote is required for every one of them**. That entity is the affiliate of the Mezzanine Lender (D-3). The Lender is therefore relying on the party with the adverse interest to consent to the protections that constrain it.

**Open.** Whether written consents have been obtained and whether Brightleaf has conditioned its consent on anything.

**Recommendation.** Require executed written consents of all three members to the Loan, the pledge, the operating agreement amendment and the SPE and independent manager provisions, delivered at closing, and require the amendment to be filed and effective before the Deed of Trust is recorded. Draft §4.1(c)(iii) and §4.1(c)(ii).

## D-3 | A 28% member received its interest for no cash in exchange for arranging the mezzanine loan from its own affiliate

**Record.** Org chart §2.3 (operating agreement §5.1(b)): Brightleaf Investors Group LLC "shall make an initial capital contribution of Zero Dollars ($0) at the time of formation" and receives a **28%** membership interest "in consideration of its commitment to arrange third-party mezzanine financing for the Project". Org chart §2.3: the mezzanine financing was arranged through Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC, "an entity under the common control of Thomas R. Chen, who also serves as Managing Member of Brightleaf Investors Group LLC". Credit Memorandum §9.3 identifies the substantive consolidation risk.

**Analysis.** Three consequences. First, no member other than Ms. Vasquez-Torres (through the land) has contributed capital, which is the premise of D-4. Second, the 28% holder has a negative control over major decisions (D-2) and its affiliate is the subordinated lender, so the same economic interest sits on both sides of the senior loan and controls the borrower's bankruptcy decision. Third, an interest issued for services rather than capital raises questions about the Borrower's true equity and about the arm's-length character of the arrangements, which the Lender should disclose to its credit approvers rather than leave in the file.

**Open.** The instrument by which the interest was issued; whether any value was ascribed to it; whether Brightleaf's consent to the Loan has conditions.

**Recommendation.** Require full disclosure of all agreements among the Borrower, Brightleaf, the Mezzanine Lender and Mr. Chen; require the independent manager and non-consolidation opinion (D-5); require the intercreditor agreement to include express acknowledgement of the affiliate relationship and a prohibition on the mezzanine lender using its affiliate's voting rights to impede the Lender; and require all affiliate transactions to be on arm's-length terms (Draft §14.7, §10.1(f) and §12.4).

## D-4 | The record gives three irreconcilable accounts of how the land was funded

**Record.** (a) Org chart §1.3 and operating agreement §5.1(a): Ms. Vasquez-Torres contributed the land "originally acquired … in a transaction funded from her personal resources" at an $8,200,000 cost basis, and Brightleaf and Mr. Osei each contributed "$0 cash to date". (b) Credit Memorandum §2.2: the land "was acquired by Ms. Vasquez-Torres on behalf of the Borrower through capital calls from all three members, including Brightleaf Investors Group LLC". (c) Detailed Budget line 1.01 (and its "Notes" column): the land was "Previously closed; funded by **mezzanine loan proceeds** and member capital calls", with the $8,200,000 shown in the "Funded by Mezzanine Loan" column and $0 in the "Funded by Borrower Equity" column.

**Analysis.** The three accounts are mutually exclusive. The Borrower's own budget allocates the land to the mezzanine column. If that is correct then (i) the Borrower has contributed no equity at all, (ii) the $9,200,000 mezzanine loan is applied $8,200,000 to land and $1,000,000 to developer fee — that is, entirely to items that are not construction costs — (iii) the "land as equity" premise in Term Sheet §5.1 and Credit Memorandum §5.1 is false, (iv) the LTC and LTV analyses rest on a capital structure that does not exist, (v) the HVCRE calculation in A-11 is wrong by an order of magnitude, and (vi) the equity-first requirement in E-7 has nothing to apply. The Credit Memorandum itself raises "circular funding" as a possibility; the Borrower's budget effectively confirms it.

**Open.** Bank statements, wire confirmations and capital call notices; the closing statement for the 2023 acquisition; the Mezzanine Loan's disbursement history.

**Recommendation.** This should be resolved before the Loan Amount, the LTC covenant and the intercreditor terms are fixed. Require a traced source of funds for not less than 90 days, executed capital call notices, and a managing member certificate; and provide in the Loan Agreement that if any portion of the Borrower's asserted equity traces to the mezzanine lender or its affiliates, the Commitment reduces dollar-for-dollar and the shortfall must be funded with new equity. Draft §7.6(c) and §14.6.

## D-5 | The independent manager and non-consolidation opinion are Credit Memorandum conditions that the Term Sheet does not contain

**Record.** Term Sheet §20 contemplates "the potential appointment of an independent manager or independent director … whose affirmative vote shall be required" as a matter the Lender "may require". Credit Memorandum §9.3(b)–(c) and condition 10.1.4 make the independent manager and the non-consolidation opinion mandatory closing conditions. Org chart §1.1 and §2.2 confirm that the operating agreement contains neither separateness covenants nor an independent manager.

**Analysis.** These are new obligations for the Borrower, not merely documentation of agreed terms, and they require a 75% member vote to implement (D-2). They are, however, the single most effective structural protection against the affiliate-controlled bankruptcy risk described in D-3, and the Lender should not proceed without them. The point to manage is expectation: the Borrower may object that the Term Sheet made them discretionary.

**Open.** Whether the Borrower has agreed; the identity and independence of the proposed independent manager.

**Recommendation.** Present the requirements as conditions of the credit approval rather than as new demands, require the operating agreement to be amended and effective at closing, vet the independent manager's independence against the criteria in Draft §12.4(a), and obtain the non-consolidation opinion from Calloway & Strauss. Draft §12.2 to §12.5 and §4.1(r).

## D-6 | The mezzanine loan's payment mechanics and maturity do not work with the senior loan's extension option

**Record.** Term Sheet §1.4: the mezzanine loan bears a fixed 12.50%, and its maturity is "coterminous with the maturity of the senior Loan described herein, including any extension period". Credit Memorandum §4.4: interest is paid in kind during construction and in cash during the mini-perm. Term Sheet §3.2 and Article 21: one six-month extension of the senior mini-perm, subject to conditions to be set in the Loan Agreement.

**Analysis.** Two issues. The economics are addressed at A-10. The structural issue is that the senior extension is worthless to the Lender if it depends on the mezzanine loan extending — because the mezzanine lender can withhold its consent, and it will have every incentive to do so if the Project cannot pay it (A-10). The intercreditor agreement must therefore either effect the extension automatically or bind the mezzanine lender to extend on the same terms.

**Open.** The mezzanine lender's extension mechanics and its consent rights over senior amendments.

**Recommendation.** Require the Intercreditor Agreement to provide that the mezzanine maturity automatically extends with the senior loan on the same terms, without further consent, and that any failure to extend constitutes a default under the Intercreditor Agreement entitling the Lender to exercise its remedies. Draft §14.5(b)(viii) and §21.2(g).

## D-7 | Perfection of the membership interest pledge is not addressed

**Record.** Term Sheet §8 requires a "Pledge of 100% of membership interests in the Borrower by all members". No document states whether the interests are certificated, whether certificates have been delivered, or whether a UCC-1 has been or will be filed. Org chart §2.5 confirms only that the mezzanine lender "currently holds a pledge".

**Analysis.** A pledge of LLC membership interests is perfected by control under UCC Article 8 (if the interests are securities) or Article 9 (by filing, or by control if certificated), and the Lender's priority depends on which method it uses and when. If the mezzanine lender has already perfected by control, a later senior pledge may not achieve the first priority the Term Sheet promises, whatever the intercreditor agreement says about contract priority.

**Open.** The form of the interests and the mezzanine lender's perfection method and date.

**Recommendation.** Require the operating agreement to provide for certificated interests; require delivery of certificates, an undated transfer power and an irrevocable proxy; require a UCC-1 filing in any event; and obtain the mezzanine lender's confirmation of its perfection method in the intercreditor agreement. Draft §14.4(b)–(c).
