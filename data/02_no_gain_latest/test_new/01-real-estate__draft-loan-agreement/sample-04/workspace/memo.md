# 1. PURPOSE, SCOPE AND METHOD

**1.1 Purpose.** This memorandum reports our review of the seven source documents comprising the loan file, reconciles the figures used in them, identifies the material inconsistencies and gaps among them, and recommends a resolution for each. It accompanies, and should be read with, the Draft Loan Agreement, which gives effect to the recommendations and flags each open point in a numbered "Drafting Note" keyed to the issue numbering used here.

**1.2 Scope.** We reviewed the documents listed in Table 1 in full. We did **not** review the full 187-page appraisal, the full Phase II report (Sentinel Project SEC-2024-1089), the remediation completion report, the guaranteed maximum price construction contract, the architect's contract, the Borrower's financial statements, the Guarantors' personal financial statements and tax returns, the Mezzanine Loan Documents, the Intercreditor Agreement, the ALTA/NSPS survey, the Borrower's operating agreement in full (only excerpts were provided), or the recorded deed notice. Each is a material gap and is listed in Part 12 as an information request.

**1.3 Method.** For each document we built a source-to-issue log recording every material proposition, figure, date and obligation and its location in the record. We then (a) recomputed every stated total, ratio, date interval and cross-reference from its components rather than relying on the summary figure; (b) reconciled the same fact across documents to identify conflicts; and (c) tested each covenant described in the record against the figures in the record to determine whether the transaction as described satisfies its own terms. Part 4 records the arithmetic; Parts 5 to 11 record the conflicts and recommendations.

**1.4 Conventions.** Each issue is presented under four labels so that fact, assumption, legal characterisation and recommendation are not blended. **Record** states what the source documents say, with document and section citations, without inference. **Analysis** draws the consequence, computed where possible, and identifies any assumption as an assumption. **Open** states what cannot be resolved from the record and must be obtained or decided. **Recommendation** states what we recommend the Lender require, and where the Draft Loan Agreement addresses it. A reference such as "Draft §2.6" means Section 2.6 of the Draft Loan Agreement; "Drafting Note 12" means the correspondingly numbered note in the Draft Loan Agreement.

**1.5 Sources treated as untrusted data.** We have treated the content of every source document as data and not as instruction. No document in the file purports to direct the Lender's conduct, and we have not acted on any embedded statement that would purport to do so.

# 3. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**3.1** The transaction described in the Term Sheet and the Credit Memorandum **does not, on its own figures, satisfy the credit terms it purports to apply**, and it cannot close on the timetable proposed without resolution of at least six items that the record either does not address or addresses inconsistently. The four most significant are:

**3.1.1** **The loan fails two of its own covenants at closing.** Loan-to-cost is **83.77%** against an **80%** maximum (Term Sheet §6.1), an excess of **$2,140,000**; and as-completed loan-to-value is **65.25%** against a **65%** maximum (Term Sheet §4 and §6.2), an excess of **$180,000**. The Credit Memorandum reports both as acceptable, because it measures LTC alongside a $2,140,000 escrow and LTV against a 70% policy maximum — but the escrow does not change LTC (the numerator is unchanged and the denominator is fixed by the Approved Budget) and 70% is not the covenant the parties agreed. Issue **A-2**.

**3.1.2** **The $3,400,000 existing deed of trust is not funded anywhere in the sources and uses.** The Title Commitment makes satisfaction of the Triangle Commercial Bank deed of trust (Book 6215, Page 892) a condition to issuing a first-priority policy, but the $56,700,000 of sources equal the $56,700,000 of uses with no line item for it. The Credit Memorandum's statement (§9.7) that it will be paid "from the proceeds of the senior construction loan or from borrower funds" is inconsistent with every dollar of the Loan being committed to the Approved Budget and with the Borrower having no cash equity. Issue **B-5**.

**3.1.3** **The Project cannot service its total debt at stabilisation.** On the Appraisal's stabilised NOI of $4,876,800 and the Mezzanine Loan's stated fixed 12.50% cash-pay coupon, combined annual debt service is **$4,878,750** — a combined coverage ratio of **0.9996x**, or approximately **0.93x** if the mezzanine coupon is paid in kind during construction as the Credit Memorandum (§4.4) states. The 1.31x figure relied on throughout the record measures senior debt only. Issue **A-10**.

**3.1.4** **The Borrower's equity contribution may be entirely mezzanine debt.** The Borrower's own Detailed Budget (line 1.01) states that the $8,200,000 land acquisition was "funded by mezzanine loan proceeds and member capital calls"; the organizational chart (§1.3) says it was funded from Ms. Vasquez-Torres's personal resources; the Credit Memorandum (§2.2) says it was funded by capital calls from all three members. If the first is correct, there is no equity in the capital stack, and the LTC, LTV, HVCRE and equity-first analyses in the Credit Memorandum all rest on a false premise. Issue **D-4**.

**3.2** Subject to the business decisions those items require, the collateral package, the completion and carry guaranties, the guarantor support (combined net worth $41,300,000 and combined liquidity of $9,300,000 against a $5,000,000 covenant) and the LTV position on the Appraisal support the credit — but only if the structural and environmental conditions described below are made hard closing conditions rather than conditions the Lender may waive at its discretion.

# 5. VALUATION, COVENANTS AND LOAN QUANTUM

## A-1 | The as-completed LTV exceeds the 65% covenant at closing

**Record.** Term Sheet §4 states that "the Lender's maximum loan-to-value ratio shall not exceed 65% on an as-completed basis", and §6.2 sets a 65% maximum on both an as-completed and an as-stabilised basis. Term Sheet §4 and Credit Memorandum §6.1 both compute the as-completed LTV as $47,500,000 ÷ $72,800,000 = 65.25%. Credit Memorandum §6.1 applies a "Bank Policy" column of "Max 70%" and reports the metric as "Pass"; §12 repeats "maximum LTV (70% for construction loans)".

**Analysis.** Measured against the executed Term Sheet, 65.25% exceeds 65.00% by 0.25 percentage points, or **$180,000** (72,800,000 × 0.65 = 47,320,000). The Credit Memorandum never states this. There is no evidence that the Senior Loan Committee was asked to approve a departure from the agreed 65% covenant; the table presents the metric as compliant because the comparator column was changed to the 70% policy ceiling. Two distinct limits are being conflated: a policy maximum (70%) and a negotiated term (65%). A covenant that is breached the moment it is made is unenforceable in practice and creates an immediate default if the Lender later wishes to rely on it.

**Open.** Whether the Senior Loan Committee approved 65% or 70% for this transaction, and why the Term Sheet (the executed document) records 65%.

## A-2 | LTC exceeds 80% and the proposed equity escrow does not cure it

**Record.** Term Sheet §6.1: maximum LTC of 80% of Total Project Costs, with Total Project Costs stated at $56,700,000. Two genuine cures exist: reduce the Commitment dollar-for-dollar to $45,360,000 (with the equity replacing the cancelled commitment), or enlarge the denominator by adding costs that are presently omitted — the $3,400,000 Existing Mortgage Payoff Amount (B-2) and approximately $680,000 of pre-closing demolition and remediation costs (C-8) — and fund them with equity, which produces 47,500,000 ÷ 60,100,000 = **79.03%** if the payoff is equity-funded, or 50,900,000 ÷ 60,100,000 = 84.69% if it is loan-funded (in which case the breach is worse).

**Open.** Whether the Lender's credit approval is for $47,500,000 or for a maximum LTC of 80%, and whether any reduction is acceptable to the Borrower and to the Mezzanine Lender (whose $9,200,000 is sized to fill the gap in a $56,700,000 budget).

On those figures Total Project Costs become $60,780,000 and LTC is 47,500,000 ÷ 60,780,000 = **78.15%**, so the separate $2,140,000 escrow is not required at all; the equity cure and the payoff are the same money. If the Borrower cannot fund $4,080,000 of genuinely new equity, reduce the Commitment to $45,360,000. If the Borrower cannot fund it, reduce the Commitment to $45,360,000. ## A-7 | The interest reserve has been modelled three ways and the results differ by up to $1.28 million

## A-10 | The combined senior and mezzanine debt service has never been tested and does not work

## A-11 | The HVCRE analysis counts borrowed money as contributed capital

**Recommendation.** Add express covenants to comply with the declaration, to pay assessments when due, and to obtain ARC approval before any exterior modification (Draft §9.1(q) and §20.3); add the declaration to the Permitted Exceptions in the qualified form in Draft Schedule 2; require the Borrower to deliver the full recorded declaration and the current assessment notice; and require the Lender's consent to any plan change that would require ARC approval (Draft §6.4(c)).

## B-7 | The Appraisal relies on a 20-foot alley that does not appear in title

## B-8 | Work performed before closing creates a mechanic's lien priority risk

# 7. ENVIRONMENTAL MATTERS

## C-1 | The NFA Letter has four conditions; the Term Sheet describes one and the Credit Memorandum two

**Record.** The Phase I ESA §7 records the NFA Letter dated September 30, 2024 as conditional upon (1) a soil vapour mitigation system beneath any enclosed structure over the affected area, with as-built drawings and a performance verification report to NCDEQ within 90 days of completion of the building foundation; (2) restriction of the affected approximately 0.38 acres to non-residential use; (3) a NCDEQ-approved **soil management plan** before any subsurface disturbance in the affected area; and (4) a **deed notice** recorded within 60 days. Credit Memorandum §8.2 and §9.5 describe two conditions (vapour system and land use restriction).

**Analysis.** Both the Term Sheet and the Credit Memorandum omit the soil management plan and the deed notice from the conditions the Borrower is required to satisfy and the Lender is required to monitor. The Borrower's obligations in the Loan Agreement would therefore be narrower than its obligations to NCDEQ, which means the Lender could have no contractual remedy for the two most likely breaches. This is a straightforward gap between the regulatory instrument and the finance documents and should be closed.

**Open.** The NFA Letter itself (only summarised in the record) and any subsequent NCDEQ correspondence.

**Recommendation.** Reproduce all four conditions in the Loan Agreement as affirmative covenants with compliance as an Event of Default, add a compliance schedule to the reporting requirements, and make satisfaction of each a condition to closing (or, for the vapour system, to the relevant advance and the retainage release). Draft §13.2, §13.5, §16.1(k), Schedule 7 and Drafting Note 12.

## C-2 | The deed notice appears to be overdue

See **B-4**, which analyses the same facts from the title perspective. From the environmental perspective the point is narrower: the deadline was **November 29, 2024**, the Phase I ESA recorded non-recording as of October 28, 2024, and nothing in the record shows that it was later recorded or that NCDEQ has been asked to extend or forgive the deadline. An unrecorded deed notice is a present breach of a condition of the Borrower's NFA determination, and the Lender should not accept a representation that it has been recorded without a certified copy of the recorded instrument.

**Recommendation.** Obtain the recorded instrument or a written NCDEQ response before closing; if the deadline has been missed, require an application to NCDEQ for confirmation that the NFA remains effective, and consider whether the Borrower's failure should be treated as an Event of Default waived only by the closing. Draft §13.2(d) and §4.1(e)(iii).

## C-3 | The claim that 14 residential units sit above the restricted area is unsupported, and the exposure is material

**Record.** Credit Memorandum §8.2 and §9.5 assert as fact that the plans "position 14 residential units in the building above this area". The Phase I ESA §8.5 states that the consultant "did not independently verify whether any residential units would be positioned directly above the affected 0.38-acre area … as this determination requires a detailed overlay of the proposed building floor plans with the environmental delineation map". The Appraisal §8 states that the plans "include residential units on the upper floors … positioned above this area", expresses "no opinion as to the legal permissibility", and made no valuation adjustment. Term Sheet §17 makes no reference to any unit conflict at all. The figure "14" does not appear in any document other than the Credit Memorandum, and corresponds to the number of three-bedroom units in the unit mix.

**Analysis.** The Credit Memorandum's own sensitivity is that losing 14 units reduces NOI by $377,160 (14 × $2,245 × 12) to $4,499,640 and reduces the DSCR to 1.21x, below the 1.25x covenant. That analysis is arithmetically correct and establishes that the issue is material if it exists. But the record does not establish that it exists, and the Lender is at risk of two errors simultaneously: assuming a conflict that does not exist (and demanding a redesign that is unnecessary), or accepting a representation that there is no conflict on the basis of documents that expressly decline to confirm it. The NFA Letter's restriction is on *land use of the affected area*; whether enclosed residential space above grade-level restricted soil is a prohibited "residential use" of the affected area is a question for NCDEQ and North Carolina environmental counsel, not for the Lender or the appraiser.

**Open.** The Architect's overlay of the approved plans on Figure 3 of the Phase I ESA; NCDEQ's interpretation; the number and type of units actually affected.

**Recommendation.** Require the Architect's overlay before closing, and require one of three resolutions as a closing condition: (a) a written NCDEQ modification permitting residential use of the affected area (which the Phase I ESA warns "would likely require additional environmental investigation, risk assessment, and potentially additional remediation"); (b) written NCDEQ confirmation that residential occupancy of space above the restricted area is permissible with the vapour system installed and operating; or (c) an approved redesign. Require an addendum to the Appraisal addressing value without the affected units, and withhold advances for work in or above the affected area until the point is resolved. Draft §4.1(e)(vi), §13.4, §13.4(d)–(e) and Drafting Note 13.

## C-4 | The soil management plan is a pre-construction condition that no document requires

**Record.** Phase I ESA §7, Condition 3: any excavation, grading or soil disturbance in the affected area must follow a NCDEQ-approved soil management plan, which "must be submitted to and approved by NCDEQ prior to commencement of any construction activities involving subsurface disturbance within the affected area". Recommendation 4 of the Phase I ESA advises that the plan be incorporated into the construction specifications and made available to the Contractor and subcontractors. Neither the Term Sheet nor the Credit Memorandum requires it.

**Analysis.** Foundation and site work will begin at or immediately after closing. If the plan is not approved by then, work in the affected area cannot proceed lawfully, and the Borrower will be in breach of the NFA Letter on the first day of construction. The Phase I ESA notes that residual concentrations above residential screening levels remain along the eastern boundary at 10 to 14 feet below grade, so the plan is not a formality — it governs screening, stockpiling, disposal and worker protection in the area where the Contractor will be excavating.

**Open.** Whether a plan has been drafted, and NCDEQ's approval timeline.

**Recommendation.** Make NCDEQ approval of the soil management plan a condition to closing (or, at the latest, to the first advance in respect of site work), incorporate it into the Construction Contract by change order, and make compliance a condition to each advance in the affected area and an Event of Default if breached. Draft §4.1(e)(iv), §13.2(c), §13.5 and §9.1(l).

## C-5 | The vapour mitigation system is more prescriptive than the documents describe and its cost is not separately identified

**Record.** Phase I ESA §7, Condition 1: the system must be a **passive sub-slab depressurization system with the capability of conversion to an active depressurization system**, designed by a professional engineer licensed in North Carolina, installed in accordance with NCDEQ guidelines, with as-built drawings and a performance verification report submitted within 90 days of completion of the building foundation. Detailed Budget line 2.08 ($3,590,000, parking structure) notes only that it "Includes soil vapor mitigation system per NCDEQ NFA requirements"; there is no separate cost.

**Analysis.** The system must be designed and durably maintained, with a conversion capability that implies ongoing monitoring and a possible future capital cost. Because it sits inside a single budget line, the Lender cannot test the adequacy of the amount allowed, and it cannot identify any reduction if the scope is later value-engineered. The 90-day reporting period also creates a compliance milestone shortly after foundation completion that no document tracks.

**Open.** The engineer's design and budget for the system; whether the Contractor's GMP price includes the conversion capability and the verification report.

**Recommendation.** Require the design to be included in the Approved Plans and the GMP contract, require a separate breakdown of the system's cost in the Approved Budget, prohibit any change order affecting it without the Lender's consent and NCDEQ approval, require annual inspection and testing with results delivered to the Lender, and add the 90-day NCDEQ submission to the reporting schedule and to the Release Conditions. Draft §13.3, §6.4(a)(iii), §6.7(f) and Schedule 7.

## C-6 | The record gives three different accounts of the underground storage tank

# 8. STRUCTURE, SINGLE-PURPOSE ENTITY AND INTERCREDITOR

## D-1 | Both loans are secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests and no intercreditor agreement exists

**Record.** Term Sheet §1.4: the mezzanine loan "shall be secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests in the Borrower"; an intercreditor agreement "is currently under negotiation and must be executed prior to or simultaneously with the closing". Term Sheet §8 lists a senior pledge of 100% by all members. Org chart §1.2 and §2.5 state that the mezzanine pledge is in place and that the intercreditor agreement has been "negotiated but is not yet executed". Credit Memorandum §4.2, §9.4 and condition 10.1.3 recommend "option (b)": senior first, mezzanine second, with a 90-day standstill, cure rights, a purchase option and bankruptcy protections.

**Analysis.** Two first-priority pledges of the same collateral cannot both exist. Until the intercreditor agreement is executed there is no mechanism for determining priority, and in an insolvency the dispute itself would delay the Lender's remedies. The record is also silent on perfection: a pledge of LLC interests is perfected by control (delivery of certificates with transfer powers, or a control agreement for uncertificated interests) or by filing, and the operating agreement does not address certifications.

**Open.** Whether certificates have been issued; whether the mezzanine pledge has already been perfected; the draft intercreditor agreement.

**Recommendation.** Make execution of the intercreditor agreement on the Credit Memorandum's terms a hard closing condition (not waivable); require the senior pledge to be perfected by delivery of certificates, an undated transfer power and an irrevocable proxy, with a UCC-1 in any event; and require the operating agreement amendment described in D-2. Draft §14.4, §14.5 and §4.1(q).

## D-2 | The operating agreement requires a 75% member vote for the Loan itself and permits pledges only with the managing member's approval

**Record.** Org chart §2.2 (operating agreement §3.2): the prior written approval of members holding at least **75%** of the membership interests is required for "any financing or refinancing of the Property in a principal amount in excess of $1,000,000", "any amendment to this Operating Agreement" and "any voluntary dissolution of the Company or the filing of any petition for relief under any federal or state bankruptcy or insolvency law". §9.1: no member may pledge its interest without the prior written consent of the managing member and members holding at least 75%.

**Analysis.** The senior Loan itself requires a 75% member approval, and so do the amending and restating of the operating agreement (needed for the SPE covenants and the independent manager) and the express authorisation of the senior pledge. On the ownership percentages (62% / 28% / 10%), Ms. Vasquez-Torres and Mr. Osei together hold only 72% — so **Brightleaf Investors Group LLC's vote is required for every one of them**. That entity is the affiliate of the Mezzanine Lender (D-3). The Lender is therefore relying on the party with the adverse interest to consent to the protections that constrain it.

**Open.** Whether written consents have been obtained and whether Brightleaf has conditioned its consent on anything.

**Recommendation.** Require executed written consents of all three members to the Loan, the pledge, the operating agreement amendment and the SPE and independent manager provisions, delivered at closing, and require the amendment to be filed and effective before the Deed of Trust is recorded. Draft §4.1(c)(iii) and §4.1(c)(ii).

## D-3 | A 28% member received its interest for no cash in exchange for arranging the mezzanine loan from its own affiliate

**Record.** Org chart §2.3 (operating agreement §5.1(b)): Brightleaf Investors Group LLC "shall make an initial capital contribution of Zero Dollars ($0) at the time of formation" and receives a **28%** membership interest "in consideration of its commitment to arrange third-party mezzanine financing for the Project". Org chart §2.3: the mezzanine financing was arranged through Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC, "an entity under the common control of Thomas R. Chen, who also serves as Managing Member of Brightleaf Investors Group LLC". Credit Memorandum §9.3 identifies the substantive consolidation risk.

**Analysis.** Three consequences. First, no member other than Ms. Vasquez-Torres (through the land) has contributed capital, which is the premise of D-4. Second, the 28% holder has a negative control over major decisions (D-2) and its affiliate is the subordinated lender, so the same economic interest sits on both sides of the senior loan and controls the borrower's bankruptcy decision. Third, an interest issued for services rather than capital raises questions about the Borrower's true equity and about the arm's-length character of the arrangements, which the Lender should disclose to its credit approvers rather than leave in the file.

**Open.** The instrument by which the interest was issued; whether any value was ascribed to it; whether Brightleaf's consent to the Loan has conditions.

**Recommendation.** Require full disclosure of all agreements among the Borrower, Brightleaf, the Mezzanine Lender and Mr. Chen; require the independent manager and non-consolidation opinion (D-5); require the intercreditor agreement to include express acknowledgement of the affiliate relationship and a prohibition on the mezzanine lender using its affiliate's voting rights to impede the Lender; and require all affiliate transactions to be on arm's-length terms (Draft §14.7, §10.1(f) and §12.4).

## D-4 | The record gives three irreconcilable accounts of how the land was funded

**Record.** (a) Org chart §1.3 and operating agreement §5.1(a): Ms. Vasquez-Torres contributed the land "originally acquired … in a transaction funded from her personal resources" at an $8,200,000 cost basis, and Brightleaf and Mr. Osei each contributed "$0 cash to date". (c) Detailed Budget line 1.01 (and its "Notes" column): the land was "Previously closed; funded by **mezzanine loan proceeds** and member capital calls", with the $8,200,000 shown in the "Funded by Mezzanine Loan" column and $0 in the "Funded by Borrower Equity" column.

**Analysis.** The three accounts are mutually exclusive. The Borrower's own budget allocates the land to the mezzanine column. If that is correct then (i) the Borrower has contributed no equity at all, (ii) the $9,200,000 mezzanine loan is applied $8,200,000 to land and $1,000,000 to developer fee — that is, entirely to items that are not construction costs — (iii) the "land as equity" premise in Term Sheet §5.1 and Credit Memorandum §5.1 is false, (iv) the LTC and LTV analyses rest on a capital structure that does not exist, (v) the HVCRE calculation in A-11 is wrong by an order of magnitude, and (vi) the equity-first requirement in E-7 has nothing to apply. The Credit Memorandum itself raises "circular funding" as a possibility; the Borrower's budget effectively confirms it.

**Open.** Bank statements, wire confirmations and capital call notices; the closing statement for the 2023 acquisition; the Mezzanine Loan's disbursement history.

**Recommendation.** This should be resolved before the Loan Amount, the LTC covenant and the intercreditor terms are fixed. Require a traced source of funds for not less than 90 days, executed capital call notices, and a managing member certificate; and provide in the Loan Agreement that if any portion of the Borrower's asserted equity traces to the mezzanine lender or its affiliates, the Commitment reduces dollar-for-dollar and the shortfall must be funded with new equity. Draft §7.6(c) and §14.6.

## D-5 | The independent manager and non-consolidation opinion are Credit Memorandum conditions that the Term Sheet does not contain

**Record.** Term Sheet §20 contemplates "the potential appointment of an independent manager or independent director … whose affirmative vote shall be required" as a matter the Lender "may require". Credit Memorandum §9.3(b)–(c) and condition 10.1.4 make the independent manager and the non-consolidation opinion mandatory closing conditions. **Analysis.** These are new obligations for the Borrower, not merely documentation of agreed terms, and they require a 75% member vote to implement (D-2). They are, however, the single most effective structural protection against the affiliate-controlled bankruptcy risk described in D-3, and the Lender should not proceed without them. The point to manage is expectation: the Borrower may object that the Term Sheet made them discretionary.

**Open.** Whether the Borrower has agreed; the identity and independence of the proposed independent manager.

**Recommendation.** Present the requirements as conditions of the credit approval rather than as new demands, require the operating agreement to be amended and effective at closing, vet the independent manager's independence against the criteria in Draft §12.4(a), and obtain the non-consolidation opinion from Calloway & Strauss. Draft §12.2 to §12.5 and §4.1(r).

## D-6 | The mezzanine loan's payment mechanics and maturity do not work with the senior loan's extension option

**Record.** Term Sheet §1.4: the mezzanine loan bears a fixed 12.50%, and its maturity is "coterminous with the maturity of the senior Loan described herein, including any extension period". Credit Memorandum §4.4: interest is paid in kind during construction and in cash during the mini-perm. Term Sheet §3.2 and Article 21: one six-month extension of the senior mini-perm, subject to conditions to be set in the Loan Agreement.

**Analysis.** Two issues. The economics are addressed at A-10. The intercreditor agreement must therefore either effect the extension automatically or bind the mezzanine lender to extend on the same terms.

**Open.** The mezzanine lender's extension mechanics and its consent rights over senior amendments.

**Recommendation.** Require the Intercreditor Agreement to provide that the mezzanine maturity automatically extends with the senior loan on the same terms, without further consent, and that any failure to extend constitutes a default under the Intercreditor Agreement entitling the Lender to exercise its remedies. Draft §14.5(b)(viii) and §21.2(g).

## D-7 | Perfection of the membership interest pledge is not addressed

**Record.** Term Sheet §8 requires a "Pledge of 100% of membership interests in the Borrower by all members". No document states whether the interests are certificated, whether certificates have been delivered, or whether a UCC-1 has been or will be filed. Org chart §2.5 confirms only that the mezzanine lender "currently holds a pledge".

**Analysis.** A pledge of LLC membership interests is perfected by control under UCC Article 8 (if the interests are securities) or Article 9 (by filing, or by control if certificated), and the Lender's priority depends on which method it uses and when. If the mezzanine lender has already perfected by control, a later senior pledge may not achieve the first priority the Term Sheet promises, whatever the intercreditor agreement says about contract priority.

**Open.** The form of the interests and the mezzanine lender's perfection method and date.

**Recommendation.** Require the operating agreement to provide for certificated interests; require delivery of certificates, an undated transfer power and an irrevocable proxy; require a UCC-1 filing in any event; and obtain the mezzanine lender's confirmation of its perfection method in the intercreditor agreement. Draft §14.4(b)–(c).

# 9. CONSTRUCTION AND DISBURSEMENT

## E-1 | The Term Sheet calls the facility a "single advance facility" while scheduling nine draws

**Record.** Term Sheet §3.1: "The Loan shall be structured as a single advance facility, with loan proceeds to be disbursed in accordance with the approved draw schedule set forth in Section 9."

**Analysis.** The phrase is self-contradictory: a single-advance facility is fully funded at closing, whereas a draw-scheduled construction loan is a single commitment with multiple advances. The distinction is not semantic — it determines whether the Lender has a continuing commitment, whether the unused line fee is payable, whether future advances must be insured by title, and whether a future advance can be withheld on the basis of a default.

**Open.** None — this is a drafting correction.

**Recommendation.** Describe the facility as a single committed, non-revolving facility with multiple advances. Draft §2.1 does so and contains a Drafting Note recording the correction (Drafting Note 1).

## E-2 | The Construction Maturity Date precedes the projected substantial completion date in every schedule

**Record.** Term Sheet §3.2: 30-month Construction Period from a February 28, 2025 closing, giving a Construction Maturity Date of **August 28, 2027**; projected substantial completion **August 31, 2027** (Term Sheet §2; Credit Memorandum §3.1; Appraisal §3; Summary sheet of the construction budget). Term Sheet §7.1 requires the Guarantors to guarantee completion "on or before the Construction Maturity Date of August 28, 2027". Term Sheet §15 treats a delay of more than 90 days behind schedule as a Construction Delay Default. The Borrower's Draw Schedule dates Draw 8 (Substantial Completion/TCO) to **August 2027** and Draw 9 (retainage release) to **October 2027**, both on or after the maturity date. Credit Memorandum §9.6 identifies the three-day gap.

**Analysis.** On the record's own schedule the Borrower cannot satisfy the completion covenant, the Completion Guaranty is breached on the day it is given, and the Loan is scheduled to disburse during the mini-perm period on a construction basis. The Credit Memorandum treats the mini-perm period as a "buffer", but a buffer that the Borrower enters in default of the completion covenant is not a buffer: it is a default with a 12-month cure period during which the Lender can make no further advances except the retainage release.

**Open.** The Contractor's contractual completion date under the guaranteed maximum price contract; whether an extension of the construction period is acceptable to the Borrower and to the mezzanine lender.

**Recommendation.** Extend the Construction Maturity Date to **December 31, 2027** (a four-month buffer over the projected August 31, 2027 completion) and conform the Completion Guaranty, the Construction Delay Default and Schedule 10; alternatively require substantial completion by June 30, 2027 while leaving the maturity date unchanged. Either way, the schedule must show the Loan converting to the mini-perm period only after substantial completion. Draft §1.1(q) (with the alternative bracketed), §5.8, §15.1(a), Schedule 10 and Drafting Note 23.

## E-3 | Retainage withheld and retainage released differ by $200,000

**Record.** Term Sheet §9.1: a 10% retainage holdback on all hard construction cost disbursements, "held by the Lender and released only upon satisfaction of the conditions … for Draw 9". Term Sheet §9.2 and Credit Memorandum §7.1: Draw 9 is $3,275,000. Ten percent of the $34,750,000 guaranteed maximum price is **$3,475,000**.

**Analysis.** Either $200,000 of retainage is never released (in which case the Contractor will not be paid in full from the Loan and the Completion Guaranty will be engaged for a sum certain), or the difference is being funded from another line, which is not stated. The Credit Memorandum notes that the $3,275,000 "represents approximately 9.4% of the hard construction cost GMP" without observing that the holdback is 10%.

**Open.** The Contractor's understanding of the retainage and release mechanics.

**Recommendation.** Provide for Retainage of 10% released in full on the Release Conditions, with the build-up and release computed on the actual contract sum including approved change orders. Draft §6.6, §6.7 and Schedule 4; Drafting Note 7.

## E-4 | The draw schedule does not reconcile and the developer fee and contingency have no draw line

**Record.** Term Sheet §9.2 and Credit Memorandum §7.1 both show nine draws totalling $47,500,000, of which Draw 1 is $9,475,000 (soft costs $4,150,000 + interest reserve $4,850,000 + origination fee $475,000) and Draws 2 to 8 are hard cost amounts totalling $34,750,000, with Draw 9 at $3,275,000. The Borrower's "Draw Schedule" sheet adds columns for hard costs, retainage, soft costs, interest reserve, fees, developer fee and contingency: (a) the components sum to **$48,500,000** against a stated total of $47,500,000; (b) the Draw 9 row shows $3,475,000 of hard costs less $3,475,000 of retainage, plus $200,000 developer fee and $600,000 contingency, which is $4,275,000, not the stated $3,275,000; (c) the developer fee of $2,835,000 is allocated in full across senior draws although the Detailed Budget funds only **$1,835,000** of it from the senior Loan and $1,000,000 from the mezzanine loan; and (d) the contingency of $1,440,000 is disbursed on a scheduled basis in full by Draw 9.

**Analysis.** The $1,000,000 overstatement is exactly the mezzanine-funded portion of the developer fee, so the error has a single identifiable cause. The consequence is that the schedule cannot be used to administer the Loan: on its face it allocates to the senior Loan $1,000,000 of cost that the Approved Budget allocates to the mezzanine loan, and it leaves the senior-funded developer fee and the contingency without any draw line. Separately, the Term Sheet schedule deducts nothing for retainage when stating draws 2 to 8 and then releases only $3,275,000, so its cumulative drawn figures overstate the cash actually advanced.

**Open.** None — this is an arithmetic correction to be agreed with the Borrower.

**Recommendation.** Replace the schedule with a line-item schedule keyed to the Approved Budget, showing (i) gross hard cost, (ii) retainage, (iii) net cash, (iv) other loan-funded items, (v) total advance and (vi) cumulative advances, with the aggregate equal to the Commitment and every row footing. Draft Schedule 4 does this, and the accompanying reconciliation table sets out each correction against the source.

## E-5 | The contingency is drawn on a schedule and the developer fee is funded in part by the mezzanine loan

**Record.** Borrower's Draw Schedule: contingency amounts of $521,250 (Draw 2), $102,500 (Draws 6 and 7), $113,750 (Draw 8) and $600,000 (Draw 9) — the full $1,440,000. Developer fee of $695,000 (Draw 3), $695,000 (Draw 4), $521,250 (Draw 5), $245,000 (Draws 6 and 7), $233,750 (Draw 8) and $200,000 (Draw 9) — the full $2,835,000, the first instalments falling at 35% and 55% completion. Detailed Budget 5.01: senior loan $1,835,000, mezzanine $1,000,000.

**Analysis.** A contingency is a reserve against the unknown; drawing it on a schedule converts it into a budget line and removes the protection it exists to provide. Paying 100% of a developer fee, beginning at 35% completion, and funding 35% of it from subordinate debt that itself cannot be serviced (A-10) transfers value to the sponsor before completion and dilutes the Lender's position in a workout.

**Open.** Whether the Borrower's operating agreement or the mezzanine documents restrict distributions.

**Recommendation.** Permit contingency disbursement only against approved change orders or documented cost increases (Draft §6.5(a)); gate the developer fee to 50% completion for the first instalment, subject to no default and no overrun, and fund it from the sources in the Approved Budget (Draft §6.5(c)); and prohibit any distribution other than the developer fee during the construction period (Draft §10.1(g)). Note that Term Sheet §14 already prohibits distributions "except for payment of the developer fee", which confirms the intent.

## E-6 | The entire soft cost budget is funded at closing

**Record.** Term Sheet §9.2: Draw 1 of $9,475,000 includes "initial soft costs $4,150,000". Detailed Budget Section III: $4,150,000, of which $250,000 is "Marketing & Lease-Up", $125,000 is construction inspection, $310,000 is the Lender's legal fees, $245,000 is title and recording and $210,000 is builder's risk premium.

**Analysis.** One hundred percent of the soft cost budget, including marketing and lease-up costs not incurred until well after completion and fees for services not yet rendered, is advanced at closing. This front-loads the loan, increases the average outstanding balance and therefore the interest cost, and means the Lender has funded costs that are never incurred if the Project fails early.

**Open.** The Borrower's preference; the cost of escrow administration.

**Recommendation.** Deposit the soft cost portion in a soft cost escrow and disburse against invoices as costs are incurred (Draft §5.5), or fund at closing only the items properly incurred at closing (title, recording, the origination fee and the Lender's documented fees). The former is preferable and is achievable because the title company is already acting as escrow agent.

## E-7 | The "equity first" requirement has nothing to apply to

**Record.** Credit Memorandum §7.2: "The Borrower's equity (including the equity escrow deposit, if required per Section 5.3) must be fully invested in the Project before any loan advances are made, other than the initial closing advance (Draw 1)". Condition 10.2.8 repeats it.

**Analysis.** Because the only equity in the structure is the land (which was acquired before closing) and possibly the disputed $2,140,000, and because Draw 1 — $9,475,000, or 20% of the Commitment — is expressly carved out, the requirement is satisfied by construction without any cash being at risk ahead of the Lender. On the record the Borrower's only contribution has already been made and may itself be debt-funded (D-4).

**Open.** Whether the Borrower can fund genuine new equity.

**Recommendation.** Apply the Equity Deposit to the soft costs and fees that would otherwise be in Draw 1, so that Loan proceeds are not advanced against costs before the equity is at risk; require the equity to be at least $4,080,000 as recommended at A-2; and make equity-first a condition to every advance after the first. Draft §7.6(b), §7.6(d) and §5.1(i).

## E-8 | No payment or performance bond is required from the Contractor

**Record.** Term Sheet §11 and §13 require the construction contract, the assignment of the construction contract, builder's risk insurance and the Architect's professional liability insurance, but no payment or performance bond. Credit Memorandum §9.8 recommends that the Bank "verify the financial strength and payment and performance bonding capacity of the contractor prior to closing" and include the Contractor's financial statements and bonding capacity in the closing deliverables.

**Analysis.** On a $34,750,000 ground-up construction with a single general contractor, a payment and performance bond is the conventional substitute for the Contractor's balance sheet and the most direct protection against a payment default and mechanic's liens. The record contains no Contractor financial information at all.

**Open.** The Contractor's financial statements, its surety, and the cost of bonding (which, if not in the guaranteed maximum price, is a budget item).

**Recommendation.** Require either (a) a payment and performance bond in the full contract amount from a surety acceptable to the Lender and named in the Loan Documents as obligee, or (b) if no bond is available, the Contractor's audited financial statements, a parent guaranty and a completion agreement in form acceptable to the Lender, together with a subguard or equivalent. At minimum, obtain what the Credit Memorandum recommends. Draft §4.1(l) and §9.1(v).

## E-9 | Construction sequencing creates permit, environmental and change-order risks that the documents do not control

**Record.** Term Sheet §13 requires the Lender's consent to any material change in plans, specifications or scope, without a threshold. Term Sheet §11 requires all building permits to be obtained as a condition to closing. The Declaration requires architectural review approval (or 60-day deemed approval) before a building permit can issue (B-5). The NFA Letter requires a NCDEQ-approved soil management plan before subsurface disturbance (C-4). The Borrower's Draw Schedule assumes construction begins at closing on February 28, 2025.

**Analysis.** Three conditions precedent to the first day of work — building permit, architectural review and the soil management plan — are all unconfirmed, and the schedule has no float. In addition, an unthresholded consent requirement on plan changes is unworkable in practice and will either be ignored (creating a technical default) or will delay change orders (creating schedule risk).

**Open.** The permit status, the ARC status and the soil management plan status.

**Recommendation.** Add a dollar and percentage threshold for change orders (Draft §6.4(a): $250,000 or 2% of the guaranteed maximum price, on a cumulative basis), require ARC approval before the Lender's consent to any change order affecting the exterior (Draft §6.4(c)), require the soil management plan as a condition to any advance for site work, and confirm the permit position before the closing date is fixed. Draft §4.1(j), §4.1(k), §5.1(m) and Schedule 10.

# 10. COMMERCIAL TERMS, FEES AND INSURANCE

## F-1 | The Lender's own fees and costs are funded from the Loan

**Record.** Detailed Budget 3.04 (Lender's legal and title/escrow costs, $310,000), 3.06 (appraisal, $35,000), 3.07 (title and recording, $245,000) and 4.02 (origination fee, $475,000) are all shown as "Funded by Senior Loan". Term Sheet §21.5 provides that "the Borrower shall pay all costs and expenses incurred by the Lender in connection with the origination, documentation, closing, and administration of the Loan".

**Analysis.** Financing $1,065,000 of transaction costs increases the effective LTC and reduces the Borrower's own funds at risk, and is in tension with §21.5. It is common in construction lending to fund the origination fee from proceeds; it is less common to fund the Lender's own legal fees, and doing so creates an appearance issue if the fees are later disputed. The appraisal and the title premium are properly Project Costs, but they still reduce the equity cushion.

**Open.** The Lender's internal policy on funding its own fees from proceeds.

**Recommendation.** Require the Lender's legal fees and the origination fee to be paid by the Borrower from the Equity Deposit at closing rather than advanced from the Loan, or, if they are to be funded, disclose the effect on the LTC calculation. Draft §3.5(a) and (e), §18.2 and Drafting Note 4.

## F-2 | The rate conventions are under-specified

**Record.** Term Sheet §3.3: Daily Simple SOFR + 3.25% (construction) and + 3.50% (mini-perm), with a 1.00% floor, interest calculated on actual days over a 360-day year, and ARRC-consistent benchmark replacement provisions. No lookback convention, no rounding convention and no definition of "U.S. Government Securities Business Day" is specified.

**Analysis.** Daily Simple SOFR requires a determination convention (typically a five-day lookback without observation shift), and the absence of one creates room for dispute about any given day's rate. The benchmark replacement language is described but not drafted.

**Open.** Confirmation of the Lender's standard convention.

**Recommendation.** Specify a five-business-day lookback without observation shift, a rounding convention, the definition of a U.S. Government Securities Business Day, and hardwired replacement language. Draft §1.1(tt), §3.3.

## F-3 | The default rate may not be enforceable at the level proposed

**Record.** Term Sheet §3.3: default rate equal to the applicable contract rate plus 5.00%; §15 allows a five-day grace period for monetary defaults.

**Analysis.** At a SOFR of 4.35% the default rate is 12.60% to 12.85%. North Carolina permits commercial parties to agree to a rate (N.C.G.S. §24-2), but a post-default increase that operates as a penalty may be reduced, and the point has not been addressed in any document. A usury savings clause is included in the Draft Loan Agreement but does not resolve whether the increase is a penalty.

**Open.** The Lender's standard position and its counsel's view.

**Recommendation.** Retain the 5.00% increase but include the usury savings and "lesser of" language in the Note and the Loan Agreement (Draft §3.11), and do not rely on the default rate as a remedy in any workout discussion.

## F-4 | Several commercial points are missing from the Term Sheet and require the Borrower's agreement

**Record.** The Term Sheet contains no late charge, no prepayment provision (and no statement that prepayment is permitted), and no provision for the Lender's costs on a failed closing beyond the $75,000 expense deposit. Term Sheet §22 describes the expense deposit as "non-refundable" and then provides that "if the Loan does not close for any reason, any unused portion of the expense deposit shall be returned to the Borrower".

**Analysis.** Each is a standard term that the Lender will want and the Borrower has not agreed. The expense deposit provision is internally contradictory: it cannot be both non-refundable and refundable, and the drafting should be resolved in the Lender's favour or rewritten to describe a deposit applied against costs with the balance returned.

**Open.** The Borrower's position on each.

**Recommendation.** Present the late charge (Draft §3.9), the prepayment right and the exit fee mechanics (Draft §3.6) and the expense provisions (Draft §18.2) as additions for negotiation, flagged as such (Drafting Note 4). The expense deposit contradiction should be resolved by amendment before closing regardless.

## F-5 | The exit and unused line fees should be tested against the likely repayment profile

**Record.** Term Sheet §3.4: exit fee of 0.50% of the outstanding balance if repaid during the construction period, 0.25% during the first six months of the mini-perm, and zero thereafter; unused line fee of 0.25% per annum on the average daily undrawn commitment from the first quarterly date after closing.

**Analysis.** The unused line fee is payable on the undrawn commitment, which starts at $38,025,000 and declines over 30 months — on a rough average undrawn balance of $15,000,000 that is approximately $37,500 per annum, which is immaterial. The exit fee structure creates an incentive to repay before the mini-perm period, which is inconsistent with the Lender's interest in a scheduled take-out, and it produces nothing if the loan runs to maturity. Neither is a defect, but the Credit Memorandum does not quantify either fee, so the Committee has not seen the economics.

**Open.** None.

**Recommendation.** Quantify both fees in the credit file and confirm that the exit fee is payable on acceleration and on foreclosure (or, if it is not, that the omission is deliberate). Draft §3.5(b) and §3.6 apply the exit fee on voluntary prepayment; the Lender should decide whether it applies on acceleration.

## G-1 | The insurance requirements do not match the risk

**Record.** Term Sheet §19 and Credit Memorandum §8.1 require builder's risk of not less than $34,750,000, commercial general liability of $5,000,000 per occurrence and $10,000,000 aggregate, umbrella of $10,000,000, workers' compensation at statutory limits and, conditionally, flood insurance. Credit Memorandum §8.1 adds professional liability of $2,000,000 for the Architect. Detailed Budget 3.09 allows $210,000 for builder's risk.

**Analysis.** Builder's risk at $34,750,000 insures the hard cost, not the replacement cost of the completed improvements (the Appraisal's as-completed value is $72,800,000); the conventional standard is full replacement cost, with the guaranteed maximum price as a floor. The record contains no rental loss coverage (which matters from stabilisation), no ordinance or law coverage (a live issue for a podium and parking structure in a historic district), and no equipment breakdown coverage.

**Open.** The insurance broker's programme and the cost of the additional coverages.

**Recommendation.** Require builder's risk at full replacement cost with $34,750,000 as a floor and with soft cost, ordinance or law and debris removal endorsements; add rental loss from substantial completion, equipment breakdown, automobile liability and the Architect's professional liability; and specify mortgagee endorsement, additional insured status, primary and non-contributory wording, waiver of subrogation and 30 days' notice of cancellation (10 days for non-payment). Draft §19.1, §19.2 and Schedule 5.

## G-2 | The two flood determinations in the record cite different FIRM panels

**Record.** Appraisal §2: Flood Zone X per FIRM panel **3710406400J**, effective **May 2, 2019**, and "flood insurance is not required by federal regulation". Phase I ESA §2.3: Flood Zone X per FIRM panel **3720098700J**, effective **March 2, 2009**.

**Analysis.** Both conclude that the site is not in a Special Flood Hazard Area, so the credit conclusion is the same, but the panel and effective date cannot both be right and one source may be relying on a superseded map. For a national bank the flood determination is a compliance item (Flood Disaster Protection Act; part 339 for savings associations and part 22 for national banks), not merely an underwriting note, and the file should contain a current determination from a reliable vendor.

**Open.** A current standard flood hazard determination.

**Recommendation.** Obtain a current determination before closing and correct the Appraisal's and the Phase I ESA's references; if any part of the site is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, require flood insurance in the required amount and evidence of it at closing. Draft §4.1(w), §19.1(f) and Drafting Note 21.

# 11. LEGAL AND DOCUMENTATION MATTERS

## H-1 | Guarantor support is adequate in aggregate but concentrated and illiquid

**Record.** Credit Memorandum §2.3: combined net worth $41,300,000 (Ms. Vasquez-Torres $29,800,000; Mr. Osei $11,500,000); combined liquidity $9,300,000 ($6,100,000 and $3,200,000); liquidity covenant $5,000,000; each Guarantor's partial repayment exposure $11,875,000. Credit Memorandum §2.3.3 notes that Mr. Osei's exposure is 103.3% of his net worth and that both guarantors' net worth is weighted to real estate (65% and 59% respectively).

**Analysis.** The aggregate support is strong. The weakness is the second guarantor: Mr. Osei's guarantee exceeds his net worth and his liquid assets cover only 27% of it. In a default requiring the guarantors to perform, the practical recourse will be to Ms. Vasquez-Torres. That is not a reason to decline the credit; it is a reason to size the guarantees against the person who can actually pay, and to test liquidity rather than net worth.

**Open.** Verification of the personal financial statements and the real estate valuations behind them.

**Recommendation.** Retain the aggregate $5,000,000 liquidity covenant, add the net-worth covenant at A-4, require quarterly liquidity certifications (Draft Schedule 7), and consider whether the guarantee should be apportioned rather than joint and several if the Lender's objective is collection rather than litigation.

## H-2 | North Carolina's anti-deficiency regime affects the guaranty structure

**Record.** Credit Memorandum §4.3 ("North Carolina Considerations") flags that North Carolina is a judicial foreclosure state, that deficiency judgments are subject to the fair price confirmation requirements of N.C.G.S. §45-21.36, and that outside counsel should review the guaranty structure for compliance, including "the enforceability of the guaranty obligations independent of the underlying indebtedness and the effect of anti-deficiency protections on guarantor liability". No document addresses the point further.

**Analysis.** North Carolina's power-of-sale foreclosure is subject to the notice and hearing requirements of N.C.G.S. §45-21.16, and on a deficiency motion the obligor is entitled to a hearing and to credit for the fair market value of the property sold under N.C.G.S. §45-21.36. A guaranty that purports to waive those rights may be unenforceable in part and may taint the balance of the waiver provisions. The Loan Documents should preserve rather than purport to waive non-waivable rights, and should be drafted so that a successful challenge to one provision does not invalidate the guarantee.

**Open.** North Carolina counsel's opinion on the enforceability of each waiver and on election of remedies if the Lender pursues the guarantors before completing a foreclosure.

**Recommendation.** Include an express savings provision preserving the fair-value credit and a severability clause specific to the guaranties; require the guaranty to be a guarantee of payment and not of collection but without purporting to waive §45-21.36; and obtain a North Carolina enforceability opinion addressed to the Lender covering the guaranties and the deed of trust. Draft §15.4, §17.4 and §4.1(s).

## H-3 | The North Carolina excise tax on the Deed of Trust appears to be unfunded

**Record.** Detailed Budget 3.07 allows **$245,000** for "Title Insurance & Recording — Lender's title policy, endorsements, recording fees". No document identifies the North Carolina excise tax on instruments evidencing indebtedness. N.C.G.S. §105-228.28 imposes a tax of $1.00 per $100.00 of the principal indebtedness secured by a deed of trust on North Carolina real property.

**Analysis.** On a deed of trust securing $47,500,000 recorded at the full committed amount, the tax is **$475,000** — almost twice the entire combined title and recording line, before any premium. Whether a construction deed of trust may be recorded at the amount of the initial advance (with the balance added by an increase endorsement as advances are made) is a matter of North Carolina practice and Title Company policy, and we have not assumed that it is available; if it is, the exposure is materially reduced. Either way, the budget line appears inadequate and no source of funds has been identified for the difference.

**Open.** Confirmation from Aldersgate Title & Escrow and from North Carolina counsel of the recording amount and the tax computation; the title premium quote.

**Recommendation.** Obtain a written tax and premium computation from the Title Company before closing; increase the Approved Budget line or require the Borrower to fund the excess; and confirm that the Deed of Trust does not need to be re-recorded at a higher amount on each advance. Draft §18.2 and Schedule 3.

## H-4 | The land acquisition date is stated four different ways

**Record.** Phase I ESA §2.2: "Harborview Capital Partners LLC acquired the Subject Property in **2022**". Title Commitment Schedule A, Item 5: title vested by Special Warranty Deed "recorded in Book 7598, Page 443 … dated **June 12, 2023**". Appraisal §6.1: the $8,200,000 "was the price paid by Harborview Capital Partners LLC in **early 2024**". Org chart §1.3: the land was contributed to the Borrower "upon formation of the project entity" — the Borrower was formed on **March 14, 2019**.

**Analysis.** Four dates cannot all be right, and the discrepancy goes to the integrity of the file rather than to the credit: the acquisition date determines when the land entered the capital stack, whether the pre-development spend was capitalised, and whether the Existing Mortgage was assumed at acquisition. A file in which the acquisition date cannot be agreed is a file in which the Borrower's cost basis cannot be relied on.

**Open.** The 2023 closing statement and deed; the Borrower's capital account records.

**Recommendation.** Obtain the acquisition documents and correct the narrative in the Credit Memorandum and any appraisal addendum; do not repeat the Appraisal's "early 2024" statement in the closing file. Draft Schedule 3 and Drafting Note 9.

## H-5 | The claim that the Project is permitted "as of right" is unverified

**Record.** Term Sheet §2: "The proposed development is permitted as of right under the current zoning designation. No special use permit, conditional zoning, or variance is required." Credit Memorandum §3.1 and §13 repeat it. Title Commitment Item 7 states only that the Land "is currently zoned MU-D" and that the Company "makes no representation as to compliance with applicable zoning ordinances or regulations"; Exception 12 excepts the Unified Development Ordinance in its entirety, including setbacks, height, floor area ratio, parking minimums and maximums, open space, public art and design overlay requirements. The Appraisal §2 states that the maximum height in MU-D is 120 feet and that the Project at approximately 78 feet complies, and that minimum parking has been satisfied by 276 spaces.

**Analysis.** "Permitted as of right" is a conclusion that requires (i) a zoning compliance or verification letter from the City, (ii) confirmation of the parking ratio calculated on the correct unit and retail mix, and (iii) the design overlay requirements of the Downtown Development Tier — none of which is in the file. A parking shortfall or an open-space or public-art condition cannot be cured by variance once construction has begun.

**Open.** The zoning compliance letter; the civil and parking calculations.

**Recommendation.** Make the City of Durham zoning compliance letter a closing condition (Draft §4.1(j)) and require a zoning endorsement to the Title Policy (Draft §4.2(iv)); have the Architect certify the parking, open space and public art calculations against the ordinance.

## H-6 | Several documents required by the record are missing, and the Guarantors' addresses are not stated

**Record.** The Term Sheet and Credit Memorandum require, among other things, the Guarantors' personal financial statements and two years of tax returns, the Borrower's audited statements, certificates of good standing, member resolutions, legal opinions, the non-consolidation opinion, the intercreditor agreement and the Assignment of Construction Contract. None is in the file. Neither Guarantor's street address appears in any document; both are identified only by city of residence.
