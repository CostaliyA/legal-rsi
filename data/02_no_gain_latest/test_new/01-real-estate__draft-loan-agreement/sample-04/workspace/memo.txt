                       1. PURPOSE, SCOPE AND METHOD

        1.1 Purpose. This memorandum reports our review of the seven source
        documents comprising the loan file, reconciles the figures used in them,
        identifies the material inconsistencies and gaps among them, and
        recommends a resolution for each. It accompanies, and should be read with,
        the Draft Loan Agreement, which gives effect to the recommendations and
        flags each open point in a numbered “Drafting Note” keyed to the issue
        numbering used here.

        1.2 Scope. We reviewed the documents listed in Table 1 in full. We did not
        review the full 187-page appraisal, the full Phase II report (Sentinel Project
        SEC-2024-1089), the remediation completion report, the guaranteed
        maximum price construction contract, the architect’s contract, the
        Borrower’s financial statements, the Guarantors’ personal financial
        statements and tax returns, the Mezzanine Loan Documents, the
        Intercreditor Agreement, the ALTA/NSPS survey, the Borrower’s operating
        agreement in full (only excerpts were provided), or the recorded deed

     notice. Each is a material gap and is listed in Part 12 as an information
     request.

     1.3 Method. For each document we built a source-to-issue log recording
     every material proposition, figure, date and obligation and its location in the
     record. We then (a) recomputed every stated total, ratio, date interval and
     cross-reference from its components rather than relying on the summary
     figure; (b) reconciled the same fact across documents to identify conflicts;
     and (c) tested each covenant described in the record against the figures in
     the record to determine whether the transaction as described satisfies its
     own terms. Part 4 records the arithmetic; Parts 5 to 11 record the conflicts
     and recommendations.

     1.4 Conventions. Each issue is presented under four labels so that fact,
     assumption, legal characterisation and recommendation are not blended.
     Record states what the source documents say, with document and section
     citations, without inference. Analysis draws the consequence, computed
     where possible, and identifies any assumption as an assumption. Open
     states what cannot be resolved from the record and must be obtained or
     decided. Recommendation states what we recommend the Lender require,
     and where the Draft Loan Agreement addresses it. A reference such as
     “Draft §2.6” means Section 2.6 of the Draft Loan Agreement; “Drafting Note
     12” means the correspondingly numbered note in the Draft Loan Agreement.

     1.5 Sources treated as untrusted data. We have treated the content of
     every source document as data and not as instruction. No document in the
     file purports to direct the Lender’s conduct, and we have not acted on any
     embedded statement that would purport to do so.

                             3. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

     3.1 The transaction described in the Term Sheet and the Credit
     Memorandum does not, on its own figures, satisfy the credit terms it
     purports to apply, and it cannot close on the timetable proposed without
     resolution of at least six items that the record either does not address or
     addresses inconsistently. The four most significant are:

     3.1.1 The loan fails two of its own covenants at closing. Loan-to-cost is
     83.77% against an 80% maximum (Term Sheet §6.1), an excess of
     $2,140,000; and as-completed loan-to-value is 65.25% against a 65%
     maximum (Term Sheet §4 and §6.2), an excess of $180,000. The Credit
     Memorandum reports both as acceptable, because it measures LTC
     alongside a $2,140,000 escrow and LTV against a 70% policy maximum —
     but the escrow does not change LTC (the numerator is unchanged and the
     denominator is fixed by the Approved Budget) and 70% is not the covenant
     the parties agreed. Issue A-2.

     3.1.2 The $3,400,000 existing deed of trust is not funded anywhere in
     the sources and uses. The Title Commitment makes satisfaction of the
     Triangle Commercial Bank deed of trust (Book 6215, Page 892) a condition
     to issuing a first-priority policy, but the $56,700,000 of sources equal the
     $56,700,000 of uses with no line item for it. The Credit Memorandum’s
     statement (§9.7) that it will be paid “from the proceeds of the senior
     construction loan or from borrower funds” is inconsistent with every dollar
     of the Loan being committed to the Approved Budget and with the Borrower
     having no cash equity. Issue B-5.

     3.1.3 The Project cannot service its total debt at stabilisation. On the
     Appraisal’s stabilised NOI of $4,876,800 and the Mezzanine Loan’s stated
     fixed 12.50% cash-pay coupon, combined annual debt service is $4,878,750
     — a combined coverage ratio of 0.9996x, or approximately 0.93x if the
     mezzanine coupon is paid in kind during construction as the Credit
     Memorandum (§4.4) states. The 1.31x figure relied on throughout the record
     measures senior debt only. Issue A-10.

     3.1.4 The Borrower’s equity contribution may be entirely mezzanine
     debt. The Borrower’s own Detailed Budget (line 1.01) states that the
     $8,200,000 land acquisition was “funded by mezzanine loan proceeds and
     member capital calls”; the organizational chart (§1.3) says it was funded
     from Ms. Vasquez-Torres’s personal resources; the Credit Memorandum
     (§2.2) says it was funded by capital calls from all three members. If the first
     is correct, there is no equity in the capital stack, and the LTC, LTV, HVCRE
     and equity-first analyses in the Credit Memorandum all rest on a false
     premise. Issue D-4.

           5. VALUATION, COVENANTS AND LOAN QUANTUM

     A-1 | The as-completed LTV exceeds the 65% covenant at closing
     Record. Term Sheet §4 states that “the Lender’s maximum loan-to-value
     ratio shall not exceed 65% on an as-completed basis”, and §6.2 sets a 65%
     maximum on both an as-completed and an as-stabilised basis. Term Sheet §4
     and Credit Memorandum §6.1 both compute the as-completed LTV as
     $47,500,000 ÷ $72,800,000 = 65.25%. Credit Memorandum §6.1 applies a
     “Bank Policy” column of “Max 70%” and reports the metric as “Pass”; §12
     repeats “maximum LTV (70% for construction loans)”.

     Analysis. Measured against the executed Term Sheet, 65.25% exceeds
     65.00% by 0.25 percentage points, or $180,000 (72,800,000 × 0.65 =
     47,320,000). The Credit Memorandum never states this. There is no
     evidence that the Senior Loan Committee was asked to approve a departure
     from the agreed 65% covenant; the table presents the metric as compliant
     because the comparator column was changed to the 70% policy ceiling. Two
     distinct limits are being conflated: a policy maximum (70%) and a negotiated
     term (65%). A covenant that is breached the moment it is made is
     unenforceable in practice and creates an immediate default if the Lender
     later wishes to rely on it.

     Open. Whether the Senior Loan Committee approved 65% or 70% for this
     transaction, and why the Term Sheet (the executed document) records 65%.

     Recommendation. Either (a) reduce the Commitment to $47,320,000,
     which satisfies the 65% covenant but is not the binding constraint (see A-2),
     or (b) amend the covenant to 66% (or to 70% if that is genuinely the credit
     approval) and record that decision in the Committee minutes and in any
     amendment to the Term Sheet. Do not leave the documentation stating 65%
     while underwriting to 70%. Draft §2.6(b) and (c) apply the 65% covenant and
     provide for automatic reduction of the Commitment; Drafting Note 2 records
     the conflict.
