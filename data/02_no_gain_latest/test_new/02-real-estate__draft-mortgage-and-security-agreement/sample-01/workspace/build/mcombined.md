# 1. PURPOSE, SCOPE AND SUMMARY OF CONCLUSIONS

**1.1 Purpose.** This memorandum accompanies the initial draft of the Mortgage and Security Agreement (the "**Draft Mortgage**") for the captioned loan (the "**Loan**") and records (a) the issues identified in the source record before and during drafting, (b) the independent recomputation of every figure, date and ratio used in the Draft Mortgage, (c) the drafting decisions taken, including provisions added beyond the executed Term Sheet, and (d) the items that must be resolved or documented by the client before the Draft Mortgage is circulated to borrower's counsel or executed. It responds to your markup notes of March 18, 2025 and addresses each instruction item in turn.

**1.2 Scope and standard.** The record consists of the eight documents listed in Section 2. All statements of fact in this memorandum are cited to the source document and section. No fact has been assumed without labeling it; where the record is silent, the gap is identified rather than filled. All arithmetic has been recomputed from source components and not taken from any summary or prior calculation.

**1.3 Summary of conclusions.** The Draft Mortgage is in a form suitable for internal partner review. The instrument is not, however, ready to be sent to borrower's counsel in its present state, and the Loan should not close, until four matters are resolved:

> **(a) Loan sizing and credit approval.** The file's own projections do not support a $14,750,000 loan at the stated terms. Even on the most favorable stabilized assumptions in the rent roll, projected net operating income ($622,772) produces a debt service coverage ratio of 0.55x on the post-interest-only amortizing debt service and 0.72x on interest-only debt service, against a required 1.25x. The appraisal's income approach likewise does not reconcile to its concluded values (implied capitalization rates of 1.86% as-is and 2.52% as-stabilized, against the 5.75%–6.50% range the appraiser identifies as market). The DSCR covenant in Article 12 will be breached at the first test date as a matter of arithmetic. A documented credit approval or exception is required before we finalize the covenant, the cure mechanism and the cash management provisions.
>
> **(b) Execution authority.** Borrower's Operating Agreement requires the approval of Members holding at least 75% of the membership interests for any financing in excess of $1,000,000. Graydon Development Group LLC holds 72%. Mr. Graydon's signature alone, through GDG as Managing Member, is therefore not sufficient authority to execute the Mortgage and the other Loan Documents. Certified written consents of GDG plus at least one Passive Member (and ideally all three Members) must be obtained, and the title company will require the same evidence under Requirement 8 of the commitment.
>
> **(c) Single-purpose entity and Operating Agreement Amendment.** The Operating Agreement contains none of the single-purpose entity provisions the Term Sheet requires (no independent manager, no bankruptcy consent, no separateness covenants, no single-purpose purpose clause, no change-of-control restriction). The Draft Mortgage assumes the amendment is delivered as a closing condition and is executed by all three Members. Tidewater GP Holdings LLC's authority to bind Tidewater Opportunity Fund II LP, and Harborline Capital Advisors LLC's governing documents, are not in the record.
>
> **(d) Title to the Anson Lane strip and the SCDHEC land use restriction.** The strip is insured as part of the fee estate on the face of the commitment but was conveyed by quitclaim deed only, and the title officer expressly reserves the right to charge an additional premium or limit coverage by endorsement. Affirmative coverage must be negotiated and confirmed before closing (see Section 5.1). The land use restriction on Lot 7 requires bespoke covenants, an event of default and reporting mechanics, which the Draft Mortgage contains in Articles 8 and 11 (see Section 5.2).

**1.4 Format implications.** The Draft Mortgage incorporates an absolute assignment of leases and rents (Article 4) rather than a separate instrument, as you directed; it contains a self-contained yield maintenance formula (Article 13) built on the Term Sheet rather than on the formulation in your notes, which I recommend against adopting as drafted (see Section 7.1); and it does not grant a power of sale (Section 14.4).

# 4. CREDIT CAPACITY, VALUATION AND LOAN SIZING

The four issues in this section are analytical rather than drafting points in the narrow sense, but they determine whether the covenant, reserve and cure architecture we have drafted can function. I have recomputed each figure from source components.

**4.1 Debt service capacity and the DSCR covenant — recomputation.**

**Source components.** Loan $14,750,000 (Term Sheet §3.1); interest rate Daily SOFR plus 285 basis points with a 3.00% SOFR floor, i.e. 5.85% at the floor (Term Sheet §3.6); interest-only for months 1–24, then 25-year amortization from May 15, 2027 (Term Sheet §3.9); DSCR covenant of 1.25x beginning after month 24 (Term Sheet §7.1); stabilized NOI of $622,772 on the borrower's best-case projection (Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule rows 8–18, being $755,549 gross revenue less $37,777 vacancy/credit loss less $95,000 net landlord expense burden).

**Recomputation.**

| Metric | Amount | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Annual interest during interest-only period | $862,875 | $14,750,000 × 5.85% (the rent roll shows $863,375 — a $500 rounding error in the file) |
| Monthly payment, 25-year amortization at 5.85% | $93,686.61 | Standard amortization formula, 300 periods |
| Annual debt service after the interest-only period | $1,124,239 | $93,686.61 × 12 (the rent roll shows $1,122,000 — understated by $2,239) |
| DSCR on amortizing debt service | **0.55x** | $622,772 ÷ $1,124,239 |
| DSCR on interest-only debt service | **0.72x** | $622,772 ÷ $862,875 |
| NOI required to meet 1.25x on amortizing debt service | **$1,405,299** | $1,124,239 × 1.25 |
| NOI required to meet 1.25x on interest-only debt service | **$1,078,594** | $862,875 × 1.25 |
| Shortfall against the amortizing requirement | $782,527 (125.6%) | $1,405,299 − $622,772 |
| Debt yield | **4.22%** | $622,772 ÷ $14,750,000 |
| Loan size supported at 1.25x DSCR (25-year amortization, 5.85%) | ≈ **$6,536,000** | $622,772 ÷ 1.25 ÷ 0.0762196 |
| Loan size supported at a 9.0% debt yield | ≈ **$6,920,000** | $622,772 ÷ 0.09 |

**Conclusion.** On the file's own numbers the Loan is approximately twice the size that the Property's income supports. The interest due during the interest-only period ($862,875) exceeds the Property's projected gross revenue at stabilized occupancy ($755,549). The Vacancy Schedule states the same conclusion in terms (row 25: "DOES NOT MEET 1.25x COVENANT"; row 39: "DSCR compliance at month 24 is at significant risk regardless of lease-up pace"). The Term Sheet's own requirement for an Interest Shortfall Guaranty covering the first fourteen months after closing (Term Sheet §5.5(c)) is consistent with — and corroborates — the proposition that the parties expect the Property not to cover debt service during the renovation and lease-up period.

**Options, for your discussion with Marcus Prideaux.** (i) Reduce the loan amount to a supportable level; (ii) lengthen the interest-only period to or beyond the first DSCR test date and reset the covenant (the Term Sheet already ends interest-only at month 24, one month before amortization begins in the same window in which the covenant is first tested); (iii) retain the loan amount but require a DSCR cure deposit, a cash-management sweep at a sub-1.10x trigger and an extension of the Interest Shortfall Guaranty through at least the first test date; (iv) treat the DSCR covenant as a placeholder and obtain a documented credit exception. What we should not do is paper the covenant as drafted and say nothing: a covenant that fails on the closing-date projections is either a default trigger from day one or evidence that the covenant was not intended to be enforced, and neither posture serves the Bank well in a workout. **Draft:** Article 12 implements the covenant precisely (Section 12.1), defines Net Operating Income and Annual Debt Service (Section 1.1), and adds a limited DSCR cure right (Section 12.6) — the Term Sheet's "without cure" language in §11.1(i) presupposes a cure mechanic that the Term Sheet never supplies. The cure mechanic is our construct and requires your approval (Section 10).

**Recomputation.** A 6.25% capitalization rate applied to NOI of $332,000–$345,000 indicates a value of **$5,312,000–$5,520,000**, not $18,200,000. A 5.85% rate applied to stabilized NOI of $582,000–$592,000 indicates a value of **$9,948,718–$10,119,658**, not $23,500,000. Either the NOI figures or the value conclusions are wrong; the discrepancy is not a rounding matter. This is also the answer to the question you raised with Marcus about whether the as-is LTV "runs a little hot": at 81.04% it is well above customary CRE leverage, and 82.87% of the purchase price ($14,750,000 ÷ $17,800,000), and on the appraiser's own income figures the ratio would exceed 100%.

# 7. LOAN TERMS REQUIRING PRECISION

**7.1 Yield maintenance (your item 5).**

**What the Term Sheet says.** Section 4.2 provides for prepayment in whole (not in part) in months 25–48 on 30 days' notice, with a premium equal to the **greater of** (a) 1.00% of the outstanding principal balance, or (b) the present value of the excess of (i) interest that would have accrued on the prepaid principal at the Note rate for the period from prepayment through the end of the 48th month, over (ii) interest that would accrue at "the treasury rate plus fifty (50) basis points" for that period, "discounted to present value at the treasury rate."

**What your notes propose.** A formula in which the premium equals the positive excess of the present value of all remaining scheduled payments of principal and interest (discounted at the Treasury Rate) over the outstanding principal prepaid, "in no event less than zero."

**Why I did not adopt the formulation in your notes as drafted.** It departs from the executed Term Sheet in three respects, each of which favors the borrower:

> (i) it omits the **1.00% floor**, which the Term Sheet makes part of a "greater of" test — under your formula a premium of zero is possible;
>
> (ii) it computes the differential by reference to the **Note rate stream discounted at the Treasury Rate** and subtracts principal, rather than computing the **interest differential between the Note rate and the Treasury Rate plus 50 basis points**; the two approaches produce materially different numbers when the curve is steep, and the Term Sheet's construct is the bargained-for one; and
>
> (iii) it leaves the "treasury rate + 50 bps" ambiguity unresolved, whereas the Term Sheet uses the spread in the comparison stream.

**What the Draft Mortgage does.** Section 13.2 implements the Term Sheet's "greater of" structure: (a) 1.00% of the principal prepaid; or (b) the present value, discounted at the Treasury Rate, of the excess of interest at the Note rate (after the SOFR floor) over interest at the Treasury Rate plus 50 basis points, for the period from prepayment to the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period, with monthly discounting, a proration convention for a mid-month prepayment, a floor of zero on the computed excess (the floor operates through the 1.00% minimum in clause (a), so the premium can never be less than 1.00%), and a conclusive-calculation clause. "Treasury Rate" is defined in Section 1.1(qq) as the H.15 yield, determined as of the second Business Day preceding prepayment, on the security maturing closest to but not exceeding the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period, with linear interpolation if there is no exact match. The Yield Maintenance Period is defined as ending April 15, 2029 (month 48).

**This resolves your four questions** as follows: (a) CMT, matched to the end of the Yield Maintenance Period, interpolated; (b) discount rate is the Treasury Rate alone, and the 50 basis points is applied to the comparison stream; (c) the premium is the greater of the 1% floor and the computed amount; (d) the computed amount floors at zero but the 1% floor applies regardless. **Two items still need the client:** first, confirm the interpretation of the 50 basis points (we apply it in the comparison stream, consistent with the Term Sheet's text, rather than adding it to the Note rate as you suggested as an alternative reading); second, confirm whether the Bank wants a different interpolation convention (e.g. matched to the remaining loan term rather than to the end of the Yield Maintenance Period). **Enforceability:** the formula is deterministic and objectively calculable, which is the protection against a penalty characterization; I have also avoided any subjective inputs.

**7.6 Default mechanics — two gaps and one sequencing problem.**

> **(a) Late charge versus payment default.** Term Sheet §3.11 assesses a 5% late charge on any monthly installment not received within ten **calendar** days of the due date; Term Sheet §11.2(a) gives a cure period of five **business** days for payment defaults. The default therefore occurs before the late charge is triggered. The Draft Mortgage preserves both mechanisms independently: the late charge accrues under §2.6 whether or not a default has occurred and whether or not notice has been given, and the payment default arises under §14.2(a) after five business days, expressly without affecting the late charge or default interest. This is the correct commercial result but should be confirmed as intentional.
>
> **(b) Insurance defaults have no cure period.** Term Sheet §11.1(f) makes a failure to maintain insurance an Event of Default. Term Sheet §11.2(b) excludes "insurance defaults" from the 30-day non-monetary cure period, and §11.2(c) gives no cure period only for SPE and transfer defaults. The result is a gap: insurance defaults fall outside every stated cure category. The Draft Mortgage supplies a ten-day cure period after written notice (§14.2(d)) — a drafting solution, flagged in Section 10.
>
> **(c) Land use restriction defaults.** Neither the Term Sheet nor the commitment specifies the cure mechanics for an LUR breach. The Draft Mortgage provides ten business days after notice, and immediate default where SCDHEC has issued a notice of violation or order or the breach is not curable (§14.2(e)). This implements Title Closer's Note 1 while preserving a short cure window.

**7.7 Interest computation — three terms the Term Sheet leaves open.** The Term Sheet defines "Daily SOFR" (rate published on the applicable business day, or the preceding business day if not published) and imposes a 3.00% floor, but says nothing about (i) the day-count convention, (ii) whether interest for a monthly period is computed on the Daily SOFR in effect on a single day or compounded daily in arrears, or (iii) any requirement for an interest rate cap. Section 2.2 of the Draft Mortgage adopts **actual/360**, a monthly period rate based on Daily SOFR as determined for the applicable Business Day, and no cap requirement. Actual/360 rather than actual/365 adds approximately 1.4% to the effective annual yield on a 5.85% rate. Because the Loan cannot service at the floor rate (Section 4.1), the floating-rate exposure is material: each 100 basis point increase raises annual interest by $147,500, and the term sheet contains no cap, no cap purchase covenant and no rate-lock alternative. **Recommendation:** confirm the day count and compounding convention with Marcus (and conform the Note and the borrower disclosure), and consider requiring a capped or hedged rate at least until the DSCR test date.

**7.8 Guaranty set and characterization.**

**Facts.** Term Sheet §5.5 is headed "Guaranty" and opens by describing a "**full recourse personal guaranty**," then describes (a) a non-recourse carve-out guaranty, (b) a completion guaranty, and (c) a payment guaranty limited to interest shortfalls during the first fourteen months. Term Sheet §14.3(e) lists a single "Guaranty Agreement" among the closing documents, and §5.4 requires a separate Environmental Indemnity Agreement executed by Borrower and Guarantor.

**Issues.** (i) The "full recourse" description is inconsistent with the non-recourse structure in §5.5(a) and with the limitation of liability the Bank's own structure implies; (ii) the document list does not match the number of guaranties described; (iii) the Interest Shortfall Guaranty's fourteen-month period ends June 15, 2026, exactly the Substantial Completion Deadline, and there is then no credit support between June 2026 and the first DSCR test at the end of June 2027 — the precise period in which the Vacancy Schedule projects the greatest shortfall.

**Draft.** Article 16 treats the Guaranty as three instruments plus the Environmental Indemnity Agreement, cross-references each by name, limits Borrower's and the Members' liability to the Collateral except for the carve-outs (§16.2), sets out the Carve-Out Events (§16.3), preserves the full recourse character of the environmental obligations (§16.4), and describes the Interest Shortfall Guaranty's scope without reducing Borrower's payment obligations (§16.6). The Draft Mortgage does **not** characterise the Guaranty as a full repayment guaranty, because on the Term Sheet's terms it is not. **Action:** confirm with Marcus whether the Bank intends one combined Guaranty Agreement or three separate instruments, and conform the closing checklist and the §14.3 document list. Consider also extending the Interest Shortfall Guaranty to the first DSCR test date.


# 8. RENOVATION AND CONSTRUCTION ISSUES

**8.1 The draw schedule ignores the 10% retainage (high).** Term Sheet §8.4 requires Lender to "retain ten percent (10%) retainage from each disbursement from the Renovation Holdback until substantial completion," released on receipt of satisfactory evidence of substantial completion, final lien waivers and a final title date-down endorsement. The Draw Schedule does not reflect any retention: cumulative lender draws reach the full $2,250,000 in month 14 (Draw Schedule, column R, row 15). If a 10% retainage were applied, cumulative lender disbursements at the point of substantial completion should be $2,025,000, with $225,000 held back for release after completion, final lien waivers and the final date-down.

**Why it matters.** (i) The Draw Schedule cannot be used as the disbursement benchmark unless it is corrected; (ii) the schedule's month 14 disbursement falls exactly on the Substantial Completion Deadline of June 15, 2026, so on its own timeline the holdback is exhausted on the last permissible day with no room for punch list, and no retention remains to secure completion; (iii) the draw conditions in the Draft Mortgage (Section 8.6, retainage; Section 8.15, release only on final waivers, final date-down and certificate of occupancy) follow the Term Sheet and therefore do **not** match the Draw Schedule. **Action:** return the Draw Schedule to Borrower for conforming; the $225,000 retention is also the only realistic leverage for punch list completion given that the Completion Guaranty is a credit backstop rather than an operational one.

**8.2 Equity funding timing and a $5,000 arithmetic overrun.**

The chosen construction should be reflected in §12.1(m) of the closing conditions, and the $5,000 overrun should be corrected in the budget.

**8.3 The TI/LC Reserve is not reflected in the budget or the sources and uses.**

**Facts.** Term Sheet §6.3 requires a $150,000 TI/leasing commission reserve to be deposited at closing, disbursed only for tenant improvements and leasing commissions on vacant space, and replenished to $150,000 from Borrower's own funds within 30 days of demand. The Renovation Budget separately contains a "Tenant Improvement Allowances" line of $550,000 (Budget Detail line 4), of which $450,000 is allocated to the Renovation Holdback and $100,000 to Borrower equity. The Vacancy Schedule models TI costs of $95,000 (Bay 2, at $25/SF) and $215,600 (Suite B, at $22/SF), totaling $310,600, plus leasing commissions of $28,500 and $61,250, totaling $89,750 — and, in the worst case, a further $76,000 of TI and $28,500 of commissions if Harmon & Wren does not renew Bay 3.

**Recomputation and observations.** The budgeted TI allowance is $40.44/SF blended ($550,000 ÷ 13,600 SF of vacant/at-risk space); the modeled TI need is $22.84/SF ($310,600 ÷ 13,600 SF), or $28.43/SF in the Bay 3 non-renewal case. Three separate pools ($450,000 holdback allocation, $100,000 borrower equity, $150,000 TI/LC Reserve) are therefore available against a modeled need of $310,600–$504,850 including commissions. The TI/LC Reserve is not included in the Total Project Cost, the Budget Detail or the Draw Schedule, so there is a genuine risk of either double funding the same tenant improvements or leaving the reserve unfunded at closing (see Section 4.4). **Draft:** Article 10 implements the reserve with the replenishment covenant (§10.3) and confines its use to TI and leasing commissions; Article 8 permits TI draws from the holdback against the budget line (§8.2, §8.4). **Action:** obtain a written disbursement priority from Borrower (holdback TI line first, then the reserve) and confirm whether the $550,000 TI line should be reduced to reflect the reserve, which would also reduce the holdback need.

**8.5 Mechanics' liens, date-downs and future-advance priority.** South Carolina mechanics' liens attach in favor of those who furnish labor or materials, and a recorded mortgage has priority over liens for work commenced afterwards. The protections are therefore procedural: record the Mortgage before any work begins; obtain a pre-closing date-down and owner's affidavit to delete the standard exceptions (Requirements 9–12); require the date-down at every draw; require conditional and unconditional lien waivers (subcontractors over $25,000); and retain 10%. The Draft Mortgage implements each of these (§3.8, §8.4(b)(iii), §8.5(f), §8.10), together with the future-advance clause and maximum principal amount (§3.4) and the representation that no work has been performed that could give rise to a lien (§5.13). **Action:** confirm at closing that no work has commenced at the Property (the Draw Schedule contemplates "mobilization, permits, architectural/engineering fees" in month 1 and an elevator equipment order deposit, which should be confirmed as not constituting work at the Property), and confirm that the future advance endorsement is issued.

Neither the Term Sheet nor the record addresses liquidated damages or a guaranteed maximum price beyond the contract sum; if the Bank wants a GMP representation or a cost-overrun guaranty, it must come from the Completion Guaranty (which covers completion within budget) or be added. **Action:** obtain the GC Contract and BAR approvals; confirm the Completion Guaranty's "within the Renovation Budget" language.
