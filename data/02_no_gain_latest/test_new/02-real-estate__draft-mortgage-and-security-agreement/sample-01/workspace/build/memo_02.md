
# 6. ENTITY AUTHORITY AND SINGLE-PURPOSE ENTITY ISSUES

**6.1 Execution authority — the 75% member approval requirement (critical).**

**Facts.** The Operating Agreement vests the Managing Member with authority to execute "loan documents, promissory notes, deeds of trust, mortgages, security agreements, guaranties, and related instruments" (§3.1(e)), but §3.2 makes "any financing or refinancing of the Property in an amount in excess of $1,000,000" a "Major Decision" requiring the prior written approval of Members holding at least **seventy-five percent (75%)** of the outstanding membership interests. GDG holds **72%**; Tidewater 18%; Harborline 10%. The Operating Agreement summary expressly notes that "GDG alone cannot approve any Major Decision."

**Consequence.** A $14,750,000 mortgage is a Major Decision. GDG's signature — and therefore Mr. Graydon's signature as sole member and manager of GDG — is **not** sufficient authority to bind Borrower. At least one Passive Member must join (GDG plus Tidewater = 90%; GDG plus Harborline = 82%). Title Requirement 8(b) and 8(c) require satisfactory evidence of the authority of the persons executing the Mortgage and of GDG, and the title company will read the Operating Agreement. The legal opinion required by Term Sheet §12.1(h)(ii) (due authorization) cannot be given without it.

**Draft.** Section 5.1 of the Draft Mortgage contains an affirmative representation, in terms, that execution has been authorized by Members holding not less than 75% under Operating Agreement §3.2(ii). That representation must be true at signing. **Action (before execution):** obtain a written consent or resolution signed by GDG and at least one Passive Member (recommend all three, which also supports the Operating Agreement Amendment — see §6.2), certified as true and complete, together with the incumbency certificates.

**6.2 Single-purpose entity provisions are absent and must be papered (critical).**

**Facts — what the Operating Agreement does contain and what it does not.**

| Requirement | Position under the Operating Agreement | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Independent manager with bankruptcy consent | No provision; "does not impose any requirement for GDG to appoint or maintain an independent director, independent manager, or springing member" (§3.1) | GDG (i.e. Mr. Graydon) can file Borrower into bankruptcy unilaterally |
| Bankruptcy filing as a Major Decision | The Major Decisions list (§3.2) omits bankruptcy, insolvency or reorganization | No supermajority or third-party consent required |
| Separateness covenants | None; no prohibition on commingling, no requirement of separate accounts or records, no arms'-length requirement | Substantive consolidation risk destroys the non-recourse structure |
| Single-purpose purpose clause | Purpose is "…to acquire, own, hold, manage, lease, renovate, improve, operate, finance, refinance, and ultimately dispose of" the Property "and to engage in any and all activities necessary, incidental, or related thereto" (§1.4) | The catch-all is broad; the agreement "does not contain any language restricting the Company to single-purpose entity status" |
| Lender consent to dissolution | No provision; dissolution on 75% member consent or sale of the Property (§8.1) | Borrower can dissolve without Lender consent |
| Amendment of SPE provisions | Amendments require 75% member consent, plus each affected Member for changes to economic rights or capital obligations (§9.1); no lender consent requirement | SPE protections could be removed without Lender's knowledge |
| Change of control | No provision capturing indirect or upstream changes; §4.1 applies only to transfers of membership interests in Borrower (§4.2, note following) | See §6.4 below |

**One correction to your notes.** Your markup describes the purpose clause as allowing "any lawful business." The Operating Agreement summary actually quotes a property-specific purpose clause with a broad incidental-activities catch-all (§1.4). The difference matters because the representation we draft must match the document: the Draft Mortgage's §7.6(b)(iii) requires an amended purpose clause restricting the business to the Property and incidental activities, and §5.10 makes the representation by reference to the Operating Agreement **as amended by the Operating Agreement Amendment**. I have deliberately not drafted a representation that the current agreement already complies, because it does not (see next paragraph).

**Draft treatment.** You asked that the SPE covenants be drafted as though the Operating Agreement already complies, with separate representations. I have taken a slightly different approach, for one reason: a representation that the organizational documents contain compliant SPE provisions would be knowingly false as of today, is an Event of Default and a recourse carve-out on day one, and would give the borrower's counsel an argument that we drafted around a known fact. Instead:

>
> (b) Section 7.6 defines the **Operating Agreement Amendment**, requires it to be executed by all three Members and effective not later than the Closing Date, specifies its contents (independent manager, separateness, single-purpose purpose clause, unanimity plus Lender consent for amendment of those provisions), and prohibits any amendment, modification or waiver of any single-purpose entity provision without Lender's consent in each instance (§7.6(a)–(b));
>
> (c) Section 5.10 makes the representation that the organizational documents, **as amended by the Operating Agreement Amendment**, contain the required provisions, and that the amendment has been adopted; and
>
> (d) Section 7.6(b) contains an express acknowledgment that the Operating Agreement did not contain these provisions as of the Term Sheet date, so that the record is honest and the amendment is a documented closing condition rather than a fiction.

# 7. LOAN TERMS REQUIRING PRECISION

**7.1 Yield maintenance (your item 5).**

**What the Term Sheet says.** Section 4.2 provides for prepayment in whole (not in part) in months 25–48 on 30 days' notice, with a premium equal to the **greater of** (a) 1.00% of the outstanding principal balance, or (b) the present value of the excess of (i) interest that would have accrued on the prepaid principal at the Note rate for the period from prepayment through the end of the 48th month, over (ii) interest that would accrue at "the treasury rate plus fifty (50) basis points" for that period, "discounted to present value at the treasury rate."

**What your notes propose.** A formula in which the premium equals the positive excess of the present value of all remaining scheduled payments of principal and interest (discounted at the Treasury Rate) over the outstanding principal prepaid, "in no event less than zero."

**Why I did not adopt the formulation in your notes as drafted.** It departs from the executed Term Sheet in three respects, each of which favors the borrower:

> (i) it omits the **1.00% floor**, which the Term Sheet makes part of a "greater of" test — under your formula a premium of zero is possible;
>
> (ii) it computes the differential by reference to the **Note rate stream discounted at the Treasury Rate** and subtracts principal, rather than computing the **interest differential between the Note rate and the Treasury Rate plus 50 basis points**; the two approaches produce materially different numbers when the curve is steep, and the Term Sheet's construct is the bargained-for one; and
>
> (iii) it leaves the "treasury rate + 50 bps" ambiguity unresolved, whereas the Term Sheet uses the spread in the comparison stream.

**What the Draft Mortgage does.** Section 13.2 implements the Term Sheet's "greater of" structure: (a) 1.00% of the principal prepaid; or (b) the present value, discounted at the Treasury Rate, of the excess of interest at the Note rate (after the SOFR floor) over interest at the Treasury Rate plus 50 basis points, for the period from prepayment to the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period, with monthly discounting, a proration convention for a mid-month prepayment, a floor of zero on the computed excess (the floor operates through the 1.00% minimum in clause (a), so the premium can never be less than 1.00%), and a conclusive-calculation clause. "Treasury Rate" is defined in Section 1.1(qq) as the H.15 yield, determined as of the second Business Day preceding prepayment, on the security maturing closest to but not exceeding the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period, with linear interpolation if there is no exact match. The Yield Maintenance Period is defined as ending April 15, 2029 (month 48).

**This resolves your four questions** as follows: (a) CMT, matched to the end of the Yield Maintenance Period, interpolated; (b) discount rate is the Treasury Rate alone, and the 50 basis points is applied to the comparison stream; (c) the premium is the greater of the 1% floor and the computed amount; (d) the computed amount floors at zero but the 1% floor applies regardless. **Two items still need the client:** first, confirm the interpretation of the 50 basis points (we apply it in the comparison stream, consistent with the Term Sheet's text, rather than adding it to the Note rate as you suggested as an alternative reading); second, confirm whether the Bank wants a different interpolation convention (e.g. matched to the remaining loan term rather than to the end of the Yield Maintenance Period). **Enforceability:** the formula is deterministic and objectively calculable, which is the protection against a penalty characterization; I have also avoided any subjective inputs.

**7.2 Prepayment during the lockout period — a gap in the Term Sheet.** Term Sheet §4.1 prohibits prepayment during months 1–24 and then says "any prepayment made in violation of this restriction shall be subject to a prepayment premium as described in Section 4.2." But §4.2's premium is defined only for prepayments made *during months 25–48*; there is no defined premium for a lockout-period prepayment. Section 13.1 of the Draft Mortgage resolves this by (a) making the prohibition absolute and permitting Lender to refuse any tender, and (b) providing that if Lender elects to accept a tender, the Yield Maintenance Premium is computed as though the prepayment had been made on the first day of the Yield Maintenance Period, plus all other amounts due. That is a drafting solution to an ambiguity, and it should be confirmed. Also confirm that a voluntary prepayment during the lockout period is intended to be an Event of Default (the Draft Mortgage does not make it one; it treats the tender as refusable and, if accepted, premium-bearing).

**7.3 Casualty and condemnation.** The Term Sheet is silent on (a) the thresholds above which the Bank controls insurance proceeds, (b) whether proceeds are applied to the Loan or released for restoration, and (c) whether any prepayment premium applies to an involuntary prepayment. You asked me to confirm the casualty/condemnation carve-out with the client. The Draft Mortgage (Article 15) provides: Lender participation in adjusting claims over $250,000; Borrower restoration permitted where the loss is under $500,000, no default exists and proceeds are sufficient; otherwise Lender's election between restoration (on conditions) and application to the Loan; application **at par** with no premium, penalty or yield maintenance (§13.5, §15.5); and coordination with the LUR where restoration affects the Bay 1 slab (§15.4(d)). The $250,000/$500,000/12-month thresholds are our proposed figures, not deal terms (Section 10). Confirm with Marcus: (i) whether the Bank wants a premium on involuntary prepayments (you noted this is the minority position), and (ii) the thresholds.

The Term Sheet is the controlling document and is internally coherent: a quarterly test measured on trailing twelve-month NOI must be measured at a quarter end, and April 15, 2027 is not a quarter end. The Draft Mortgage follows the Term Sheet (Section 12.1(a): the calendar quarter ending June 30, 2027, and quarterly thereafter) and defines Net Operating Income and Annual Debt Service on a trailing-twelve-month and forward-looking basis respectively (Section 1.1(aa) and (b)). **7.6 Default mechanics — two gaps and one sequencing problem.**

> **(a) Late charge versus payment default.** Term Sheet §3.11 assesses a 5% late charge on any monthly installment not received within ten **calendar** days of the due date; Term Sheet §11.2(a) gives a cure period of five **business** days for payment defaults. The default therefore occurs before the late charge is triggered. The Draft Mortgage preserves both mechanisms independently: the late charge accrues under §2.6 whether or not a default has occurred and whether or not notice has been given, and the payment default arises under §14.2(a) after five business days, expressly without affecting the late charge or default interest. This is the correct commercial result but should be confirmed as intentional.
>
> **(b) Insurance defaults have no cure period.** Term Sheet §11.1(f) makes a failure to maintain insurance an Event of Default. Term Sheet §11.2(b) excludes "insurance defaults" from the 30-day non-monetary cure period, and §11.2(c) gives no cure period only for SPE and transfer defaults. The result is a gap: insurance defaults fall outside every stated cure category. The Draft Mortgage supplies a ten-day cure period after written notice (§14.2(d)) — a drafting solution, flagged in Section 10.
>
> **(c) Land use restriction defaults.** Neither the Term Sheet nor the commitment specifies the cure mechanics for an LUR breach. The Draft Mortgage provides ten business days after notice, and immediate default where SCDHEC has issued a notice of violation or order or the breach is not curable (§14.2(e)). This implements Title Closer's Note 1 while preserving a short cure window.

**7.7 Interest computation — three terms the Term Sheet leaves open.** The Term Sheet defines "Daily SOFR" (rate published on the applicable business day, or the preceding business day if not published) and imposes a 3.00% floor, but says nothing about (i) the day-count convention, (ii) whether interest for a monthly period is computed on the Daily SOFR in effect on a single day or compounded daily in arrears, or (iii) any requirement for an interest rate cap. Section 2.2 of the Draft Mortgage adopts **actual/360**, a monthly period rate based on Daily SOFR as determined for the applicable Business Day, and no cap requirement. Actual/360 rather than actual/365 adds approximately 1.4% to the effective annual yield on a 5.85% rate. Because the Loan cannot service at the floor rate (Section 4.1), the floating-rate exposure is material: each 100 basis point increase raises annual interest by $147,500, and the term sheet contains no cap, no cap purchase covenant and no rate-lock alternative. **Recommendation:** confirm the day count and compounding convention with Marcus (and conform the Note and the borrower disclosure), and consider requiring a capped or hedged rate at least until the DSCR test date.

**7.8 Guaranty set and characterization.**

**Facts.** Term Sheet §5.5 is headed "Guaranty" and opens by describing a "**full recourse personal guaranty**," then describes (a) a non-recourse carve-out guaranty, (b) a completion guaranty, and (c) a payment guaranty limited to interest shortfalls during the first fourteen months. Term Sheet §14.3(e) lists a single "Guaranty Agreement" among the closing documents, and §5.4 requires a separate Environmental Indemnity Agreement executed by Borrower and Guarantor.

**Issues.** (i) The "full recourse" description is inconsistent with the non-recourse structure in §5.5(a) and with the limitation of liability the Bank's own structure implies; (ii) the document list does not match the number of guaranties described; (iii) the Interest Shortfall Guaranty's fourteen-month period ends June 15, 2026, exactly the Substantial Completion Deadline, and there is then no credit support between June 2026 and the first DSCR test at the end of June 2027 — the precise period in which the Vacancy Schedule projects the greatest shortfall.

**Draft.** Article 16 treats the Guaranty as three instruments plus the Environmental Indemnity Agreement, cross-references each by name, limits Borrower's and the Members' liability to the Collateral except for the carve-outs (§16.2), sets out the Carve-Out Events (§16.3), preserves the full recourse character of the environmental obligations (§16.4), and describes the Interest Shortfall Guaranty's scope without reducing Borrower's payment obligations (§16.6). The Draft Mortgage does **not** characterise the Guaranty as a full repayment guaranty, because on the Term Sheet's terms it is not. **Action:** confirm with Marcus whether the Bank intends one combined Guaranty Agreement or three separate instruments, and conform the closing checklist and the §14.3 document list. Consider also extending the Interest Shortfall Guaranty to the first DSCR test date.
