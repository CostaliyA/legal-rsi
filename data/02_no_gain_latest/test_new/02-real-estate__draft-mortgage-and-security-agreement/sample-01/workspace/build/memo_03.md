
# 8. RENOVATION AND CONSTRUCTION ISSUES

**8.1 The draw schedule ignores the 10% retainage (high).** Term Sheet §8.4 requires Lender to "retain ten percent (10%) retainage from each disbursement from the Renovation Holdback until substantial completion," released on receipt of satisfactory evidence of substantial completion, final lien waivers and a final title date-down endorsement. The Draw Schedule does not reflect any retention: cumulative lender draws reach the full $2,250,000 in month 14 (Draw Schedule, column R, row 15). If a 10% retainage were applied, cumulative lender disbursements at the point of substantial completion should be $2,025,000, with $225,000 held back for release after completion, final lien waivers and the final date-down.

**Why it matters.** (i) The Draw Schedule cannot be used as the disbursement benchmark unless it is corrected; (ii) the schedule's month 14 disbursement falls exactly on the Substantial Completion Deadline of June 15, 2026, so on its own timeline the holdback is exhausted on the last permissible day with no room for punch list, and no retention remains to secure completion; (iii) the draw conditions in the Draft Mortgage (Section 8.6, retainage; Section 8.15, release only on final waivers, final date-down and certificate of occupancy) follow the Term Sheet and therefore do **not** match the Draw Schedule. **Action:** return the Draw Schedule to Borrower for conforming; the $225,000 retention is also the only realistic leverage for punch list completion given that the Completion Guaranty is a credit backstop rather than an operational one.

**8.2 Equity funding timing and a $5,000 arithmetic overrun.**

**Facts.** Three statements of the Borrower's equity obligation are in the record, and they do not agree:

| Source | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Term Sheet §3.4 | Borrower Equity Contribution of $6,200,000, approximately 29.6% of Total Project Cost |
| Term Sheet §12.1(m) | Receipt by Lender of Borrower's equity contribution of $6,200,000, or satisfactory evidence of the funding thereof, **on or prior to the date of closing** |
| Renovation Budget, Budget Detail row 9 | Borrower Equity Allocation for renovation = $900,000 (28.57% of the $3,150,000 budget) |
| Draw Schedule, column S and row 15 | Borrower equity deployed monthly over 14 months, totaling **$905,000**, leaving a negative remaining commitment of **−$5,000** at month 14 (column V, row 15) |
| Draw Schedule, column Q and row 16 | Total combined draws **$3,155,000** against a Renovation Budget of $3,150,000 |

**Recomputation.** The Draw Schedule's monthly Borrower equity column sums to $905,000 ($75,000 + $90,000 + $100,000 + $90,000 + $85,000 + $100,000 + $60,000 + $70,000 + $70,000 + $70,000 + $40,000 + $30,000 + $10,000 + $15,000), and combined draws sum to $3,155,000. Both exceed the committed amounts by $5,000. The schedule is arithmetically inconsistent with the Budget Detail and with Term Sheet §12.1(m). In addition, the timing conflict is real: if the entire $6,200,000 is funded at closing as §12.1(m) requires, the renovation equity sits in Borrower's account while Lender disburses the holdback, which defeats the "equity first" logic the Draw Schedule assumes; if instead the renovation equity is drawn over fourteen months, the closing condition in §12.1(m) is not satisfied.

**Draft.** Section 8.3 requires Borrower to fund the Renovation Equity and imposes a proportional funding test — aggregate disbursements from the Renovation Holdback may not exceed 71.43% of aggregate Renovation Costs incurred and certified (being $2,250,000 ÷ $3,150,000) — which implements the equity-first economics of the Draw Schedule without requiring the full $6,200,000 to be spent at closing. **Action:** decide with Marcus which construction the Bank wants: (i) full equity deposit at closing with the renovation portion held in a Lender-controlled escrow and released against draws; (ii) the proportional test in the draft; or (iii) a standby letter of credit for the renovation equity. The chosen construction should be reflected in §12.1(m) of the closing conditions, and the $5,000 overrun should be corrected in the budget.

**8.3 The TI/LC Reserve is not reflected in the budget or the sources and uses.**

**Facts.** Term Sheet §6.3 requires a $150,000 TI/leasing commission reserve to be deposited at closing, disbursed only for tenant improvements and leasing commissions on vacant space, and replenished to $150,000 from Borrower's own funds within 30 days of demand. The Renovation Budget separately contains a "Tenant Improvement Allowances" line of $550,000 (Budget Detail line 4), of which $450,000 is allocated to the Renovation Holdback and $100,000 to Borrower equity. The Vacancy Schedule models TI costs of $95,000 (Bay 2, at $25/SF) and $215,600 (Suite B, at $22/SF), totaling $310,600, plus leasing commissions of $28,500 and $61,250, totaling $89,750 — and, in the worst case, a further $76,000 of TI and $28,500 of commissions if Harmon & Wren does not renew Bay 3.

**Recomputation and observations.** The budgeted TI allowance is $40.44/SF blended ($550,000 ÷ 13,600 SF of vacant/at-risk space); the modeled TI need is $22.84/SF ($310,600 ÷ 13,600 SF), or $28.43/SF in the Bay 3 non-renewal case. Three separate pools ($450,000 holdback allocation, $100,000 borrower equity, $150,000 TI/LC Reserve) are therefore available against a modeled need of $310,600–$504,850 including commissions. The TI/LC Reserve is not included in the Total Project Cost, the Budget Detail or the Draw Schedule, so there is a genuine risk of either double funding the same tenant improvements or leaving the reserve unfunded at closing (see Section 4.4). **Draft:** Article 10 implements the reserve with the replenishment covenant (§10.3) and confines its use to TI and leasing commissions; Article 8 permits TI draws from the holdback against the budget line (§8.2, §8.4). **Action:** obtain a written disbursement priority from Borrower (holdback TI line first, then the reserve) and confirm whether the $550,000 TI line should be reduced to reflect the reserve, which would also reduce the holdback need.

**8.4 The Draw Schedule and the Vacancy Schedule contradict each other on Suite B.**

| Item | Draw Schedule | Vacancy Schedule |
|---|---|---|
| Suite B marketing start | Not stated; assumes lease executed month 9 (January 2026) (Draw Schedule column X, row 10: "TI — 2nd Floor Suite B lease executed, build-out commences") | July 2026 (post-substantial completion) |
| Suite B lease execution | Month 9 — January 2026 | November 2026 |
| Suite B rent commencement | Implied month 10–11 (February–March 2026) | February 1, 2027 (21.5 months post-closing) |
| Suite B TI completion | Month 13–14 (May–June 2026) | Not modeled within the renovation period |

These cannot both be right. The Vacancy Schedule's narrative is the more plausible one, because Suite B is a 9,800 SF second-floor office suite that cannot be shown while HVAC, elevator and common-area work is under way — the schedule itself says "Lease-up cannot meaningfully commence until renovation substantially complete." If the Vacancy Schedule is correct, then (i) the $450,000 lender TI allocation and $61,250 commission fall outside the fourteen-month renovation period, (ii) the final draws in months 13–14 cannot be funded on the stated schedule, and (iii) the $225,000 retention question in §8.1 becomes an accounting exercise because the holdback will not be fully drawn by substantial completion. Note also that the Vacancy Schedule flags only 2.5 months between the projected Suite B rent commencement (February 1, 2027) and the DSCR test (April 15, 2027, or the quarter ending June 30, 2027) and labels the timeline "AGGRESSIVE." **Action:** require Borrower to reconcile the two schedules and to produce a single integrated renovation and lease-up schedule; the Draft Mortgage's draw conditions do not depend on the schedule, but the Bank's monitoring will.

**8.5 Mechanics' liens, date-downs and future-advance priority.** South Carolina mechanics' liens attach in favor of those who furnish labor or materials, and a recorded mortgage has priority over liens for work commenced afterwards. The protections are therefore procedural: record the Mortgage before any work begins; obtain a pre-closing date-down and owner's affidavit to delete the standard exceptions (Requirements 9–12); require the date-down at every draw; require conditional and unconditional lien waivers (subcontractors over $25,000); and retain 10%. The Draft Mortgage implements each of these (§3.8, §8.4(b)(iii), §8.5(f), §8.10), together with the future-advance clause and maximum principal amount (§3.4) and the representation that no work has been performed that could give rise to a lien (§5.13). **Action:** confirm at closing that no work has commenced at the Property (the Draw Schedule contemplates "mobilization, permits, architectural/engineering fees" in month 1 and an elevator equipment order deposit, which should be confirmed as not constituting work at the Property), and confirm that the future advance endorsement is issued.

**8.6 Change orders, Board of Architectural Review approvals and the GC Contract.** The GC Contract is not in the file, although Term Sheet §12.1(l) and §8.1 make delivery of the executed contract (with all schedules, exhibits and amendments) a condition precedent. The Renovation Budget states that the $625,000 façade restoration is "subject to Board of Architectural Review oversight" and includes balcony repair; no BAR approval is in the record. The Draft Mortgage therefore (i) requires Lender consent for any "Major Change" — a defined term covering changes that increase the contract sum or budget, reduce scope, affect structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire protection, elevator or façade systems, affect the Bay 1 slab, or delay substantial completion (§8.8); (ii) requires the General Contractor and architect to deliver a consent to the collateral assignment of the GC Contract and Plans (§8.8); (iii) requires all permits and BAR approvals (§8.7); and (iv) reserves lender consent for amendments to the Plans that constitute a Major Change (§8.7). Neither the Term Sheet nor the record addresses liquidated damages or a guaranteed maximum price beyond the contract sum; if the Bank wants a GMP representation or a cost-overrun guaranty, it must come from the Completion Guaranty (which covers completion within budget) or be added. **Action:** obtain the GC Contract and BAR approvals; confirm the Completion Guaranty's "within the Renovation Budget" language.
