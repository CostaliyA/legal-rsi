## PART I — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND TOP-RISK REGISTER

The enclosed Mortgage and Security Agreement (the "**Draft Mortgage**") is a complete first draft prepared from the executed Term Sheet dated April 1, 2025 (the "**Term Sheet**"), the title commitment, the Phase I ESA executive summary, the redacted operating agreement summary, the rent roll and lease abstracts, the renovation budget and draw schedule, and the appraisal summary. The Draft Mortgage is, in our view, ready for internal review and borrower delivery **subject to the open items in Part VII**. Those items are not stylistic. Three of them bear directly on whether this loan should close in its current structure, and we ask that you address them with the loan committee before the draft is sent to borrower's counsel:

**1. The interest-only and DSCR structure cannot be satisfied at any achievable occupancy (Issue E-1).** On the numbers in the record, annual debt service after month 24 is approximately $1,124,239 (25-year amortization at 5.85%), and the best-case stabilized NOI in the borrower's own rent roll is $622,772 — a DSCR of approximately **0.55x** against a required 1.25x. Even interest-only, the ratio is 0.72x. To achieve 1.25x on amortizing debt service, NOI would need to be $1,405,299, or $45.33 per square foot on 31,000 square feet, versus market rents of $19.00–$28.00 per square foot. Even if all 31,000 square feet were leased at the rent roll's stabilized rents and the property carried **zero** operating expenses, gross revenue of $755,549 would produce a DSCR of only 0.67x. As currently structured, the DSCR covenant in Section 7.1 of the Term Sheet and Section 16.1(g) of the Draft Mortgage will be breached at the first test date and will generate an Event of Default on the second. Either the loan amount, the amortization, the interest-only period, the DSCR thresholds or the covenant itself must be re-cut, or the covenant must be supported by a debt service reserve, a cash sweep, an extended interest-shortfall guaranty and a cure mechanism. The Draft Mortgage includes a DSCR cure-deposit mechanism (Section 13.5) and a carve-out structure (Article 18), but those are mitigants, not a substitute for a commercially achievable covenant.

**2. The appraisal's income approach does not reconcile to its value conclusions, and the LTV on which the Loan was sized depends on that appraisal (Issue E-2).** The appraisal states as-is NOI of approximately $332,000–$345,000 and stabilized NOI of $582,000–$592,000, at selected capitalization rates of 6.25% and 5.85% respectively. Applied to those NOI figures, the stated cap rates imply values of approximately **$5.42 million** and **$10.03 million** — not $18,200,000 and $23,500,000. Equivalently, the stated values imply going-in cap rates of approximately **1.86%** and **2.50%**. The sales-comparison data in the appraisal does support approximately $587 per square foot ($18.2 million), so the inconsistency lies in the income approach or in the rent roll from which the NOI figures were derived. Because as-is LTV of 81.04% already sits at or above customary policy limits (and above the level at which your markup notes observed that "as-is LTV runs a little hot"), we recommend a USPAP Standard 3 appraisal review and a reconciliation of the income approach to the rent roll before closing. We cannot reconcile the appraisal internally, and we have not attempted to substitute our own value conclusion.

**3. Sources and uses do not balance once transaction costs are included (Issue E-3).** The Term Sheet's Total Project Cost of $20,950,000 comprises only purchase price ($17,800,000) and Renovation Budget ($3,150,000). Total sources at that same figure are the Loan ($14,750,000) plus Borrower equity ($6,200,000). Transaction costs are therefore unfunded: the origination fee ($147,500) and the South Carolina mortgage recording fee ($54,575) alone total $202,075, and adding the $150,000 tenant improvement and leasing commission reserve required at closing takes the identifiable shortfall to **$352,075**, before title premium, survey, appraisal, environmental, legal fees and other closing costs. Because $900,000 of the $6,200,000 equity is earmarked for renovation, only $5,300,000 of equity is available at closing, and $12,500,000 + $5,300,000 equals the purchase price exactly. Borrower must fund additional equity at closing or the closing statement will not balance.

## PART IV — ISSUE ANALYSIS

### A. Collateral, title and legal description

**A-1. The Anson Lane strip is insured, but it was conveyed by quitclaim deed and the title company reserves its position.**

*Record basis:* Title Commitment, Chain of Title Note 2 and Closer Note 3; Prior Counsel Notes §2.

*Analysis:* Title to the vacated Anson Lane strip derives from a quitclaim deed from the City of Charleston recorded in Deed Book 1128 at Page 443. The commitment states that the Company "is satisfied that the City of Charleston held sufficient interest … however, no warranties of title were provided"; that its willingness to insure "should not be construed as a representation that the chain of title for this parcel is equivalent to that of Parcel One"; and that the Company "reserves the right to require an additional premium for extended coverage on this parcel and/or may limit such coverage by endorsement." The strip provides rear access and loading for the building and connects Lots 7 through 9; its loss would impair access and, potentially, the approved site plan. Note that the commitment does **not** except the strip from Schedule B, Part II, so the concern is one of coverage quality rather than exclusion. Your markup notes asked whether an "ALTA 9 or equivalent" endorsement should be requested; the title company's own recommendation is ALTA Endorsement 34 (Identified Exception) or "other affirmative coverage specific to this parcel," which we suggest is the better vehicle.

*Draft treatment:* §4.5 requires Borrower to represent that fee simple title to the strip is vested and insured without any exception excluding it, describing it as less than fee simple, or qualifying insurability; Borrower must cooperate to obtain endorsements and bear the cost (§22.9). Exhibit B includes the strip within the insured estate and does not carve it out.

*Recommendation:* Before closing, obtain from Prestige Title written confirmation (i) that the Policy will insure fee simple title to Parcel Two, (ii) of the premium for any extended coverage, and (iii) that an ALTA 34 endorsement (or equivalent) will be issued. If coverage cannot be obtained on acceptable terms, we should revisit the collateral description and consider whether the strip should be excluded from the mortgaged estate — a commercially unattractive result that should be escalated rather than assumed.

**A-2. There is no surveyed metes and bounds description in the record, and Exhibit A therefore contains a record description with an express placeholder.**

*Record basis:* Title Commitment Schedule A §4 and Requirement 9; Term Sheet §2.1 ("full legal description shall be confirmed from the title commitment and ALTA/NSPS survey"); Prior Counsel Notes §2.

*Analysis:* The only descriptions available are the plat-reference description (Plat Book 41, Page 218) and a description of the Anson Lane strip by reference to the Cartwright Land Surveying LLC survey recorded in Plat Book 64 at Page 91. The survey itself is not in the file. We have not drafted metes and bounds; inventing a description would risk recording an instrument that does not match the insured estate.

*Draft treatment:* Exhibit A sets out the record description and a drafting note stating that the surveyed description is "[TO BE INSERTED]" and will replace the record description before recording.

*Recommendation:* Obtain the certified ALTA/NSPS survey (2021 Minimum Standard Detail Requirements, Table A items 1, 2, 3, 4, 6(a), 6(b), 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 16 and 19 per Closer Note 4), certified to Lender, the title company and Borrower, dated or re-certified within 90 days of closing; insert the metes and bounds before execution. The placeholder must be removed before the Mortgage is sent for signature.

**A-3. The balcony encroachment requires an endorsement the prior markup notes did not identify, and a permit inquiry.**

*Record basis:* Title Commitment Exception 5 and Closer Note 2; Appraisal §2.1; Renovation Budget line 3.0 (façade restoration "including King Street balcony").

*Analysis:* A decorative iron balcony extends approximately 2.5 feet over the King Street right-of-way. The Company makes no representation as to any permit, license or encroachment agreement and recommends a survey/encroachment endorsement (ALTA 28.1 or equivalent). ALTA 9 (comprehensive) coverage, referenced in your markup notes, has not included encroachments since the 2006 forms revision; 28.1 is the operative endorsement here, with ALTA 9 retained for covenants, conditions and restrictions. The façade restoration scope expressly includes repair of the balcony, and the appraisal notes that city-mandated removal would require additional façade work beyond the budget.

*Draft treatment:* §4.4 approves the encroachment as a Permitted Exception, requires Borrower to obtain and maintain any encroachment license or permit and to deliver copies to Lender, and requires city-mandated removal or modification to be completed at Borrower's cost, supported by the Completion Guaranty. Exhibit B item 4 records the approval as without prejudice.

*Recommendation:* Request ALTA 28.1 and a zoning endorsement; ask Borrower to produce any encroachment permit or license from the City of Charleston. If none exists, document the risk decision — the appraisal treats it as immaterial to value, and we concur that it is unlikely to be a loan-level issue, but the lender should not be the last party to learn that the City requires removal.

**A-4. Standard exceptions and the gap are open, and the commitment will need a date-down before the initial advance.**

*Record basis:* Title Commitment Schedule B, Part II, Exception 1(a)–(e), Requirements 9–12; Term Sheet §5.6.

*Analysis:* Exceptions 1(b) through 1(e) are to be deleted upon delivery of a satisfactory survey and owner's affidavit; Exception 1(a) is a gap exception requiring a gap indemnity or closing-date disbursement. The commitment is effective February 28, 2025 and expires August 28, 2025 — within the Term Sheet's outside closing date of May 15, 2025, but not if closing slips.

*Draft treatment:* Exhibit B states expressly that the standard exceptions are **not** approved as Permitted Exceptions; §20.1(c) and (e) condition the initial advance on the Policy (or pro forma) and on the gap indemnity and affidavits.

*Recommendation:* Confirm with Prestige Title the closing mechanics (gap indemnity, disbursement, recording order) and calendar the commitment expiration.

**A-5. The utility easement is described inconsistently as to location.**

*Analysis:* These may be reconcilable if the rear boundary of the assemblage meets the westerly boundary of Lot 9, but the record does not demonstrate that. **A-6. The Renovation Holdback is an obligatory future advance and the Mortgage must say so to preserve priority.**

*Record basis:* Term Sheet §§3.3(b), 5.6 (future advance endorsements), 8.4; Title Commitment Requirement 3 (mortgage "in the principal amount of $14,750,000.00").

*Analysis:* The Holdback is a contractual obligation of Lender, disbursed over 14 months. If the Mortgage did not secure obligatory future advances with the same priority, a later advance could be subordinated to intervening liens — including mechanics' liens, which are a live risk in a renovation.

*Draft treatment:* §2.5 states that all advances, including Holdback advances and protective advances under §9.14, are secured with the same priority as the initial advance; the recorded principal amount is stated as $14,750,000 plus interest, costs and other amounts. §20.2 conditions each Holdback advance on a current date-down endorsement.

*Recommendation:* Confirm that the Title Policy will include a future advance endorsement covering the Holdback (Term Sheet §5.6 requires it) and that the recording fee is computed on $14,750,000 (see §22.9 and Part V).

### B. Environmental — the Controlled Recognized Environmental Condition and the land use restriction

**B-1. The land use restriction is the single most document-intensive environmental item in this deal, and it is not a standard template covenant.**

*Record basis:* Title Commitment Exception 4 and Closer Note 1; Phase I §§5.1–5.4, 6, 7; Term Sheet §12.1(c).

*Analysis:* A CREC on Lot 7 arises from PCE and petroleum solvent contamination migrating from the former King Street Cleaners operation at 518 King Street (approximately 1968–2005). SCDHEC issued a No Further Action letter dated August 12, 2021 (Site ID SC-29403-0772) subject to institutional controls recorded at Deed Book 1203, Page 89: (i) the ground floor of Lot 7 may not be used for residential purposes, and (ii) a vapor barrier beneath the Bay 1 slab must be maintained and periodically inspected, may not be penetrated without SCDHEC approval, and any work affecting the slab must include inspection and, where necessary, repair. The NFA status is contingent on continued compliance. The title company's closer note expressly directs that the Mortgage and the Environmental Indemnity "contain specific covenants addressing the SCDHEC land use restriction … including the vapor barrier maintenance and periodic inspection obligation and the prohibition on residential use," and that "the Mortgage should contain an event of default triggered by any failure to comply with … said land use restriction." The Phase I report makes the same recommendation (Section 6(d)).

*Draft treatment:* Article 12 is purpose-built rather than templated. §12.2(a)–(g) impose the use prohibition, the vapor barrier maintenance obligation (including annual and pre/post-construction inspection by a licensed environmental professional), advance SCDHEC notification, the requirement of Lender consent to any modification or vacation of the LUR, record-keeping and delivery obligations. §16.1(l) makes any failure an Event of Default; §18.2(d) makes it a springing full-recourse carve-out; §7.9 and §7.2(g) and §20.2(e) control work affecting the Bay 1 slab; Exhibit E sets out the LUR in full for the closing file.

### E. Loan economics

**E-1. The DSCR covenant cannot be satisfied at any occupancy, on the borrower's own projections.**

*Record basis:* Term Sheet §§7.1 (1.25x minimum, testing from month 24), 11.1(i) (two consecutive quarters below 1.25x is an Event of Default); Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule tab (stabilized best-case NOI $622,772; debt service $93,500 per month / $1,122,000 per year; DSCR 0.555x; "DOES NOT MEET 1.25x COVENANT"; realistic scenario NOI $314,782; DSCR 0.281x).

*Analysis:* We recomputed the debt service rather than adopting the workbook's figure: 25-year amortization of $14,750,000 at 5.85% produces $93,686.61 per month and $1,124,239 per year. Against best-case stabilized NOI of $622,772, that is a DSCR of **0.554x**; on an interest-only basis, $622,772 ÷ $862,875 = **0.722x**; on the workbook's "realistic" scenario, **0.280x**. The requirement of 1.25x on amortizing debt service implies required NOI of **$1,405,299**, or $45.33 per square foot across 31,000 square feet, against market rents of $24.00–$32.00 per square foot for ground-floor retail and $19.00–$25.00 per square foot for Class B office. Even with all 31,000 square feet leased at the rent roll's stabilized rents ($755,549 of gross revenue) and zero operating expenses, the DSCR would be 0.672x. There is no occupancy outcome that satisfies the covenant. This is not a lease-up timing risk; it is a structural mismatch between the loan amount, the debt service and the Property's income.

*Draft treatment:* §13.1 defines DSCR, Net Operating Income and Annual Debt Service with the specificity the Term Sheet leaves open (Term Sheet §7.1: "The precise definitions … shall be set forth in the Loan Documents"); Net Operating Income excludes non-recurring items, insurance and condemnation proceeds, capital expenditures and expenses funded from reserves, caps management fees at 4% of gross revenue, and Annual Debt Service is calculated on the 25-year amortization at the greater of the current rate and the floor rate. §13.1(d) grosses up debt service to the full $14,750,000 even before full disbursement. §13.5 provides a cure-deposit mechanism (a deposit equal to the excess of 1.25 times Annual Debt Service over Net Operating Income, held as additional Collateral, released after two consecutive compliant test dates and capped at two deposits over the term). §16.1(g) implements the Term Sheet's two-quarter default trigger.

*Recommendation:* This requires a credit decision, not a drafting decision. The options, in order of structural soundness: (i) reduce the loan amount or extend the interest-only period so that the covenant is achievable on stabilized NOI; (ii) replace the amortizing DSCR test with an interest-only DSCR test at a lower threshold (for example, 1.00x on interest-only debt service, which still implies required NOI of $862,875 — itself 39% above the best-case projection); (iii) retain the covenant but require a funded debt service reserve equal to 12 months of debt service plus an extended interest-shortfall guaranty running beyond the first test date; (iv) require a cash sweep at any DSCR below 1.10x with a hard lockbox. As drafted, the Loan will be in default by the second test date absent one of these changes. It is worth quantifying what the cure mechanism would require: on the borrower's own best-case stabilized projection, the §13.5 deposit at the first DSCR test date would be **$782,527** ($1,405,299 required NOI less $622,772 projected NOI), payable within 30 days of the compliance certificate due date. A cure that is eight times the projected cash flow shortfall is not a cure; it is a default with a price tag. A covenant the lender knows will be breached is worse than no covenant — it invites a waiver practice and, in a bankruptcy, an argument that the lender never expected compliance.

*Draft treatment:* §5.7 prohibits voluntary prepayment during the lockout, permits Lender to refuse and return any tendered amount, and provides that if Lender accepts in its sole discretion, the premium equals the amount that would be payable under §5.8 had the prepayment occurred on the last day of the lockout.

*Recommendation:* Confirm this treatment, and confirm the casualty and condemnation carve-out in §5.7 and §15.3(c) (proceeds applied at par without premium). Your markup notes asked us to confirm that carve-out with the client; the Term Sheet is silent, and we have taken the borrower-favorable position because an involuntary prepayment premium is difficult to defend as liquidated damages.

**E-6. Interest mechanics are not specified in the Term Sheet.**

*Record basis:* Term Sheet §3.6 (Daily SOFR plus 2.85%; floor of 3.00%; published rate or prior Business Day's rate).

*Analysis:* The Term Sheet does not specify the day-count convention (actual/360 versus actual/365), whether interest is compounded, how partial accrual periods are handled, or what happens if Daily SOFR is permanently discontinued. Each has a real cost effect: actual/360 produces approximately 1.4% more interest than actual/365 on the same nominal rate.

*Draft treatment:* §5.2(b) adopts actual/360 daily accrual; §5.2(c) makes Lender's determination conclusive absent manifest error and requires a monthly rate statement; §5.2(d) provides a successor-rate mechanism (New York Fed recommended successor rate plus any recommended spread adjustment, giving effect to the floor). §5.1 also specifies that, for a floating-rate loan, the amortizing installment is recalculated on each Payment Date using the then-outstanding principal balance and the then-applicable rate — the Term Sheet §3.9 wording leaves the recast frequency open, and an un-recast payment would not amortize to zero over 25 years.

*Recommendation:* Confirm actual/360 with you and with the loan servicing group; document the convention in the Note and the loan system so that billing matches documentation.

**E-7. The default rate and late charge should be reviewed for enforceability.**

*Record basis:* Term Sheet §§3.7 (default rate = applicable rate + 5.00%), 3.11 (5% late charge after 10 calendar days).

*Analysis:* At the SOFR floor, the default rate is 10.85%. *Draft treatment:* §§5.3 (default rate), 5.4 (late charge, with express statement that it compensates administrative expense and is not a penalty), 5.13 (usury savings clause that limits the aggregate to the maximum lawful rate, applies any excess to principal, and provides for pro rata allocation over the term).

*Recommendation:* Confirm with South Carolina counsel that the 10.85% default rate and the 5% late charge are enforceable as drafted; we have included the savings clause as a backstop but have not rendered an opinion on the maximum lawful rate.

**E-9. The Term Sheet describes the Guaranty as "full recourse" and simultaneously as a non-recourse carve-out guaranty.**

*Record basis:* Term Sheet §5.5 (introducing "a full recourse personal guaranty," then describing "(a) Non-Recourse Carve-Out Guaranty" — the Loan to be "structured as non-recourse to Borrower, except for certain 'carve-out' events … that trigger springing full recourse liability of Guarantor for the entire outstanding indebtedness" — plus (b) the Completion Guaranty and (c) the Payment Guaranty); Guarantor Acknowledgment (referring to "the Guaranty Agreement, Completion Guaranty, Payment Guaranty"); Term Sheet §§14.3 (Loan Document list includes only a "Guaranty Agreement").

### I. Transfer restrictions and change of control

**I-1. The "transfers among existing members" carve-out in the Term Sheet could permit a change of control without Lender consent.**

*Record basis:* Term Sheet §10.3 (no transfer of ownership interests exceeding 10% individually or 25% in the aggregate without consent, "except for transfers among the existing members of Borrower"); OA Summary §§4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4.

*Analysis:* Read literally, the exception permits GDG to transfer its entire 72% interest to Tidewater or Harborline (a transfer "among the existing members") without Lender consent — converting the guarantor-controlled borrower into a fund-controlled borrower and defeating the non-recourse carve-out structure that depends on Graydon's incentives. The Operating Agreement does not fill the gap: Section 4.1 restricts transfers of membership interests but requires 75% approval calculated excluding the transferring member, Section 4.2(c) permits Tidewater to transfer to a "successor fund" without any consent, and Section 4.2 expressly does not address upstream changes in the ownership or control of any Member.

*Draft treatment:* §10.2(b)(iii) narrows the carve-out to transfers among existing Members that do not increase the GDG/Graydon aggregate interest above 72%, do not reduce GDG below 51%, do not result in any person other than GDG serving as Managing Member, and do not otherwise change control; §10.2(c) brings indirect and upstream changes within the consent requirement, including transfers of interests in GDG, replacement of Tidewater's general partner, and "successor fund" transfers; §10.2(e) makes a violation void ab initio and an Event of Default with no cure period (§16.1(k), §16.2(c)); §18.2(e) makes it a full-recourse carve-out.

*Recommendation:* **This is a deliberate narrowing of an executed commercial term and requires your approval.** The Term Sheet permits Lender to require additional provisions that are "not materially inconsistent" with its terms (§14.4); a transfer covenant that captures change of control is standard in non-recourse lending and, in our view, is consistent with the Term Sheet's evident purpose (protecting the Guarantor's control), but it is more restrictive than the literal carve-out and borrower's counsel will notice. Raise it with Marcus before the draft goes out.

**I-2. Upstream transfers are the real risk, not transfers of membership interests.**

*Record basis:* OA Summary §4.2 (illustrations: Mr. Graydon could sell his interest in GDG; Tidewater GP Holdings LLC could be acquired or replaced; Harborline's internal membership could change entirely — none of which would be a transfer of a membership interest in the Company); §4.5 (Affiliate definition).

*Analysis:* Because the SPE structure and the guaranty both depend on who controls GDG, an indirect change of control is functionally identical to a direct transfer of interests.

*Draft treatment:* §10.2(c) covers any change of control of a Controlling Member; §9.13(f) requires notice of any change in the identity of any Member, the Managing Member, the general partner of any Member, or any manager or officer of Borrower.

*Recommendation:* Require the Operating Agreement Amendment to add a parallel change-of-control restriction at the Member level — the Operating Agreement as delivered has none, and a loan-document-only restriction leaves the organizational documents silent (and potentially in conflict).

## PART IX — DRAFTING CONVENTIONS, ASSUMPTIONS AND SCOPE LIMITS

**Conventions.** The Draft Mortgage uses 23 articles, 174 numbered sections and five exhibits, with manual numbering so that cross-references are stable and verifiable; every internal cross-reference has been checked programmatically against the section list. Defined terms are used consistently and are collected in §1.1. Obligations are drafted in the covenant-format ("Borrower shall…"), and remedies are drafted as Lender-protective but subject to a non-waivable-rights savings clause (§17.9).

**Assumptions made in drafting.** (a) The Mortgage is dated April 15, 2025, the anticipated closing date in Term Sheet §3.8, and defines "Closing Date" so that a conformed date may be inserted; (b) the assignment of leases and rents is incorporated in the Mortgage rather than executed separately, per the Term Sheet's election and the markup notes; (c) South Carolina law governs, the Mortgage grants no power of sale, and foreclosure is judicial (§17.1); (d) interest accrues actual/360 on Daily SOFR with a 3.00% floor (§5.2); (e) the Treasury Rate for yield maintenance is the interpolated H.15 constant maturity yield (§5.9(b)); (f) the Guaranty, Completion Guaranty and Payment Guaranty are three separate instruments (§19.1); (g) the notarial acknowledgment is a South Carolina certificate with two witness lines, subject to confirmation with the title company (J-4); (h) the statutory citation for the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act is carried as cited in the markup notes and should be verified (C-1); (i) Institutional controls described in Exhibit E are taken from the Phase I executive summary and the title commitment, not from the recorded LUR itself.

**Matters not covered by this deliverable.** No Promissory Note, Guaranty, Completion Guaranty, Payment Guaranty, Environmental Indemnity, Operating Agreement Amendment, separate Assignment of Leases and Rents, UCC-1 or fixture filing forms, tenant estoppel or SNDA forms, or closing checklist instrument forms are included. We have not rendered any opinion on: value; the enforceability of the yield maintenance premium, default rate or late charge under South Carolina law; the maximum lawful rate of interest; the sufficiency of the Phase I report for all appropriate inquiries purposes; the sufficiency of the title commitment or survey; or tax consequences, including the South Carolina recording fee treatment of future advances. Each of those items requires separate confirmation, and the relevant open items are identified above.
