## PART III — ISSUE ANALYSIS

Each item below states the proposition relied on, its source, the observation, the drafting treatment adopted in the Mortgage (with section references), and the action required. Items are ordered by subject, not by priority; the priority register at the end of this Part sorts them.

### A. Collateral, Title and Survey

**A.1 — The Anson Lane parcel is a quitclaim conveyance, and the title commitment gives no affirmative coverage over that fact.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §2.1 and Exhibit A; Title Commitment Schedule A §4 and the "Vesting Deed Information" addendum; Kit's notes, ¶2.
*Observation.* Parcel Two (the vacated Anson Lane strip, Deed Book 1128, Page 443) was conveyed by quitclaim deed from the City of Charleston. The title commitment insures fee simple title to the Land, including Parcel Two, and does not except the parcel or qualify the estate to less than fee simple — it satisfies Kit's first test. However, the commitment's addendum states expressly that "no warranties of title were provided in connection with this conveyance," that the Company's willingness to insure "should not be construed as a representation that the chain of title for this parcel is equivalent to that of Parcel One," and that the Company "reserves the right to require an additional premium for extended coverage on this parcel and/or may limit such coverage by endorsement."
*Treatment.* Section 3.2 of the Mortgage contains a Borrower warranty that it has caused the Title Insurer to insure fee simple title to Parcel Two without any Schedule B-II exception excluding or qualifying the estate, and a covenant to deliver either a clean policy as to Parcel Two or an endorsement (expressly including ALTA Endorsement 34 or equivalent, per the commitment's closer note 3) at Borrower's expense. Failure is expressly tied to the condition precedent in Term Sheet §12.1(b). Section 3.2(d) also prohibits Borrower from releasing any access, loading or parking right over Parcel Two without Lender's consent.
*Action.* (i) Ask Prestige Title in writing, this week, to confirm (a) the premium for extended coverage on Parcel Two, (b) whether any endorsement limiting coverage will be attached, and (c) that ALTA 34 is available. (ii) Decide whether the Bank will accept a premium-sharing arrangement; the Term Sheet leaves all title costs with Borrower, so the cost is not the issue — the coverage terms are. (iii) The Bank should understand that a quitclaim-derived parcel that also carries the property's rear access is the single weakest link in the collateral chain.

**A.2 — The record is inconsistent on the size and location of Parcel Two and on rear access to Lots 8 and 9.**
*Source:* Title Commitment Schedule A §4 (strip "adjacent to and contiguous with Lot 7"); Phase I ESA §2 (strip "runs along the rear (northwest) boundary of Lots 7, 8, and 9 and currently provides rear access and loading for the building"); Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (each tenant: "shared use of rear lot (6 spaces)").
*Observation.* If, as the Phase I ESA and the leases suggest, the strip provides rear access and loading for the entire building and the tenants' six parking spaces, but the insured strip is contiguous only with Lot 7, then rear access to Lots 8 and 9 and the parking count rest either on the strip's actual configuration or on unrecorded rights over adjoining land. The title commitment does not except any easement or encroachment for that access.
*Treatment.* Section 7.5 and Section 3.2(d) protect Lender's position but cannot create rights Borrower does not have. Section 6.6 includes a compliance representation that does not reach access. The draft does not warrant the access.
*Action.* Obtain from Cartwright Land Surveying LLC the certified ALTA/NSPS survey and confirm (i) the metes and bounds of Parcel Two, (ii) whether it abuts Lots 8 and 9 as well as Lot 7, and (iii) whether the six rear parking spaces are within the insured parcel. If any portion of the access or parking lies outside the Land, obtain an easement and a title endorsement, and add the easement to Exhibit B.

**A.3 — The balcony encroachment is insured only negatively.**
*Source:* Title Commitment Schedule B-II Exception 5 and closer note 2; Appraisal §2.1 and §5; Renovation Budget Line 3 (façade restoration including "balcony encroachment area repair").
*Observation.* A 2.5-foot decorative iron balcony encroaches over the King Street right-of-way. The commitment recommends ALTA 28.1 or equivalent; no encroachment permit or license is in the record. The Renovation Budget commits $625,000 to façade restoration, including the balcony, subject to Board of Architectural Review oversight (Budget Detail, Line 3 note).
*Treatment.* Section 3.3 of the Mortgage: Borrower warrants no authority has asserted a violation; covenants to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain a City license within 90 days after closing and to deliver it; to procure ALTA 28.1 at closing if available on commercially reasonable terms; to include the balcony in the façade scope; and not to remove or modify it without consent. Section 9.4(c) prevents demolition or material alteration outside the Renovation.
*Action.* (i) Ask Borrower's counsel (Cartwright & Lyons LLP) whether an encroachment license exists; the appraisal and the commitment both suggest it may. (ii) If none exists, the Bank should decide whether ALTA 28.1 without an underlying permit is acceptable; a survey endorsement insures over the survey discrepancy, not over the City's right to order removal. (iii) Flag for the Bank that removal would trigger unbudgeted façade cost, which is an environmental-of-collateral risk, not a title risk.

**A.4 — The survey must be re-certified, and the standard exceptions must actually come off.**
*Source:* Title Commitment Requirement 9 ("dated or re-certified to a date not more than ninety (90) days prior to the date of closing"); Schedule B-II Exception 1(a)–(e); Term Sheet §12.1(e).
*Observation.* The survey is dated January 15, 2025. A closing on April 15, 2025 is exactly 90 days later, so the survey is at the outer limit and any delay in closing puts it out of compliance. The commitment conditions removal of standard exceptions (b) through (e) on a satisfactory survey and affidavit, and (a) on a gap indemnity.
*Treatment.* Exhibit B, item 7 of the Mortgage provides that a standard exception not removed will not constitute a Permitted Exception, and that failure to cause removal is a failure of the condition precedent in Term Sheet §12.1(b). This closes a gap in the template, which would otherwise treat all Schedule B-II items as permitted.
*Action.* Order the re-certified survey now, dated or re-certified within 30 days of closing. Confirm the gap indemnity and owner's affidavit are on Prestige Title's closing checklist.

**A.5 — The utility easement's recorded location does not match the survey description.**
*Source:* Title Commitment Exception 3 (SCE&G easement, Deed Book 987, Page 112, affecting "the westerly boundary of Lot 9"); Phase I ESA §2 (easement "across the rear of the Subject Property"); ESA §2 physical description (Lot 9 is the "northeastern parcel").
*Observation.* An easement on the westerly boundary of the northeastern parcel would run through the interior of the assemblage rather than across the rear. Either the parcel lettering in one document is inverted, or the easement description is being paraphrased loosely. The distinction matters for the elevator modernization and HVAC work, both of which involve rooftop and shaft work that could conflict with an interior utility corridor.
*Treatment.* The easement is listed as Permitted Exception 2 in Exhibit B without adopting a location.
*Action.* Have the surveyor plot the easement and confirm it does not run under the elevator shaft or the HVAC risers. If it does, confirm the General Contractor's scope accounts for it and that no work requires utility relocation.

**A.6 — The legal description is by plat reference; the metes-and-bounds description is not yet in the record.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §2.1 and Exhibit A ("Full legal description to be confirmed from the title commitment and ALTA/NSPS survey"); Title Commitment Schedule A §4; Kit's notes, ¶2.
*Observation.* The Term Sheet and the commitment both describe the Land by plat reference (Plat Book 41, Page 218; and, for Parcel Two, Plat Book 64, Page 91). Kit asks for a metes-and-bounds description from the surveyor in addition.
*Treatment.* Exhibit A uses the commitment's description verbatim, including both plat references, and adds a supersession clause: the certified surveyor's metes-and-bounds description, when delivered, "shall be attached to and shall supersede this Exhibit A for all purposes upon delivery to Lender, without the necessity of amendment of this Mortgage." This avoids either (i) recording a mortgage with a bracketed placeholder or (ii) requiring a re-recording when the survey description arrives later.
*Action.* Confirm with Borrower's counsel and the surveyor that the metes-and-bounds description will be delivered before the recording package is assembled, so the supersession clause does not have to be relied on. If it will not, record with Exhibit A as drafted and attach the survey description by recorded affidavit or amendment.

**A.7 — Timing: the commitment expires August 28, 2025 and the Term Sheet expires May 15, 2025.**
*Source:* Title Commitment cover page and Conditions ¶2; Term Sheet §14.5.
*Observation.* The commitment is effective February 28, 2025 for six months and states that the Company's liability ceases on August 28, 2025. The Term Sheet terminates Lender's obligation to fund if closing does not occur by May 15, 2025. A closing on April 15, 2025 sits comfortably inside both.
*Treatment.* The Mortgage is dated as of the Closing Date (April 15, 2025) and defines the Maturity Date, the Lockout Period, the Yield Maintenance Period and the Substantial Completion Deadline by reference to the Closing Date, not to fixed calendar dates, so the schedule moves with an actual closing date. The descriptive parentheticals (for example, "that is, through April 15, 2027") will be re-set at signing.
*Action.* Confirm the actual closing date before execution and conform the parenthetical dates in Sections 13.2 and 13.3, and Recital C, to it. If closing slips past May 15, 2025, the Term Sheet must be extended in writing by the Bank.

**A.8 — Two FEMA panels are cited, and the flood insurance requirement exceeds what the federal program can supply.**
*Source:* Appraisal §2.1 and §5 (FEMA Community Panel No. 45019C0418J, effective November 4, 2014); Phase I ESA §2 (FEMA Panel No. 45019C0520J, effective December 4, 2020); Term Sheet §9.3 (flood insurance not less than $5,000,000; Zone X; "Notwithstanding that flood insurance may not be required by federal regulation for properties in Zone X, Lender requires flood insurance coverage as a condition of this Loan").
*Observation.* The two reports cite different panels with different effective dates. Both agree the Property is in Zone X. The Term Sheet requires $5,000,000 of flood coverage as a contractual matter. The National Flood Insurance Program's maximum commercial building limit is $500,000 per building, so a $5,000,000 requirement cannot be met through NFIP coverage alone.
*Treatment.* Section 10.3 of the Mortgage requires $5,000,000 of flood coverage, states expressly that the requirement is contractual and not federally mandated (protecting the Bank from a Borrower argument that the requirement is inapplicable), and requires the maximum available NFIP limit plus private or excess flood coverage for the balance, from a carrier acceptable to Lender.
*Action.* (i) Confirm the current effective FIRM panel before binding coverage; the appraisal's 2014 panel is stale. (ii) Confirm the Bank's standard flood requirement is $5,000,000 here and that it does not exceed the insurable value of the improvements at replacement cost (approximately $3,150,000 of renovation plus existing shell); a requirement above insurable value invites a Borrower objection. (iii) Confirm whether the Bank's policy requires flood coverage in Zone X at all, and if it does, document the exception to policy.

### B. Environmental

**B.1 — The LUR is the Bank's principal environmental exposure, and the Term Sheet does not contain the covenants that manage it.**
*Source:* Phase I ESA §§5.3–5.5 and §6(d); Title Commitment Exception 4, Requirement 6 and closer note 1; Term Sheet §5.4; Appraisal §5.
*Observation.* The LUR (Deed Book 1203, Page 89) prohibits residential use of the ground floor of Lot 7 (Bay 1) and requires maintenance and periodic inspection of the Vapor Barrier beneath the Bay 1 slab. The NFA status is contingent on continued compliance; a breach could cause SCDHEC to reopen Site ID SC-29403-0772 at the owner's expense. The Term Sheet requires an Environmental Indemnity (Section 5.4) but its enumerated content is general; the *title commitment* is more specific than the Term Sheet, requiring the Environmental Indemnity to address the CREC, the vapor barrier maintenance and periodic inspection obligation, and the SCDHEC land use restriction (Requirement 6), and requiring the *Mortgage* to contain an event of default for failure to comply with, or any modification or termination of, the LUR (closer note 1).
*Treatment.* Section 7.1 of the Mortgage implements the commitment's instruction directly and in detail: annual (and on-request) inspection of the Vapor Barrier by a qualified environmental professional acceptable to Lender, at Borrower's expense; no penetration or slab work without SCDHEC approval, notice to Lender and an approved repair plan; prohibition on residential use of Bay 1 (reinforced in Section 9.4(b)); advance written notice to SCDHEC of any Bay 1 slab work; five-business-day notice to Lender of any SCDHEC communication; 30-day delivery of evidence of compliance on request; and no modification, termination, release or amendment of the LUR without Lender's consent. Section 14.1(l) makes any breach an Event of Default.
*Action.* (i) Confirm the Environmental Indemnity (not in the record) contains the same provisions; Requirement 6 of the commitment will not be satisfied otherwise, and Lender should not accept an indemnity that addresses environmental liability generally but omits the vapor barrier and LUR obligations. (ii) Confirm the Bank wants the LUR covenants in *both* the Mortgage and the Environmental Indemnity; the draft assumes belt-and-braces, as the commitment's closer note contemplates.

**B.2 — Environmental defaults carry no cure period in the draft, which is defensible but should be a conscious decision.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §11.1(b), §11.2(b) (non-monetary defaults other than SPE, transfer and insurance defaults receive a 30-day cure, extendable to 90).
*Observation.* The Term Sheet does not list environmental defaults among the excepted categories, which would imply they get the standard 30-day cure. The title commitment's closer note, however, requires an event of default for LUR non-compliance, implying immediate consequence. The draft takes the middle course of making an environmental default an Event of Default (Section 14.1(l)) while Section 14.3(f) gives no cure period, consistent with the seriousness of the exposure and with the inability to "cure" a regulatory reopening.
*Action.* Confirm. A court may be receptive to a Borrower argument that a 30-day cure should apply to a curable environmental covenant (for example, a late inspection report). Suggested compromise if the Bank prefers: provide a 10-business-day cure for administrative breaches of Section 7.1(a) and (f), with no cure for slab penetration, residential use, LUR modification or a regulatory reopening. We have not included that compromise in the draft because it was not instructed.

**B.3 — The renovation will be performed directly over the Vapor Barrier, and the draw conditions do not currently require protection of it.**
*Source:* Phase I ESA §5.3(b), §5.4 and §6(c); Renovation Budget Line 1 (HVAC replacement) and Line 3 (façade restoration); Title Commitment closer note 1.
*Observation.* The Phase I ESA warns that any work affecting the Bay 1 floor slab must include inspection and repair of the vapor barrier with prior notice to SCDHEC, and recommends that the contractor be informed of its location. The Term Sheet's draw conditions (Section 8.4) do not mention the vapor barrier at all.
*Treatment.* Section 12.4(h) of the Mortgage adds a draw condition requiring contractor and subcontractor written acknowledgment of the Vapor Barrier and the LUR, and advance notice to SCDHEC of any Bay 1 slab work. Section 12.4(g)(iii) requires Lender consent for any change order involving Bay 1 slab work.
*Action.* This is an addition beyond the Term Sheet and is listed in Part IV for approval. There is no credit cost to it.

**B.4 — The Phase II waiver is documented only by reference and should be in the file.**
*Source:* Phase I ESA §6 (waiver "documented in correspondence dated January 22, 2025" between Marcus Prideaux and Clearfield).
*Observation.* The waiver letter itself is not in the record. Section 12.1(c) of the Term Sheet makes receipt of a satisfactory Phase I ESA (indicating "no recognized environmental conditions requiring remediation or further investigation, unless otherwise acceptable to Lender") a condition precedent. A CREC is not a REC, so the condition is satisfied on the report's face; the waiver is belt-and-braces.
*Treatment.* Section 7.1 of the Mortgage recites that Lender waived the Phase II requirement on the basis of the CREC status, the NFA determination and the recorded LUR. This converts an oral/administrative position into a documented acknowledgment by Borrower of the basis on which the Bank relied — which matters if environmental liability is later asserted.
*Action.* Obtain a copy of the January 22, 2025 correspondence for the file and confirm Marcus's description of it.

**B.5 — The SCDHEC remediation file has not been obtained.**
*Source:* Phase I ESA §6(a) (recommends the purchaser obtain and review the complete SCDHEC file including all sampling data, remediation reports, monitoring results and the NFA letter with all conditions).
*Observation.* Nothing in the record shows that the file has been obtained or reviewed by anyone other than the environmental consultant's summary.
*Treatment.* Section 3.4(d) of the Mortgage requires Borrower to deliver a complete copy of the SCDHEC file within ten business days after closing (or at closing if available).
*Action.* Recommend the Bank require delivery *before* closing, not after. If the NFA letter contains conditions beyond those summarized in the Phase I ESA, the Section 7.1 covenants may be incomplete. This is a due-diligence item, not a drafting item, and should be assigned to Marcus.

### C. Leases, Rents and the Assignment

**C.1 — The assignment of rents has been converted to an absolute assignment, as instructed.**
*Source:* Kit's notes, ¶3; Term Sheet §5.2 (assignment "may be included within the Mortgage and Security Agreement or executed as a separate instrument, at the discretion of Lender's counsel"); Title Commitment Requirement 5 (expressly permits inclusion within the Mortgage).
*Observation.* The template's collateral assignment is insufficient under the assignment-of-rents statute Kit identifies, which gives an absolute assignment effect on execution (with enforcement triggered by default).
*Treatment.* Article IV of the Mortgage is drafted as an absolute and present assignment: "unconditionally, absolutely and presently grants, bargains, sells, conveys, transfers, assigns and sets over," with an express statement that it is "not merely a collateral assignment or an assignment as additional security." Section 4.2 ties perfection to recording and enforcement to default. Section 4.3 grants Borrower a revocable license to collect until default, which is the market-standard way to preserve the absolute characterization while allowing the Borrower to operate. Section 4.4 permits direct notice to tenants on default without a court order or receiver. No separate Assignment of Leases and Rents will be recorded, satisfying both Term Sheet §5.2 and Title Requirement 5 by inclusion.
*Action.* Confirm the Bank is content with a single recorded instrument (it reduces recording cost and documentary stamp exposure, and it avoids a split-enforcement argument). Note that the Term Sheet's loan documents list at §14.3(c) contemplates a separate assignment; the draft supersedes that by election under §5.2.

**C.2 — Verify the statutory citation before the draft goes out.**
*Source:* Kit's notes, ¶3, citing "the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act (S.C. Code § 29-3-100 *et seq.*)."
*Observation.* The South Carolina enactment of the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act is cited in practice to Title 29, Chapter 3 of the South Carolina Code, and the section numbers should be verified against the current Code before they are printed in a recorded instrument. We have not independently confirmed the citation given in the markup notes.
*Treatment.* Article IV refers to "the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act as enacted in the State of South Carolina (the 'UARAA')" without a section citation. This is deliberate: a recorded instrument should not carry an unverified citation, and the operative effect does not depend on the citation.
*Action.* Have a paralegal pull the current Code sections and, if Kit confirms the citation, insert it at Section 4.2 in the next turn of the draft. This is a five-minute item and should not be allowed to hold up circulation.

**C.3 — The Lowcountry Lease has no assignment or subletting provision at all. This is the most important lease issue in the deal.**
*Source:* Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (Bay 1: "No provision. Lease is silent on assignment or subletting rights and does not contain a consent requirement, prohibition, or landlord approval mechanism"); Appraisal §3.2 (appraiser notes the omission and does not opine on it); Kit's notes, ¶3 (three-step protocol).
*Observation.* As Kit notes, the statutory assignment is enforceable against the tenant on notification regardless of the lease's silence. The risk is practical: a tenant that never agreed to an assignment may resist or litigate at the moment of enforcement, and the tenant also has no contractual obligation to deliver an estoppel.
*Treatment.* All three steps in Kit's protocol are implemented:
(i) *Representation.* Section 4.5(b) of the Mortgage: Borrower warrants it has "good right, full power and lawful authority to absolutely assign the Leases and the Rents, including without limitation the Lowcountry Lease, notwithstanding the absence of any assignment, subletting or consent-to-assignment provision in the Lowcountry Lease."
(ii) *Estoppel as a condition precedent.* Section 4.7(a) records the Term Sheet condition (Section 12.1(j)) and states expressly that "Lender does not intend to waive such condition with respect to the Lowcountry Lease"; Section 4.7(c) adds a 90-day post-closing covenant to deliver a Lowcountry estoppel that acknowledges the Mortgage and the assignment, and agrees to pay Rents to Lender on notice after default.
(iii) *Lease amendment at the next opportunity.* Section 4.7(c) adds a commercially reasonable efforts covenant to obtain a customary assignment-consent provision at the next renewal, extension or amendment, and upon Lender's request after a default.
*Action.* Marcus should know that the Lowcountry estoppel cannot be waived: the tenant has no contractual duty to give one, so if it is not obtained at closing, it may never be obtained. Recommend the Bank treat the Lowcountry estoppel as a hard closing condition and obtain it early — before closing, not in the last week. Also confirm whether the Lowcountry tenant has responded to any estoppel request already.

**C.4 — The Bay 2 right of first refusal is not a title exception and does not appear in the Term Sheet at all.**
*Source:* Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (Harmon & Wren: ROFR on Bay 2; 15 business days to accept or decline; survives renewal); Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule (Bay 2 marketing constrained; 30–60 day delay); Appraisal §3.2 and §6.3 (adds 60–90 days to absorption); Title Commitment Exception 6 (tenants "without any option to purchase or right of first refusal to purchase").
*Observation.* The ROFR is a *lease* right over Bay 2, not a purchase right, so the title exception is technically accurate. But it constrains the Bank's principal lease-up lever: a third-party offer for Bay 2 must first be presented to Harmon & Wren. The Term Sheet's DSCR covenant and its leasing covenant (Section 10.6) do not acknowledge it.
*Treatment.* Section 4.9 of the Mortgage requires compliance with the ROFR, delivery of any bona fide offer and any ROFR notice, and states that Lender takes no position on its enforceability or scope. Section 11.6 requires a quarterly leasing status report that addresses the ROFR while occupancy is below 90%. Exhibit B, item 5 discloses the ROFR as a Permitted Exception. Exhibit D, note 2 discloses it in the rent roll.
*Action.* No credit decision is required, but the Bank's lease-up timeline should be adjusted by 60–90 days, as the appraiser recommends. If the Bank intends to enforce any lease-up benchmark, that benchmark must be set with the ROFR in mind.

**C.5 — The Term Sheet requires estoppels and SNDAs from all tenants, and the leases make that harder for one tenant and easier for two.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §12.1(j), (k); Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (Lowcountry: "Lease does not contain a provision requiring tenant to deliver estoppel certificates"; no SNDA provisions; Harmon & Wren: estoppel within 15 business days, subordinate with commercially reasonable non-disturbance; Tidemark: estoppel within 10 business days, agrees to execute SNDA on request).
*Observation.* Two of three tenants have contractual estoppel and subordination obligations. Lowcountry has neither. The Term Sheet treats all three identically.
*Treatment.* Sections 4.7(a)–(c) of the Mortgage carry the conditions precedent and add ongoing estoppel obligations; Section 4.7(b) requires an SNDA concurrently with any future lease.
*Action.* Confirm whether the Bank's standard SNDA form is acceptable to the tenants' lenders or franchisees (Lowcountry is a grocery; confirm whether it has a franchise or lender consent requirement). Start the SNDA circulation now.

**C.6 — Bay 3 expires on the same day the renovation must be complete, and its renewal notice deadline falls during construction.**
*Source:* Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (Harmon & Wren expiration June 30, 2026; one three-year renewal at 95% of FMV with six months' notice, deadline December 31, 2025); Term Sheet §8.3 (Substantial Completion Deadline June 15, 2026); Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule ("Bay 3 (At-Risk)").
*Observation.* The tenant's renewal decision is due December 31, 2025, in the middle of the renovation, with an expiration two weeks after substantial completion. If the tenant does not renew, the Property loses 3,800 square feet of ground-floor income at the very moment the DSCR test period begins, and the analysis in the rent roll shows the alternative (re-tenanting) costs $76,000 of TI and $28,500 of commissions.
*Treatment.* The Mortgage does not impose a lease-up or renewal covenant beyond Term Sheet §10.6 and the general leasing consent provisions in Section 4.8. Section 11.6 requires reporting.
*Action.* This is a credit monitoring item for the Bank. Recommend a covenant (not currently in the Term Sheet) requiring Borrower to deliver evidence of the renewal notice, or a copy of the renewal, within ten days after December 31, 2025. It is listed in Part IV as an optional addition.

**C.7 — The Tidemark lease gives the tenant disproportionate leverage over future leasing consents.**
*Source:* Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (Tidemark: assignment requires consent "which may be withheld in Landlord's sole discretion"; two three-year renewals at FMV).
*Observation.* Not a defect, but note that Lender's consent standard under Section 4.8 of the Mortgage ("not unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed") is narrower than the landlord's own standard in the Tidemark lease. There is no conflict, because Lender's consent is a separate requirement from the landlord's.
*Treatment.* Section 4.8 as drafted; no change required.
