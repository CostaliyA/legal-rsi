### D. Economics, Arithmetic and the Credit Structure

This group contains the findings that should be resolved before the draft is sent to the Bank, because three of them go to the structure of the Loan rather than to its documentation.

**D.1 — The 1.25x DSCR covenant is not achievable at any credible operating level of this Property.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §7.1 (minimum DSCR 1.25x; quarterly testing beginning after month 24; definitions of "Net Operating Income" and "Annual Debt Service" to be set in the Loan Documents); Term Sheet §3.6 (Daily SOFR + 2.85%, with a 3.00% SOFR floor, so a floor rate of 5.85%); Term Sheet §3.9 (25-year amortization from month 25); Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule (pro forma NOI $622,772; debt service $1,122,000; DSCR 0.555x; conclusion that the covenant fails even in the best case); Rent Roll, Summary ("Current in-place NOI of ~$320,100 is insufficient to meet 1.25x DSCR").
*Observation.* Recomputation from source components (Part V, checks 12–16):

• *Annual interest at the floor rate:* $14,750,000 × 5.85% = **$862,875**.

• *Annual amortizing debt service* (25 years, 5.85%, beginning month 25): monthly payment **$93,674**, annual **$1,124,082**.

• *NOI required for 1.25x during the interest-only period:* **$1,078,594**.

• *NOI required for 1.25x after amortization begins:* **$1,405,103**.

• *Best-case stabilized NOI on the Borrower's own pro forma (all space leased by the test date):* **$622,772** → 0.72x interest-only, 0.55x amortizing.

• *In-place NOI:* approximately **$320,100** → 0.37x interest-only, 0.28x amortizing.

• *Debt yield at stabilization:* 4.2% ($622,772 ÷ $14,750,000).

The Loan is sized at $14,750,000 against NOI that, even at full stabilization and full escalation, supports roughly $6.5 million of debt at a 1.25x amortizing coverage test and roughly $8.5 million at 1.25x interest-only coverage. The DSCR covenant is therefore not a performance test; it is a scheduled default at the first test date.
*Treatment.* Sections 11.1 and 14.1(i) of the Mortgage implement the Term Sheet faithfully: 1.25x, tested quarterly on a trailing-twelve-month basis, with an Event of Default only after two consecutive failures. The Mortgage defines Net Operating Income and Debt Service as the Term Sheet delegates (Section 1.1), including a 4% management fee cap, a $0.25/SF replacement reserve deduction, and Lender's right to normalize non-recurring items, below-market rents and partial-year leases. No cure right is included, because none was instructed and inventing one would be a Borrower-favorable change the Bank has not approved. Section 11.1(d) adds a distribution lock-up below 1.10x, which is a partial, drafting-level mitigation on which the Bank should opine (Part IV).
*Action required — decision of the Bank.* The Bank must choose one of the following, and the draft will be conformed to the choice:

• (i) reduce the covenant to a level the Property can meet (but note that a covenant at or about 0.60x has no credit function); or

• (ii) extend the interest-only period beyond month 24 and accept a lower interim covenant, with the 1.25x test deferred to a stabilization date tied to occupancy (for example, the first quarter after the Property achieves 90% occupancy for two consecutive quarters); or

• (iii) retain 1.25x but add a contractual cure: Borrower may cure a failure by prepaying principal (without premium) or by depositing cash collateral with Lender in an amount sufficient to restore 1.25x for the applicable period; or

• (iv) retain 1.25x with no cure, and accept that the Loan will default at the first test date, which is only workable if the Bank intends to use the covenant as a renegotiation trigger.

**Recommendation.** We recommend (iii). It preserves the covenant's credit function, gives the Bank a defined remedy other than foreclosure in a rising-rate or slow-lease-up scenario, and is straightforward to draft: a new Section 11.1(e) and a cross-reference in Section 14.3(d). If the Bank selects (i), (ii) or (iii), the Term Sheet should be amended before closing, because Section 7.1 states the 1.25x requirement as a bargained term and a Loan Document that departs from it invites a claim under Term Sheet §14.4 ("Lender reserves the right to require additional provisions ... provided that such additional provisions are not materially inconsistent with the terms of this Term Sheet").

**D.2 — The appraisal's income approach does not support the concluded values, which undermines the as-stabilized loan-to-value ratio.**
*Source:* Appraiser's Report §6.2 (as-is NOI $332,000–$345,000; 6.25% cap rate), §6.3 (stabilized NOI $582,000–$592,000; 5.85% cap rate), §7 (concluded values $18,200,000 and $23,500,000), Key Ratios table (as-is LTV 81.04%; as-stabilized LTV 62.77%); Term Sheet §3.5 and §2.4.
*Observation.* Applying the appraiser's own capitalization rates to the appraiser's own net operating income produces values that do not approximate the conclusions:

| Scenario | NOI relied on | Capitalization rate | Value indicated | Value concluded | Implied cap rate on concluded value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| As-is | $332,000–$345,000 | 6.25% | $5,312,000–$5,520,000 | $18,200,000 | 1.82%–1.90% |
| As-stabilized | $582,000–$592,000 | 5.85% | $9,949,000–$10,120,000 | $23,500,000 | 2.48%–2.52% |

The sales comparison approach is internally coherent ($600, $550 and $620 per square foot for three 2024–2025 comparables, against $587 per square foot as-is). The income approach is not. The appraiser states that it reconciled the income approach with the sales comparison and cost approaches, but the summary report does not disclose how, and the addenda that might explain it are not in the record.
*Treatment.* The Mortgage does not restate appraised values, and correctly does not — an instrument should not incorporate a valuation that may change. The consequences are recorded here rather than in the document.
*Action required — decision of the Bank.* (i) Obtain the complete appraisal with all addenda, and specifically the discounted cash flow analysis the appraiser says supports the conclusion. (ii) Consider ordering a review appraisal under the Bank's own policy; the income approach indicates approximately 30% of the concluded as-is value and approximately 43% of the concluded as-stabilized value, a disparity a reviewer will raise. (iii) Until this is resolved, the Bank should not rely on the 62.77% as-stabilized loan-to-value ratio for credit approval or for any covenant keyed to value. (iv) Note also that the as-is ratio of 81.04% exceeds a customary 65%–75% range; Kit's closing note asks Marcus to confirm whether the loan committee has approved exceptions for this Loan, and this memorandum repeats that request in writing (Part VIII).

**D.3 — Occupancy is stated as 68% in two documents and is 56.13% on the verified rent roll; in-place rent is stated at $415,100 and is approximately $422,966 after scheduled escalations.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §2.3 ("Current occupancy is approximately sixty-eight percent (68%)" and, in the same paragraph, "Vacant space consists of Bay 2 ... and 2nd Floor Suite B ..., totaling 13,600 square feet, or approximately 43.9% of total building area"); Appraisal §1 and §3.1 ("68% (21,400 SF of 31,000 SF occupied)"; "vacant area is 13,600 SF (32%)"); Rent Roll, Summary (17,400 SF occupied; 56.13%; 13,600 SF vacant; 43.87%); Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (rent escalation schedules).
*Observation.* The Term Sheet contradicts itself in a single paragraph: 68% occupancy and 43.9% vacancy cannot both be true. The rent roll is correct: occupied area is Bay 1 (3,800) + Bay 3 (3,800) + Suite A (9,800) = 17,400 SF = 56.13%; vacant is 13,600 SF = 43.87%. The appraisal's supporting sentence ("21,400 SF of 31,000 SF occupied ... 13,600 SF (32%)") does not add to 31,000 at all (21,400 + 13,600 = 35,000), and 21,400 ÷ 31,000 is 69.03%, not 68%. Separately, the rent roll's "Base Rent (Current)" column has not been updated for the escalations its own escalation schedules describe:

| Space | Stated current rent | Escalation per lease abstract | Rent in effect on April 15, 2025 | Annualized |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bay 1 | $28.50/SF ($108,300) | 3% each January 1 from January 1, 2025 → $29.36/SF | $29.36/SF (or $28.50 × 1.03 = $29.36) | $111,549 |
| Bay 3 | $24.00/SF ($91,200) | 2.5% each July 1 from July 1, 2023 → $24.60 (2023), $25.22 (2024) | $25.22/SF | $95,837 |
| Suite A | $22.00/SF ($215,600) | 2.5% each April 1 from April 1, 2026 | $22.00/SF | $215,600 |
| Total | $415,100 | | | **$422,986** |

The rent roll's own "Realistic Scenario" line uses $422,700 for the same three occupied spaces, confirming that the Summary tab figure is stale rather than a different assumption.
*Treatment.* Exhibit D of the Mortgage presents the rent roll as delivered, with a note requiring Borrower to deliver at closing a certified rent roll conformed for all scheduled escalations, and making that certification a representation under Section 6.3. Section 4.5(a) warrants that the rent roll is true and correct as of the Closing Date. We have not restated the term sheet's 68% figure anywhere in the Mortgage, and the draft does not repeat it. Exhibit D also states the correct occupancy of 56.13%.
*Action.* (i) Ask Borrower's counsel to confirm the rent actually invoiced for January 2025 (Bay 1) and July 2024 (Bay 3) — the escalation clauses may be drafted so that the rent adjusting mechanism operates differently from the schedule in the abstract, and only the ledgers settle it. (ii) Correct the occupancy figure in the credit approval memorandum and in any investor or rating presentation; a 12-point overstatement of occupancy in a loan file is the kind of error that is remembered after a default. (iii) Note that correcting the rent upward does not materially change the DSCR conclusion in D.1 (it improves stabilized NOI by roughly $8,000).

**D.4 — Sources and uses do not balance once closing costs and the required reserve deposit are included.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §3.4 (Total Project Cost $20,950,000; Borrower equity $6,200,000, "representing approximately 29.6% of the Total Project Cost"); Term Sheet §3.3(a) (Initial Advance "to be applied toward the purchase price, closing costs, and funded reserves"); Term Sheet §6.3 ($150,000 tenant improvement and leasing commission reserve deposited by Borrower at closing); Term Sheet §13.1 (origination fee $147,500); Term Sheet §13.2 (Borrower pays all closing costs); Kit's notes, ¶6 (documentary stamp tax 0.37% = $54,575).
*Observation.* The stated Total Project Cost is exactly the sum of the Loan and the equity contribution ($17,800,000 + $3,150,000 = $20,950,000 = $14,750,000 + $6,200,000). It therefore contains no allowance for:

| Item | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Loan origination fee (1.00%) | $147,500 | Term Sheet §13.1 |
| South Carolina documentary stamp tax on the Mortgage (0.37%) | $54,575 | Kit's notes ¶6; S.C. recording schedule |
| Tenant improvement and leasing commission reserve | $150,000 | Term Sheet §6.3 |
| Title insurance premium and endorsements | not quantified in record | Term Sheet §13.2(d), §5.6 |
| ALTA/NSPS survey | not quantified | Term Sheet §13.2(e) |
| Appraisal, Phase I ESA, and any review | not quantified | Term Sheet §13.2(b), (c) |
| Lender's legal fees (Fielding, Ashworth & Cole LLP) | not quantified | Term Sheet §13.2(a) |
| **Quantified subtotal** | **$352,075** | |

The equity contribution of $6,200,000 is itself committed by the Members in fixed proportions ($4,464,000 / $1,116,000 / $620,000) under Operating Agreement §2.2, so an additional call requires either a further capital call or a reallocation of the Initial Advance — which would reduce the cash available to the seller at closing and leave the purchase price unfunded.
*Treatment.* The Mortgage does not restate project cost (correct for an instrument). Section 17.11 places all recording, documentary stamp, title and closing costs on Borrower, consistent with the Term Sheet, and Section 12.1(m)-type evidence of equity funding is carried as a closing condition under the Term Sheet rather than a Mortgage covenant.
*Action required.* Confirm with Marcus how closing costs and the $150,000 reserve deposit are funded. Three possibilities: (i) the equity contribution is in fact larger than $6,200,000 and the Operating Agreement summary is out of date; (ii) the Bank is funding the origination fee and reserve out of Loan proceeds, which reduces the cash to the seller and requires the seller's consent; or (iii) the shortfall is being met from Borrower's operating cash outside the committed capital. The draft assumes (iii) and does not condition disbursement on a sources-and-uses statement, but the Bank's closing checklist should, and we recommend adding one.

**D.5 — Tenant improvement and leasing commission costs are budgeted three times over.**
*Source:* Renovation Budget Line 4 ($550,000 "Tenant Improvement Allowances" for Bay 2 and Suite B, "budgeted at ~$40.44/SF blended"); Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule (TI allowance assumed $25.00/SF for Bay 2 = $95,000 and $22.00/SF for Suite B = $215,600; aggregate $310,600; leasing commissions $89,750); Term Sheet §6.3 ($150,000 separate TI/LC reserve funded at closing).
*Observation.* Three different buckets address the same cost: $550,000 inside the Renovation Budget, $310,600 in the lease-up schedule, and $150,000 in a separate reserve, with no stated interaction. The renovation budget line ($40.44/SF blended) is 78% higher than the lease-up schedule's assumption ($22.84/SF blended: $310,600 ÷ 13,600 SF). Separately, Line 4 is described as "Various (to be contracted per lease terms)" in the budget detail and yet is included in the GC Contract sum of $2,850,000, as discussed in D.10.
*Treatment.* Exhibit C of the Mortgage reproduces the Renovation Budget as delivered, allocates the Line 4 amount between the Renovation Holdback ($450,000) and Borrower equity ($100,000), and adds a note requiring the scope of the GC Contract — including whether Line 4 sits inside or outside it — to be confirmed by the executed contract and change orders. Section 12.3 preserves the separate $150,000 reserve and its 30-day replenishment obligation, which is the only one of the three that gives the Bank a replenishment right.
*Action.* Ask Borrower to reconcile the three figures and to state which costs are funded from the Renovation Holdback, which from the reserve, and which from Borrower equity. If Line 4 is in truth $310,600 rather than $550,000, then either the Renovation Budget is overstated by $239,400 (which weakens the 71.43% holdback ratio and the as-stabilized value assumption) or the excess is available for other costs. This must be resolved before the Bank approves the draw schedule, because the draw conditions in Section 12.4 are tied to the Line 4 allocation.

**D.6 — The DSCR test date is stated three different ways.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §7.1 (testing commences 24 months after closing; first test date "the first full calendar quarter ending after the twenty-fourth month following closing (anticipated to be approximately July 15, 2027, for the quarter ending June 30, 2027)"); Rent Roll, Summary ("DSCR Covenant Testing Commencement: April 15, 2027 (Month 24 post-closing)"); Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule (same, April 15, 2027); Kit's notes, ¶6 ("first test date April 15, 2027").
*Observation.* Three positions: (i) quarter-ending basis → the first Test Period ends June 30, 2027; (ii) Month 24 date → April 15, 2027; (iii) the Term Sheet's own parenthetical oddity, which describes the quarter ending June 30, 2027 but labels the test date July 15, 2027 — which is only 15 days after quarter end, faster than any lender can compute a trailing twelve-month test. The lease-up analysis in the rent roll was built on the April 15, 2027 date (its "realistic scenario" assumes Suite B is not stabilized by then), so the choice matters to the pro forma.
*Treatment.* Section 11.1(b) of the Mortgage adopts the Term Sheet's quarter-ending formulation: testing commences with the Test Period ending June 30, 2027, with Borrower certification within 45 days after the Test Period ends (that is, by August 14, 2027, not July 15, 2027). "Test Period" is defined in Article I as a trailing twelve-month period ending on the last day of a fiscal quarter.
*Action.* Confirm the 45-day certification convention with Marcus, and correct the Term Sheet's July 15, 2027 parenthetical in the closing checklist or by side letter so the Bank and the Borrower's counsel are working from one date. Note that the April 15, 2027 date in the rent roll is what the Borrower's own adviser used; if the Bank intends the earlier date, the lease-up analysis becomes materially more aggressive than it already is.

**D.10 — The scope of the GC Contract is inconsistent between the Term Sheet, the budget detail and the line-item vendor designations.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §3.4 and §8.1 (contract sum $2,850,000 "covering hard costs of construction"; $300,000 for soft costs and contingency outside the GC Contract); Renovation Budget, Budget Detail (SUBTOTAL — Hard Costs (GC Contract) = $2,850,000 labelled "Items 1–4," but Line 4's vendor is "Various (to be contracted per lease terms)"; Line Items 1–3 total $2,300,000).
*Observation.* If the GC Contract covers only Lines 1 through 3, the contract sum should be $2,300,000, and $550,000 of hard costs would be contracted separately. If it covers Lines 1 through 4, then the tenant improvement allowance work is being contracted to the General Contractor rather than, as the line item says, "per lease terms." *Treatment.* Exhibit C, Part 2 of the Mortgage states the GC Contract sum at $2,850,000 for Line Items 1–4, consistent with the Term Sheet and the budget subtotal, and adds a note requiring the scope to be confirmed by the executed contract and change orders delivered under Term Sheet §12.1(l) and Section 12.4(g). *Action.* Obtain the executed GC Contract before closing (it is already a condition precedent) and reconcile.
