## PART I — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Purpose.** This memorandum accompanies the first draft of the Mortgage and Security Agreement (the "**Mortgage**") prepared for the above-referenced loan (the "**Loan**") from Calverley Heritage Bank ("**Lender**" or the "**Bank**") to Palmetto Gateway Partners LLC ("**Borrower**"). It records (a) each material discrepancy identified among the source documents, (b) the drafting treatment adopted in the Mortgage and the source basis for that treatment, (c) the items requiring a decision by the Bank or by Kit before the draft is released, and (d) the independent recomputations performed on the figures used in the draft. It does not repeat Kit's March 18, 2025 markup notes; it responds to them and records where we departed from them, and why.

**Overall assessment.** The Mortgage is substantially complete as a first draft and can be circulated to the Bank once the items flagged **Priority A** below are resolved. Three findings are material and require a decision before, not after, circulation:

1. **The 1.25x DSCR covenant as written cannot be satisfied by this Property, at any plausible level of lease-up.** On the Bank's own rent roll and pro forma, stabilized best-case net operating income is $622,772 against annual debt service of $1,124,082 after the interest-only period (0.55x) and $862,875 during the interest-only period (0.72x). To achieve 1.25x during the interest-only period, NOI would have to rise to $1,078,594, and after amortization to $1,405,103 — against in-place NOI of approximately $320,100. The covenant, as drafted from Section 7.1 of the Term Sheet, functions as a scheduled default at the first test date rather than as a performance test. See Part III, Section D.1.

2. **The appraisal's income approach does not support the appraised values.** The appraisal concludes $18,200,000 as-is and $23,500,000 as-stabilized on the basis of a 6.25% and 5.85% capitalization rate respectively, but the net operating income it reports ($332,000–$345,000 as-is; $582,000–$592,000 stabilized) implies capitalization rates of approximately 1.87% and 2.50% on those values. The sales approach supports $587 per square foot; the income approach does not. The as-stabilized value underpins the reported 62.77% as-stabilized loan-to-value ratio, and therefore the Bank's collateral conclusion. See Part III, Section D.2.

3. **The sources and uses of funds do not balance once closing costs and required reserves are included.** Total project cost of $20,950,000 equals the sum of the Loan ($14,750,000) and the equity contribution ($6,200,000) exactly, leaving no source for the $147,500 origination fee, the $54,575 documentary stamp tax on the Mortgage, the $150,000 tenant improvement and leasing commission reserve (which the Term Sheet requires Borrower to deposit at closing from its own funds), or title, survey, appraisal, environmental and legal costs. The identified, quantified shortfall alone is $352,075. See Part III, Section D.4.

**Second-order findings.** Beyond those three, the most consequential items are (a) the gap in the payment guaranty, which covers only the first 14 months of debt service against a 24-month interest-only period and therefore leaves months 15 through 24 — precisely the pre-stabilization window — unguaranteed; (b) the operating agreement's absence of any independent manager, separateness covenant or bankruptcy consent requirement, which is inconsistent with the single-purpose entity covenants the Term Sheet requires and with the representations the Mortgage makes; (c) the complete absence of any indirect transfer or change-of-control restriction in either the Term Sheet or the operating agreement, which permits a sale of the 72% managing member without Lender consent; and (d) the Anson Lane parcel, which was conveyed by quitclaim deed only and which the title commitment insures without affirmative coverage over the absence of warranties.

**Verification.** Every figure used in the Mortgage has been recomputed from source components rather than carried forward from a summary. Part V records 24 checks; 15 agree with the source documents, 9 do not and are flagged. The most significant of those are the occupancy figure (68% as stated in the Term Sheet and appraisal against 56.13% verified from the rent roll) and the in-place rent roll, which carries base rents from before the last scheduled escalation ($415,100 as stated against approximately $422,966 recomputed).

**Drafting posture.** The Mortgage is drafted to the Term Sheet, not to the template and not to the markup notes, where those three differ. Where Kit's markup notes instructed a departure from the Term Sheet (most importantly the yield maintenance floor, discussed in Part III, Section E.1), the draft follows the Term Sheet and the point is flagged for a decision. Part IV lists every provision in the draft that is not derived from the Term Sheet and requires the Bank's approval.

**What the draft does not do.** The Mortgage cannot fix a loan that is undersized against its own coverage covenant, and it cannot create collateral value that the appraisal does not support. Those are credit decisions for the Bank, documented in Part III, Sections D.1 and D.2. The draft is structured so that a cure mechanism, a cash-management waterfall or a revolver-to-permanent conversion can be inserted without restructuring the document if the Bank elects one of those courses.

### A. Collateral, Title and Survey

**A.1 — The Anson Lane parcel is a quitclaim conveyance, and the title commitment gives no affirmative coverage over that fact.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §2.1 and Exhibit A; Title Commitment Schedule A §4 and the "Vesting Deed Information" addendum; Kit's notes, ¶2.
*Observation.* Parcel Two (the vacated Anson Lane strip, Deed Book 1128, Page 443) was conveyed by quitclaim deed from the City of Charleston. The title commitment insures fee simple title to the Land, including Parcel Two, and does not except the parcel or qualify the estate to less than fee simple — it satisfies Kit's first test. However, the commitment's addendum states expressly that "no warranties of title were provided in connection with this conveyance," that the Company's willingness to insure "should not be construed as a representation that the chain of title for this parcel is equivalent to that of Parcel One," and that the Company "reserves the right to require an additional premium for extended coverage on this parcel and/or may limit such coverage by endorsement."
*Treatment.* Section 3.2 of the Mortgage contains a Borrower warranty that it has caused the Title Insurer to insure fee simple title to Parcel Two without any Schedule B-II exception excluding or qualifying the estate, and a covenant to deliver either a clean policy as to Parcel Two or an endorsement (expressly including ALTA Endorsement 34 or equivalent, per the commitment's closer note 3) at Borrower's expense. Failure is expressly tied to the condition precedent in Term Sheet §12.1(b). Section 3.2(d) also prohibits Borrower from releasing any access, loading or parking right over Parcel Two without Lender's consent.
*Action.* (i) Ask Prestige Title in writing, this week, to confirm (a) the premium for extended coverage on Parcel Two, (b) whether any endorsement limiting coverage will be attached, and (c) that ALTA 34 is available. (ii) Decide whether the Bank will accept a premium-sharing arrangement; the Term Sheet leaves all title costs with Borrower, so the cost is not the issue — the coverage terms are. (iii) The Bank should understand that a quitclaim-derived parcel that also carries the property's rear access is the single weakest link in the collateral chain.

### B. Environmental

**B.1 — The LUR is the Bank's principal environmental exposure, and the Term Sheet does not contain the covenants that manage it.**
*Source:* Phase I ESA §§5.3–5.5 and §6(d); Title Commitment Exception 4, Requirement 6 and closer note 1; Term Sheet §5.4; Appraisal §5.
*Observation.* The LUR (Deed Book 1203, Page 89) prohibits residential use of the ground floor of Lot 7 (Bay 1) and requires maintenance and periodic inspection of the Vapor Barrier beneath the Bay 1 slab. The NFA status is contingent on continued compliance; a breach could cause SCDHEC to reopen Site ID SC-29403-0772 at the owner's expense. The Term Sheet requires an Environmental Indemnity (Section 5.4) but its enumerated content is general; the *title commitment* is more specific than the Term Sheet, requiring the Environmental Indemnity to address the CREC, the vapor barrier maintenance and periodic inspection obligation, and the SCDHEC land use restriction (Requirement 6), and requiring the *Mortgage* to contain an event of default for failure to comply with, or any modification or termination of, the LUR (closer note 1).
*Treatment.* Section 7.1 of the Mortgage implements the commitment's instruction directly and in detail: annual (and on-request) inspection of the Vapor Barrier by a qualified environmental professional acceptable to Lender, at Borrower's expense; no penetration or slab work without SCDHEC approval, notice to Lender and an approved repair plan; prohibition on residential use of Bay 1 (reinforced in Section 9.4(b)); advance written notice to SCDHEC of any Bay 1 slab work; five-business-day notice to Lender of any SCDHEC communication; 30-day delivery of evidence of compliance on request; and no modification, termination, release or amendment of the LUR without Lender's consent. Section 14.1(l) makes any breach an Event of Default.
*Action.* (i) Confirm the Environmental Indemnity (not in the record) contains the same provisions; Requirement 6 of the commitment will not be satisfied otherwise, and Lender should not accept an indemnity that addresses environmental liability generally but omits the vapor barrier and LUR obligations. (ii) Confirm the Bank wants the LUR covenants in *both* the Mortgage and the Environmental Indemnity; the draft assumes belt-and-braces, as the commitment's closer note contemplates.

**B.2 — Environmental defaults carry no cure period in the draft, which is defensible but should be a conscious decision.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §11.1(b), §11.2(b) (non-monetary defaults other than SPE, transfer and insurance defaults receive a 30-day cure, extendable to 90).

The draft takes the middle course of making an environmental default an Event of Default (Section 14.1(l)) while Section 14.3(f) gives no cure period, consistent with the seriousness of the exposure and with the inability to "cure" a regulatory reopening.
*Action.* Confirm. A court may be receptive to a Borrower argument that a 30-day cure should apply to a curable environmental covenant (for example, a late inspection report). Suggested compromise if the Bank prefers: provide a 10-business-day cure for administrative breaches of Section 7.1(a) and (f), with no cure for slab penetration, residential use, LUR modification or a regulatory reopening. ### C. Leases, Rents and the Assignment

**C.1 — The assignment of rents has been converted to an absolute assignment, as instructed.**
*Source:* Kit's notes, ¶3; Term Sheet §5.2 (assignment "may be included within the Mortgage and Security Agreement or executed as a separate instrument, at the discretion of Lender's counsel"); Title Commitment Requirement 5 (expressly permits inclusion within the Mortgage).
*Observation.* The template's collateral assignment is insufficient under the assignment-of-rents statute Kit identifies, which gives an absolute assignment effect on execution (with enforcement triggered by default).
*Treatment.* Article IV of the Mortgage is drafted as an absolute and present assignment: "unconditionally, absolutely and presently grants, bargains, sells, conveys, transfers, assigns and sets over," with an express statement that it is "not merely a collateral assignment or an assignment as additional security." Section 4.2 ties perfection to recording and enforcement to default. Section 4.3 grants Borrower a revocable license to collect until default, which is the market-standard way to preserve the absolute characterization while allowing the Borrower to operate. Section 4.4 permits direct notice to tenants on default without a court order or receiver. No separate Assignment of Leases and Rents will be recorded, satisfying both Term Sheet §5.2 and Title Requirement 5 by inclusion.
*Action.* Confirm the Bank is content with a single recorded instrument (it reduces recording cost and documentary stamp exposure, and it avoids a split-enforcement argument). Note that the Term Sheet's loan documents list at §14.3(c) contemplates a separate assignment; the draft supersedes that by election under §5.2.

**C.2 — Verify the statutory citation before the draft goes out.**
*Source:* Kit's notes, ¶3, citing "the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act (S.C. Code § 29-3-100 *et seq.*)."
*Observation.* The South Carolina enactment of the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act is cited in practice to Title 29, Chapter 3 of the South Carolina Code, and the section numbers should be verified against the current Code before they are printed in a recorded instrument. We have not independently confirmed the citation given in the markup notes.
*Treatment.* Article IV refers to "the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act as enacted in the State of South Carolina (the 'UARAA')" without a section citation. This is deliberate: a recorded instrument should not carry an unverified citation, and the operative effect does not depend on the citation.
*Action.* Have a paralegal pull the current Code sections and, if Kit confirms the citation, insert it at Section 4.2 in the next turn of the draft. This is a five-minute item and should not be allowed to hold up circulation.

**C.3 — The Lowcountry Lease has no assignment or subletting provision at all. This is the most important lease issue in the deal.**
*Source:* Rent Roll, Lease Abstracts (Bay 1: "No provision. Lease is silent on assignment or subletting rights and does not contain a consent requirement, prohibition, or landlord approval mechanism"); Appraisal §3.2 (appraiser notes the omission and does not opine on it); Kit's notes, ¶3 (three-step protocol).
*Observation.* As Kit notes, the statutory assignment is enforceable against the tenant on notification regardless of the lease's silence. The risk is practical: a tenant that never agreed to an assignment may resist or litigate at the moment of enforcement, and the tenant also has no contractual obligation to deliver an estoppel.
*Treatment.* All three steps in Kit's protocol are implemented:
(i) *Representation.* Section 4.5(b) of the Mortgage: Borrower warrants it has "good right, full power and lawful authority to absolutely assign the Leases and the Rents, including without limitation the Lowcountry Lease, notwithstanding the absence of any assignment, subletting or consent-to-assignment provision in the Lowcountry Lease."
(ii) *Estoppel as a condition precedent.* Section 4.7(a) records the Term Sheet condition (Section 12.1(j)) and states expressly that "Lender does not intend to waive such condition with respect to the Lowcountry Lease"; Section 4.7(c) adds a 90-day post-closing covenant to deliver a Lowcountry estoppel that acknowledges the Mortgage and the assignment, and agrees to pay Rents to Lender on notice after default.
(iii) *Lease amendment at the next opportunity.* Section 4.7(c) adds a commercially reasonable efforts covenant to obtain a customary assignment-consent provision at the next renewal, extension or amendment, and upon Lender's request after a default.
*Action.* Marcus should know that the Lowcountry estoppel cannot be waived: the tenant has no contractual duty to give one, so if it is not obtained at closing, it may never be obtained. Recommend the Bank treat the Lowcountry estoppel as a hard closing condition and obtain it early — before closing, not in the last week. ### D. Economics, Arithmetic and the Credit Structure

**D.1 — The 1.25x DSCR covenant is not achievable at any credible operating level of this Property.**

*Observation.* Recomputation from source components (Part V, checks 12–16):

• *Annual interest at the floor rate:* $14,750,000 × 5.85% = **$862,875**.

• *Annual amortizing debt service* (25 years, 5.85%, beginning month 25): monthly payment **$93,674**, annual **$1,124,082**.

*Action required — decision of the Bank.* The Bank must choose one of the following, and the draft will be conformed to the choice:

• (i) reduce the covenant to a level the Property can meet (but note that a covenant at or about 0.60x has no credit function); or

• (ii) extend the interest-only period beyond month 24 and accept a lower interim covenant, with the 1.25x test deferred to a stabilization date tied to occupancy (for example, the first quarter after the Property achieves 90% occupancy for two consecutive quarters); or

• (iii) retain 1.25x but add a contractual cure: Borrower may cure a failure by prepaying principal (without premium) or by depositing cash collateral with Lender in an amount sufficient to restore 1.25x for the applicable period; or

• (iv) retain 1.25x with no cure, and accept that the Loan will default at the first test date, which is only workable if the Bank intends to use the covenant as a renegotiation trigger.

**Recommendation.** We recommend (iii). It preserves the covenant's credit function, gives the Bank a defined remedy other than foreclosure in a rising-rate or slow-lease-up scenario, and is straightforward to draft: a new Section 11.1(e) and a cross-reference in Section 14.3(d). If the Bank selects (i), (ii) or (iii), the Term Sheet should be amended before closing, because Section 7.1 states the 1.25x requirement as a bargained term and a Loan Document that departs from it invites a claim under Term Sheet §14.4 ("Lender reserves the right to require additional provisions ... provided that such additional provisions are not materially inconsistent with the terms of this Term Sheet").

### E. Prepayment and Yield Maintenance

Kit's instruction on yield maintenance was the most detailed in the markup notes, and it is the area where the draft departs from the notes most substantially — not by oversight, but because the notes and the Term Sheet state different economics. The Bank must choose which controls.

**E.1 — Kit's proposed formula omits the 1% floor that the Term Sheet requires. The draft restores it.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §4.2 (premium "equal to the greater of: (a) one percent (1.00%) of the outstanding principal balance ... or (b) the present value of the excess of (i) the interest that would have accrued ... at the applicable Note rate ... over (ii) the interest that would accrue ... at the treasury rate plus fifty (50) basis points ... discounted to present value at the treasury rate"); Kit's notes, ¶5 (proposed formula with "In no event shall the Yield Maintenance Premium be less than zero," and no 1% floor).
*Observation.* The Term Sheet's premium is a **greater of** two amounts, with a 1% floor. Kit's proposed clause computes only the present value differential and floors it at zero, which eliminates the 1% minimum. The difference is material in a rising-rate environment: if Treasury yields rise above the Note rate, the differential goes to zero and, under Kit's formula, so does the premium; under the Term Sheet, the Bank still collects 1% of the prepaid principal — $147,500 on a full prepayment at the original balance.
*Treatment.* Section 13.3(b) of the Mortgage implements the Term Sheet: the premium is "the greater of" (i) 1.00% of the outstanding principal balance as of the date of prepayment and (ii) the present value differential, with the differential floored at zero and the premium itself therefore never below 1%.
*Action required.* Confirm with Marcus which economics the Bank intends. Kit's proposed language may have been a deliberate tightening of the Borrower's position (unlikely, given the Bank's interest) or an inadvertent omission. Our view: the Term Sheet controls, the Bank should keep the floor, and Kit's clause should not be used. If the Bank prefers Kit's version, the Term Sheet must be amended, because the Borrower will notice the change.

**E.2 — The Term Sheet and Kit's notes compute the differential on different bases.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §4.2(b) (compares the interest stream at the Note rate against the interest stream at Treasury + 50 bps, discounted at the Treasury rate); Kit's notes, ¶5 (compares the present value of "all remaining scheduled monthly payments of interest and principal" discounted at the Treasury Rate against "the outstanding principal amount being prepaid").
*Observation.* These produce different answers. The Term Sheet's version is a classic yield maintenance calculation: the lender is made whole for the difference between the loan's yield and the reinvestment yield on the remaining term. Kit's version is closer to a defeasance/opportunity-cost calculation and, because it discounts principal and interest rather than interest alone, will generally be lower than the Term Sheet's figure at the same rate environment. Neither is unenforceable; they simply are not the same bargain.
*Treatment.* Section 13.3(b) implements the Term Sheet's construction. The formula is self-contained: it fixes the measurement period (prepayment date through the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period, that is, the last day of month 48), the interest basis (monthly, 30/360), the comparison rate (Treasury Rate + 0.50%), the discount rate (Treasury Rate), the floor (zero) and the minimum premium (1.00%), and it provides that Lender's computation is conclusive absent manifest error.
*Action.* Confirm the construction with Marcus (see E.1). Whichever construction is chosen, the *Note* must contain the identical formula — see E.5.

**E.3 — The 50 basis points are allocated to the comparison rate, not to the loan rate. This resolves the Term Sheet's ambiguity, but the Bank should confirm.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §4.2(b)(ii) ("the interest that would accrue on such prepaid principal amount at the treasury rate plus fifty (50) basis points"); Kit's notes, ¶5 (recommends adding the 50 bps to the loan rate instead, and asks for confirmation from Marcus).
*Observation.* The Term Sheet allocates the spread to the treasury leg. Kit reads the phrase "treasury rate + 50 bps spread" as ambiguous and would prefer to add the spread to the Note rate, which increases the premium. The two approaches are economically equivalent only if the spread is added to one leg: adding 50 bps to the Note rate increases the differential by 50 bps; adding 50 bps to the Treasury rate *decreases* the differential by 50 bps. They are opposites.
*Treatment.* Section 13.3(b)(ii)(B) follows the Term Sheet literally: the comparison stream accrues "at a rate per annum equal to the Treasury Rate plus fifty (50) basis points (0.50%)." This reduces the premium relative to Kit's preferred reading.
*Action required.* This is a Bank decision with a dollar consequence. Confirm with Marcus. If the Bank wants Kit's reading, the draft changes one clause and the Term Sheet must be amended or the discrepancy accepted as an interpretive position taken in the Bank's favor — which we would not recommend without the Borrower's written agreement, because it is the kind of ambiguity a Borrower's counsel will litigate after a prepayment.

**E.4 — The Treasury rate definition contains an internal inconsistency that should be resolved on its face.**
*Source:* Kit's notes, ¶5 (Treasury Rate means the yield "of the United States Treasury security ... having a maturity date closest to, but not exceeding, the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period. If no published maturity exactly matches, interpolate linearly between the two nearest published maturities.").
*Observation.* "Closest to but not exceeding" and "interpolate between the two nearest" cannot both be applied to the same fact pattern; the first selects a security and the second implies a synthetic one.
*Treatment.* Section 13.3(c) of the Mortgage resolves it in favour of determinism: the Treasury Constant Maturity series with a maturity date closest to the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period, with linear interpolation between the two nearest maturities (one above and one below) only where no published constant maturity has a maturity within ninety days of that date. The reference is to Federal Reserve Statistical Release H.15, as Kit proposed, and to any successor publication.
*Action.* None substantive; confirm with Kit that the resolution is acceptable.

**E.5 — The Note has not been reviewed, and the Mortgage says the Note controls the premium calculation.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §14.3(a) (Promissory Note to be prepared by Lender's counsel); Section 13.1 of the Mortgage (in case of inconsistency as to the amount or calculation of any prepayment premium, the Note controls).
*Observation.* The Mortgage cannot be the only document with a defensible formula. If the Note contains a summary formula (as Notes often do — "yield maintenance as computed by Lender") and the Mortgage contains the detailed formula, the disparity will be read against the Bank. Conversely, if the Note contains the 1% floor and the Mortgage does not (or vice versa), the Borrower will rely on whichever is lower.
*Treatment.* Section 13.1 subordinates the Mortgage's Article XIII to the Note as to quantum, so the Note is the operative economic document, and Article XIII supplies the mechanics only to the extent consistent with it.
*Action required.* Before execution, circulate the Note (draft or executed form) and conform Article XIII to it clause by clause. If the Note is issued in the form described in Kit's markup notes rather than in the Term Sheet form, that choice should be made deliberately, once, and reflected in both documents.

**E.6 — Involuntary prepayments are applied at par without premium. This is a drafting election, not a Term Sheet term.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §4.1–4.3 (silent on casualty and condemnation); Kit's notes, ¶5 ("Confirm this casualty/condemnation carve-out with the client — some lenders want a premium even on involuntary prepayments, though that is the minority position").
*Observation.* Sections 10.7, 10.8, 13.2(c) and 13.5 of the Mortgage provide that casualty proceeds, condemnation awards and post-default acceleration payments are applied at par with no premium.
*Action required.* Confirm with Marcus. If the Bank wants a premium on casualty or condemnation applications (a minority position, and one that is harder to defend as liquidated damages because the prepayment is involuntary), Sections 10.7, 10.8 and 13.5 change in three places. We recommend leaving the carve-out; it is market and it avoids a penalty argument.

**E.7 — Acceleration after default: no premium. This should also be a conscious position.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §4.1 (prepayment "in violation of this restriction shall be subject to a prepayment premium"); the Term Sheet does not address acceleration.
*Observation.* Section 13.5 of the Mortgage applies amounts paid on acceleration at par. Some lenders draft yield maintenance as payable on acceleration as well, to remove any incentive to default into a prepayment.
*Action required.* Confirm. If the Bank wants the premium on acceleration, that is a commercially aggressive position that South Carolina courts may scrutinize as a penalty when the acceleration is the lender's election rather than the borrower's.

**E.8 — Enforceability: characterize the premium carefully, and keep the formula deterministic.**
*Source:* Kit's notes, ¶5 ("SC courts have generally enforced yield maintenance premiums ... but the formula must be specific, mathematically deterministic, and objectively calculable ... If the formula is ambiguous ... a court could deem it an unenforceable penalty").
*Observation.* The draft supports enforceability in four ways: the formula is deterministic (fixed measurement period, published index, stated day count and discount convention); Lender's computation is conclusive only "absent manifest error," which preserves a check on arbitrary calculation; Section 13.3(e) recites that the premium is a reasonable estimate of loss and not a penalty; and the 1% floor is small relative to any plausible loss, which supports the "reasonable estimate" characterization rather than an inference of punitive intent.
*Action.* None. Do not add language describing the premium as a "fee" or as "additional interest," which weakens the liquidated-damages characterization.

### F. Single-Purpose Entity Status and the Operating Agreement

The Term Sheet requires full single-purpose entity covenants (Section 10.1). The Operating Agreement, as summarized, contains almost none of them. The distance between the two is the largest documentation gap in the transaction, and the draft's representation in Section 6.4 cannot be given truthfully until it is closed.

**F.1 — No independent manager, and therefore no bankruptcy remoteness.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §10.1; Operating Agreement Summary §3.1 ("The Operating Agreement does not impose any requirement for GDG to appoint or maintain an independent director, independent manager, or springing member. There is no provision ... requiring the consent of any independent party prior to the Company filing a voluntary petition under the United States Bankruptcy Code ... The Operating Agreement does not contain any bankruptcy remoteness provisions").
*Observation.* Graydon Development Group LLC holds 72% and, through Neil Ashford Graydon as its sole member and manager, controls Borrower outright. Nothing prevents Guarantor from causing Borrower to file. That defeats the purpose of the non-recourse structure (Term Sheet §5.5(a)(iii) treats a voluntary filing as a full-recourse carve-out, which mitigates but does not cure the issue — the carve-out preserves the Bank's claim against Guarantor, but a filing still stays the foreclosure).
*Treatment.* Article VIII of the Mortgage requires the Operating Agreement to provide for at least one Independent Manager; requires the Independent Manager's affirmative consent before Borrower may file or consent to a bankruptcy, make an assignment for creditors, admit inability to pay debts, or dissolve, merge or consolidate; requires a springing member mechanism on the resignation, removal, death or incapacity of an Independent Manager, with a successor approved by Lender within ten business days; prohibits removal or replacement of the Independent Manager without Lender's consent; and prohibits amendment of those provisions without Lender's consent. Section 8.2 imposes the standard separateness covenants, Section 8.5 requires each Member's written consent to be bound, and Section 14.1(j) makes a breach immediate (no cure) consistent with Term Sheet §11.2(c).
*Action.* The Operating Agreement Amendment described in Kit's markup notes, ¶4(b) must be drafted and executed by all three Members (and by Tidewater GP Holdings LLC for Tidewater) as a condition precedent. Note: the Term Sheet does not list the Operating Agreement Amendment among the Section 12.1 conditions precedent, but Section 10.1(g) requires the organizational documents to be amended "prior to closing" — so the Amendment is required by the Term Sheet even though the closing checklist omits it. Recommend adding it expressly to the checklist (Part VII).

**F.2 — The purpose clause is a "catch-all" and the separateness covenants are absent.**
*Source:* Operating Agreement Summary §1.4 (purpose clause ends "and to engage in any and all activities necessary, incidental, or related thereto," described by Borrower's counsel as "a broad catch-all that is not otherwise limited or defined"); §3.1 (no separateness covenants); Kit's notes, ¶4(2)–(3).
*Observation.* A single-purpose representation cannot be given against a purpose clause that permits any related activity, and the absence of separateness covenants (separate books, accounts, stationery, arm's-length dealings, no affiliate guarantees) is a substantive bankruptcy-consolidation risk in an entity 72% owned by an operating developer with other projects.
*Treatment.* Section 8.1 of the Mortgage restricts Borrower's purpose to the Property and incidental activities; Section 8.2 imposes twelve enumerated separateness covenants; Section 9.3 prohibits amendment of those provisions without Lender's consent.
*Action.* The Operating Agreement Amendment must narrow the purpose clause to the Property. Borrower's counsel should be told that a representation in Section 6.4 will be required at closing and cannot be given against the current text.

**F.3 — Bankruptcy is not a "Major Decision," and the 75% threshold does not help.**
*Source:* Operating Agreement Summary §3.2 (Major Decisions require 75% member approval; the list "does not include the filing of a voluntary petition for bankruptcy ... This decision is not subject to any heightened approval requirement"); §8.1(b) (dissolution on 75% consent).
*Observation.* Because GDG holds 72%, it cannot act alone on a Major Decision, which gives the passive members real leverage on sales, financing, budgets and dissolution. But bankruptcy is not on the list, so the leverage does not extend to the one decision that matters most to the Bank.
*Treatment.* Section 8.3(b) of the Mortgage requires the Independent Manager's consent for bankruptcy filings and dissolutions; Section 8.4 is a standalone prohibition; Section 9.7 prohibits dissolution, merger, consolidation or division without Lender's consent and, where applicable, unanimous member consent.
*Action.* Include the bankruptcy item in the Operating Agreement Amendment's Major Decisions list, with a unanimity requirement, in addition to the Independent Manager consent. Belt and braces; the passive members will support it because it protects their position too.

**F.4 — Tidewater's general partner must be bound, and its authority must be evidenced.**
*Source:* Operating Agreement Summary §2.1 (Tidewater GP Holdings LLC "has the exclusive authority to manage and direct the affairs of Tidewater, including its investments"); §10 signature page (Tidewater executes through Tidewater GP Holdings LLC); Kit's notes, ¶4 (last paragraph).
*Observation.* The Operating Agreement Amendment must be executed by Tidewater Opportunity Fund II LP acting through its general partner, and the general partner's authority should be evidenced rather than assumed.
*Treatment.* Section 6.4(c) of the Mortgage represents that the Operating Agreement Amendment has been executed by each Member and by Tidewater GP Holdings LLC as general partner; Section 7.11 requires delivery of evidence of that authority as a condition precedent.
*Action.* Request either the relevant provisions of the limited partnership agreement or a general partner authorization certificate. Do this now; it is a common last-week delay.

**F.5 — Two inconsistencies in Borrower's organizational records.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §1.2 (registered agent "Capitol Registered Agents Inc., 1201 Main Street, Suite 410, Columbia"); Operating Agreement Summary §1.3 (registered agent "Statehouse Registered Agents, Inc., 1201 Main Street, Suite 410, Columbia").
*Observation.* Same address, different agent name. One of the two documents is wrong, and the Bank's good standing certificate and title requirements (Commitment Requirement 8(a)) will turn on the Secretary of State's record.
*Treatment.* Section 7.9 of the Mortgage requires Borrower to maintain its registered agent as identified in the Operating Agreement and to notify Lender of any change. The draft does not name the agent.
*Action.* Confirm the current registered agent from the Secretary of State's records and correct the Term Sheet or the Operating Agreement as appropriate. Also confirm that the March 1, 2025 amendment to the Operating Agreement (executed by all three Members, per the summary's signature page) is not the amendment that the March 1, 2025 version already reflects — the summary says the amendment's "specific terms and modifications ... have not been summarized," which means the Bank has not seen the operative document.

**F.6 — The Operating Agreement requires only reviewed financial statements within 90 days; the Term Sheet requires audited statements within 120 days.**
*Source:* Operating Agreement Summary §6.2(b) (annual statements "reviewed (but not audited) by an independent certified public accountant ... within ninety (90) days"); Term Sheet §7.3(a) (audited statements by an auditor acceptable to Lender within 120 days).
*Observation.* Borrower's organizational documents commit to a lower standard than the Loan Documents require. A managing member complying with the Operating Agreement would deliver reviewed statements, and could resist an audit as an unbudgeted expense.
*Treatment.* Section 11.3(a) of the Mortgage requires audited statements within 120 days, as the Term Sheet does. Section 11.5 provides a 30-day cure after notice for failing to deliver audited statements, which mitigates the transition risk in year one.
*Action.* Either amend the Operating Agreement's reporting standard in the Operating Agreement Amendment, or accept the risk with the extended cure. Recommend amending: it costs nothing now and prevents an argument later.

**F.7 — Distributions, preferred returns and unilateral capital calls create leakage risk that the draft only partly controls.**
*Source:* Operating Agreement Summary §5.1 (8% compounding preferred return; return of capital; 80/20 promote); §5.2 (70/30 promote on capital events); §5.3 (quarterly tax distributions); §2.2 (Managing Member has "unilateral authority to determine the timing, amount, and purpose of additional capital calls"); §3.4 (asset management and development management fees, terms redacted).
*Observation.* An 8% compounding preferred return accrues from inception and is payable before any return of capital; in a value-add deal with a two-year rent-up, distributions may be funded from capital rather than cash flow. The Managing Member's unilateral capital call power is also a leakage route: capital calls that are used to fund distributions or fees would breach the separateness covenants and the negative covenants in the Mortgage.
*Treatment.* Section 9.6 of the Mortgage prohibits distributions while an Event of Default is continuing or if a distribution would cause a breach of the net worth or liquidity covenants; Section 11.1(d) adds a lock-up below 1.10x DSCR. Section 8.2(h) requires arm's-length affiliate dealings. The capital call power is not addressed directly, because a capital contribution is not a distribution; the capital replacement and TI/LC reserve obligations are the Bank's protection.
*Action.* Confirm with Borrower's counsel that the March 1, 2025 amendment did not modify the waterfall. Consider adding a covenant that capital calls may not be funded by, or used to pay, distributions or affiliate fees while the DSCR is below 1.10x. That is an addition beyond the Term Sheet (Part IV).

### G. Recourse, Guaranties and Exculpation

**G.1 — The payment guaranty covers 14 months; the interest-only period runs 24. There is an unguaranteed ten-month gap in the weakest part of the loan.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §5.5(c) (Guarantor guarantees the interest shortfall "covering the first fourteen (14) months following closing"); Term Sheet §3.9 (interest-only for the first 24 months; amortization begins with the payment due May 15, 2027); Term Sheet §7.1 (DSCR testing begins after month 24); Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule (Suite B not projected to commence rent until February 1, 2027).
*Observation.* The guaranty expires in June 2026. From July 2026 through April 2027 the Loan is interest-only, Suite B is not yet generating rent, Bay 3's renewal is unresolved, and there is no guaranty of any shortfall. The guarantee therefore expires immediately after the point of maximum construction disruption (substantial completion, June 15, 2026) and before the point of maximum interest cost with the least income. It is the reverse of the coverage a lender usually wants.
*Treatment.* The Mortgage does not restate the guaranty (correctly — the Guaranty is the operative instrument) but Section 16.1 confirms that recourse is governed by the Note, the Guaranty and the Environmental Indemnity, and Section 7.10 requires Borrower to cause Guarantor to comply. Section 14.1(f) cross-defaults the Guaranty.
*Action required — decision of the Bank.* Recommend extending the payment guaranty to at least the end of the interest-only period (month 24, April 15, 2027), and preferably through the first DSCR test. This is a credit term, requires a Term Sheet amendment and Guarantor consent, and should be raised with Marcus before the Guaranty is drafted rather than after.

**G.2 — The Term Sheet describes three guaranties and its loan-documents list names one.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §5.5(a)–(c) (non-recourse carve-out guaranty, completion guaranty, payment guaranty); Term Sheet §14.3(e) ("Guaranty Agreement"); Term Sheet Guarantor Acknowledgment ("the Guaranty Agreement, Completion Guaranty, Payment Guaranty, Environmental Indemnity Agreement"); Title Commitment Requirement 7 ("a Guaranty Agreement from Neil Ashford Graydon").
*Observation.* The Term Sheet is internally inconsistent as to whether there is one Guaranty Agreement covering three guaranties or three instruments. The title commitment requires "a Guaranty Agreement," singular.
*Treatment.* Article I of the Mortgage defines "Guaranty" collectively, as "the guaranty or guaranties of even date herewith executed by Guarantor ... comprising the Guaranty Agreement, the Completion Guaranty and the Payment Guaranty, whether executed as one or more instruments," and the definitions of "Completion Guaranty" and "Payment Guaranty" cross-refer to Term Sheet §5.5(b) and (c). This accommodates either structure and satisfies title Requirement 7 either way.
*Action.* Confirm the structure with the Bank (recommend one instrument with three articles, for closing efficiency and to satisfy Requirement 7 unambiguously) and align the loan-documents schedule.

**G.3 — The carve-out list should be conformed across the Note, the Guaranty and the Mortgage.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §5.5(a)(i)–(v) (fraud or intentional misrepresentation; misapplication of rents, deposits, insurance or condemnation proceeds; voluntary bankruptcy; environmental liability from acts or omissions; violation of transfer restrictions).
*Observation.* The five carve-outs are the only route to full recourse against Guarantor. If the Note's list differs from the Guaranty's, the Borrower and Guarantor will argue that recourse is limited to the narrower list.
*Treatment.* Section 16.1 of the Mortgage recites the five carve-outs and states that the Mortgage does not modify, limit or expand them, and that the Note controls in the event of conflict. Section 14.1(l), together with Section 7.1, ensures that an environmental breach is independently an Event of Default under the Mortgage, so the Bank is not dependent on the carve-out to reach its collateral.
*Action.* Circulate the Note and Guaranty drafts for a line-by-line conform. Also confirm the treatment of a *consent to* an involuntary petition: the Term Sheet covers a voluntary filing only, and a consent or collusive involuntary filing is the usual companion carve-out.

**G.4 — The Mortgage's non-recourse section must not accidentally create liability.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §5.5 (loan structured non-recourse to Borrower except for carve-outs).
*Observation.* A mortgage that recites that the Obligations are "secured by all of the Mortgaged Property" without a recourse limitation invites an argument that the Borrower is personally liable. Equally, a mortgage that recites exculpation could be read to override a Note provision making the Borrower liable for carve-out amounts.
*Treatment.* Section 16.1 of the Mortgage limits the statement to the structure, expressly defers to the Note and the Guaranty, and states that nothing in the Mortgage creates personal liability of any Member, manager, officer or Affiliate. Section 16.2 limits Guarantor's obligations to those in the Guaranty, the Environmental Indemnity and the Completion Guaranty, and provides that Guarantor's execution of a consent to the Mortgage does not expand them. The Guarantor's consent at the end of the Mortgage says the same thing expressly.
*Action.* Confirm the Note contains an exculpation clause consistent with this section; if the Note is silent (unlikely), the Mortgage's Section 16.1 will be the only exculpation, and it defers to a document that says nothing — a result to be avoided.

### H. Covenants, Defaults and Remedies

**H.1 — The net worth and liquidity covenants are imposed on different parties in the Term Sheet and in the markup notes.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §7.2 ("Borrower shall maintain ... a net worth of not less than $1,000,000 ... and liquid assets ... of not less than $500,000"); Kit's notes, ¶6 ("Guarantor minimum net worth of not less than $1,000,000 ... Guarantor minimum liquid assets of not less than $500,000").
*Observation.* The Term Sheet places both tests on Borrower; Kit's note places them on Guarantor. Imposing them on Guarantor would be practically meaningless: Guarantor's net worth is approximately $28,000,000 (Term Sheet §1.3), so a $1,000,000 test would never bind. Imposing them on Borrower is the operative test, and a $1,000,000 net worth floor against a $6,200,000 equity commitment is a thin constraint — Borrower could distribute $5,200,000 of equity and still comply.
*Treatment.* Section 11.2 of the Mortgage imposes both tests on Borrower, as the Term Sheet does, tested annually, with net worth determined under GAAP and excluding any value attributable to the Property above its then-appraised value (which prevents Borrower from meeting the test by revaluing its own asset).
*Action.* Confirm the Term Sheet position, and consider whether the Bank wants a higher Borrower net worth floor or a Guarantor test at a meaningful level. Both are credit decisions, not drafting decisions, and neither is in the draft.

**H.2 — The Term Sheet's DSCR default has no cure; the draft does not add one. See D.1.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §11.1(i), §11.2(b).
*Observation and treatment* are set out at D.1. Section 14.3(d) of the Mortgage provides no cure period, which is faithful to the Term Sheet and is the reason the covenant must be fixed at the Term Sheet stage rather than left to the documents.

**H.3 — Insurance defaults carry no cure period in the draft.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §11.1(f), §11.2(b) (non-monetary defaults "other than SPE defaults, transfer restriction defaults, and insurance defaults" receive 30 days, extendable to 90).
*Observation.* The Term Sheet deliberately excludes insurance defaults from the cure regime, which means a lapse is an immediate Event of Default. That is a harsh outcome for an administrative lapse (an insurer's non-renewal, a premium invoice misdirected).
*Treatment.* Section 14.3(f) of the Mortgage provides a narrow 10-business-day cure where the lapse results solely from cancellation or non-renewal by the insurer, available once during the term. This is a mild softening of the Term Sheet and is listed in Part IV.
*Action.* Confirm. If the Bank wants the Term Sheet position strictly, delete the second sentence of Section 14.3(f).

**H.4 — The transfer restrictions require three fixes: the member-transfer exception is too broad, the aggregate test has no base, and indirect transfers are not captured at all.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §10.3 (no transfer of ownership interests "exceeding ten percent (10%) individually or twenty-five percent (25%) in the aggregate ... except for transfers among the existing members of Borrower"); Operating Agreement Summary §4.1–4.5 (transfer restrictions with a 75% consent threshold, extensive Permitted Transfers, no change-of-control provision); §4.2(c) (Tidewater may transfer to "any successor fund managed by Tidewater GP Holdings LLC or any Affiliate thereof," a phrase the summary describes as "particularly broad in scope" and undefined).
*Observation.* Three problems. First, "except for transfers among the existing members" is unbounded: read literally, Tidewater could transfer its entire 18% to Harborline, or GDG could transfer to Tidewater, without consent. Second, "twenty-five percent in the aggregate" does not say over what period or measured against what base. Third, and most important, neither the Term Sheet nor the Operating Agreement restricts an *upstream* change: Guarantor could sell his membership interest in Graydon Development Group LLC (which holds the 72%) to a third party and no "transfer of an ownership interest in Borrower" would occur, because the registered holder did not change. The Operating Agreement summary says this in terms: "The Operating Agreement contains no 'change of control' provision that would capture indirect or upstream transfers."
*Treatment.* Section 9.2 of the Mortgage addresses all three:

• (i) the member-transfer exception is conditioned on four things — no change in Control, the transferee being an existing Member, ten business days' notice, and GDG continuing to hold at least 51% and to serve as Managing Member;

• (ii) "in the aggregate" is measured against the membership interests held on the Closing Date (by defining the test by reference to Transfers during the term of the Loan);

• (iii) Section 9.2(c) adds a definition of Transfer that captures indirect transfers, expressly including a transfer of Guarantor's interest in GDG, any merger or conversion of a Member, any change in Tidewater's general partner, any transfer by Tidewater to a "successor fund" under Operating Agreement §4.2(c), any change in the manager of Harborline, and the admission of a new Member. Section 9.2(c) also states that no transfer designated a "Permitted Transfer" under the Operating Agreement is effective for purposes of the Loan Documents unless made in compliance with Section 9.2.

• (iv) Section 9.2(d) preserves a genuine estate-planning carve-out for Guarantor (revocable trust, or transfer on death), because a blanket prohibition on transfers at death is not enforceable in practice and would be resisted.

*Action required.* The indirect-transfer language and the 51% floor are additions beyond the Term Sheet and are listed in Part IV. Recommend the Bank accept them: the Term Sheet's prohibition is otherwise removable by a sale of the managing member's own equity, which is the single most likely exit route for Guarantor. Also confirm whether the Operating Agreement should be amended (in the Operating Agreement Amendment) to add a change-of-control restriction so the organizational documents and the Loan Documents are consistent.

**H.5 — Completion: the deadline, the extension, the guaranty and the LUR interact.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §8.3 (Substantial Completion Deadline June 15, 2026; 14 months); Term Sheet §8.4 (10% retainage; no disbursement during an Event of Default); Term Sheet §11.1(h) (failure to achieve substantial completion is an Event of Default); Term Sheet §5.5(b) (completion guaranty); Renovation Budget, Draw Schedule (substantial completion at Draw 14, June 15, 2026).
*Observation.* The deadline is 14 months after an assumed April 15, 2025 closing. If closing slips to May 15, 2025 (the Term Sheet's outer limit), substantial completion moves to July 15, 2026 unless the Mortgage is conformed — which is why the draft defines the deadline by reference to the Closing Date with the June 15, 2026 date stated as a description. The completion guaranty is the backstop, but a completion guaranty is only as good as the guarantor's liquidity, and Guarantor's guarantee is already supporting the interest shortfall.
*Treatment.* Section 12.7 of the Mortgage defines Substantial Completion (architect's certificate plus certificate of occupancy) and ties the deadline to the Closing Date, with the June 15, 2026 date described. Section 14.1(h) makes failure an Event of Default; Section 14.3(e) permits Lender, in its sole discretion and on 30 days' prior request, to extend by up to 90 days for force majeure where Borrower has funded its share and no other Event of Default exists. Section 14.3(e) is an addition (the Term Sheet has no extension right) and is listed in Part IV.

Note that the Market has historically been unable to procure long-lead elevator equipment on schedule — the budget detail itself describes the elevator as "long-lead equipment; order placed at closing, installation months 4–9" — so a 90-day forced extension right is realistic risk allocation, not a concession of value.

**H.6 — Foreclosure mechanics: judicial only. The draft says so explicitly and avoids a common error.**

*Observation.* Template mortgages frequently include a power-of-sale provision borrowed from another state. Recording such an instrument in Charleston County creates an argument that the mortgage is a deed of trust in substance and invites a challenge to the foreclosure.

Section 15.1(b) frames foreclosure as "an action for the foreclosure of this Mortgage and the sale of the Mortgaged Property under the supervision of the court." *Action.* None. Note, however, that Section 15.5 preserves the "election-of-remedies" and "one-satisfaction" rules by reference rather than by reciting them, because the South Carolina position on a mortgagee's ability to pursue a deficiency judgment after foreclosure and on the fair-value credit is a matter we should confirm with local counsel before the first default rather than draft blind. Recommend a short South Carolina opinion at closing that covers foreclosure mechanics, the assignment of rents and the enforceability of the yield maintenance formula.

**H.7 — Late charge and default rate are consistent with the Term Sheet.**
*Source:* Term Sheet §3.7 (Default Rate = applicable rate + 5.00%); §3.11 (late charge 5% after 10 calendar days).
*Observation and treatment.* Sections 14.4 and 14.5 of the Mortgage reproduce both. Section 14.5 adds the standard recitals that the late charge is a reasonable estimate of administrative expense and not a penalty, which is the language South Carolina courts look for. The usury savings clause is at Section 17.4.

**H.8 — Lease-up reporting is an addition.**
*Source:* Not in the Term Sheet. Derived from the Appraisal §3.2 and §6.3 (ROFR delaying absorption) and the Rent Roll, Vacancy Schedule ("AGGRESSIVE TIMELINE").
