[A]III. SUMMARY OF THE DRAFT AND THE STRUCTURAL DECISIONS MADE
[P]The Mortgage is drafted as a Article II granting clause plus an integrated Article III absolute assignment of rents, followed by representations, affirmative and negative covenants, a dedicated renovation and disbursement article, a dedicated environmental article addressing the SCDHEC restriction, default and remedy articles, and five exhibits. The following structural decisions should be noted, because each departs from the firm template or resolves an ambiguity in the record:
[1]**(a) No separate loan agreement is contemplated.** The term sheet lists the Loan Documents in Section 14.3 and does not include a loan agreement. Consequently, every covenant that would ordinarily live in a loan agreement — DSCR, net worth and liquidity, reporting, SPE, transfer restrictions, leasing, reserves — has been drafted into the Mortgage. This makes the Mortgage the principal covenant instrument for the transaction, which is unusual for a loan of this size and puts significant weight on a recorded instrument. If the Bank intends to syndicate or securitize, I recommend converting those provisions to a separate Loan Agreement before closing. I have not done so without instruction.
[1]**(b) The assignment of rents is absolute, not collateral.** Article III is drafted as an unconditional and present assignment, expressly not "as additional security," with a revocable license back to Borrower in Section 3.2 and enforcement on default in Section 3.3 without a court order or receiver. This follows your instruction and the South Carolina Uniform Assignment of Rents Act. Please note the term sheet itself is permissive: Section 5.2 allows the assignment to be included in the Mortgage or executed separately, while Sections 12.1(a) and 14.3(b)–(c) list an Assignment of Leases and Rents as a document to be executed, and Section 14.3(c) refers to it being recorded "if prepared as a separate instrument." I have followed your preference and Section 5.2, and have added Section 3.8 obliging Borrower to execute a separate instrument on demand. See Issue 6.
[1]**(c) The assignment of rents is in the Mortgage but the UCC collateral is also a fixture filing.** Section 2.3 authorizes both a UCC-1 with the Secretary of State and a fixture filing with the RMC Office, consistent with your note and Requirement 4 of the title commitment.
[1]**(d) No power of sale.** Consistent with your instruction, Section 2.8 states affirmatively that no power of sale is granted and Article XIII forecloses only judicially. I have added an express acknowledgment that South Carolina does not permit power-of-sale foreclosure, which reads oddly in a commercial mortgage but is useful if the Loan is ever assigned to an out-of-state holder.
[1]**(e) The environmental obligations are article-length, not a standard covenant.** Because the title commitment's Special Instruction 1 and the Phase I ESA's recommendation 6(d) both require specific covenants addressing the SCDHEC land use restriction, a vapor barrier maintenance obligation and a default triggered by non-compliance, I have drafted Article VIII as a stand-alone article. A generic environmental compliance clause would not have satisfied either document.
[1]**(f) The representations are drafted to be true when made.** This is the one point on which I have departed from your instruction, and I have set out my reasoning in Issue 18. In short, I do not think we can draft a representation that Borrower's organizational documents contain the required SPE provisions when we know they do not; the representation would be false on its face, would be a misrepresentation to the Bank's credit file, and would itself trigger the fraud carve-out in Section 14.15. I have instead drafted the representation to speak "on and as of the Closing Date, and after giving effect to the Operating Agreement Amendment delivered at Closing," and made delivery of that amendment a condition precedent.
[A]IV. ISSUES REQUIRING INSTRUCTION BEFORE THE DRAFT IS FINALIZED
[H]**Issue 1 — The 1.25x DSCR covenant cannot be satisfied, on any projection in the file.**
[1]**Source:** Term Sheet §§7.1, 11.1(i); Appraisal §6.2–6.3; Rent Roll workbook, "Vacancy Schedule" tab.
[P]This is the single most serious problem in the transaction. The term sheet requires a DSCR of not less than 1.25x, tested quarterly from the first full calendar quarter after month 24, with a default triggered by two consecutive quarters below the covenant. I have computed the DSCR on every income figure in the file, at the note rate of 5.85% (the SOFR floor of 3.00% plus the 285 basis point spread) on $14,750,000:
[T]
[R]NOI basis | NOI | DSCR on amortizing debt service ($1,124,239/yr) | DSCR on interest-only debt service ($862,875/yr)
[R]In-place, per rent roll | $320,100 | 0.28x | 0.37x
[R]As-is, per appraisal (low–high) | $332,000 – $345,000 | 0.30x – 0.31x | 0.38x – 0.40x
[R]Stabilized, per appraisal (low–high) | $582,000 – $592,000 | 0.52x – 0.53x | 0.67x – 0.69x
[R]Stabilized, per rent roll best case | $622,772 | 0.55x | 0.72x
[P]On the stabilized best case, the NOI required to satisfy 1.25x is $1,405,299 on amortizing debt service, or $1,078,594 on interest-only debt service. The projected stabilized NOI is $587,000 at the appraisal's midpoint. Put another way, the largest loan that 1.25x DSCR would support, at the note rate and on a 25-year amortization, is approximately $6.16 million — the Loan is roughly 2.4 times the amount the covenant supports. The rent roll workbook itself reaches the same conclusion and labels it "CRITICAL SHORTFALL."
[P]The consequence for drafting is direct. The first test date (month 24–27) coincides with the end of the interest-only period and the beginning of amortization, so the covenant will be tested at the moment debt service steps up by roughly 30%. Unless the loan is restructured, the Bank will hold a document that guarantees a default — which is exactly the outcome a covenant of this kind is meant to prevent, and which a borrower's counsel will characterize as evidence that the covenant was never a genuine term.
[1]**Recommendation:** Raise with Marcus Prideaux before the draft is delivered. The options, in order of my preference, are: (i) reduce the Loan amount or require a substantial principal paydown from lease-up proceeds; (ii) extend the interest-only period to month 36 and defer DSCR testing accordingly; (iii) set the covenant at a level the asset can meet (roughly 0.90x–1.00x on interest-only debt service at stabilization) with a cash sweep or DSCR cure deposit obligation; or (iv) retain 1.25x but add a defined DSCR cure right — a cash deposit or a letter of credit in the amount of the shortfall — so that a shortfall is a cure-and-collateral event rather than an immediate default. I have not selected among these; it is a credit decision for the Bank.
[H]**Issue 2 — "Annual Debt Service" is undefined in the term sheet, and the definition determines whether the covenant is capable of performance.**
[1]**Source:** Term Sheet §7.1 ("The precise definitions of 'Net Operating Income' and 'Annual Debt Service' shall be set forth in the Loan Documents").
[P]The term sheet deliberately leaves both definitions open. I have drafted them in Section 1.1: NOI on a trailing twelve-month basis net of operating expenses but before debt service, capital expenditures, tenant improvements and leasing commissions; Annual Debt Service computed on the basis actually payable — interest-only during the Interest-Only Period and on the 25-year amortization thereafter. Those are the definitions most favorable to the Bank and they are the reason the covenant is unattainable. If the Bank prefers a covenant it can live with, the definition of Annual Debt Service is the lever: computing it on an interest-only equivalent basis raises every DSCR in the table above by about 30%.
[1]**Recommendation:** Confirm the definition with the Bank. Do not leave it to be negotiated by borrower's counsel after the other terms are settled, because it is a substantive credit term masquerading as a definitional one.
[H]**Issue 3 — Occupancy is misstated in the term sheet and the appraisal, and the error has propagated into the rent analysis.**
[1]**Source:** Term Sheet §2.3; Appraisal §1 and §3.1; Rent Roll workbook, "Summary" tab.
[P]The term sheet and the appraisal both state current occupancy as 68%. The rent roll states 56.13%, and 56.13% is correct: three of five units are occupied, comprising Bay 1 (3,800 SF), Bay 3 (3,800 SF) and Suite A (9,800 SF), for 17,400 SF of 31,000 SF. The vacancy figure of 13,600 SF (Bay 2 and Suite B) is 43.87% of the building, and the term sheet itself recites that 43.9% vacancy figure two sentences after reciting 68% occupancy — the two statements cannot both be right. The appraisal is internally inconsistent in the same way: it states "68% (21,400 SF of 31,000 SF occupied)" and "vacant area is 13,600 SF (32%)," but 31,000 minus 13,600 is 17,400, and 13,600 divided by 31,000 is 43.87%, not 32%. The 21,400 SF figure appears to be the source of the error, and it also explains the appraisal's blended rent of $19.40/SF: $415,100 divided by 21,400 SF is $19.40, whereas divided by the correct 17,400 SF it is $23.86, which is the figure the rent roll states.
[P]This is not a drafting problem, but it is not a trivial one either. It affects the LTV and loan-to-cost commentary in the Bank's credit file, the 95% stabilized occupancy assumption, the absorption timeline, and any occupancy-based mechanic we might otherwise have drafted (a guaranty burn-off, a leasing covenant benchmark, or a release price). The Mortgage does not recite occupancy as a representation; Exhibit D states the correct 56.13% and 17,400 SF. I recommend the appraisal be corrected by addendum before it is placed in the credit file, and that the Bank's underwriting summary be conformed.
[1]**Recommendation:** Ask Pinnacle for a corrected addendum; confirm to Marcus that the occupancy in the appraisal is overstated by 4,000 SF; correct the term sheet file copy (the executed term sheet itself cannot be amended, but the credit file should carry a memo).
[H]**Issue 4 — The yield maintenance formula is ambiguous on four variables, and your suggested formula does not match the term sheet.**
[1]**Source:** Term Sheet §§4.1–4.3; your markup notes, Section 5.
[P]The term sheet's formula is a "greater of" structure: (a) 1.00% of the outstanding principal balance at prepayment, or (b) the present value of the excess of interest at the note rate over interest at treasury plus 50 basis points, discounted at the treasury rate. You identified four open variables: the Treasury tenor, interpolation, the discount rate, and the treatment of a zero or negative result. I have resolved each in Section 4.5 in the manner you suggested — the Security shall be the H.15 yield to maturity of the Treasury issue maturing closest to but not exceeding the end of the Yield Maintenance Period, with linear interpolation, discounted monthly at the Treasury Rate divided by twelve, and a floor at the 1.00% minimum.
[P]Two points require your attention before it goes to the Bank. *First*, your suggested formula and the term sheet are not the same formula. Yours computes the present value of all remaining scheduled payments of principal and interest minus the principal prepaid; the term sheet computes a present-value interest differential against treasuries plus 50 basis points. The two produce different numbers in a rising rate environment — yours can be zero where the term sheet's is not, and vice versa — and the term sheet is the executed, binding document. I have followed the term sheet and used your language only for the defined variables. *Second*, your instruction that the premium shall "in no event be less than zero" is inconsistent with the term sheet's 1.00% minimum, which makes a zero result impossible. I have drafted the 1.00% minimum as controlling and retained the "not less than zero" sentence as a belt-and-braces statement that cannot operate to reduce the minimum. If you intended the zero floor to override the 1.00%, we need to say so expressly and explain to the Bank why we are giving up a bargained-for term.
[P]On the question you flagged — whether the 50 basis points is added to the loan rate or to the treasury rate — the term sheet is clear that it is added to the treasury rate ("the interest that would accrue on such prepaid principal amount at the treasury rate plus fifty (50) basis points"). Your note suggested the alternative. I have followed the term sheet. This should still be confirmed with Marcus, because if the Bank's intent was the other reading, the premium is materially larger and Borrower's counsel will resist a change after execution.
[1]**Also flagged:** the term sheet says the Loan "may not be prepaid" during the lockout (Section 4.1) but then says "any prepayment made in violation of this restriction shall be subject to a prepayment premium as described in Section 4.2" — which contemplates that such a prepayment can happen. I have drafted the lockout as an absolute prohibition with a right in Lender to refuse a tender, and a premium if Lender accepts one (Sections 4.3 and 4.7). I have also followed your instruction that casualty and condemnation proceeds are applied at par without premium; the term sheet does not address it, and you asked me to confirm it with the client. **This remains an open item.**
[H]**Issue 5 — The Guaranty is internally inconsistent, and the interest carry does not reach the DSCR problem.**
[1]**Source:** Term Sheet §5.5(a)–(c).
[P]Section 5.5 is headed "full recourse personal guaranty" and then provides in (a) that "the Loan shall be structured as non-recourse to Borrower, except for certain 'carve-out' events." Those are different instruments, and the term sheet does not reconcile them. The Mortgage does not characterize the Guaranty's recourse (Section 14.15 describes the Borrower-level carve-outs and defers to the Guaranty Agreement for Guarantor), which keeps the Mortgage neutral. The Guaranty drafter needs a decision.
[P]Separately, Section 5.5(c) is a payment guaranty covering "any shortfall in debt service payments during the renovation and initial lease-up period, covering the first fourteen (14) months following closing." Fourteen months takes the guaranty to about June 2026 — before substantial completion, before the first DSCR test, and before amortization begins. On the figures in Issue 1, the interest shortfall is not a 14-month problem: in-place NOI of about $320,000 against annual interest of $862,875 leaves a shortfall of roughly $543,000 per year for as long as the vacancy persists, and stabilized NOI of about $587,000 still leaves a shortfall of roughly $276,000 per year even after full lease-up. The interest carry is therefore calibrated to the wrong period.
[1]**Recommendation:** If the Bank wants a guaranty that supports the loan while the property leases up, the interest carry should run from closing through the first DSCR test date (at least month 27, i.e., roughly 33 months), and should be measured against actual debt service including amortization, or converted into a DSCR guaranty. I have not drafted that; it is a Guaranty term, and I will draft it on instruction.

[A]V. TITLE, COLLATERAL AND LEGAL DESCRIPTION
[H]**Issue 8 — Title is vested in Hampstead Village Holdings LLC, not Borrower; the deed must be recorded before the Mortgage.**
[1]**Source:** Title Commitment, Schedule A §3 and Schedule B, Part I, Requirement 2(a); Term Sheet §2.1 and Exhibit A.
[P]The title commitment shows fee simple title vested in Hampstead Village Holdings LLC by virtue of a general warranty deed recorded September 14, 2018 in Deed Book 1095 at Page 322. Title passes to Borrower only on recording of the deed contemplated by Requirement 2(a). The Mortgage must therefore be executed and recorded by Borrower after the deed is recorded — and the recording sequence matters, because a mortgage given by a person who is not yet the record owner creates a priority and enforceability question that the title insurer will not resolve for us. The Mortgage recites the acquisition in Recital B and represents in Section 5.3 that Borrower "will, upon recording of the deed described in Recital B and immediately prior to the recording of this Mortgage, be the sole fee simple owner" — a formulation that is true when made and at closing. Practical steps: close the acquisition and the financing as a simultaneous transaction with a single disbursement, instruct Prestige Title in writing on the recording sequence (deed, then Mortgage, then any separate assignment, then the fixture filing), and obtain a gap indemnity or an escrow arrangement for the interval.

[1]**(a) Registered agent name.** The term sheet names "Capitol Registered Agents Inc." at 1201 Main Street, Suite 410, Columbia; the operating agreement summary names "Statehouse Registered Agents, Inc." at the same address. Confirm against the Secretary of State filing, because a default notice misaddressed to the agent is a notice problem in a foreclosure.
[1]**(b) Harborline's principals.** The operating agreement summary states that the members and managers of Harborline Capital Advisors LLC (10%) have not been disclosed. Borrower has represented in Section 5.16 that no member is an OFAC-listed person, but the Bank cannot verify that representation for an undisclosed 10% owner. Obtain a beneficial ownership certification for all three members and the Guarantor.
[1]**(c) Tidewater GP authority.** Tidewater GP Holdings LLC must sign the Operating Agreement Amendment on behalf of Tidewater Opportunity Fund II LP. Obtain a general partner authorization certificate or a resolution, since the limited partnership agreement is not in the file.
[1]**(d) Audited financial statements.** Section 7.3(a) of the term sheet requires annual audited statements within 120 days; the operating agreement requires only reviewed statements within 90 days (Section 6.2(b)). A single-asset LLC will find audited statements expensive and Borrower's counsel will ask for reviewed-with-certification. Section 6.6 follows the term sheet. If the Bank will accept reviewed statements, say so before the draft circulates, because it is easier to concede now than after execution. Note also that the two regimes have different due dates (120 days versus 90 days).
[1]**(e) Net worth and liquidity covenant obligor.** Term sheet Section 7.2 imposes the $1,000,000 net worth and $500,000 liquidity covenants on **Borrower**; your notes describe them as Guarantor covenants. I have followed the term sheet (Section 6.5(b)–(c)). Applied to Borrower, the net worth covenant is nearly meaningless — Borrower's assets are the Property at roughly $20,950,000 against $14,750,000 of debt, so net worth is approximately $6.2 million at closing and the covenant is satisfied with a $5 million cushion. I suspect the covenant was intended for Guarantor. Recommend adding a Guarantor net worth covenant (in the Guaranty, tested annually against the $28,000,000 stated net worth) and leaving the Borrower covenant as drafted or deleting it.
[1]**(f) Construction contract consent.** Section 9.12 requires a collateral assignment of the Beaufort Builders Group LLC contract and the contractor's consent. AIA-form contracts commonly prohibit assignment without consent; obtain the consent now rather than at the first draw.
[1]**(g) Notices.** Section 14.4 lists Lender, Borrower, Guarantor and copies to counsel. The firm's street address must be inserted for the "prepared by" statement on the first page before recording; South Carolina requires the preparer's name and address on a recorded instrument, and we have not been given a Charleston street address for the firm.
[1]**(h) Title commitment term.** The commitment is effective February 28, 2025 and expires August 28, 2025 (Conditions of Commitment ¶2). The commitment term sheet expires May 15, 2025. Both dates sit inside the expected closing window, but the six-week gap between the effective date and closing means the Bank should obtain a date-down endorsement at closing and a gap indemnity — which Requirement 12 contemplates and Section 6.8 requires.
[1]**(i) Flood map discrepancy.** The appraisal cites FEMA panel 45019C0418J effective November 4, 2014; the Phase I cites panel 45019C0520J effective December 4, 2020. Both conclude Zone X, so the conclusion is unaffected, but the Mortgage should rely on neither: Section 5.15 represents Zone X status generally, and Section 11.3 requires $5,000,000 of flood insurance regardless of zone, as the term sheet requires. Obtain a current flood determination certificate for the file.
[1]**(j) Jury waiver.** Section 14.3 contains a jury trial waiver. Its enforceability in South Carolina is not beyond doubt; it is drafted prominently and separately (as it must be to have any chance), but the Bank should not treat it as a substitute for the receivership and rents remedies, which are its real protection.
[1]**(k) Appraisal as-is NOI.** The appraisal states as-is NOI of $332,000–$345,000 but the figure cannot be reproduced from its own inputs: $415,100 in-place rent less 5% vacancy/credit loss less $85,000–$95,000 of landlord operating expenses yields $299,345–$309,345. The stabilized NOI range ($582,000–$592,000) does reproduce ($728,400 less 5% less $100,000–$110,000 = $581,980–$591,980). The as-is figure should be corrected by the addendum requested in Issue 3; it is another instance of the occupancy error propagating.
