## 3. Executive summary and top-risk register

3.1 The transaction is financeable, but it cannot be documented and closed as the record currently stands. The executed Term Sheet is internally inconsistent on loan sizing (it states a maximum 80% loan-to-cost and a maximum 65% as-completed loan-to-value, and then sets a loan amount that breaches both), and the Borrower's capital structure contains no genuine cash equity: the sources and uses presented to the Bank funds 100% of a $56,700,000 budget — including the $8,200,000 land cost — with $56,700,000 of debt. Five further items are closing conditions in substance rather than form: an unsatisfied $3,400,000 prior deed of trust for which there is no payoff figure and no source of funds in the budget; an environmental use restriction that appears to be inconsistent with the residential program and a required deed notice that appears not to have been recorded; a mechanical defect in the draw schedule that prevents the stated 10% retainage from being withheld and released; an interest reserve that is underfunded on the Bank's own current-rate assumption; and a membership-interest pledge that is promised to two lenders at once.

## 6. Detailed issues and recommended resolutions

### A. Loan sizing and the capital structure

#### ISSUE 1 — The Loan breaches the Term Sheet's loan-to-cost covenant (High).

*Source.* Term Sheet §§3.1 (loan amount $47,500,000) and 6.1 (LTC not to exceed 80% of Total Project Costs, stated to be $56,700,000); CM §§5.1, 5.3 and 6.1 (identifies an 83.77% LTC and a $2,140,000 shortfall).

*Analysis.* $47,500,000 ÷ $56,700,000 = 83.7743%. The Bank executed a term sheet containing a covenant that its own loan amount breaches. The difficulty is not the identification of the problem (the CM identifies it) but the proposed cure: the Bank's condition is a $2,140,000 cash deposit into a Bank-controlled equity escrow "applied to project costs first, ahead of loan advances, to bring the effective LTC into compliance" (CM §§5.3, 10.1.2). Depositing cash does not change either the numerator or the denominator of the ratio. The cure works only if the amount advanced under the senior loan is correspondingly reduced, either by permanently reducing the commitment to $45,360,000 or by making 80% of Total Project Costs a borrowing-base limitation on outstanding principal. We recommend the latter, because it preserves the commitment if approved change orders increase Total Project Costs (in which case the equity requirement falls pro rata), and it also converts the covenant in Term Sheet §6.1 into an operable condition to each advance rather than an annual certification.

A second point on the definition: Term Sheet §6.1 defines Total Project Costs as the aggregate of all costs in the budget, which includes the $4,850,000 interest reserve and the $475,000 origination fee. Treating financing costs as "project costs" for LTC purposes is generous. If they are excluded, the base is $51,375,000, the 80% amount is $41,100,000, and the equity requirement rises from $2,140,000 to $6,400,000. The Bank should confirm its policy treatment before the Draft is finalised; the definition is outcome-determinative and is flagged at Draft §8.1.

*Recommendation.* (a) Confirm the LTC base (whether the interest reserve and financing fees are included). (b) Adopt the borrowing-base mechanic at Draft §2.1(b) so that outstanding principal may never exceed 80% of Total Project Costs. (c) Require the $2,140,000 Equity Deposit at Draft §4.8, allocated to hard and soft costs only. (d) Alternatively, reduce the commitment to $45,360,000 and conform the Note, deed of trust, title policy amount, origination fee (1.00% × $45,360,000 = $453,600), exit fee and draw schedule.

#### ISSUE 2 — As-completed loan-to-value breaches the Term Sheet's 65% ceiling (High).

*Source.* Term Sheet §4 ("The Lender's maximum loan-to-value ratio shall not exceed 65% on an as-completed basis") and §6.2 (65% as-completed and as-stabilized); CM §6.1 (ratio table applying "Bank Policy: Max 70%" and marking both LTV ratios "Pass").

*Analysis.* $47,500,000 ÷ $72,800,000 = 65.2473%. The Term Sheet's ceiling is 65.00%; the excess is 0.2473 points, or $180,000 (65% of $72,800,000 = $47,320,000). The CM does not test this ratio against the Term Sheet: its ratio table applies a 70% policy maximum and reports a pass. Two possibilities exist and the record does not resolve them: either the Bank's 70% construction policy governs and Term Sheet §§4 and 6.2 contain a drafting error, or the 65% ceiling governs and the Loan amount is wrong. #### ISSUE 4 — The equity escrow, if applied to the developer fee, is circular (High).

*Source.* CM §§5.3 and 10.1.2 (condition of a $2,140,000 equity escrow "applied to project costs first"); Draw Schedule, Detailed Budget line 5.01 (developer fee $2,835,000, of which $1,835,000 senior and $1,000,000 mezzanine); Term Sheet §14 (distributions limited to the developer fee).

#### ISSUE 5 — The developer fee base and funding (Medium).

*Source.* Term Sheet §5.2 and Appraisal §3 (developer fee 5.0% "of total project cost" = $2,835,000); Draw Schedule line 5.01.

*Analysis.* $2,835,000 ÷ $56,700,000 = 5.00%, but the base includes the fee itself, the $4,850,000 interest reserve and the $1,440,000 contingency. ### B. Mezzanine debt, intercreditor and sponsor control

#### ISSUE 6 — The 100% membership-interest pledge is promised twice (High).

*Source.* Term Sheet §8 (senior collateral includes a pledge of 100% of membership interests by all members); Term Sheet §1.4 and CM §4.4 (the mezzanine loan is "secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests in the Borrower"); CM §9.4 (identifies the conflict); Org Chart §2.5 (operating agreement §9.1 permits a pledge with the Managing Member's approval and does not address competing pledges).

*Analysis.* Only one lender can hold a first-priority security interest in the same collateral, and the operating agreement contains no mechanism to rank two pledges of the same interests. The CM's recommended resolution — an intercreditor agreement giving the Bank first priority and the mezzanine lender a subordinated second — is correct. Two mechanical points are missing from the record and are addressed in the Draft: (a) the interests are uncertificated and the operating agreement does not elect Article 8, so a pledgee cannot obtain "control" and foreclosure on the interests would be a UCC Article 9 sale with notice and valuation risk; the Draft requires certificated interests, an Article 8 election, delivery of the certificates and undated transfer instruments (Draft §9.6(b)); and (b) the pledge to the mezzanine lender already exists, so its subordination — not merely its granting — must be documented, and Brightleaf Investors Group LLC (through Mr. Chen, who controls both entities) must consent.

*Recommendation.* Make the intercreditor agreement, with the terms listed at Draft §13.2, a non-waivable condition to the first advance; require the operating agreement amendment and member consents at Draft §§3.2 and 9.6(e).

#### ISSUE 7 — Pay-in-kind mezzanine interest is not analysed (Medium).

*Source.* CM §4.4 (mezzanine "interest accrues and is paid-in-kind during construction; cash pay during mini-perm"); Term Sheet §1.4 (silent on payment mechanics); Org Chart §1.2.

*Analysis.* At 12.50% payable in kind for 30 months, $9,200,000 accretes to approximately $12,554,600. Combined debt at construction maturity would be $60,054,600 against an as-completed value of $72,800,000 — combined leverage of 82.49%, above the 80% level the Bank uses for the senior loan alone, and increasing to approximately $13,300,000 of mezzanine by the mini-perm maturity. The CM does not test combined leverage and the Term Sheet does not regulate mezzanine payments.

*Recommendation.* Confirm the payment mechanic in writing; cap capitalised interest; prohibit any mezzanine payment while a senior default exists or after acceleration; test combined LTV at each advance; and permit the mezzanine lender to cure a senior default only from new money. Draft §§7.10 and 13.3 do so.

#### ISSUE 8 — The 75% supermajority gives the mezzanine lender's affiliate a veto over the Bank's exit (High).

*Source.* Org Chart §2.2 (operating agreement §3.2: sale of substantially all assets, any financing over $1,000,000, admission of a member, amendment and voluntary bankruptcy all require 75% approval); Org Chart §2.2 (analysis showing Ms. Vasquez-Torres 62% + Mr. Osei 10% = 72%, below the threshold, so Brightleaf Investors Group LLC (28%) holds an effective veto).

*Analysis.* The affiliate of the mezzanine lender can therefore block any refinancing over $1,000,000 and any sale of the Property — precisely the events the Bank relies on for repayment at the mini-perm maturity. This is a more serious structural issue than the pledge conflict because it does not depend on any default; it operates in the ordinary course. It also complicates the independent-manager mechanism: an independent manager protects against a bad-faith bankruptcy filing but does not cure a member-level veto over refinancing.

*Recommendation.* Amend the operating agreement before closing to (a) carve out the refinancing and repayment of the senior loan from the 75% list (Draft §13.4), (b) require independent-manager approval for insolvency filings (Draft §6.9(l)), and (c) add the Bank's written consent right over any further amendment to the major-decision provisions (Draft §7.5(a)).

#### ISSUE 9 — Two members hold their interests without cash contribution, and there are no SPE covenants (High in combination).

*Source.* Org Chart §2.3 (operating agreement §5.1(b): Brightleaf Investors Group LLC receives 28% for a $0 contribution "in consideration of its commitment to arrange third-party mezzanine financing"; §5.1(c): Mr. Osei receives 10% for $0 "in consideration of development management services"); Org Chart §1.1 (the operating agreement "does not currently include provisions for an independent director or independent manager, nor does it contain separateness covenants"); Term Sheet §20 (SPE covenants); CM §§2.1, 9.3, 10.1.4 (SPE amendment, independent manager and non-consolidation opinion required).

*Recommendation.* Make the operating agreement amendment, the independent manager, the member resolutions and the non-consolidation opinion closing conditions (Draft §§3.2, 3.10, 6.9), and take a certificate as to historical separateness practice so the Bank knows whether it is receiving a true representation or a qualified one.

*Source.* Phase I ESA §2.2 and §3.3 (a 2,000-gallon diesel tank, installed approximately 1972, decommissioned approximately 1998; no closure records found — a data gap); Appraisal §8 (a tank "originally installed in the 1950s ... used for the storage of heating oil ... not in active use since approximately 1988"); Phase I ESA §6.1 (approximately 1,200 cubic yards excavated); Appraisal §8 (approximately 320 cubic yards); CM §8.2 (approximately 340 cubic yards); Phase I ESA §11 (aggregate consultant liability limited to the approximately $12,500 fee); Phase I ESA §9 (AAI shelf life 180 days, expiring April 26, 2025); Phase I ESA opening page (the Lender's address is given as "200 South Tryon Street" rather than 215).

None of these conflicts changes the regulatory conclusion (the NFA letter governs), but they bear on the reliability of the data gap described at ESA §3.3 and on any pollution legal liability underwriting. The liability cap means the report offers no meaningful recourse if the delineation proves wrong. The 180-day AAI shelf life is comfortable for a February 28, 2025 closing but will lapse if closing is delayed beyond April 26, 2025.

*Recommendation.* Obtain the Phase II report and the remediation completion report; confirm the tank facts with the consultant; obtain an updated reliance letter running to the Bank and its successors, assigns and participants; record the AAI expiry in the closing checklist; and price a pollution legal liability policy (Phase I ESA Recommendation 6) for consideration by the Bank and the Borrower.

### F. Construction, disbursement and the interest reserve

#### ISSUE 22 — The interest reserve is underfunded on the Bank's own rate assumption (High).

*Source.* Term Sheet §10 ($4,850,000); CM §6.2 (concludes the reserve is "tight ... at the lower boundary of adequacy", estimates a $565,000 shortfall at 4.35% SOFR and $1,278,000 at +100 bps); Draw Schedule, Interest Reserve Analysis (concludes "APPEARS ADEQUATE" with a $221,325 surplus, on an assumed 3.50% SOFR).

*Analysis.* The two analyses reach opposite conclusions because they assume different SOFR levels, and the Draw Schedule's assumption (3.50% when the current rate is approximately 4.35%) is 85 basis points below current market. Applying a monthly model to the schedule's own draw pattern, the average outstanding balance is $27,429,167; total interest at an all-in 6.75% is $4,628,672 (reproducing the Draw Schedule exactly), at 7.60% it is $5,211,542 (a $361,542 shortfall), and at 8.60% it is $5,897,271 (a $1,047,271 shortfall). The CM's $565,000 figure uses a $28,500,000 average balance and overstates the shortfall by approximately $203,000, but its conclusion — that the reserve is inadequate at current rates — is right and the Draw Schedule's conclusion is wrong. The Draw Schedule's sensitivity table also does not tie to its own monthly model (its 4.35% row states $5,215,830 against $5,211,542 on a recalculation, a rounding artefact of about $4,300).

Two further points. The reserve is funded from loan proceeds, so an increase in the reserve increases the loan requirement (and the LTC breach) unless offset by equity. And the Term Sheet contemplates quarterly adequacy testing (§10) whereas the CM requires testing "at each draw request" (§6.2) — the stricter standard should govern.

*Recommendation.* (a) Size the reserve at a stressed rate: $5,500,000 to $5,900,000 covers 8.00% to 8.60% all-in. (b) If the reserve remains at $4,850,000, adopt the replenishment obligation (funding within five business days), the adequacy test at each advance, and the monthly reconciliation (Draft §§2.10(c)–(f), 4.2(g), 10.16). (c) Confirm which party bears the shortfall: the Carry Guaranty covers interest after the reserve is exhausted, but a guarantor is a backstop, not a source of construction-period liquidity.

#### ISSUE 23 — The draw schedule does not reconcile and misapplies the retainage (High).

*Source.* Term Sheet §9.1 (10% retainage on all hard cost disbursements) and §9.2 (draw table); CM §7.1 (table; reconciliation statement); Draw Schedule, Draw Schedule tab.

*Analysis.* Four defects. First, the draws labelled 2 to 8 are stated in gross hard cost amounts (15%, 20%, 20%, 15%, 10%, 10% and 10% of the guaranteed maximum price) and cumulatively total $34,750,000 — 100% of hard costs — despite the 10% retainage column; the net column shows the retainage being withheld, which is inconsistent with the gross draw amount funded. Second, the ninth draw of $3,275,000 is described as the retainage release, but 10% of $34,750,000 is $3,475,000 and $3,275,000 is 9.42%; the remaining undrawn commitment of $3,275,000 cannot fund a $3,475,000 release, and the final draw's own components (retainage $3,475,000 + developer fee $200,000 + contingency $600,000) total $4,275,000 against a stated $3,275,000. Third, the schedule's column components total $48,500,000 against a $47,500,000 commitment — an over-allocation of exactly $1,000,000, which corresponds to the developer fee that the Detailed Budget allocates to the mezzanine loan. Fourth, the developer fee and contingency are allocated across the draw rows in the amounts of $923,750 and $918,750 respectively, rather than the $2,835,000 and $1,440,000 in the uses of funds, so most of the fee and the whole of the contingency have no funding source in the schedule. In substance, the schedule advances $38,225,000 to hard costs against a $34,750,000 guaranteed maximum price.

*Recommendation.* Replace the schedule with a reconciled version on a net-of-retainage basis; this is done at Draft Schedule 1.2, which preserves the milestone percentages and gross amounts from the Borrower's schedule but (a) advances 90% of each milestone ($31,275,000 in aggregate), (b) releases $3,475,000 of retainage as the final advance on the Draw 9 conditions, (c) funds the developer fee ($1,835,000) and contingency ($1,440,000) as separately controlled budget lines, and (d) reconciles to the commitment net of the $2,140,000 Equity Deposit ($45,360,000). The Borrower should confirm the revised amounts.

#### ISSUE 24 — Construction maturity precedes completion, and the final advance falls after maturity (Medium).

*Source.* Term Sheet §3.2 (construction maturity August 28, 2027); Term Sheet §2 and Appraisal §3 (projected substantial completion August 31, 2027); Draw Schedule (draw 8 at substantial completion in August 2027; draw 9 in October 2027); CM §9.6 (identifies the three-day gap).

*Analysis.* The construction maturity date is three days before projected substantial completion, so any slippage triggers a maturity default before the Borrower has received the final draw. The final draw is scheduled for October 2027 — after maturity, in the mini-perm period. In addition, the Term Sheet defines the periods as running "from the date of closing" but then fixes the maturity dates as though closing occurs on February 28, 2025; if closing slips, the Borrower loses construction time.

*Recommendation.* Define the construction maturity date as 30 months after the actual closing date, subject to an outside date; align the Completion Guaranty date and the extension mechanics to the same definition; and expressly permit the final advance during the mini-perm period subject to the Draw 9 conditions and no default. Draft §§2.13, 4.4, 6.1 and Schedule 1.2 note 2 do so.

#### ISSUE 25 — Contingency, "single advance" language and unbudgeted carrying costs (Medium).

*Source.* CM §5.2 (contingency of $1,440,000 "approximately 4.1% of hard construction costs"); Draw Schedule lines 6.01 and 6.02 (hard cost contingency $1,140,000; soft cost contingency $300,000); Term Sheet §3.1 ("structured as a single advance facility"); Term Sheet §3.4 (unused line fee, exit fee) and §19 (insurance); Title Commitment Exs. 7 and 11(b) (2024 taxes of $49,065.56; CC&R assessments).

*Analysis.* The hard-cost contingency is 3.28%, not 4.14%; the 4.14% figure includes soft-cost contingency. For a 30-month, Type III-A wood-frame-over-podium project, 3.28% on top of a guaranteed maximum price is thin, and the completion guaranty is the real cushion. The "single advance facility" description contradicts the milestone draw mechanics and should be removed. Finally, several recurring costs have no budget line and must be funded by the Borrower: the unused line fee at 0.25% per annum on the undrawn balance (approximately $187,500 over the term at a $30,000,000 average undrawn balance), the exit fee if the Loan is repaid during construction ($237,500), the extension fee (0.25% of the then outstanding balance, approximately $118,750 at the current size), construction-period real property taxes (2024 combined: $49,065.56, rising as improvements are assessed), the CC&R assessment (approximately $34,500 per annum) and audit fees under the reporting covenant.

*Recommendation.* Correct the contingency description; delete "single advance facility" (Draft §2.1(c)); and confirm the Borrower has budgeted the carrying costs outside the Approved Budget, or add a carrying-cost reserve. The Carry Guaranty is the backstop but, again, a guaranty is not liquidity.

### G. Financial covenants and reporting

#### ISSUE 26 — The Borrower net worth covenant is unachievable as drafted (High).

*Source.* Term Sheet §6.4 (minimum net worth $12,000,000; current net worth "approximately $14,200,000, based on financial statements provided to the Lender"); CM §2.1 (the same figure, said to provide "a cushion of approximately $2,200,000 above the covenant minimum"); Org Chart §1.1 ("current net worth of the Borrower entity is approximately $14,200,000 based on the book value of its assets and liabilities"); Title Commitment Sch. A ¶5 (the Borrower's principal asset is the land, subject to the Triangle Commercial Bank deed of trust).

*Analysis.* Two problems. First, the $14,200,000 figure is not supported by any balance sheet in the record: an entity whose only asset is land acquired for $8,200,000 and encumbered by a $3,400,000 deed of trust cannot have $14,200,000 of book net worth unless the land is carried far above cost. Second, and independently, the covenant fails on the first test date. Immediately after closing the Borrower's assets are the land at $8,200,000 plus construction in progress funded by the advancing loan, and its liabilities are the loan and the mezzanine loan; at full draw both sides of the balance sheet are $56,700,000 and book equity is nil, rising to approximately $2,140,000 only because of the Equity Deposit. A $12,000,000 covenant therefore cannot be met at any time during construction on a book basis.

*Recommendation.* The Bank must choose: (a) retain the covenant but define Net Worth on a fair-value basis (including the Property at its most recent Appraised Value), as drafted in Draft §1.1; (b) reset the threshold to an achievable level (for example $2,000,000 plus cumulative equity contributions); or (c) replace the covenant with a sponsor-support and equity-funding covenant tested at each advance. Draft §8.4 is bracketed accordingly. The Borrower's actual financial statements must be obtained and reviewed before any of these is settled.

#### ISSUE 27 — DSCR test mechanics are not workable at the first test date (Medium).

*Source.* Term Sheet §6.3 (minimum 1.25x, tested "at the commencement of the Mini-Perm Period and annually thereafter based on actual trailing twelve-month net operating income"); CM §11(c) (extension test at 1.15x on trailing three-month annualised NOI).

*Analysis.* At the commencement of the mini-perm period the Project will have just reached substantial completion and will have no twelve-month operating history, so the stated test cannot be performed on its own terms. The Term Sheet also does not define NOI, whether replacement reserves and the CC&R assessment are deducted, or how interest is computed for the test (contract rate versus stressed rate).

*Recommendation.* Adopt a two-stage test: a projected test at the commencement of the mini-perm period using the Appraisal's concluded stabilized NOI, and a trailing test once twelve months of history exist (with a trailing three-month annualised test in the interim), together with a defined NOI (Draft §1.1 deducts a $250 per unit replacement reserve and the CC&R assessment) and the cure mechanics in Draft §8.3(b).

*Source and analysis.* (a) Term Sheet §5.1 records "Borrower Equity (land previously acquired; see Uses below) $0" while §5.2 records the $8,200,000 land cost as a use — a self-contradiction that is the arithmetic root of Issue 3. (d) Term Sheet §15 contains no cure period for a breach of the single-purpose entity covenant even though the same section provides cure periods for lesser breaches.

## 9. Additional observations for counsel

9.1 **North Carolina foreclosure and guaranty enforcement.** The CM's reference to North Carolina as a "judicial foreclosure state" should be corrected: foreclosures proceed by trustee's power of sale under Article 2C of Chapter 45 following a clerk's order under N.C.G.S. § 45-21.16, and a deficiency is determined with the benefit of the fair-value analysis in N.C.G.S. § 45-21.36. The Draft preserves each Guarantor's inability to waive non-waivable protections (Draft §§11.2, 11.10) while confirming that the guaranty obligations are independent (Draft §9.9). We should confirm with North Carolina counsel before the guaranty forms are finalised.

9.2 **Default rate and Chapter 24.** The Default Rate could reach approximately SOFR + 8.50% per annum. The Draft includes a standard usury savings clause (Draft §2.16); North Carolina counsel should confirm the applicable maximum for a loan of this size and type before the Note is issued.

9.3 **Interest reserve and the Carry Guaranty overlap.** The Carry Guaranty and the interest reserve cover the same cost at different times. The documentation should make clear that the guarantors are not released by the existence of the reserve, that the reserve is not a cap on the guaranty, and that replenishment obligations run to the Borrower first and the guarantors second (Draft §§2.10(d), 9.7(b)).

9.4 **Wet vs dry closing.** Nothing in the record addresses whether the Bank will fund at a table-funding closing or on a disbursement basis with a pass-through of the Triangle Commercial Bank payoff. If the title company will hold funds, the closing instructions should reflect the equity-first requirement (Draft §4.8) so that the Equity Deposit is applied before any loan proceeds are released.

9.5 **Assignment and participation.** If the Bank intends to participate or syndicate the loan, the reliance letters, the non-consolidation opinion and the environmental report should be addressed to successors, assigns and participants, and the participation provisions should be agreed now rather than at the point of sale (Draft §§3.4(b), 3.10, 14.1, 14.5).
