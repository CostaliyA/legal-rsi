## Approach

1. **Inventory and full read.** All seven source documents were read in full (term sheet, credit memorandum, appraisal summary, title commitment, Phase I ESA excerpt, organization materials, construction budget/draw schedule).
2. **Independent recomputation.** Every stated total, ratio, date interval, acreage and draw amount was recomputed from its components, including a second month-by-month interest-reserve model built from the draw schedule's own timing and an actual/360 day count.
3. **Cross-document reconciliation.** Each material proposition was traced to its source and checked against every other source that speaks to the same fact; the results are recorded in the conflict matrix in Section 4 of the memorandum.
4. **Drafting from the verified record.** Where the record supports a term, the agreement states it. Where the record is silent or internally inconsistent, the agreement either adopts the Term Sheet position with a bracketed drafting note referencing the memorandum item, or leaves a bracketed election.

## Principal findings

The headline arithmetic in the record is sound, but several documents describe a different transaction from the one the Lender believes it is making:

- **Loan-to-cost.** At $47,500,000 / $56,700,000 the ratio is 83.77%, above the Term Sheet's 80% covenant — and the Credit Memorandum's prescribed $2,140,000 equity escrow cannot cure it, because adding a source of funds changes neither the numerator nor the denominator. Only a reduction of the Commitment to $45,360,000 (or $2,675,000 of verified additional cost) produces compliance.
- **Membership interest pledge.** Section 9.1 of the Operating Agreement makes any transfer without 75% member consent "null and void," so the Lender's foreclosure remedy could be defeated; the 28% member controlled by the mezzanine principal also holds an effective veto over refinancing.
- **Existing deed of trust.** The $3,400,000 Triangle Commercial Bank lien must be released at closing, but there is no payoff figure, no payoff letter, no line item in the uses of funds, and no cash equity to pay it.
- **Mechanic's lien priority.** Demolition and remediation occurred in 2024, before the Deed of Trust will be recorded, so liens may relate back ahead of it under N.C.G.S. § 44A-10.
- **Environmental.** The NCDEQ no-further-action letter is conditional; the deed notice was due November 29, 2024 and does not appear in the December 5, 2024 title commitment, and the non-residential use restriction conflicts with residential units above the affected area. If 14 units are lost, the DSCR falls to 1.21x and the LTV to 67.56%, breaching both covenants.
- **Parcel geometry.** The title descriptions (2.09 + 1.38 acres, Parcel 2 south of Parcel 1) do not match the environmental report (1.89 + 1.58 acres, "western" and "eastern" parcels).
- **Draw schedule.** The nine draws total $47,500,000, but the hard-cost column totals $38,225,000 against a $34,750,000 GMP (retainage counted twice), Draw 9 releases $3,275,000 against a $3,475,000 holdback, the developer fee and contingency are under-allocated by $1,432,500, and no retainage is in fact withheld. Exhibit D restates the schedule in a form that reconciles.
- **Guaranties, LTV and covenants.** The partial repayment guaranty is described in two different amounts; the as-completed LTV of 65.25% exceeds the Term Sheet's own 65% limit although the Credit Memorandum tests it against a 70% policy; and the guarantor net-worth default cross-refers to a threshold that does not exist.
