Section 1: Executive Summary

Harborview Capital Partners LLC (the "Borrower") has requested a $47,500,000 construction-to-mini-permanent loan from Pinnacle Savings Bank, N.A. (the "Bank") for the ground-up construction of "The Piedmont at Brightleaf," a 212-unit Class A mixed-use residential and retail development located at 601 and 615 Foster Street, Durham, Durham County, North Carolina 27701. The subject property comprises two parcels (Parcel IDs 0821-04-72-3341 and 0821-04-72-4102) with a combined land area of approximately 3.47 acres. The site is currently improved as vacant land, following the demolition of former tobacco warehouse structures in August 2024 and the completion of environmental remediation in September 2024.

The proposed development consists of a six-story mixed-use building with five residential floors constructed above a one-story podium containing ground-floor retail space and a four-level structured parking garage. The residential program comprises 212 units: 142 one-bedroom units, 56 two-bedroom units, and 14 three-bedroom units. Ground-floor retail consists of approximately 18,400 square feet across seven retail bays. The structured parking garage provides 276 parking spaces, representing a ratio of approximately 1.30 spaces per residential unit. Total gross building area is approximately 287,500 square feet. Project amenities include a rooftop terrace, fitness center, co-working lounge, pool deck, and dog park. The property is zoned MU-D (Mixed Use — Downtown), and the proposed development is permitted as of right under the applicable zoning ordinance.

The Relationship Manager for this credit is David Forsythe, SVP, Commercial Real Estate Lending. A term sheet was executed on January 10, 2025 by David Forsythe on behalf of the Bank and Elena Vasquez-Torres, Managing Member of the Borrower. The target closing date is February 28, 2025. The Bank's outside legal counsel for this transaction is Ashford, Cromdale Consulting & Laine LLP (lead partner Katharine "Kate" Drummond; associate Marcus Webb), located at 411 South Tryon Street, Suite 2800, Charlotte, North Carolina 28202. The Borrower's counsel is Calloway & Strauss PLLC (lead partner Jonathan F. Ngo), located at 324 Blackwell Street, Suite 200, Durham, North Carolina 27701.

The Commercial Real Estate Lending group recommends APPROVAL of this loan, subject to material conditions including, without limitation, (a) resolution of a loan-to-cost ratio exceedance through funding of an equity escrow deposit, (b) execution of an intercreditor agreement with the mezzanine lender, (c) implementation of single-purpose entity covenants and delivery of a non-consolidation opinion, and (d) resolution of other conditions detailed in Section 10 of this memorandum. The loan as structured presents certain underwriting concerns regarding borrower equity contribution and LTC compliance that must be addressed as conditions of closing. Additionally, this credit memorandum identifies several risk factors and structural issues — including interest reserve adequacy, affiliate relationships between a Borrower member and the mezzanine lender, conflicting membership interest pledges, environmental use restrictions, and title matters — each of which requires resolution or mitigation before or at closing. These matters are fully analyzed in Sections 5, 6, 9, and 10 of this memorandum.

Section 3: Project Description and Market Analysis

3.1 Project Description

The Piedmont at Brightleaf is a proposed Class A mixed-use residential and retail development to be constructed at 601 and 615 Foster Street, Durham, North Carolina 27701. The Project site comprises two contiguous parcels identified as Durham County Parcel IDs 0821-04-72-3341 and 0821-04-72-4102, with a combined land area of approximately 3.47 acres. The site is located in the Brightleaf Historic District of downtown Durham, within walking distance of the American Tobacco Campus, Durham Performing Arts Center, and Durham Bulls Athletic Park.

The site is currently improved as vacant land. The former tobacco warehouse structures that previously occupied the parcels were demolished in August 2024, and environmental remediation activities (relating to petroleum contamination from a former underground storage tank) were completed in September 2024. The North Carolina Department of Environmental Quality ("NCDEQ") issued a No Further Action ("NFA") letter dated September 30, 2024 for commercial use of the site, subject to certain conditions described in Section 8.2 of this memorandum.

The proposed development program is as follows:

  • Building Configuration: Six-story mixed-use structure consisting of five residential floors constructed above a one-story podium containing ground-floor retail space and the upper levels of a four-level structured parking garage.

  • Residential Units: 212 units total — 142 one-bedroom units, 56 two-bedroom units, and 14 three-bedroom units.

  • Retail Space: Approximately 18,400 square feet of ground-floor retail across seven retail bays, with street-facing frontage along Foster Street and a secondary entrance from the interior courtyard.

  • Structured Parking: Four-level garage providing 276 parking spaces, representing a ratio of approximately 1.30 spaces per residential unit. Parking will include both assigned resident spaces and unassigned visitor and retail patron spaces.

  • Total Gross Building Area: Approximately 287,500 square feet.

  • Amenities: Rooftop terrace with outdoor seating, gas grills, and fire pit areas; fitness center (approximately 3,200 square feet); co-working lounge with private offices and conference room; resort-style pool deck with cabanas; and enclosed dog park.

The general contractor is Graystone Construction Group Inc. (North Carolina General Contractor License No. 78442), located at 1200 Fayetteville Road, Raleigh, North Carolina 27603. The president of Graystone Construction Group Inc. is Mark D. Sullivan. The construction contract is structured as a Guaranteed Maximum Price ("GMP") contract with a total GMP of $34,750,000. The architect of record is Meridian Design Collective PC, a North Carolina professional corporation. Projected substantial completion is August 31, 2027. The property is zoned MU-D (Mixed Use — Downtown) under the Durham Unified Development Ordinance, and the proposed development is permitted as of right without the need for any zoning variance, special use permit, or rezoning.

3.2 Market Overview

The Durham, North Carolina multifamily market continues to demonstrate strong fundamentals driven by the area's robust economic base. The Research Triangle region — anchored by Duke University, the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, and North Carolina State University — has attracted significant investment in biotechnology, pharmaceutical research, information technology, and financial services. Downtown Durham, in particular, has experienced substantial revitalization over the past decade, with the American Tobacco Campus, Durham Innovation District, and the expanding Duke University Health System serving as major employment and demand drivers. Population growth in Durham County has averaged approximately 1.8% annually over the past five years, and the median household income in the downtown core has increased by over 15% during the same period.

Average market rents for Class A multifamily product in downtown Durham currently range from approximately $1,950 to $2,500 per month for one-bedroom units and $2,300 to $3,200 per month for two-bedroom units, depending on unit size, finishes, and amenity package. The Project's projected average rent of $2,245 per month across all unit types is positioned in the mid-range of the competitive set and is supported by comparable lease data from properties including the Van Alen, One City Center Apartments, and Beckon on Geer Street. The Bank considers this rent projection to be reasonable and achievable based on current market conditions.

Ground-floor retail rents in downtown Durham for Class A mixed-use buildings with street-level visibility range from approximately $26.00 to $35.00 per square foot on a triple-net basis. The projected retail rent of $30.00 per square foot NNN is within this market range and is considered reasonable given the Project's location and visibility along Foster Street.

Durham Class A multifamily vacancy rates have historically ranged from approximately 4% to 7%, with the most recent data indicating a vacancy rate of approximately 5.2% for comparable properties. The Project's underwritten vacancy assumption of 5% is consistent with historical trends and is considered appropriate. While there is a notable pipeline of competing multifamily projects under development or planned in the greater Durham area, market demand continues to absorb new supply at a rate that supports the Project's lease-up assumptions. The Bank's independent market assessment supports the appraised values discussed in Section 3.3.

Regarding the membership interest pledge, the Bank notes a material conflict: the term sheet includes a pledge of 100% of membership interests in the Borrower as collateral for the senior loan. Alternatively, the senior lender could forego the membership interest pledge and rely solely on the real property collateral, or the mezzanine lender could subordinate its pledge entirely. However, option (b) preserves the Bank's maximum collateral coverage while permitting the mezzanine lender to retain a subordinated security position. See Section 4.4 (Mezzanine Debt / Intercreditor) and Section 9 (Risk Factors) for further discussion.

The LTC calculation presents a compliance issue. Total project costs equal $56,700,000. The senior loan amount is $47,500,000. Therefore, LTC = $47,500,000 ÷ $56,700,000 = 83.77%. The Bank's maximum permitted LTC per the executed term sheet and per the Bank's Commercial Real Estate Lending Policy is 80%. At the maximum LTC of 80%, the maximum permitted senior loan amount would be $56,700,000 × 80% = $45,360,000. The proposed loan amount of $47,500,000 exceeds this maximum by $2,140,000. This discrepancy must be resolved prior to closing through the equity escrow mechanism described in Section 5.3 and conditioned in Section 10.

Additionally, the Bank notes that the Borrower's only equity contribution to the Project is the previously acquired land, which has an as-is appraised value of $8,900,000 and was acquired for $8,200,000. While the land value exceeds the $2,140,000 shortfall, a portion of the capital used to acquire the land was sourced from Brightleaf Investors Group LLC, an affiliate of the mezzanine lender. This creates a potential circular funding concern. The Senior Loan Committee requires, as a condition of closing, that the Borrower either: (a) deposit $2,140,000 in cash into a Bank-controlled equity escrow account prior to closing; or (b) reduce the loan amount to $45,360,000; or (c) provide additional evidence satisfactory to the Bank that the land equity is independent of the mezzanine financing.

6.2 Interest Reserve Analysis

The interest reserve of $4,850,000 is funded from loan proceeds at closing and held in a designated sub-account to cover monthly interest payments during the construction period. The adequacy of this reserve is a critical underwriting consideration, as depletion of the reserve before construction completion would require the Borrower to fund interest from its own resources.

Current Rate Assumptions. The current Daily Simple SOFR rate is approximately 4.35% as of the date of this memorandum. The construction period all-in rate is Daily Simple SOFR + 3.25% = 7.60%.

Full-Draw Scenario. If the entire $47,500,000 were drawn immediately at closing, the annual interest expense would be $47,500,000 × 7.60% = $3,610,000. Over the 30-month construction period, total interest cost would be approximately $3,610,000 × (30 ÷ 12) = $9,025,000. However, this scenario is unrealistic because the loan is drawn incrementally over the construction period based on milestone achievements.

Incremental Draw Scenario. Based on the draw schedule (Section 7.1), the loan is drawn progressively as construction milestones are achieved. Draw 1 at closing ($9,475,000) represents approximately 20% of the total loan commitment. Subsequent draws bring cumulative outstanding balances to progressively higher levels, with Draw 8 (substantial completion) bringing cumulative draws to approximately $44,225,000 and the final Draw 9 (retainage release) occurring post-certificate of occupancy. Assuming a roughly linear draw pattern over approximately 18 months to reach the full commitment level (with the remaining 12 months of the construction period at or near full draw), the average outstanding balance over the 30-month construction period is estimated at approximately $28,500,000 (roughly 60% of $47,500,000).

Using this average balance, the estimated total interest cost during the 30-month construction period is: $28,500,000 × 7.60% × (30 ÷ 12) = approximately $5,415,000.

The interest reserve of $4,850,000 is therefore approximately $565,000 short of the estimated total interest cost under current rate assumptions.

Sensitivity Analysis. If SOFR increases by 100 basis points to 5.35%, the all-in construction rate would be 8.60%. Estimated total interest cost would increase to $28,500,000 × 8.60% × (30 ÷ 12) = approximately $6,128,000, creating a potential shortfall of approximately $1,278,000.

Furthermore, the draw schedule is somewhat front-loaded, with the initial closing draw of $9,475,000 representing approximately 20% of the total commitment. This front-loading means the average outstanding balance may be higher than the linear assumption suggests, potentially increasing the total interest cost and widening the reserve shortfall.

Assessment. The interest reserve of $4,850,000 is tight under current rate assumptions and would be clearly inadequate in a rising rate environment. The Senior Loan Committee approves the interest reserve of $4,850,000 as adequate based on current rate assumptions, but notes that this amount is at the lower boundary of adequacy. The loan documentation must include the following protective provisions:

  • A mandatory borrower replenishment obligation requiring the Borrower to fund any interest reserve shortfall from its own funds (not from loan proceeds or mezzanine loan proceeds) upon demand by the Bank.

  • An interest reserve adequacy test to be performed at each draw request, comparing the remaining interest reserve balance against projected interest costs through the earlier of (a) the next anticipated draw date or (b) 90 days. If the projected costs exceed the remaining reserve balance, the Borrower must deposit additional funds into the interest reserve sub-account before the requested advance will be funded.

  • A reporting obligation requiring the Borrower to provide the Bank with a monthly reconciliation of the interest reserve balance, actual interest charges, and projected remaining interest costs through construction maturity.

Section 9: Risk Factors and Mitigants

9.1 Risk Factor: LTC Ratio Exceedance and Zero Cash Equity

Risk. The loan-to-cost ratio of 83.77% exceeds the Bank's maximum permitted LTC of 80% as set forth in both the executed term sheet and the Bank's Commercial Real Estate Lending Policy. The Borrower is contributing zero dollars ($0) in direct cash equity to the Project. The only equity in the capital stack is the previously acquired land, which has an as-is appraised value of $8,900,000 and was acquired for $8,200,000. A portion of the capital used to fund the land acquisition was sourced from Brightleaf Investors Group LLC, an affiliate of the mezzanine lender, Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC. The combined senior loan and mezzanine loan totals $56,700,000, which equals 100% of total project costs, resulting in zero true equity cushion beyond the embedded value in the land.

Impact. The Bank has an outsized exposure relative to total project costs. In a default scenario, the Bank's recovery would depend entirely on the collateral value of the Project and the guarantor resources. The absence of any cash equity contribution means the Borrower has limited "skin in the game" from a cash perspective, which increases moral hazard risk — the risk that the Borrower may be less motivated to resolve problems or fund cost overruns if the Borrower has no cash at risk. The 100% combined leverage ratio means that any decline in project value below total development costs would result in a loss position for one or both lenders, with the senior lender bearing the risk of value declines below the $47,500,000 senior loan balance.

Mitigants. (a) The Bank will require the Borrower to deposit $2,140,000 in cash into a Bank-controlled equity escrow account prior to closing, which will be disbursed ahead of loan advances to fund project costs and bring the effective LTC into compliance with the 80% maximum. (b) The land has an appraised value of $8,900,000, which is $700,000 above the acquisition cost, providing embedded equity. (c) The LTV ratios are well within the Bank's policy limits (65.25% as-completed, 62.34% as-stabilized), indicating adequate value coverage. (d) The combined guarantor net worth of $41,300,000 provides substantial credit support beyond the collateral. (e) The completion and carry guarantees obligate the guarantors to fund cost overruns and cover carrying costs, providing protection against construction budget and timeline risks.

Residual Concern. Even with the equity escrow, the Borrower's total equity contribution would be only $2,140,000 in cash plus $8,200,000 in land (at cost) = $10,340,000 out of $56,700,000 in total project costs = approximately 18.2%. This is relatively thin for a ground-up construction loan, where the Bank's policy guidance generally targets borrower equity contributions of 20% or more. However, the strong LTV position and guarantor support mitigate this concern to an acceptable level.

9.2 Risk Factor: Interest Reserve Adequacy

Risk. As analyzed in Section 6.2, the $4,850,000 interest reserve funded from loan proceeds at closing may be insufficient by approximately $565,000 under current rate assumptions and a reasonable incremental draw assumption. If SOFR increases by 100 basis points, the estimated shortfall would widen to approximately $1,278,000.

Impact. If the interest reserve is depleted before construction completion, the Borrower must fund monthly interest payments from its own resources. Given the Borrower's zero cash equity contribution, the Borrower may lack immediate liquidity to fund interest shortfalls, potentially creating a payment default during the construction period. A payment default during construction could trigger cross-defaults under the mezzanine loan and could jeopardize the construction timeline if the Bank suspends further advances.

Mitigants. (a) The loan documentation will include a mandatory borrower replenishment obligation requiring the Borrower to fund any interest reserve shortfall from its own funds upon demand by the Bank. (b) Interest reserve adequacy will be tested at each draw request, with the Borrower required to replenish the reserve if projected remaining interest costs during the construction period exceed the remaining reserve balance. (c) The guarantors maintain combined liquidity of $9,300,000, which provides a backstop for interest reserve replenishment. (d) The interest reserve analysis assumes a current SOFR of approximately 4.35%. If SOFR rates decline during the construction period, the reserve could prove adequate or even generate a surplus.

Recommendation. The Senior Loan Committee approves the $4,850,000 interest reserve as adequate based on current rate assumptions, with mandatory contractual protections as described above. The Bank should monitor SOFR rate trends closely and communicate proactively with the Borrower regarding any anticipated replenishment obligations.

9.3 Risk Factor: Mezzanine Lender Affiliate Relationship and SPE Concerns

Risk. Brightleaf Investors Group LLC (28% member of the Borrower) shares common control with Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC (mezzanine lender) through Thomas R. Chen. This common control creates a structural conflict and raises the following specific concerns:

First, the mezzanine lender is providing $9,200,000 in subordinated debt to a borrower in which its affiliate holds a significant (28%) equity interest. This interrelationship blurs the distinction between debt and equity in the Borrower's capital structure and may complicate the enforcement of remedies in a default or insolvency scenario.

Second, the affiliate relationship increases substantive consolidation risk. In a bankruptcy proceeding involving the Borrower, a court could potentially substantively consolidate the assets and liabilities of the Borrower with those of Brightleaf Investors Group LLC, Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC, or other entities under common control with Thomas R. Chen. Substantive consolidation would merge the separate estates into a single bankruptcy estate, potentially subordinating the Bank's claims, complicating the Bank's realization upon its collateral, or exposing the collateral to the claims of creditors of the affiliated entities.

Third, the Borrower's intended status as a single-purpose entity requires strict separateness from its members and their affiliates. Standard SPE covenants mandate separate books and records, no commingling of funds, arm's-length transactions, separate corporate formalities, and restrictions on affiliate dealings. The affiliate structure between a Borrower member and the mezzanine lender creates inherent tension with these separateness requirements.

Impact. If SPE covenants are inadequate or are not faithfully observed, the Bank's collateral position could be compromised in a bankruptcy or insolvency proceeding involving the Borrower or any affiliated entity. The risk of substantive consolidation, while relatively uncommon in practice, is heightened in situations where affiliated entities have overlapping ownership, shared management, commingled funds, or inadequate corporate formalities.

Mitigants. The Bank requires the following protective measures as conditions of closing:

(a) Robust SPE Covenants. The loan agreement must include comprehensive SPE covenants requiring the Borrower to: maintain its existence as a separate legal entity; maintain separate books, records, and bank accounts; conduct all transactions with affiliates on arm's-length terms; refrain from commingling its assets or funds with those of any member, affiliate, or other person; observe all limited liability company formalities; hold itself out as an entity separate and distinct from its members and their affiliates; and pay its own liabilities and expenses from its own funds.

(b) Independent Manager. The Borrower's operating agreement must be amended to require the appointment of at least one independent manager (who is not an officer, director, employee, or affiliate of the Borrower, any member, or the mezzanine lender) whose affirmative vote is required for any voluntary filing for bankruptcy, reorganization, or insolvency protection. This provision is the single most important structural safeguard against a bad-faith bankruptcy filing.

(c) Non-Consolidation Opinion. Borrower's counsel (Calloway & Strauss PLLC) must deliver, at closing, a non-consolidation opinion addressed to the Bank, opining that, subject to customary assumptions and qualifications, a court applying applicable North Carolina law would not substantively consolidate the Borrower with any of its members, affiliates, or other related entities. This opinion provides additional assurance (though not a guarantee) that the Borrower's separate existence will be respected in a judicial proceeding.

(d) Intercreditor Provisions. The intercreditor agreement must include express acknowledgments of the affiliate relationship and provisions prohibiting the mezzanine lender from filing or joining in the filing of an involuntary bankruptcy petition against the Borrower.

Market Standard. For securitization-eligible construction loans, industry-standard SPE requirements include independent director or independent manager provisions and non-consolidation opinions from borrower's counsel. While this loan is not currently intended for securitization, implementing these protections is prudent given the heightened affiliate risk. The analytical framework for non-consolidation analysis draws on Delaware LLC law principles (even though the Borrower is a North Carolina LLC), as the Delaware case law provides the most developed body of precedent on substantive consolidation in the context of structured finance transactions.

9.4 Risk Factor: Conflicting Membership Interest Pledges

Risk. Both the senior construction loan and the mezzanine loan are secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests in the Borrower. As a matter of law and commercial practice, only one lender can hold a first-priority security interest in the same collateral. As currently documented, the pledges are mutually exclusive as first-priority interests, creating an ambiguity that could result in litigation or contested priority in a default scenario.

Impact. If the conflicting pledges are not resolved, the Bank may not have the collateral position described in the term sheet. In a default, the mezzanine lender could assert a competing first-priority claim to the membership interests, and the resulting dispute could delay or prevent the Bank's exercise of remedies, including foreclosure on the membership interests or exercise of control over the Borrower.

Mitigants. Execution of the intercreditor agreement (currently negotiated but not yet executed) must be a condition precedent to closing. The intercreditor agreement must provide: (i) the senior lender (Pinnacle Savings Bank, N.A.) holds a first-priority security interest in both the real property collateral and the membership interests; (ii) the mezzanine lender (Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC) holds a subordinated second-priority security interest in the membership interests; (iii) the mezzanine lender is subject to a 90-day standstill period before exercising any remedies; (iv) the mezzanine lender has cure rights and a purchase option (right to acquire the senior loan at par plus accrued); and (v) the mezzanine lender may not modify the terms of the mezzanine loan without the Bank's prior written consent.

Alternative. If the intercreditor agreement cannot be finalized on terms satisfactory to the Bank, the Bank could forego the membership interest pledge and rely solely on its real property collateral (first-priority deed of trust, assignment of rents and leases, security interest in personal property). The LTV ratios support the real property collateral alone. However, the membership interest pledge provides an additional layer of protection and operational control that the Bank considers valuable, particularly in a construction loan context where the Bank needs the ability to step in and control the development entity if the Borrower defaults.

Recommendation. The Bank requires execution of the intercreditor agreement as a hard closing condition. The loan may not close without a fully executed and delivered intercreditor agreement in form and substance satisfactory to the Bank and its counsel.

9.5 Risk Factor: Environmental Use Restriction / Residential Conflict

Risk. The NFA letter issued by NCDEQ on September 30, 2024 restricts the approximately 0.38-acre area in the northeast corner of Parcel 0821-04-72-4102 to non-residential use. However, the current project plans position 14 residential units in the building structure directly above this area at the podium level and upper floors.

Impact. If this conflict is not resolved, those 14 units may not receive residential certificates of occupancy from the City of Durham, which would reduce the Project's rental income and adversely affect the Bank's underwriting assumptions. The estimated annual revenue impact of losing 14 residential units is approximately $377,160 (14 units × $2,245 average monthly rent × 12 months). This would reduce the stabilized NOI to approximately $4,499,640 ($4,876,800 − $377,160), and the recalculated DSCR would be approximately $4,499,640 ÷ $3,728,750 = 1.21x, which is below the Bank's minimum of 1.25x. Additionally, the as-completed and as-stabilized appraised values may be overstated if they assume occupancy and income from the affected 14 units without accounting for the use restriction.

Mitigants. The Bank requires the Borrower, as a condition of closing (or, at the Bank's discretion, as a condition precedent to advances for construction above the podium level in the affected area), to achieve one of the following resolutions: (a) obtain a modification to the NFA letter from NCDEQ permitting residential use of the area, contingent on installation and ongoing maintenance of the soil vapor mitigation system; (b) redesign the 14 affected units as commercial or retail space and provide updated financial projections; or (c) provide written confirmation from NCDEQ that residential use is permitted in the floors above the grade-level restricted area, provided that appropriate engineering controls (including the vapor mitigation system) are in place and operational.

Recommendation. The Bank flags this issue for review by outside counsel and environmental consultants. The resolution approach should be documented as a specific covenant in the loan documentation, and the Bank should retain discretion to withhold advances for construction in the affected area until the issue is resolved to the Bank's satisfaction.

9.6 Risk Factor: Construction Timeline and Market Risk

Risk. The 30-month construction period targets projected substantial completion on August 31, 2027. The construction maturity date under the loan is August 28, 2027 — only three days before the projected substantial completion date. Any construction delay, even a minor one, could push substantial completion past the construction maturity date, triggering a maturity default under the loan.

Mitigants. The mini-perm period provides an additional 12 months following the construction maturity date (extending to August 28, 2028), plus the 6-month extension option (to February 28, 2029). These periods provide a substantial buffer for the Borrower to complete construction, achieve stabilization, and arrange permanent take-out financing. The GMP contract with Graystone Construction Group Inc. provides cost certainty (subject to approved change orders), and the contingency of $1,440,000 provides approximately 4.1% cushion on hard construction costs. The completion guaranty from both guarantors provides the Bank with recourse against the guarantors' personal assets in the event construction is not completed.

Market Risk. The Durham multifamily market is currently strong, but the 30-month construction horizon introduces inherent uncertainty regarding supply, demand, and rental rate trends. The 5% vacancy assumption is adequate based on historical trends, and the Bank considers the risk of a significant market downturn during the construction period to be moderate but not negligible. The Bank will monitor market conditions throughout the construction period and may require updated market studies as a condition of the extension option.

9.7 Risk Factor: Title — Unsatisfied Prior Deed of Trust

Risk. The title commitment issued by Aldersgate Title & Escrow Inc. (Commitment No. NC-2024-88712, dated December 5, 2024, escrow officer Patricia Langley) identifies an unsatisfied deed of trust recorded in favor of Triangle Commercial Bank, dated 2018, encumbering the Property and securing indebtedness of $3,400,000. Borrower's counsel (Jonathan F. Ngo, Calloway & Strauss PLLC) represents that this deed of trust will be satisfied at closing from the proceeds of the senior construction loan or from borrower funds.

Impact. If the prior deed of trust is not satisfied and released at closing, the Bank would not hold a first-priority lien on the Property as required under the loan terms.

Mitigant. Closing is conditioned on receipt of a satisfactory title insurance policy from Aldersgate Title & Escrow Inc. insuring the first-priority lien of the Bank's deed of trust, free and clear of the prior Triangle Commercial Bank deed of trust. The Bank requires delivery of a payoff letter from Triangle Commercial Bank and simultaneous recording of the satisfaction or release of the prior deed of trust with the recording of the Bank's deed of trust. The title insurance policy must be issued without any exception for the prior deed of trust.

9.8 Additional Risk Considerations

Brightleaf Historic District Association CC&Rs. The Property is located within the Brightleaf Historic District, which is subject to Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions recorded in Book 6984, Page 1155 of the Durham County Register of Deeds. The CC&Rs include architectural review and design standards that may affect the design and construction of the Project. The Bank requires, as a condition of closing, evidence that the Brightleaf Historic District Association has reviewed and approved the Project plans, or that the CC&Rs do not apply to the Project or have been waived with respect to the Project.

Interest Rate Risk. The loan bears a floating interest rate based on Daily Simple SOFR, which exposes both the Bank and the Borrower to interest rate volatility. As demonstrated in the stress test analysis (Section 6.1), a 200-basis-point increase in SOFR would push the projected stabilized DSCR to 1.04x, well below the Bank's minimum. The Bank should consider requiring the Borrower to obtain an interest rate cap agreement for the mini-perm period and any extension period. The loan documentation must include SOFR benchmark replacement provisions consistent with the Alternative Reference Rates Committee ("ARRC") recommended fallback language.

Guarantor Concentration in Illiquid Assets. Both guarantors have personal net worth heavily concentrated in real estate holdings. Ms. Vasquez-Torres's real estate holdings constitute approximately 65% of her total net worth, and Mr. Osei's constitute approximately 59%. A decline in real estate values could erode guarantor net worth and impair their ability to perform under the guarantees.

General Contractor Risk. Graystone Construction Group Inc.'s GMP contract provides cost certainty for hard construction costs. The Bank should verify the financial strength and payment and performance bonding capacity of the contractor prior to closing. Evidence of the contractor's financial statements and bonding capacity should be included in the closing deliverables.

Section 11: Extension Option Analysis

The executed term sheet provides for one 6-month extension of the mini-perm period, extending the loan maturity from August 28, 2028 to February 28, 2029. The term sheet states that the extension is "subject to conditions" without specifying the particular conditions. The Senior Loan Committee recommends that the following extension conditions be incorporated into the loan documentation:

(a) No Default. No event of default exists and no condition exists that with notice or lapse of time would constitute an event of default under the loan documents or the intercreditor agreement.

(b) Completion. Substantial completion of the Project has been achieved, and a final certificate of occupancy has been issued by the City of Durham for all units and commercial spaces in the Project.

(c) Minimum Occupancy. Not less than 75% of the residential units in the Project are leased to tenants under executed leases with terms of at least 12 months and are physically occupied as of the date of the extension request.

(d) Minimum DSCR. The debt service coverage ratio, calculated based on actual trailing three-month annualized in-place net operating income divided by the annual debt service on the outstanding loan balance at the then-current interest rate, is not less than 1.15x. This threshold is intentionally lower than the 1.25x underwriting standard to account for the expectation that the property may still be in lease-up at the time of the extension.

(e) Extension Fee. Payment of an extension fee equal to 0.25% of the then-outstanding principal balance of the loan.

(f) Interest Rate Cap. Delivery of evidence of an interest rate cap agreement for the extension period, with a cap strike rate approved by the Bank in its reasonable discretion, from a counterparty with a minimum long-term credit rating of A- (S&P) or A3 (Moody's).

(g) Intercreditor Agreement. The intercreditor agreement between the Bank and Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC remains in full force and effect, and no default exists thereunder.

(h) Updated Appraisal. Delivery of an updated appraisal (from an MAI-certified appraiser acceptable to the Bank) confirming that the LTV ratio based on the outstanding loan balance and the as-stabilized appraised value does not exceed 70%.

(i) Representations and Warranties. All representations and warranties contained in the loan documents remain true and correct in all material respects.

(j) Notice. Written notice of the Borrower's election to exercise the extension option, delivered to the Bank not less than 60 days prior to the mini-perm maturity date of August 28, 2028.

These extension conditions are designed to ensure that the Bank's credit position remains satisfactory at the time of the extension and that the Project is on a trajectory toward stabilization and permanent take-out financing.

HVCRE Classification. This loan likely qualifies as a High Volatility Commercial Real Estate ("HVCRE") exposure under the Basel III capital rules (12 C.F.R. Part 3, Subpart D for national banks). HVCRE classification applies to acquisition, development, and construction loans where the borrower's contributed capital is less than 15% of the real estate's appraised as-completed value. The Borrower's contributed capital, including the proposed equity escrow of $2,140,000 and the land at acquisition cost of $8,200,000, totals $10,340,000. As a percentage of the as-completed appraised value of $72,800,000, the contributed capital equals $10,340,000 ÷ $72,800,000 = 14.2%, which is below the 15% threshold. To avoid HVCRE classification, total contributed capital would need to be at least $72,800,000 × 15% = $10,920,000, requiring an additional $580,000 beyond the proposed equity escrow of $2,140,000. The Senior Loan Committee accepts the HVCRE classification for this loan and acknowledges the resulting 150% risk weight for regulatory capital purposes.
