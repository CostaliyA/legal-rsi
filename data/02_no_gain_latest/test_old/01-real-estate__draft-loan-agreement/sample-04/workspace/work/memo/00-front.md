## 1. Purpose, Scope and Method

::: {custom-style="Body Text"}
**1.1 Purpose.** The Bank's Credit Memorandum dated January 22, 2025 recommends approval of a $47,500,000 construction-to-mini-permanent loan to Harborview Capital Partners LLC, subject to conditions of approval and to resolution of enumerated risk factors. Our instruction is to convert the executed Term Sheet dated January 10, 2025 and the approved credit into definitive documentation, and to report on the material issues and inconsistencies in the record before the Draft is circulated. This memorandum does that report.

**1.2 Method.** We read each source document in full. We then re-performed every material computation in the record rather than accepting the stated result: the sources and uses, the loan-to-cost, loan-to-value and debt service coverage ratios, the interest reserve projection, the draw schedule footings, the stabilization analysis, the guaranty percentages, the acreage and frontage figures, and each date interval. Where a document stated a result that we could not reproduce, we have said so and shown our arithmetic. Every proposition in Section 5 is tied to a specific document and section. Where the record is silent we have said so rather than assumed a fact.

**1.3 What this memorandum is not.** We have not re-underwritten the credit, and we express no view on whether the Bank should make the Loan. Our commentary on credit metrics is limited to testing internal consistency and to identifying where a document's stated conclusion does not follow from its own figures — because those are matters that will be relied on in documentation and, potentially, in a bankruptcy or enforcement proceeding. We have not reviewed the full Phase II Environmental Site Assessment, the NFA letter itself, the GMP contract, the architect's agreement, either party's financial statements, or the operating agreement in unexcerpted form; none is in the record furnished to us (see Issue 44).

**1.4 Convention.** "Term Sheet" means the executed term sheet dated January 10, 2025. "Credit Memorandum" means the Senior Loan Committee Credit Memorandum dated January 22, 2025. "Appraisal" means the executive summary of the self-contained appraisal report by Ridgeline Appraisal Services Inc. dated November 15, 2024 (File RAS-2024-1147). "Phase I ESA" means the excerpted Phase I Environmental Site Assessment by Sentinel Environmental Consulting LLC dated October 28, 2024 (Project SEC-2024-1147). "Title Commitment" means Commitment No. NC-2024-88712 issued by Aldersgate Title & Escrow Inc., effective December 5, 2024. "Organizational Chart" means the borrower organizational chart and operating agreement excerpts prepared by Calloway & Strauss PLLC dated January 2025. "Draw Schedule" means the Borrower-prepared construction and draw schedule dated January 8, 2025. References to "$" are: all amounts are in United States dollars.

## 3. Executive Summary

::: {custom-style="Body Text"}
The Loan can be documented, but the record as furnished does not support several of the conclusions the Credit Memorandum relies on, and three of those conclusions go to the core of the credit rather than to documentation niceties. They are:

**(a) The Project has no borrower equity, and the Loan is oversized against the Bank's own limits.** The sources and uses allocates one hundred percent of the $56,700,000 of Total Project Costs to the $47,500,000 senior loan and the $9,200,000 mezzanine loan, with Borrower cash equity stated at **$0** (Draw Schedule, "Summary" and "Detailed Budget" worksheets; Credit Memorandum §5.1 and Appendix C). The $8,200,000 land cost is shown in the Detailed Budget as funded by **Mezzanine Loan proceeds**, not by member equity. That is irreconcilable with the Term Sheet (§5.2: land "funded from prior capital contributions by the Borrower's members"), the Credit Memorandum (§2.2: "capital calls from all three members"), and the Organizational Chart (§1.3: contributed by Ms. Vasquez-Torres "from her personal resources"). On the face of the sources and uses, the Mezzanine Loan is a land take-out: at closing it repays whoever funded the land. If that is right, then the "land equity" that the Credit Memorandum credits at $8,200,000 in its equity analysis (Credit Memorandum § 5.3, § 9.1: $2,140,000 cash + $8,200,000 land = $10,340,000, or 18.2%) is the same $8,200,000 that the Credit Memorandum's own sources table has already counted as mezzanine debt. It is counted twice. True equity is $0, or $2,140,000 if the equity escrow is funded with genuinely independent cash. See Issue 5.

**(b) The Loan exceeds both of the Bank's leverage limits as set in the executed Term Sheet, and the prescribed cure does not work.** Loan-to-cost is 83.77% against an 80% maximum (a $2,140,000 excess). As-completed loan-to-value is 65.25% against the 65% maximum in Term Sheet §§ 4 and 6.2 (a $180,000 excess) — a breach the Credit Memorandum does not report, because it measures against a 70% policy limit that contradicts the executed Term Sheet. The Credit Memorandum's cure — deposit $2,140,000 of equity — is presented as an alternative to reducing the Loan Amount, but on its own it cannot work: because every dollar of Total Project Costs is already allocated to the two loans, adding $2,140,000 of equity to the sources column while leaving the Loan at $47,500,000 produces $58,840,000 of sources against $56,700,000 of uses. The equity must **displace** Loan proceeds. The two "alternatives" in Credit Memorandum §5.1 and §10.1.2 are in fact two halves of a single fix: reduce the Loan Amount to $45,360,000 **and** fund $2,140,000 of equity. See Issues 1, 2 and 3.

**(c) The interest reserve is not adequate, and the record gives three different answers to how inadequate.** The Draw Schedule projects a **surplus** of $221,325 on a Daily Simple SOFR assumption of 3.50%; the same worksheet's own sensitivity table at 4.35% projects a **shortfall** of $(365,830); and the Credit Memorandum projects a shortfall of $(565,000). All three purport to measure the same thing. The difference is methodology: the Draw Schedule's month-by-month accrual is arithmetically sound but is built on a rate assumption 85 basis points below the rate used everywhere else in the record, and the Credit Memorandum's average-balance shortcut overstates total interest by roughly $200,000 at the same rate. On the Draw Schedule's own draw pattern at 4.35% Daily Simple SOFR, total construction-period interest is approximately $5,211,500 and the shortfall is approximately $361,500. The reserve is short, and the Senior Loan Committee approved it as "adequate." See Issues 9 and 10.
:::

::: {custom-style="Body Text"}
Beyond those three, the record contains a cluster of issues that individually are capable of resolution but collectively indicate that the transaction documents were assembled from inconsistent inputs:

- **The Draw Schedule does not foot.** Draw 9 shows $4,275,000 of components against a stated draw of $3,275,000, and the component columns aggregate to $48,500,000 against a $47,500,000 facility. The Detailed Budget's "Net Disbursable" total is overstated by $725,000. The 10% retainage is $3,475,000, but the documents release $3,275,000. See Issues 11, 12 and 13.
- **The environmental record is materially incomplete.** The NFA letter requires a deed notice to be recorded by **November 29, 2024**. The Phase I ESA (October 28, 2024) records no evidence of recording. The Appraisal (November 15, 2024) states it **is** recorded. The Title Commitment (effective December 5, 2024) searches the public records and **does not show it**. It is also absent from the Term Sheet's list of Permitted Exceptions. If the notice was not recorded, the NFA determination is out of compliance and the site's regulatory status is impaired; if it was recorded, the Title Policy will carry an unlisted exception. Either way the Bank needs the recorded instrument. The Phase II report and the NFA letter are not in the record at all. See Issues 21, 23 and 27.
- **The residential use restriction has not been resolved and the record does not establish its scope.** The Credit Memorandum asserts that fourteen residential units sit above the restricted 0.38 acres. The Phase I ESA expressly says that this "requires a detailed overlay" and was **not** determined. The number fourteen appears to have been taken from the unit count of three-bedroom units. The Appraisal expressly declines to adjust value for the restriction. See Issue 24.
- **Two dates are three days apart and cannot both be met.** Projected Substantial Completion is August 31, 2027; the Construction Maturity Date and the guaranteed completion date are August 28, 2027. Draw 8 is scheduled in the thirty-month of a thirty-month construction period and Draw 9 falls after maturity. See Issue 28.
- **Borrower's net worth cannot be reconciled and the covenant would be breached on day one.** Borrower's only identified asset is the Property, acquired for $8,200,000, encumbered by a $3,400,000 deed of trust. That balance sheet yields net worth of approximately **$4,800,000**, not the $14,200,000 asserted, and the proposed $12,000,000 covenant would fail immediately. See Issue 31.
:::
