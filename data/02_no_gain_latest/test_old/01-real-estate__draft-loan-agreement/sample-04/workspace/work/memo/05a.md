## Part A — Capital Structure and Loan Sizing

### Issue 1 — Loan-to-Cost Ratio Exceeds the 80% Maximum, and the Prescribed Equity Escrow Does Not Cure It

**Severity: Critical.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 3.1 ($47,500,000), § 5.2 (Total Uses $56,700,000), § 6.1 (maximum LTC 80%); Credit Memorandum §§ 5.1, 5.3, 9.1, 10.1.2, 14.1; Draw Schedule, "Summary" worksheet (LTC Excess / Equity Shortfall $2,140,000).

**Finding.** The arithmetic is right and the conclusion is wrong. Loan-to-cost is $47,500,000 ÷ $56,700,000 = **83.7743%**, which exceeds the 80% maximum set in Term Sheet § 6.1. At 80%, the maximum senior loan is $56,700,000 × 0.80 = **$45,360,000**; the Loan exceeds that by **$2,140,000**. To restore 80%, either the Loan must come down or Total Project Costs must rise by $2,675,000 ($47,500,000 ÷ 0.80 = $59,375,000).

The Credit Memorandum's cure — deposit $2,140,000 into a Bank-controlled equity escrow — does not by itself change the ratio. Because the sources and uses allocates 100% of the $56,700,000 to the two loans (senior $47,500,000 = 83.77%; mezzanine $9,200,000 = 16.23%; Borrower cash equity **$0.00%**; Credit Memorandum § 5.1 and Appendix C; Draw Schedule "Summary"), there is no unfunded line item for the escrowed equity to pay. Adding $2,140,000 to sources produces $58,840,000 of sources against $56,700,000 of uses — a $2,140,000 surplus — while the Loan remains $47,500,000 and is still fully drawn to complete the Project. The escrowed cash does not reduce the numerator unless it reduces the commitment.

The Credit Memorandum presents the equity escrow (condition 10.1.2), a reduction of the Loan to $45,360,000, and additional evidence of independent land equity as **alternatives** ("either: (a) ... or (b) ... or (c) ..."). They are not alternatives. Option (a) requires option (b) to have any effect on LTC.

**Recommendation.** Treat the two as a single fix: reduce the Loan Amount to **$45,360,000** and require the **$2,140,000** equity contribution. That closes: $45,360,000 senior + $9,200,000 mezzanine + $2,140,000 equity = $56,700,000. LTC becomes exactly 80.00%. Obtain the Senior Loan Committee's written confirmation that the commitment is reduced (this is a change to the credit approval and to the Term Sheet loan amount, so it requires Committee action, not merely documentation). Alternatives worth presenting to the Bank: (i) restate Total Project Costs upward by adding the omitted costs in Issue 8 — note that adding the Triangle Commercial Bank payoff alone at its $3,400,000 original principal would bring Total Project Costs to $60,100,000 and LTC to 79.03%, but would require $3,400,000 of new funding (the same circularity); or (ii) substitute the Appraisal's $8,900,000 land value for the $8,200,000 cost basis in the budget, giving Total Project Costs of $57,400,000 and LTC of 82.75% — still above 80%. Neither alternative works without new equity.

**Draft reference:** § 8.1 (testing language and the arithmetic), § 3.1(g) (equity escrow condition with the reservation noted).

### Issue 2 — The As-Completed Loan-to-Value Ratio Exceeds the Executed Term Sheet's 65% Limit

**Severity: Critical.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 4 ("The Lender's maximum loan-to-value ratio shall not exceed 65% on an as-completed basis") and § 6.2 (65% as-completed and as-stabilized); Credit Memorandum § 6.1 and § 12 (maximum LTV 70% for construction loans; as-completed 65.25% reported as "Pass"); Appraisal § 10 ($72,800,000 as-completed; $76,200,000 as-stabilized; LTV 65.25% and 62.34%).

**Finding.** $47,500,000 ÷ $72,800,000 = **65.2473%**. That exceeds the Term Sheet's 65% cap by 0.25 percentage points, or **$180,000** in principal amount ($72,800,000 × 0.65 = $47,320,000). The as-stabilized ratio ($47,500,000 ÷ $76,200,000 = 62.3360%) is compliant.

The Credit Memorandum does not report this. Its underwriting table (¶ 6.1) and its regulatory section (¶ 12) both state the applicable maximum as **70%**, described as "Bank Policy," and mark the ratio as a pass. The executed Term Sheet says 65%, twice. The executed Term Sheet is the operative commercial agreement; a policy maximum more permissive than the deal-specific maximum does not override it. The credit file should be corrected so that the Committee approved against the limit the term sheet actually contains.

**Recommendation.** The Issue 1 fix cures this as well: at $45,360,000, the as-completed ratio is 62.31% and the as-stabilized ratio is 59.53%. Conform the covenant in the Loan Agreement to **65%** (not 70%). Ask the Bank to correct the credit file and to confirm the 70% figure was not the basis of approval.

**Draft reference:** § 8.2 (both limits stated as 65%, with the computation and the conflict disclosed).

### Issue 3 — Recommended Restructure and Loan Sizing

**Severity: Critical (structuring).**

**Sources:** All of the above; Credit Memorandum §§ 5.1, 5.3, 9.1, 12.

**Finding.** The record supports four internally consistent structures. We recommend the first.

| | Structure | Senior loan | Mezzanine | True equity | LTC | LTV (as-completed) | HVCRE |
|:--|:----------|------------:|----------:|------------:|----:|-------------------:|:------|
| A | **As documented** | $47,500,000 | $9,200,000 | $0 | 83.77% | 65.25% | Yes (14.20%) |

**Recommendation.** Adopt Structure C and offer Structure D to the Bank as a means of addressing the regulatory capital issue in Issue 4. In each case obtain: (i) Senior Loan Committee approval of the reduced commitment; (ii) a revised Term Sheet or written confirmation of the reduced Loan Amount so that the Loan Documents and the Term Sheet agree; (iii) a source-of-funds certification for the $2,140,000 (Issue 5); and (iv) restated sources and uses. Note that any reduction in the Loan Amount reduces the Origination Fee ($475,000 → $453,600 at 1.00%) and the available Interest Reserve, and that the Draw Schedule in Exhibit C of the Draft must then be re-based — we have not attempted that adjustment because the Draw Schedule needs to be rebuilt in any event (Issue 11).

### Issue 4 — HVCRE Classification

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Credit Memorandum § 12; Appraisal § 10 (as-completed value $72,800,000); Draw Schedule (land at $8,200,000, no borrower equity).

**Finding.** The Credit Memorandum computes contributed capital as $2,140,000 equity plus $8,200,000 land at cost = $10,340,000, or **14.2033%** of the $72,800,000 as-completed value, against the 15% threshold ($10,920,000), a $580,000 shortfall. We reproduce those figures. Two observations. First, the calculation only reaches 14.2% because the Credit Memorandum credits the land at $8,200,000; on the sources and uses as actually constructed, the land is funded by the Mezzanine Loan and contributed capital would be **$2,140,000, or 2.94%** (Issue 5). Second, if the land is contributed at its **appraised** value of $8,900,000, contributed capital is $8,900,000 + $2,140,000 = $11,040,000, or **15.16%** — which would avoid HVCRE classification and the 150% risk weight, with no additional cash from the sponsor.

**Recommendation.** Present Structure D (Issue 3) to the Bank. Contributing the land at appraised value is the cheapest available route to the 15% threshold and is a matter of how the contribution is documented and evidenced, not of new money. Confirm with the Bank's regulatory reporting group that the land may be counted at appraised value under the applicable rule before the credit file is finalized.

**Draft reference:** § 8.1 (drafting note), § 5.4 (capital contribution schedule).

### Issue 5 — The Record Gives Three Irreconcilable Accounts of How the $8,200,000 Land Cost Was Funded; There Is No Borrower Equity

**Severity: Critical.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 5.2 ("The land ... was acquired by the Borrower prior to the closing of the Loan and is contributed to the Project as the Borrower's equity. The land acquisition was funded from prior capital contributions by the Borrower's members"); Credit Memorandum § 2.2 ("acquired by Ms. Vasquez-Torres on behalf of the Borrower through capital calls from all three members, including Brightleaf Investors Group LLC, at a total acquisition cost of $8,200,000"); Organizational Chart § 1.3 (land "contributed to Harborview Capital Partners LLC by Elena Vasquez-Torres at a cost basis of $8,200,000 ... originally acquired by Ms. Vasquez-Torres in a transaction funded from her personal resources"; Brightleaf Investors Group LLC and Mr. Osei each contributed **$0**); Draw Schedule, "Detailed Budget" line 1.01 (Land Acquisition — Vendor "Prior Acquisition — Closed"; Funded by Senior Loan **$0**; Funded by Mezzanine Loan **$8,200,000**; Funded by Borrower Equity **$0**; note: "Previously closed; funded by mezzanine loan proceeds and member capital calls"); Title Commitment Schedule A ¶ 5 (title vested by Special Warranty Deed recorded in Book 7598, Page 443 (Parcel 1) and Page 445 (Parcel 2), dated June 12, 2023, from Foster Street Properties LLC).

**Finding.** Three accounts, each inconsistent with the others and with the record evidence:

1. **Member capital contributions** (Term Sheet; Credit Memorandum — "all three members, including Brightleaf Investors Group LLC").
2. **Ms. Vasquez-Torres's personal resources** (Organizational Chart; Brightleaf Investors Group LLC and Mr. Osei each at $0).
3. **Mezzanine Loan proceeds** (Draw Schedule Detailed Budget — the only place in the record that states a funding source for the land, and it states a lender source, not an equity source).

The recorded deed supports none of the equity accounts in the form given: it shows a **purchase by Harborview directly from Foster Street Properties LLC on June 12, 2023** — not a contribution of land to a newly formed entity, and not a purchase by Ms. Vasquez-Torres. The Organizational Chart's statement that the land was contributed "upon formation of the project entity" is inconsistent with a formation date of March 14, 2019 and an acquisition date of June 12, 2023.

The accounts also conflict on the acquisition **date**: the Phase I ESA says Harborview "acquired the Subject Property in 2022" (§ 2.2); the Appraisal says the $8,200,000 was "the price paid by Harborview Capital Partners LLC in early 2024" (§ 6.1); the deed says June 12, 2023.

The consequence is not semantic. If the land was acquired in 2023 with member money and is refinanced at closing with Mezzanine Loan proceeds, then at closing the sponsor is repaid $8,200,000 (less the Prior Deed of Trust payoff) and the Project ends with **no equity at all**. The Credit Memorandum would then have double-counted the land: once as a use funded by mezzanine debt in its sources and uses, and again as $8,200,000 of "land equity" in its equity analysis (§ 5.3, § 9.1) and in its HVCRE analysis (§ 12). That is the single most important finding in this review, and it is the reason the LTC, LTV and HVCRE conclusions in the Credit Memorandum cannot be relied on.

**Recommendation.** Before the Draft is circulated, require from Borrower (and from Borrower's counsel) a **schedule of all capital contributions and member advances since formation**, on a cash-movement basis, identifying for each: date, amount, contributor, bank account, and the use to which the funds were applied; together with the settlement statement for the June 2023 acquisition, the source of the $8,200,000 purchase price (including the extent to which the Prior Deed of Trust was assumed rather than paid), and a certification that no member advance will be repaid from the proceeds of the Loan or the Mezzanine Loan. Verify the cash movements against bank statements, not just against a schedule. Then restate the sources and uses so that equity and debt are each shown once, and present the restated table to the Committee. If the Mezzanine Loan does reimburse member advances at closing, that is a **Distribution** of loan proceeds to the sponsor on day one; it must be identified as such, approved explicitly, and carved out of (or, better, prohibited by) the negative covenant. The Draft prohibits it absent express permission (§ 7.5(d)) and requires the Disclosure Schedule (§ 5.4) — but the prohibition is only as good as the disclosure behind it.

**Draft reference:** § 5.4 and Schedule 5.4; § 7.5(d); § 5.11; § 2.3; § 3.1(g).

### Issue 6 — Mezzanine Debt Service Is Not Funded Anywhere, and Combined Debt Service Coverage Is 0.93x

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 1.4 (Mezzanine Loan $9,200,000, 12.50% fixed, coterminous); Credit Memorandum § 4.4 (interest "accrues and is paid-in-kind during construction; cash pay during mini-perm"); Draw Schedule Detailed Budget (no line item for mezzanine interest); Appraisal § 7 (Stabilized NOI $4,876,800); Credit Memorandum § 6.1 (DSCR 1.31x, senior only).

**Finding.** No document in the record funds Mezzanine Loan debt service. The Approved Project Budget of $56,700,000 contains no line for mezzanine interest, in either its PIK or its cash-pay form. At 12.50% on $9,200,000:

- PIK accretion over a 30-month Construction Period: $9,200,000 × 12.50% × 2.5 = **$2,875,000**, producing an accreted balance of approximately **$12,075,000** at the start of the Mini-Perm Period.
- Cash interest during the 12-month Mini-Perm Period on that balance: **$1,509,375**.
- If interest is paid in cash from the outset, the monthly Mezzanine Loan interest is $95,833, is not in the budget, and Borrower has no cash with which to pay it (Issue 5).

Every coverage figure in the record is a **senior-only** figure. On a combined basis, using the Appraiser's Stabilized NOI and the Credit Memorandum's 4.35% SOFR assumption: combined debt service is $3,728,750 senior + $1,509,375 mezzanine = **$5,238,125**, and combined coverage is **0.93x**. Combined debt at stabilization would be $47,500,000 + $12,075,000 = $59,575,000, or **78.18%** of the $76,200,000 as-stabilized value, and the combined debt yield is **8.19%** (senior-only debt yield 10.27%). The property does not cover total debt service from operations; the mini-perm exit depends on refinance proceeds, not on cash flow.

**Recommendation.** (a) Require disclosure of the Mezzanine Loan's full payment mechanics before closing, and require quarterly reporting of accreted balances (Draft § 16.4). (b) Add a **debt yield covenant** of not less than 8.50% (Draft § 8.4) — a rate-insensitive measure, which the Term Sheet lacks entirely. (c) Require a formal take-out/refinancing analysis at lease-up and as a condition to the Extension Option, including the combined leverage ratio. (d) Do not represent in the credit file that the Loan is self-liquidating; on a whole-loan basis it is not.

**Draft reference:** § 16.4 (with the computation noted), § 8.4, Exhibit G(k).

### Issue 7 — Loan Proceeds Fund a $1,835,000 Developer Fee Payable to the Borrower Before Any Equity Is at Risk

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 5.2 (Developer Fee $2,835,000, "5% of total project cost"); § 14 (no distributions during the Construction Period "except for payment of the developer fee in accordance with the approved Project Budget"); Draw Schedule Detailed Budget line 5.01 (Vendor: **Harborview Capital Partners LLC**; Senior Loan $1,835,000; Mezzanine Loan $1,000,000); Draw Schedule "Draw Schedule" worksheet, draws 6 through 9 (Developer Fee allocated).

**Finding.** The Developer Fee is 5.00% of Total Project Costs and is payable to **Borrower itself**. $1,835,000 of it is funded by the senior Loan and $1,000,000 by the Mezzanine Loan. Combined with Issue 5, the economics are: the loans fund 100% of costs, of which 5% ($2,835,000) flows to the sponsor-controlled Borrower as a fee. That is not equity in any sense; it is negative equity.

The Term Sheet's distribution carve-out is also incoherent as drafted. Borrower is the payee, so a developer fee paid to Borrower is not a Distribution at all (there is no transfer to a member); and if the fee is then distributed to members during the Construction Period, that **is** a Distribution that the carve-out prohibits. The Draft resolves this by defining "Distributions" to capture both (§ 1.1), permitting the fee only to Borrower (§ 6.11), and conditioning it.

**Recommendation.** (a) Defer the Developer Fee: it should be earned only on Substantial Completion, and paid only if no Cost Overrun, no Interest Reserve deficiency, no unfunded equity obligation and no Event of Default exists, and only after the Construction Inspector confirms sufficient funds to complete. (b) Cap the senior-funded portion at the $1,835,000 allocated in the budget and prohibit payment of the $1,000,000 mezzanine-funded portion from Loan proceeds. (c) Require that the fee be paid to Borrower and not to any member or Affiliate (already the budget's vendor, but should be a covenant). (d) Consider subordinating the entire fee to repayment of the Loan — the strongest available answer to the equity problem, and one the Bank can reasonably require given that it is lending 100% of cost.

**Draft reference:** § 6.11; § 7.5(a) and (d); definition of "Distributions" in § 1.1.
