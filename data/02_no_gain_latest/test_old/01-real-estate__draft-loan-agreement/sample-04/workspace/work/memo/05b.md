## Part B — Budget, Interest Reserve and Disbursement

### Issue 8 — The Approved Project Budget Omits Known Costs

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 5.2 and Exhibit C; Credit Memorandum § 5.2 and Appendix C; Draw Schedule Detailed Budget; Term Sheet §§ 3.4, 12, 18; Credit Memorandum § 9.7; Title Commitment Schedule B–II ¶ 10.

**Finding.** The budget totals $56,700,000 and includes land, hard costs, soft costs, the Interest Reserve, the Origination Fee, the Developer Fee and contingency. It does not include any of the following, each of which is a real cost of this transaction:

| Omitted item | Source of the obligation | Estimated amount |
|:-------------|:-------------------------|-----------------:|
| Payoff of the Prior Deed of Trust (Triangle Commercial Bank) | Title Commitment Sch. B–II ¶ 10; Credit Memorandum § 9.7 | Up to $3,400,000 (original principal; **current balance not in record** — the title officer notes counsel's advice that the loan was "partially repaid") |
| Unused line fee, 0.25% p.a. on average undrawn balance | Term Sheet § 3.4 | ≈$125,400 over 30 months (average undrawn balance computed from the Draw Schedule's own draw pattern: $20,070,815) |
| Exit Fee, 0.50% of outstanding balance | Term Sheet § 3.4 | $237,500 if repaid during construction; $118,750 in the first six months of the mini-perm |
| Extension Fee, 0.25% | Credit Memorandum § 11(e) (not in the Term Sheet) | ≈$118,750 |
| Interest Rate Cap premium | Credit Memorandum §§ 6.1, 9.8 (recommended) | Not quantified anywhere in the record |
| Mezzanine Loan interest | Issue 6 | ≈$2,875,000 (PIK, 30 months) |

The Prior Deed of Trust point deserves emphasis. The budget contains no line for its payoff, yet the Bank's own title condition requires it to be satisfied at closing, and the Credit Memorandum says Borrower's counsel "represents that this deed of trust will be satisfied at closing from the proceeds of the senior construction loan or from borrower funds" (§ 9.7). Funding a $3,400,000 payoff from senior Loan proceeds would create a $3,400,000 hole in a budget in which every dollar is already allocated. Funding it from "borrower funds" is not credible given Issue 5. The most likely reading — which we recommend testing with the closing statement — is that the $8,200,000 "Land Acquisition" line is in substance a **refinancing** of the land (payoff of the Prior Deed of Trust plus reimbursement of member advances), funded by the Mezzanine Loan. If so, the sources and uses is internally consistent but confirms zero equity (Issue 5).

**Recommendation.** Obtain a current payoff letter before the budget is finalized, and require Borrower to present a restated sources and uses that shows the payoff, all financing fees and the cap premium as uses, with an identified source for each. Add the items to Exhibit B, or list them in a schedule of costs to be funded outside the Loan (as Exhibit B ¶ 2 of the Draft now does). Confirm in writing that the Loan will not fund any portion of the payoff.

**Draft reference:** § 5.14 and Schedule 5.14; Exhibit B ¶ 2; § 2.9(b)–(d).

### Issue 9 — The Interest Reserve Is Inadequate, and the Record Contains Three Mutually Inconsistent Measurements

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 10 ($4,850,000); Credit Memorandum § 6.2 and § 9.2 (shortfall $565,000; at +100 bps, $1,278,000); Draw Schedule "Draw Schedule" worksheet, Section 2 (surplus $221,325 at 3.50% SOFR; shortfall $(365,830) at 4.35%); Draw Schedule Detailed Budget line 4.01 ("assumes SOFR at 3.50%").

**Finding.** Three documents, three answers, one question:

| Source | SOFR assumption | All-in rate | Total construction interest | Surplus / (shortfall) vs $4,850,000 |
|:-------|:----------------|:-----------|---------------------------:|------------------------------------:|
| Draw Schedule, Section 2 (month-by-month accrual) | 3.50% | 6.75% | $4,628,675 | **$221,325** |
| Draw Schedule, Section 2 sensitivity table | 4.35% | 7.60% | $5,215,830 | **$(365,830)** |
| Credit Memorandum § 6.2 (average-balance shortcut) | 4.35% | 7.60% | $5,415,000 | **$(565,000)** |

We reproduced the Draw Schedule's month-by-month accrual and it is arithmetically sound: eight balance steps (Draws 1 through 8), each at balance × 6.75% ÷ 12, aggregate to **$4,628,675**, an implied average outstanding balance of **$27,429,185** over 30 months. Two defects follow.

First, the reserve was sized at **3.50%** Daily Simple SOFR. Every other document in the record — Term Sheet § 4, Credit Memorandum §§ 6.1, 6.2 and 9.2, and the Appraisal § 10 — uses **4.35%** as the current rate. The budget line itself says "assumes SOFR at 3.50%." The reserve is therefore sized on a rate 85 basis points below the rate the Bank used to approve the credit.

Second, the Credit Memorandum's $565,000 shortfall is overstated. Its method is $28,500,000 × 7.60% × 2.5 = $5,415,000, using an assumed average balance of $28,500,000 described as "roughly 60%" of the commitment. The actual average balance implied by the Draw Schedule's own draw pattern is **$27,429,185**, not $28,500,000. The correct total at 7.60% is therefore approximately **$5,211,545** and the correct shortfall approximately **$(361,545)** — about $203,500 less than the Credit Memorandum states. (The Draw Schedule's own sensitivity result, $(365,830), is consistent with ours; the small difference is rounding in its monthly recomputation.)

The conclusion is unchanged — the reserve is inadequate — but the magnitude matters, because it determines how much the Bank should require Borrower to contribute, and because the Senior Loan Committee approved $4,850,000 as "adequate based on current rate assumptions" (¶ 6.2, ¶ 9.2). It is not adequate at the current rate.

**Recommendation.** (a) Re-size the Interest Reserve by re-running the Draw Schedule accrual at the then-current Daily Simple SOFR at closing (4.35% per the record), producing approximately $5,211,500, or approximately **$361,500 more** than the $4,850,000 budgeted. (b) Correct the Credit Memorandum's § 6.2 and § 9.2 analysis and advise the Committee that its adequacy finding was based on a stale rate. (c) Retain in the Loan Agreement the mandatory replenishment obligation (Term Sheet § 10; Credit Memorandum § 6.2) together with the adequacy test at each draw, which the Term Sheet does not require and the Credit Memorandum only recommends. (d) Note that any reduction in the Loan Amount under Issue 1 reduces the funds available for the Interest Reserve, so the reserve and the loan amount must be sized together.

**Draft reference:** § 4.5 (including the Interest Reserve Adequacy Test at § 4.5(d) and replenishment at § 4.5(e)); § 14.1(b).

### Issue 10 — Interest Reserve Methodology

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 3.3 (interest on actual days over a 360-day year); Draw Schedule Section 2 (monthly interest computed as balance × rate ÷ 12).

**Finding.** The Draw Schedule computes each month's accrual as balance × all-in rate ÷ 12. That is a 30/360 convention. The Term Sheet requires **actual days elapsed over a 360-day year**. Over a 30-month period the difference is small (a few thousand dollars) but it is systematic, and it compounds whenever the reserve is tested. More significantly, the Draw Schedule assumes each draw occurs at the **start** of a month and that no draw occurs in the same month as another; the actual timing will differ, and because the reserve is funded at closing and accrues interest on itself, timing errors run in one direction. We recommend the reserve be sized on a month-by-month basis using actual/360 and the projected draw dates in the Draw Schedule rather than a level average balance.

**Recommendation.** Rebuild the interest reserve analysis on an actual/360 basis keyed to the estimated draw dates (February 2025 through October 2027). Use the same schedule as the basis for the Interest Reserve Adequacy Test so that the test and the sizing are consistent. Do not use the average-balance method in the Credit Memorandum for either purpose.

**Draft reference:** § 2.4(b); § 4.5(d).

### Issue 11 — The Draw Schedule Does Not Foot

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Draw Schedule, "Draw Schedule" worksheet, Section 1; Term Sheet § 9.2; Credit Memorandum § 7.1.

**Finding.** We re-added every column. Every draw row foots **except Draw 9**:

| Draw | Sum of components (net hard cost + soft + reserve + fee + developer fee + contingency) | Stated total draw | Difference |
|:----:|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------:|------------------:|-----------:|
| 1 | $9,475,000 | $9,475,000 | $0 |
| 2 | $5,212,500 | $5,212,500 | $0 |
| 3 | $6,950,000 | $6,950,000 | $0 |
| 4 | $6,950,000 | $6,950,000 | $0 |
| 5 | $5,212,500 | $5,212,500 | $0 |
| 6 | $3,475,000 | $3,475,000 | $0 |
| 7 | $3,475,000 | $3,475,000 | $0 |
| 8 | $3,475,000 | $3,475,000 | $0 |
| **9** | **$4,275,000** | **$3,275,000** | **$1,000,000** |
| Total | **$48,500,000** | **$47,500,000** | **$1,000,000** |

Draw 9 shows the $3,475,000 retainage release plus a $200,000 developer fee allocation plus a $600,000 contingency allocation — $4,275,000 — against a stated draw of $3,275,000. As a result the component columns aggregate to **$48,500,000** against a $47,500,000 facility. The $1,000,000 difference is exactly the portion of the Developer Fee that the Detailed Budget allocates to the **Mezzanine Loan** (5.01: senior $1,835,000, mezzanine $1,000,000): the draw schedule disburses the full $2,835,000 from the Loan and then plugs Draw 9 downward by $1,000,000 to force the total to $47,500,000.

Two consequences. First, the schedule as delivered would have the Loan disburse $1,000,000 of Developer Fee that the budget says the Mezzanine Loan funds. Second, because the plug is applied to a row whose hard-cost component **is** the retainage release, a reviewer cannot tell from the schedule whether the retainage is fully released (Issue 13).

**Recommendation.** Reject the schedule. Substitute **Exhibit C** to the Draft, which we rebuilt as follows: Draws 1 through 8 unchanged (each foots); Draw 9 = full $3,475,000 retainage release, with no developer fee or contingency allocated to it; developer fee and contingency shown separately as reserve items, disbursed only on approval. The rebuilt schedule foots to $47,500,000 and reconciles to the Detailed Budget's funding columns. Borrower must re-issue the schedule in this form, certified by the Managing Member, before it is attached as an exhibit.

**Draft reference:** § 4.1 (reserves are not scheduled disbursements); § 4.10; Exhibit C and its notes.

### Issue 13 — Retainage: 10% Is $3,475,000, but the Documents Release $3,275,000

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 9.1 ("a 10% retainage holdback"); § 9.2 (Draw 9 "Retainage Release" $3,275,000); Credit Memorandum § 7.1 (same; and ¶ 7.1 states the retainage "represents approximately 9.4% of the hard construction cost GMP of $34,750,000"); Draw Schedule Detailed Budget (Retainage $3,475,000 = exactly 10%).

**Finding.** 10% of the $34,750,000 GMP is **$3,475,000**. The Term Sheet, the Credit Memorandum and the Draw Schedule all state the release as **$3,275,000**. The Credit Memorandum notices the discrepancy and describes the release as "approximately 9.4%" rather than correcting it: $3,275,000 ÷ $34,750,000 = **9.4245%**. The $200,000 difference is unexplained and appears to be a plug (Issue 11). Either the retainage is 10% and the release is $3,475,000, or it is 9.42% and the documents should say so.

**Recommendation.** Conform to 10% / $3,475,000. Retainage is the Bank's principal protection against mechanics' lien exposure during the statutory lien period; understating the release by $200,000 while describing the holdback as 10% invites an argument at the retainage draw that the Bank is obliged to release more than the schedule shows.

**Draft reference:** § 4.3; Exhibit C ¶ 2.

### Issue 14 — "Equity First" Is Inconsistent with a $9,475,000 Draw at 0% Completion

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Credit Memorandum § 7.2 ("Equity First. The Borrower's equity ... must be fully invested in the Project before any loan advances are made, other than the initial closing advance (Draw 1)"); § 10.2.8; Draw Schedule (Draw 1 = $9,475,000 at 0% cumulative completion); Term Sheet § 9.2.

**Finding.** Draw 1 disburses **$9,475,000** — 19.95% of the facility — at zero percent completion, comprising 100% of Soft Costs ($4,150,000), the entire Interest Reserve ($4,850,000) and the Origination Fee ($475,000). The equity-first principle is therefore satisfied only by an express carve-out for the largest single leakage in the facility. In substance, the Bank funds 100% of soft costs and the whole interest reserve at closing while no construction has occurred and no borrower cash is at risk.

Two further points on Draw 1. Disbursing 100% of soft costs up front is aggressive; soft costs are typically drawn as incurred, and a front-loaded soft cost draw means the Bank has no visibility into actual soft cost spend until much later. And the Interest Reserve is funded into the Loan and accrues interest on itself — so approximately $4.85 million of the draw earns interest that is itself paid from the same $4.85 million. That is conventional for construction loans, but it should be sized on a correctly stated rate (Issue 9).

**Recommendation.** (a) Keep the express Draw 1 carve-out but reduce Draw 1: disburse the Origination Fee and the Interest Reserve at closing, and disburse soft costs **as incurred**, against invoices, in the first several draws. That would reduce Draw 1 by up to $4,150,000 and materially improve the Bank's position without changing the facility amount. (b) If the Bank prefers to keep the single soft-cost draw, add a covenant that any soft cost line that comes in under budget is credited against the Loan, not retained by Borrower.

**Draft reference:** § 4.2(i) (carve-out with the drafting note); § 4.2(d)–(f).

### Issue 15 — Contingency Is Disbursed as a Scheduled Draw Rather Than Held as a Reserve

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Draw Schedule Section 1 (contingency amounts allocated in Draws 2 through 9: $521,250, $695,000, $695,000, $521,250, $102,500, $102,500, $113,750, $600,000); Credit Memorandum § 5.2 (contingency $1,440,000, i.e., 4.14% of hard costs).

**Finding.** The contingency is 4.14% of hard costs, which is within normal range. But the Draw Schedule treats it as a scheduled cost: $521,250 of contingency is disbursed at Draw 2 (foundation complete), before any change order exists, and the allocations step down across later draws. A contingency is a reserve — it should be held and disbursed only against an identified cost, because its entire purpose is to absorb the unforeseen. Disbursing it on a schedule converts the cushion into working capital.

**Recommendation.** Treat the contingency as a reserve (Draft § 4.1 and § 4.10): advanced only on the Bank's written approval of a specific requisition identifying the cost, its cause, and why a budgeted line does not cover it. Require the Construction Inspector to opine on the adequacy of the remaining contingency at each draw.

**Draft reference:** § 4.1; § 4.10(a); Exhibit C.
