## Part C — Title, Survey, Legal Description and Insurance

### Issue 16 — The Flood Zone Determination Is Internally Inconsistent

**Severity: Low.**

**Sources:** Appraisal § 2 ("Flood Zone X ... Community Panel Number 3710406400J, with an effective date of May 2, 2019"); Phase I ESA § 2.3 ("FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map Panel 3720098700J, effective March 2, 2009 ... Zone X").

**Finding.** Two different FEMA panel numbers and two different effective dates for the same site. Both conclude Zone X (minimal hazard) and both conclude that flood insurance is not required, so the conclusions agree even though the identifiers do not — one of the two is simply wrong or out of date. The Term Sheet (§ 19) requires flood insurance only if the Property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, and the Credit Memorandum (§ 8.1, § 12) says a determination will be obtained prior to closing.

**Recommendation.** Obtain a current standard flood hazard determination from a vendor acceptable to the Bank (a requirement of the Bank's own flood compliance program in any event) and do not rely on either document. Insert the determination in the closing file. Do not issue the Loan without a compliant determination, because the penalties for lending on improved real property in a SFHA without flood insurance are the Bank's, not the Borrower's.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(s); § 11.1(e).

### Issue 17 — The Legal Description Is Internally Inconsistent by 7.78 Feet

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Title Commitment Schedule A ¶ 6 (both parcel descriptions).

**Finding.** Parcel 1 is described as a rectangle with a north-south dimension of 298.62 feet and an east-west dimension of **305.41 feet**, beginning at a point in the eastern right-of-way margin of Foster Street. Parcel 2 begins at the **southwestern corner of Parcel 1** and its second call runs "South 87°45'27" East, **297.63 feet** to a new iron pipe, **being the southeastern corner of Parcel 1**." That is not possible: the southeastern corner of Parcel 1 is 305.41 feet east of its southwestern corner, not 297.63 feet. The description is short by **7.78 feet**, and Parcel 2's closing call ("thence along the southern boundary of Parcel 1, North 87°45'27" West, 297.63 feet to the point and place of BEGINNING") cannot close on Parcel 1's southern boundary for the same reason.

We checked the areas and they do reconcile (298.62 × 305.41 = 91,201.5 sf = 2.094 acres, against the stated 2.09; 201.88 × 297.63 = 60,085.5 sf = 1.379 acres, against the stated 1.38; total 151,287 sf = 3.473 acres), which suggests the error is in one distance call rather than in the overall geometry. But an error of 7.78 feet on a boundary that abuts a neighbouring parcel is exactly the kind of defect that produces a boundary dispute, an adverse possession claim, or a title exception that the Bank cannot remove.

The Term Sheet leaves Exhibit A blank: "*[Legal descriptions to be provided by Aldersgate Title & Escrow Inc. and confirmed by survey prior to closing.]*" The Draft reproduces the title commitment descriptions with the defect flagged.

**Recommendation.** Require the ALTA/NSPS survey as a hard closing condition (it already is under Title Commitment Schedule B–I ¶ 5 and Term Sheet § 18, but it has not been obtained) and require the surveyor to reconcile the two calls and to certify a corrected description. Conform Exhibit A to the certified survey, obtain the Title Company's confirmation that it will insure the corrected description, and delete or modify standard exceptions 2 and 3 (encroachments, overlaps, boundary disputes) on the basis of the survey and an owner's affidavit.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(i); Exhibit A and its drafting note.

### Issue 18 — Frontage and Parcel Acreage Figures Conflict Across the Record

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Appraisal § 2 ("approximately 420 feet of frontage along Foster Street on the western boundary"; combined 3.47 acres); Title Commitment Schedule A ¶ 6 (Parcel 1 2.09 acres; Parcel 2 1.38 acres; combined 3.47 acres); Phase I ESA § 1 and § 2.1 (Parcel 0821-04-72-3341 "approximately 1.89 acres"; Parcel 0821-04-72-4102 "approximately 1.58 acres"; combined 3.47 acres); Term Sheet § 2 (3.47 acres combined).

**Finding.** The combined acreage is consistently 3.47 acres, but the split between the parcels is not: title says 2.09 / 1.38; the environmental consultant says 1.89 / 1.58. Both sum to 3.47, which suggests one of them transposed the figures. The legal descriptions support the title figures (see Issue 17). Frontage is also inconsistent: the Appraisal says 420 feet along Foster Street; the two legal descriptions together give 298.62 + 201.88 = **500.50 feet**. Neither figure can be derived from the other.

This matters beyond tidiness. The environmental restriction applies to the northeast corner of **Parcel 0821-04-72-4102**, and the acreage of that parcel determines what proportion of it is restricted (0.38 ÷ 1.38 = 27.5% on the title figures; 0.38 ÷ 1.58 = 24.1% on the ESA figures). It also matters because the Phase I ESA's site orientation is inconsistent with the legal description: the ESA says the site "fronts Foster Street to the north" and that Parcel 0821-04-72-3341 is the "western parcel," while the legal descriptions place Foster Street on the **western** boundary and describe Parcel 2 as lying directly **south** of Parcel 1. The ESA's Figure 3 narrative repeats the error ("Foster Street is depicted along the northern boundary"; parcels arranged east-west).

**Recommendation.** Have the surveyor fix the parcel areas and frontage, and have the environmental consultant (or the Bank's environmental consultant) confirm the location of the Environmental Restricted Area **by reference to the recorded deed notice and the survey**, not by reference to the ESA's figure. The Loan Agreement should define "Environmental Restricted Area" by metes and bounds tied to the deed notice, which the Draft does (§ 1.1), precisely because the ESA's spatial description cannot be mapped onto the legal description.

**Draft reference:** § 1.1 ("Environmental Restricted Area"); Exhibit E Part 1; § 3.1(i).

### Issue 19 — Title Mechanics: Commitment Expiry, Underwriter, Escrow Officer and Lien Priority

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Title Commitment (Notes and Conditions; Note 4; Note 6); Term Sheet § 18.

**Finding.** Four points, none individually serious but each a closing-file defect if not addressed:

(a) **The commitment expires June 5, 2025.** The target Closing Date is February 28, 2025, so there is time; but if closing slips past June 5, 2025 (which is plausible given the open items in Section 8), the commitment must be extended before closing. Add to the closing checklist.

(b) **The issuing underwriter is not identified.** The commitment describes Aldersgate as "a title insurance company authorized to transact business in the State of North Carolina." A commitment should name the underwriting company whose policy will issue. Confirm whether Aldersgate is a direct writer or an agent, identify the underwriter, and confirm its financial strength and its acceptance of the Bank's required endorsements.

(c) **The escrow officer's email domain does not match the company.** Note 6 gives "plangley@crestviewtitle.com" for Aldersgate Title & Escrow Inc. That is either a legacy domain, a rebranding, or a template artifact. Confirm before wiring funds. Combined with Note 3 (Insured Closing Letter available on request), we recommend the Bank obtain the ICL and verify the wire instructions by telephone call-back to an independently confirmed number — a standard control that costs nothing.

(d) **Mechanics' lien priority.** Note 4 correctly flags N.C.G.S. § 44A-10: a claim of lien relates back to the first furnishing of labor or materials. If any work has begun or materials have been delivered before the Deed of Trust is recorded, the Bank's first-priority position is at risk. The record shows demolition in August 2024 and remediation in September 2024 — i.e., **substantial prior work on the site**. Whether that work gives rise to lien rights that relate back ahead of the Deed of Trust depends on whether the work was lienable and whether the claimant is the same "improver." Obtain a survey and an owner's/general contractor's affidavit confirming no labor or materials have been furnished for the **Project** before recording, and full lien releases from the demolition and remediation contractors before closing.

**Recommendation.** Add each item to the closing checklist; make the ICL and the pre-recording lien affidavits closing conditions.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(h); § 6.2(c).

### Issue 20 — Brightleaf Historic District ARC Approval: A Direct Conflict Between the Appraisal and the Title Officer

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Appraisal § 2 ("the architectural design has been reviewed and approved by the Brightleaf Historic District Association in accordance with the CC&Rs"); Title Commitment Schedule B–II ¶ 11 Note ("The Company has not received evidence that the current project plans ... have been submitted to or approved by the Brightleaf Historic District Association Architectural Review Committee"); Credit Memorandum §§ 9.8 and 10.1.14 (requires evidence of approval as a closing condition).

**Finding.** The two statements are irreconcilable. One of them is wrong. The Declaration recorded in Book 6984, Page 1155 (as amended in Book 7102, Page 388) requires prior written ARC approval for all new construction, requires submission at least **ninety (90) days** before the anticipated commencement of construction, deems approval to have been granted if the ARC does not act within **sixty (60) days** of a complete submission, and gives the Association and any affected owner a right to seek **injunctive relief to halt unapproved construction**. The Title Policy will carry the Declaration as an exception (Schedule B–II ¶ 11), so the Bank is not insured against an enforcement action.

If ARC approval has not been obtained and the deemed-approval period has not run, construction commenced on or after the Closing Date could be enjoined, which would be both a Completion Guaranty trigger and an Event of Default. As at the date of the Title Commitment (December 5, 2024), the 90-day pre-construction submission requirement could not be satisfied before a February 28, 2025 closing unless submission occurred by November 30, 2024 — and the title officer says there is no evidence of submission at all.

**Recommendation.** Treat this as a high-priority closing condition. Require from Borrower: (a) a dated copy of the ARC submission package; (b) the ARC's written approval, or (c) evidence that 60 days have elapsed since a complete submission without written action (deemed approval), together with a supporting opinion from Borrower's counsel on the sufficiency of the submission; and (d) written confirmation from the Title Company that it will not except to the absence of approval. Given the timeline, the Bank should expect that closing may need to be deferred, or the deemed-approval route relied on with a supporting legal opinion and, if available, a letter from the Association.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(l); § 6.3 (no modification to plans that would require further ARC approval).

### Issue 21 — The Permitted Exceptions Omit the Environmental Use Restriction

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 18 (four Permitted Exceptions: standard exceptions; Duke Energy easement; City stormwater easement; Brightleaf CC&Rs); Title Commitment Schedule B–II ¶¶ 7–13 (does not list an environmental use restriction); Appraisal § 8 ("This land use restriction is memorialized in a Declaration of Land Use Restrictions recorded at the Durham County Register of Deeds"); Phase I ESA § 7 Condition 4 (deed notice required to be recorded by November 29, 2024).

**Finding.** The Term Sheet enumerates four Permitted Exceptions and requires all other Schedule B–II matters to be removed at or before closing. The environmental land use restriction is a **fifth** exception that is not on the list. It cannot be removed — it is a condition of the NFA determination and must remain of record. Left unaddressed, the Term Sheet's own closing condition ("All other exceptions ... shall be removed, released, or otherwise satisfied on or before the Closing Date") is either unsatisfiable or would require the Bank to accept the Title Policy with an unapproved exception.

**Recommendation.** Add the recorded Declaration of Land Use Restrictions / deed notice as a Permitted Exception (Draft Exhibit H ¶ 6), with recording information inserted on recordation. This is also the natural place to define the Environmental Restricted Area by reference.

**Draft reference:** Exhibit H ¶ 6; § 1.1.

### Issue 22 — Builder's Risk Insurance Is Capped Below the Loan Amount

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 19 (Builder's Risk "in an amount not less than the total hard construction costs ($34,750,000)"); Credit Memorandum § 8.1 (same); Draw Schedule Detailed Budget line 3.09 (builder's risk premium $210,000).

**Finding.** The required limit equals the GMP exactly and is **$12,750,000 less than the Loan Amount**. Improvements under construction will be worth the GMP plus soft costs, developer fee and interest — that is, the Loan balance will exceed the insured value. On a total loss, the Bank's recovery would be capped at $34,750,000 against an exposure that, by the end of construction, will be approximately $47,500,000. The GMP is a cost, not an insurable value; the two are not the same measure.

**Recommendation.** Require builder's risk in an amount not less than the full insurable replacement value of the improvements (which should be not less than the GMP plus soft costs and the developer fee, and in no event less than the outstanding principal balance of the Loan). Add soft cost and, if available, delay-in-completion and ordinance-or-law coverage. The incremental premium is small relative to the coverage gap.

**Draft reference:** § 11.1(a) and its drafting note.
