## Part D — Environmental

### Issue 23 — The NFA Deed Notice Is Not Evidenced as Recorded, and the Record Conflicts on Whether It Was

**Severity: Critical.**

**Sources:** Phase I ESA § 1 and § 7 Condition 4 (deed notice to be recorded in the Durham County Registry within 60 days of the NFA letter dated September 30, 2024 — i.e., by **November 29, 2024**; and "as of the date of this Phase I ESA (October 28, 2024), the consultant was not provided evidence that the required deed notice has been recorded"); Appraisal § 8 ("memorialized in a Declaration of Land Use Restrictions recorded at the Durham County Register of Deeds"); Title Commitment (effective December 5, 2024; Schedule B–II ¶¶ 7–13 list no such instrument); Credit Memorandum § 10.1.7 (does not require the deed notice); Term Sheet § 17 (does not mention the deed notice).

**Finding.** Three documents, three positions, and the two that post-date the deadline disagree:

- Phase I ESA (October 28, 2024 — before the deadline): no evidence of recording; deadline had not yet passed.
- Appraisal (November 15, 2024 — before the deadline): states the restriction **is** recorded. The appraiser cannot have verified this; the deadline had not yet arrived when the appraisal was dated.
- Title Commitment (effective December 5, 2024 — **after** the deadline): performs a full public-records search of the Durham County Registry and lists thirteen Schedule B–II exceptions. **No declaration of land use restrictions appears.** If the deed notice had been recorded between September 30 and November 29, 2024, a commitment effective December 5, 2024 should show it.

The title officer's silence is significant evidence that the notice was **not** recorded. If it was not, the NFA determination is out of compliance with its own condition 4, and the site's regulatory status ("NFA — Conditional") is impaired. Neither the Term Sheet nor the Credit Memorandum makes the deed notice a closing condition; the Credit Memorandum's environmental condition (§ 10.1.7) addresses the vapor mitigation system, the use restriction and the Phase I/II, but not the notice.

**Recommendation.** This is a closing condition, not a diligence item. Require: (a) the recorded instrument, with recording data, or (b) written confirmation from NCDEQ that the recording deadline has been satisfied by a filing that the Title Company has not yet indexed, or (c) if the deadline was missed, written confirmation from NCDEQ that the NFA determination remains in effect notwithstanding late recording, or (d) a re-issued NFA letter. Do not accept Borrower's or Borrower's counsel's assurance; verify by direct search of the Durham County Registry. Also require an opinion of Borrower's counsel on the enforceability and scope of the recorded restriction, since a restriction that is not properly recorded may not run with the land.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(j); § 10.2(d); Exhibit H ¶ 6; Exhibit F.

### Issue 24 — The Scope of the Residential Use Restriction Has Not Been Established

**Severity: Critical.**

**Sources:** Phase I ESA § 7 Condition 2 and § 8.5 (the restriction limits the ~0.38-acre area to non-residential use; "any change in land use to residential or other sensitive use ... would require additional investigation and/or remediation ... and a modification to this NFA determination"); Phase I ESA § 8.5 and Recommendation 1 ("The consultant did not independently verify whether any residential units would be positioned directly above the affected 0.38-acre area ... this determination requires a detailed overlay of the proposed building floor plans with the environmental delineation map"; the client "should verify the spatial relationship"); Credit Memorandum §§ 8.2 and 9.5 ("the current project plans position 14 residential units in the building structure directly above this area at the podium level and upper floors"); Appraisal § 8 ("The appraiser has not adjusted the valuation conclusions to account for any potential impact of the NFA use restriction on the residential unit mix or leasability of units positioned above the restricted area"; "expresses no opinion as to the legal permissibility of the planned residential use").

**Finding.** The Credit Memorandum treats the spatial conflict as established. The Phase I ESA — the only document that examined the question — expressly declines to conclude anything and recommends that the overlay be performed. The number "14" in the Credit Memorandum corresponds exactly to the number of three-bedroom units in the Project (142 one-bedroom, 56 two-bedroom, **14 three-bedroom**); it appears that the unit count has migrated into the environmental analysis without any spatial finding behind it.

The Credit Memorandum's quantification is arithmetically correct but built on that unverified premise: 14 units × $2,245 × 12 = **$377,160** of annual revenue; NOI would fall from $4,876,800 to **$4,499,640**; DSCR would fall from 1.31x to **1.21x**, below the 1.25x minimum. If the CC&R assessment omission (Issue 29) is added on top, NOI is $4,465,140 and DSCR is **1.20x**.

We make three further observations. First, the restriction is a **land use** restriction on a defined area; whether it prohibits residential occupancy of floors built *above* that area, with a functioning vapor mitigation system, is a legal and regulatory question that requires NCDEQ's written position, not an inference. Second, the NFA letter's condition 1 requires the vapor mitigation system beneath "any enclosed structure" over the affected area — which implies that enclosed structure over the area was contemplated, but does not resolve occupancy type. Third, even if NCDEQ permits residential occupancy, the Appraiser has not adjusted value, so the $72,800,000 and $76,200,000 values on which the LTV ratios depend expressly ignore the issue.

**Recommendation.** (a) Require the Project Architect to deliver a **certified overlay** of the floor plans (levels 2–6) on the Phase II delineation, identifying every unit and every occupied space within or above the Environmental Restricted Area and stating the unit count. (b) On the basis of the overlay, require one of the three resolutions in Draft § 3.1(k): an NCDEQ modification permitting residential use; redesign eliminating residential occupancy of the affected area; or a written NCDEQ determination that the restriction does not extend to enclosed structures above the affected area where the vapor mitigation system operates. (c) At the Bank's election, permit closing and defer the resolution to a condition of advances for construction in the affected area (Draft § 10.6). (d) Correct the Credit Memorandum's § 8.2 and § 9.5 to record that the spatial relationship is **unverified**, and restate the DSCR sensitivity as a range rather than a finding. (e) Require the environmental consultant to review the architect's overlay and confirm it.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(k); § 10.2(b); § 10.6; § 10.9; § 5.7(e); definition of "Environmental Restricted Area" in § 1.1.

### Issue 25 — The NFA Conditions and Their Deadlines Are Not Carried into the Loan Documents

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Phase I ESA § 7 (four conditions: vapor mitigation system with as-builts and performance verification to NCDEQ within **90 days of completion of the building foundation**; non-residential use restriction; NCDEQ-approved soil management plan before any subsurface disturbance; deed notice within 60 days); Term Sheet § 17 (mentions only the vapor mitigation system); Credit Memorandum § 10.3 (mentions the vapor mitigation system and general environmental compliance).

**Finding.** The Term Sheet's environmental covenant addresses one of four conditions. It says nothing about the land use restriction as a covenant, nothing about the deed notice, nothing about the soil management plan, and nothing about the 90-day as-built/verification deadline. The Credit Memorandum adds the use restriction (as a closing condition) but not the operational covenants. There are therefore no enforceable documentary obligations covering three of the four NFA conditions, one of which (the soil management plan) must be in place **before** any excavation in the affected area — work that will occur during the construction period.

**Recommendation.** Article X of the Draft carries all four conditions as continuing covenants, together with (a) the as-built and performance verification deadline, (b) a prohibition on subsurface disturbance without an approved soil management plan, (c) an obligation to deliver all NCDEQ correspondence within ten business days, (d) an annual compliance certification, and (e) an Event of Default for non-compliance (Draft § 14.1(n)). The Bank should also require Borrower to deliver the NCDEQ-approved soil management plan and the vapor mitigation system design as **closing** deliverables (Draft § 3.1(j)), not as construction-period items, because the soil management plan governs the very first excavation in the affected area and the vapor system must be designed into the foundation.

**Draft reference:** Article X; § 3.1(j); § 14.1(n); Exhibit E Part 2.

### Issue 26 — Remediation Quantities and Costs Are Stated Inconsistently

**Severity: Low (but a useful diagnostic).**

**Sources:** Appraisal § 8 (approximately **320** cubic yards of petroleum-impacted soil); Credit Memorandum § 8.2 (approximately **340** cubic yards); Phase I ESA § 6.1 (approximately **1,200** cubic yards, with excavation to approximately 12 feet below grade); Phase I ESA § 6.3 (remediation cost approximately **$485,000**); Appraisal § 6.1 (demolition and remediation "cost approximately **$680,000**"); Draw Schedule Detailed Budget line 3.05 (Environmental & Geotechnical, $185,000, for "Phase I, Phase II, remediation monitoring, geotech").

**Finding.** Three different volumes for the same excavation (320 / 340 / 1,200 cubic yards — a factor of nearly four). Two different costs ($485,000 / $680,000, the latter apparently including demolition). The budget carries $185,000, which is less than the remediation cost alone, but the remediation was completed in September 2024 and is a past cost, so it is presumably embedded in the land basis rather than in the forward budget — a point neither document states.

The volume matters because it is the best available proxy for whether the remediation was complete. The Phase I ESA explains that contamination above residential standards remains in situ at depths of 10 to 14 feet along the eastern boundary "where excavation was limited by the proximity of the adjoining property and critical underground utility infrastructure" — so excavation was **partial by design**, not by accident. That is consistent with the NFA determination and the use restriction, but it should be documented accurately.

**Recommendation.** Obtain the Phase II report and the remediation completion report (Issue 27) and take the figures from primary documents. Correct the Appraisal and Credit Memorandum summaries. Do not finalize Exhibit E of the Loan Agreement until the volumes and costs are confirmed.

**Draft reference:** Exhibit E Part 1 (table, with the inconsistency flagged).

### Issue 27 — Environmental Document Gaps and Reliance

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Phase I ESA (cover page; § 5 note; § 11; Appendix list); Credit Memorandum § 13 (diligence table).

**Finding.** Four gaps:

(a) **The Phase II report is not in the record.** The Phase I ESA refers to it as a separate engagement (Sentinel Project No. **SEC-2024-1089**, dated August 30, 2024) and summarises its results "for reference and completeness." The Bank's environmental condition (Term Sheet § 11; Credit Memorandum § 10.1.7) requires "related Phase II investigation results." A lender should not close on a summary of a Phase II in a Phase I. Obtain the report.

(b) **The NFA letter is not in the record.** Its terms are known only through the Phase I ESA's summary (§ 7). Obtain the letter itself, with Site ID IHSB-24-1203 / Incident No. NONUST-24-0847.

(c) **No Phase II reliance letter.** The Phase I ESA refers to a reliance letter for the Bank dated October 28, 2024 (Appendix G). There is no equivalent for the Phase II. Without reliance, the Bank cannot sue the consultant on the Phase II.

(d) **Phase I shelf life.** Under 40 C.F.R. § 312.20(c) the Phase I ESA (October 28, 2024) has a 180-day shelf life expiring **April 26, 2025**. A February 28, 2025 closing is inside the window; a closing after that date requires an update to preserve All Appropriate Inquiries protection — which matters directly to the Bank's CERCLA secured-creditor and bona fide prospective purchaser positions. Given the open items in Section 8, a delay past April 26, 2025 is a realistic possibility.

**Recommendation.** Make the Phase II report, the NFA letter and both reliance letters closing deliverables, and add a condition requiring an updated Phase I if closing occurs after April 26, 2025 (Draft § 6.16). Consider requiring the reliance letters to name the Bank's successors and assigns, since the Loan may be participated or assigned.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(j); § 6.16.
