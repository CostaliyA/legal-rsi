## Part G — Entity, Governance and Intercreditor

### Issue 37 — The Operating Agreement's 75% Supermajority Gives the Mezzanine Lender's Affiliate a Veto Over the Loan Itself

**Severity: Critical.**

**Sources:** Organizational Chart § 1.2 (membership: Elena Vasquez-Torres 62%; Brightleaf Investors Group LLC 28%; Reginald K. Osei 10%); § 2.2 (Operating Agreement § 3.1: Managing Member's capital expenditure authority capped at $500,000 individually; § 3.2: the following require approval of members holding at least **75%**: "(a) any sale, transfer, or encumbrance of all or substantially all of the Company's assets; (b) any financing or refinancing of the Property in a principal amount in excess of $1,000,000; (c) the admission of any new Member; (d) any amendment to this Operating Agreement; (e) any voluntary dissolution ... or the filing of any petition for relief under any federal or state bankruptcy or insolvency law"); § 2.5 (Operating Agreement § 9.1: no member may pledge its interest without approval of the Managing Member and members holding at least 75%); § 1.2 and Credit Memorandum § 2.2 and § 4.4 (Thomas R. Chen is the Managing Member of both Brightleaf Investors Group LLC, a 28% member, and Brightleaf Mezzanine Capital LLC, the Mezzanine Lender).

**Finding.** The Organizational Chart itself does the arithmetic and states the conclusion: Ms. Vasquez-Torres (62%) plus Mr. Osei (10%) = **72%**, which is **below the 75% threshold**, so "Brightleaf Investors Group LLC holds an effective veto right over major decisions, as no combination of the other two members alone can reach the required supermajority."

Applied to this transaction, the consequence is significant and does not appear to have been addressed anywhere in the credit file:

1. **The Loan itself requires 75% approval.** Operating Agreement § 3.2(b) requires 75% approval for "any financing or refinancing of the Property in a principal amount in excess of $1,000,000." The $47,500,000 Loan is such a financing. Brightleaf Investors Group LLC's consent is therefore a condition of the Borrower's own authorization.
2. **The Membership Interest Pledge requires the same consent.** § 9.1 requires Managing Member approval *and* 75% approval for any pledge of a membership interest. The Bank's first-priority pledge of 100% of the membership interests cannot be granted without Brightleaf's consent.
3. **The SPE amendment and the independent manager require it too.** § 3.2(d) requires 75% approval for any amendment to the Operating Agreement (Issue 38).
4. **Every major decision during the life of the Loan will require it** — including a sale or refinancing at maturity, and any "encumbrance of all or substantially all of the Company's assets."

So the affiliate of the subordinated lender, which is also a 28% equity holder, holds a blocking position over: the senior Loan, the senior lender's collateral, the SPE amendment the senior lender requires, and the take-out sale or refinancing on which the senior lender's repayment depends. That is a structural conflict the Bank should address expressly, and it is not addressed in the Term Sheet or the Credit Memorandum, which discuss the affiliate relationship only in terms of substantive consolidation risk (Credit Memorandum § 9.3).

**Recommendation.** (a) Require a **unanimous written consent of all members** (100%), not merely the 75% required by the Operating Agreement, expressly authorizing the Loan, each Loan Document, the Membership Interest Pledge, the Deed Notice, the SPE amendment, the Independent Manager appointment and the Intercreditor Agreement (Draft § 3.1(d)). (b) Require an amendment to Operating Agreement § 3.2 that carves out from the 75% requirement all matters expressly approved by the members in the closing consent and all actions taken by the Bank in the exercise of its remedies, so that the Bank is not required to obtain a consent from an affiliate of the subordinated lender in order to enforce its own collateral. (c) Obtain Borrower's counsel's written confirmation that the consents delivered satisfy §§ 3.2 and 9.1 as in effect on the Closing Date. (d) Consider requiring, as a condition to any extension or to any refinancing of the Mezzanine Loan, that the 75% threshold be reduced to a simple majority, so that Brightleaf cannot block the take-out.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(d); § 3.1(e); § 16.5; signature page (member acknowledgment and consent).

### Issue 38 — The SPE Amendment and the Independent Manager Requirement Depend on the Same 75% Consent, and the Operating Agreement Has None of the Required Provisions

**Severity: High.**

**Sources:** Organizational Chart § 1.1 and § 2.2 ("The Operating Agreement, as currently in effect, does not contain any provision requiring the appointment of an independent director or independent manager ... There are no separateness covenants ... no prohibition on commingling of funds with affiliates, and no requirement that the Company obtain a non-consolidation opinion"); Term Sheet § 20; Credit Memorandum §§ 2.1, 9.3, 10.1.4.

**Finding.** The Credit Memorandum correctly identifies the need for SPE covenants, an independent manager and a non-consolidation opinion, and makes them conditions of closing. But the Operating Agreement lacks all of them, so they can only be obtained by amendment — and any amendment requires 75% approval (Issue 37), which means Brightleaf must consent to the very protections designed to address the risk that Brightleaf's affiliate creates. The Organizational Chart confirms the current position in terms, and its certification is both undated and unsigned.

There is a further practical point the Credit Memorandum does not address: an independent manager must be **identified and appointed** before closing, not merely provided for. The Bank should interview and approve the individual, confirm independence in writing, and confirm the person will serve. A requirement in an operating agreement that nobody has agreed to perform is not a protection.

**Recommendation.** (a) Require the amended and restated operating agreement as a closing deliverable, in a form the Bank's counsel has reviewed (not merely "as amended for SPE covenants"). (b) Obtain the 100% member consent described in Issue 37. (c) Require evidence of the Independent Manager's appointment, a signed independence certificate, and a consent to act, together with a side letter acknowledging the Bank's rights under the Loan Documents. (d) Consider requiring a special purpose, bankruptcy-remote entity as the Independent Manager (a common institutional solution), so that the protection does not depend on a natural person's continued willingness to serve. (e) Require the non-consolidation opinion to be **reasoned**, addressed to the Bank, and re-delivered (or brought down) on the occurrence of any bankruptcy of a Related Entity.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(e); § 9.2; § 9.3; § 9.4; § 14.1(p).

### Issue 39 — The Two 100% Membership Interest Pledges Cannot Both Be First Priority, and the Intercreditor Agreement Is Not Executed

**Severity: Critical.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet § 1.4 (Mezzanine Loan "secured by a pledge of 100% of the membership interests in the Borrower") and § 8 (senior collateral includes a "Pledge of 100% of membership interests in the Borrower by all members"); Credit Memorandum § 4.2 (identifies the conflict and proposes that the senior lender hold the first-priority pledge and the mezzanine lender the second-priority pledge) and §§ 9.4, 10.1.3; Organizational Chart § 1.2 and § 2.5 (mezzanine pledge exists; Operating Agreement provides no mechanism to resolve competing pledges); Credit Memorandum § 4.4 and § 10.1.3 (intercreditor agreement "currently in the process of being negotiated ... but has not yet been executed").

**Finding.** The Credit Memorandum identifies this correctly: only one lender can hold a first-priority security interest in the same collateral, and as documented the two pledges are mutually exclusive as first-priority interests. The Credit Memorandum's proposed resolution — senior first, mezzanine second, with a 90-day standstill, cure rights, a purchase option, and bankruptcy protections — is the right answer and is what we have drafted (Draft § 16.3).

The issue is timing and enforceability. The intercreditor agreement is not executed, and it is the mechanism by which the Bank's collateral position is created. If the Loan closes first and the intercreditor agreement follows, the Bank may hold a first-priority pledge of membership interests from three pledgors while the mezzanine lender holds a competing 100% pledge; priority then turns on UCC filing sequence and, for a security interest in a certificated or uncertificated LLC interest, on control — which for LLC interests means the applicable UCC Article 8 or 9 control mechanics and, in practice, a control agreement or possession of the certificates if the interests are certificated. Filing sequence alone may not resolve priority, and the Operating Agreement's silence on competing pledges (Organizational Chart § 2.5) means there is no contractual priority.

Note also that the Credit Memorandum's own description of the Mezzanine Loan's collateral is inconsistent with the sources and uses: a pledge of membership interests is not a mortgage on the Property, so the mezzanine lender would be lending $9,200,000 against an equity pledge while its own proceeds fund the land (Issue 5). That is a structure question about the mezzanine lender's position, not the Bank's, but it bears on how the mezzanine lender will behave in a workout.

**Recommendation.** Treat the executed intercreditor agreement as an **unwaivable** closing condition (Draft § 3.1(c)), as the Credit Memorandum itself requires ("may not be waived," § 4.4). Confirm in the intercreditor agreement: (a) express subordination of the mezzanine pledge; (b) the Bank's first-priority position in both the real property collateral and the membership interests; (c) the 90-day standstill; (d) the purchase option at par plus accrued; (e) the prohibition on amending the mezzanine documents without the Bank's consent; (f) the bankruptcy filing prohibition and non-interference covenant; and (g) an express acknowledgment of the Thomas R. Chen common control. Also confirm whether the membership interests are certificated and, if so, obtain the certificates and undated stock (membership interest) powers; if uncertificated, obtain a control agreement from Borrower as issuer or a UCC-1 filing plus the issuer's acknowledgment. Finally, confirm that the Operating Agreement amendment described in Issue 37 removes any consent requirement that would impede the Bank's exercise of remedies against the pledged interests.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(c) (non-waivable); § 12.2; § 16.2; § 16.3; § 6.13; § 7.8.
