## Part H — Legal, Documentation and Open Items

### Issue 40 — North Carolina Law Considerations

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Credit Memorandum § 4.3 ("North Carolina is a judicial foreclosure state and ... deficiency judgments following foreclosure are subject to the fair price confirmation hearing requirements of N.C.G.S. § 45-21.36"); Term Sheet §§ 21.4 (North Carolina law and Durham venue), 8 (Deed of Trust), 16 (remedies); Title Commitment Note 4 (N.C.G.S. § 44A-10).

**Finding.** Four North Carolina points require attention before the Loan Documents are finalized, and we have drafted to each (provisions noted below), but each should be confirmed by you:

(a) **Deficiency after power of sale.** Our understanding is that North Carolina has recognized power-of-sale foreclosure since 1967 under Part 6 of Article 2A of Chapter 45 of the General Statutes (N.C.G.S. § 45-21.1 et seq.), and the Credit Memorandum's reference to "judicial foreclosure" is imprecise. The material point is that N.C.G.S. § 45-21.36 entitles the mortgagor to a credit for the fair market value of the property if it is sold for less than fair market value in a power-of-sale foreclosure, and that this credit applies in the deficiency action. The statute limits deficiency exposure in practice whenever the collateral is sold below value. We have drafted the guaranties so that (i) the Partial Repayment Guaranty is a separate payment obligation limited to the Guaranteed Amount rather than a guaranty of a deficiency, and (ii) the Completion Guaranty is an independent obligation to fund completion costs rather than a deficiency obligation. **This is the most important legal issue in the documentation and we recommend it be reviewed before the Guaranties are finalized**; the Interaction between § 45-21.36 and a "bad boy" full-recourse guaranty is not free from doubt, and the Bank should understand that its practical recourse may be to the Completion Guaranty and the Carry Guaranty rather than to a deficiency judgment.

(b) **Usury.** N.C.G.S. § 24-1.1 permits the parties to agree to any rate of interest on a loan in the principal amount of $25,000 or more. The Loan is $47,500,000. The Default Rate (contract + 5.00%) is not constrained by the 16% general limit, but we have included a savings clause (Draft § 2.11) as a matter of course.

(c) **UCC Article 9 and the pledge.** The Membership Interest Pledge must be perfected by UCC-1 filing against each pledgor (each of the three members) in the correct jurisdiction of organization, and priority over the mezzanine lender's competing pledge should be established by the intercreditor agreement rather than by filing sequence. See Issue 39.

(d) **Mechanics' lien relation-back.** N.C.G.S. § 44A-10 and the title officer's Note 4; see Issue 19(d).

**Recommendation.** Please confirm points (a) and (b) and, if you agree on (a), consider whether the Bank should take an additional completion-related guaranty or a limited payment guaranty sized to the expected § 45-21.36 shortfall.

**Draft reference:** Article XV (particularly § 15.3); § 2.11; § 12.2; § 13.4 and its drafting note.

### Issue 41 — Contractor Financial Condition and Bonding Are Not a Term Sheet Condition

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet §§ 11, 19 (closing conditions — no contractor financials or bonds); Credit Memorandum § 9.8 ("The Bank should verify the financial strength and payment and performance bonding capacity of the contractor prior to closing. Evidence of the contractor's financial statements and bonding capacity should be included in the closing deliverables"); § 13 (diligence table: GMP contract "to be reviewed by Bank's counsel and construction inspector prior to closing").

**Finding.** The Term Sheet does not require contractor financial statements or payment and performance bonds. The Credit Memorandum says both should be required. The GMP contract provides cost certainty but not performance security; if the General Contractor fails, the Completion Guaranty obliges the Guarantors to complete, and the contingency of $1,440,000 (4.14% of hard costs) is the only built-in buffer. There is no performance bond in the record, and it is not clear whether one is contemplated.

**Recommendation.** Add as closing conditions: (a) the General Contractor's audited financial statements for the two most recent fiscal years, with the Bank's credit group reviewing and approving; (b) evidence of payment and performance bonding capacity sufficient for a $34,750,000 contract, or an executed performance and payment bond; (c) the General Contractor's consent to the collateral assignment of the Construction Contract, with a direct agreement giving the Bank step-in rights; and (d) confirmation that the GMP contract contains liquidated damages for delay consistent with the construction-period milestones. Item (c) matters because a collateral assignment of a construction contract is of limited value without the contractor's acknowledgment.

**Draft reference:** § 3.1(n); § 6.2(c); § 12.1(d).

### Issue 42 — Internal Inconsistencies in the Term Sheet

**Severity: Medium.**

**Sources:** Term Sheet, as noted below.

**Finding.** Five internal inconsistencies in the executed Term Sheet, each requiring a decision in documentation:

1. **Loan-to-value.** § 4 (65% as-completed) and § 6.2 (65% as-completed and as-stabilized) versus the actual ratio of 65.25%. See Issue 2.
2. **"Single advance facility."** § 3.1 describes the Loan as "structured as a single advance facility, with loan proceeds to be disbursed in accordance with the approved draw schedule." A nine-draw construction facility is not a single-advance facility. We read it as meaning a single commitment drawn in multiple advances, and have drafted accordingly (§ 2.1).
3. **Expense deposit.** § 22 describes the $75,000 expense deposit as "non-refundable," and then in the same sentence provides that "if the Loan does not close for any reason, any unused portion of the expense deposit shall be returned to the Borrower, net of expenses incurred through the date of termination." Those cannot both be true. The second sentence is the operative one; the deposit is a retainer against expenses, not a fee.
4. **Guarantor net worth cross-reference.** § 15 refers to a threshold "set forth in Section 6.4," which is the Borrower's covenant. See Issue 32.
5. **Extension conditions.** § 3.2 delegates them to the Loan Agreement without any parameters. See Issue 36.

**Recommendation.** For (1), (4) and (5) the answer lies in Amendments to the credit and the Loan Documents, not in a revised Term Sheet (the Term Sheet is executed and non-binding as to terms). For (2) and (3), treat the drafting in the Loan Documents as controlling and confirm by side letter or in the closing instruction. Note that the Term Sheet's confidentiality, exclusivity and expense deposit provisions are expressly binding (§ 22), so (3) matters: the Bank's entitlement to retain the deposit depends on which sentence governs. The Draft preserves those three provisions (§ 18.7).

**Draft reference:** § 2.1; § 18.7; § 8.2.
