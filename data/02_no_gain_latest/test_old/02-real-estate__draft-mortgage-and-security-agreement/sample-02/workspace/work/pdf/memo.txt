I have prepared the first draft of the Mortgage and Security Agreement (the
“Mortgage”) together with this memorandum, which records every issue I
identified in the source record, every figure I recomputed, the drafting

The draft is prepared and recordable in form, but three findings in the
record are serious enough that I do not believe the loan should close on
the present documents without resolution. They are: (i) the collateral
appraisal is internally irreconcilable — its stated income approach
supports roughly $5.4 million as-is and $10.0 million as-stabilized

DSCR testing does not begin until month 24, leaving roughly $457,000 of

4. ISSUE ANALYSIS

A. Collateral Valuation, Loan Sizing and Financial Covenants
A-1 (Critical) — The appraisal’s income approach cannot support its
value conclusions.

Source: Appraisal §§6.2, 6.3 and 7. The appraiser states as-is NOI of
“approximately $332,000 to $345,000,” applies a 6.25% going-in cap rate, and
concludes an as-is value of $18,200,000; and states stabilized NOI of “$582,000–
$592,000,” applies 5.85%, and concludes $23,500,000. Recomputation: direct
capitalization of $338,000 at 6.25% yields $5,408,000; of $587,000 at 5.85%
yields $10,034,188. Conversely, an $18,200,000 value at a 6.25% cap rate
requires NOI of $1,137,500, and a $23,500,000 value at 5.85% requires NOI of
$1,374,750 — roughly 2.3 to 3.4 times the stated NOI. The implied cap rates on
the concluded values are 1.86% (as-is) and 2.50% (as-stabilized), neither of
which is a market rate and one of which the appraiser itself rejects (it selected
5.75%–6.50% as the market range).

Analysis: The as-is conclusion is separately corroborated by the sales
comparison approach ($587/SF against comparables at $550–$620/SF), so the
value may be defensible on that basis; but the report’s reconciliation is
unexplained, and the income approach as summarized cannot be replicated. The
as-stabilized conclusion at $758/SF sits 22%–38% above the highest comparable,
which is a further unsupported premium. For a state-chartered bank subject to
Title XI of FIRREA and the interagency appraisal regulations, an appraisal
whose reported approach does not support its conclusion is a supervisory and
safety-and-soundness exposure independent of the credit risk.

Recommendation: (a) obtain the complete appraisal report (the “Summary” is
described in the report’s own Addenda as an extract from a “Complete Appraisal



           Drafting Issues Memorandum — CHB CRE Loan No. 2025-CHS-0047          |     Page 6 of 37
Report”); (b) require written clarification/correction from Pinnacle Valuation
Services as to the omitted reconciliation step, and a corrected as-is and as-
stabilized opinion if the income approach was in fact as summarized; (c)
consider commissioning an appraisal review (or second appraisal) before
closing; (d) confirm the appraisal file satisfies the Bank’s appraisal policy and
that the “Summary Appraisal Report” format (a reporting option discontinued
under USPAP effective 2020) is acceptable. The Mortgage does not recite value,
so no conforming change to the draft is required; the issue is a closing condition
and credit-file item.

A-2 (Critical) — Occupancy is overstated by approximately 12 percentage
points, and the error is embedded in the appraisal.

Source: Term Sheet §2.3 recites “Current occupancy is approximately sixty-eight
percent (68%)” but in the same paragraph recites 13,600 SF of vacancy,
“approximately 43.9% of total building area.” Appraisal §1 and §3.1 recite “68%
(21,400 SF of 31,000 SF occupied)” and “vacant area is 13,600 SF (32%)” —
figures that cannot both be true (21,400 + 13,600 = 35,000 SF against a 31,000
SF building). The Rent Roll workbook independently computes 56.13%.

Recomputation: Occupied = Bay 1 (3,800) + Bay 3 (3,800) + Suite A (9,800) =
17,400 SF = 17,400 ÷ 31,000 = 56.13%. Vacant = 3,800 + 9,800 = 13,600 SF
= 43.87%. The $19.40/SF weighted average rent in Appraisal §3.1 divides
$415,100 by 21,400 SF; dividing by the correct 17,400 SF gives $23.86/SF,
which is what the rent roll workbook shows. The 68% figure appears to derive
from 21,400 ÷ 31,000 (69.03%), suggesting a single transcription error that
propagated from the Term Sheet into the appraisal.

Analysis: This matters beyond presentation. The appraisal’s as-is analysis “after
adjustment for current vacancy” is expressly premised on 68% occupancy; a 12-
point understatement of vacancy in a value-add underwriting is material to the
as-is value, to the 81.04% as-is LTV, and to the lease-up assumptions behind the
as-stabilized value. It also understates the leasing required to reach
stabilization: 95% occupancy requires leasing 12,050 SF of the 13,600 SF of
vacancy (88.6% of all vacancy), i.e., essentially all of Bay 2 and most of Suite B.

Recommendation: require written confirmation from the appraiser of actual
occupancy and whether it was used in the as-is analysis, and a corrected report
if not; correct the rent roll and occupancy recitals in all closing documents; and
note the discrepancy in the credit file. In the Mortgage I have recited only
verified figures (17,400 SF occupied, 13,600 SF vacant, 56.13%, $415,100
annual base rent) in Schedule 4.1(n) and avoided any percentage-derived
value representation.

amount to a coverage-supportable level; (b) extend the interest-only period
beyond month 24 so that the test is run on interest-only debt service; (c) reset
the covenant to a market level for a value-add asset (e.g., 1.10x interest-only,
stepping to 1.25x on stabilization), or defer the first test to a stabilization
milestone measured by occupancy rather than by date; (d) require a debt service
reserve or cash-management waterfall with a sub-1.10x cash-sweep trigger; (e)
tie any reduction in the guaranties to occupancy milestones. I have drafted the
covenant exactly as the Term Sheet provides (Mortgage §12.2) because I am not
authorized to change commercial terms, but I have added a cure mechanism (a
deposit into a DSCR Cure Reserve sized so that the DSCR recalculates to at
least 1.25x, capped at two uses, and not treated as a prepayment) because Term
Sheet §11.1(i) describes the default as occurring “without cure,” which
presupposes that a cure exists. Please confirm the cure mechanism or direct
that it be deleted.

E-1 (High) — Your proposed formula deletes the Term Sheet’s 1.00%

7. ITEMS I COULD NOT RESOLVE
In fairness to you and to Marcus, these are the points on which I am not able to
give a concluded view on the present record:

   (a) Whether the collateral value supports the Loan. The appraisal is internally
       irreconcilable (A-1) and its occupancy premise is wrong (A-2). Until one of those is
       corrected, the 81.04% as-is LTV — and therefore the credit decision — cannot be
       validated. I do not express a view on value.
   (b) Whether the DSCR covenant can be satisfied in any realistic scenario. On the
       record, it cannot (A-3). Whether the shortfall is addressed by loan size, IO extension,
       covenant level, reserve or guaranty is a credit decision for the Bank.
   (c) The treatment of the 50 bp spread in the yield maintenance formula (E-2).
       Your reading is the better commercial reading; the Term Sheet’s text supports the
       other. This needs the Bank’s written confirmation, and I have drafted to your
       instruction rather than attempting to split the difference.
   (d) The scope and quality of title coverage over the Anson Lane strip (B-1). The
       commitment does not commit to affirmative coverage and reserves the right to limit
       it. Until Prestige and Commonwealth respond in writing, I cannot confirm there is no
       gap in the insured collateral.
   (e) The extent of the strip and the access rights it supports (B-1). The title
       commitment and the Phase I ESA describe the strip differently, and no survey extract
       is in the record. This bears on the tenants’ rear access, loading and parking
       entitlements, and therefore on the lease representations and the estoppels.
   (f) South Carolina recording formalities, the applicability of the 0.37% rate to a
       mortgage instrument, and whether the Uniform Assignment of Rents Act
       citation is § 29-3-610 or § 29-3-100 (B-10, C-1). These are confirmable items
       rather than analysis, and I have flagged them rather than resolving them from
       secondary recollection.
   (g) Whether Borrower’s counsel will accept the additions in Section 5 — in
       particular the change-of-control restriction, the Independent Manager amendment
       and the no-cure LUR and SPE defaults. The Operating Agreement Amendment is the
       most likely flashpoint, because it requires all three members and alters governance
       rights that the passive members bargained for.
