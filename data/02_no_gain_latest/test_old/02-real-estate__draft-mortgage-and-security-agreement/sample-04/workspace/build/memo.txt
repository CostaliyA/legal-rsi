@H1:1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
@P:This memorandum accompanies the initial draft of the Mortgage, Assignment of Leases and Rents, Security Agreement and Fixture Filing (the "**Mortgage**") prepared for Calverley Heritage Bank (the "**Lender**") in connection with the $14,750,000 loan (the "**Loan**") to Palmetto Gateway Partners LLC (the "**Borrower**"). The draft responds to your markup notes of March 18, 2025 and implements the Executed Term Sheet dated April 1, 2025 (Deal Reference CHB CRE Loan No. 2025-CHS-0047) (the "**Term Sheet**").
@P:This memorandum records (a) the cross-document verification work performed on the record before drafting, including independent recomputation of every stated total, ratio, date interval, and threshold that the Mortgage or the closing depends upon; (b) each drafting issue identified, with the source of the proposition, the treatment adopted in the draft, and the action still required; (c) each point on which the record is internally inconsistent or in which your March 18 instructions depart from the binding Term Sheet, together with the resolution adopted and the instruction requested; and (d) the additional closing conditions that should be added to Section 12.1 of the Term Sheet.
@P:**Hierarchy applied where sources conflict.** The Term Sheet is an executed, binding commitment; the March 18 markup notes are internal drafting instructions directed to the template. Where the two conflict, the draft follows the Term Sheet and the divergence is flagged in Section 7 below, because the Term Sheet may not be materially departed from in the Loan Documents without Lender's sign-off (Term Sheet § 14.4) and because the foreclosure, assignment-of-rents, prepayment, and financial covenant provisions must be enforceable as written rather than aspirational. Each such departure is identified so that you and Marcus Prideaux can confirm or reverse it before the draft is released on April 1.
@P:**Conventions in the draft.** The Mortgage is organized in eighteen Articles with five Exhibits. Bracketed items are deliberately left to be completed at or before recording: the closing date (drafted as "April [15], 2025" pending confirmation), the deed recording references in Exhibit A, the borrower's counsel's notice address, and the witness and notarial blocks. Exhibit A contains a drafting note recording that the plat-reference description must be replaced with the surveyor's metes-and-bounds description conforming to the insured description before the instrument is recorded; that description cannot be drafted from the record available to us and should not be guessed. Section references in this memorandum to the Mortgage are to the draft as delivered with this memorandum.
@P:**Scope limitation.** This memorandum addresses the Mortgage only. It does not opine on the Note, Guaranty, Environmental Indemnity, or the title and survey matters beyond their effect on the Mortgage. Nothing herein is a legal opinion, and no conclusion on title, enforceability, or tax has been rendered.

@H2:C. Interest Rate, Prepayment, and Charges
@H3:C1. Yield maintenance — the March 18 formula departs from the Term Sheet
@P:**Source.** Term Sheet § 4.2(b) (greater of 1.00% of the outstanding principal or the present value of the excess of interest at the Note rate over interest at "the treasury rate plus fifty (50) basis points," discounted at the treasury rate); March 18 markup note § 5 (drafting instruction and four open interpretive questions).
@P:**Issue.** The formula you dictated differs from the Term Sheet in three respects. It (i) omits the one percent (1.00%) minimum, substituting a "positive excess, if any" measure with a floor of zero; (ii) measures the premium as the present value of all remaining scheduled payments less the principal prepaid, rather than as the present value of the net interest differential; and (iii) instructs that the fifty basis points be added to the Note rate rather than to the treasury rate.
@P:**Analysis.** Each difference matters. Omitting the 1% floor would give away value and contradicts an express Term Sheet term; the Term Sheet is a binding commitment and § 14.4 permits additional provisions only if not "materially inconsistent" with it. The "present value of remaining payments less principal" measure is a legitimate yield maintenance formulation, but it is not the one the parties agreed, and it can produce a premium that the agreed formula would not. Adding the spread to the Note rate rather than the reinvestment rate inverts the Term Sheet's structure: § 4.2(b)(ii) expressly applies the fifty basis points to the treasury rate. Your interpretive questions are answered by the Term Sheet itself: (c) the premium is the greater of the 1% minimum and the yield maintenance computation, so there is a minimum; and (d) a zero or negative computed differential is cured by the 1% minimum, so the floor question does not arise during the Yield Maintenance Period.
@P:**Draft treatment.** Section 3.9 implements the Term Sheet formula: the greater of (a) 1.00% of the principal prepaid and (b) the present value of the excess of interest at the Note Rate over interest at the Treasury Rate plus 0.50%, discounted at the Treasury Rate, monthly, over the remaining months to the last day of the Yield Maintenance Period. The Treasury Rate is defined by reference to the constant-maturity yield reported in Federal Reserve Statistical Release H.15, interpolated linearly where no published maturity matches, with a fallback to an on-the-run yield reported by a pricing service. Lender's calculation is conclusive absent manifest error. Exhibit D contains a worked illustration demonstrating that no element of the calculation requires the exercise of discretion — a point we will want if the formula is ever attacked as a penalty.
@P:**Action.** Confirm with Marcus: (i) that the Term Sheet formulation governs and your draft language is superseded; and (ii) whether Lender wishes to add a prepayment premium payable on a prepayment after a casualty or condemnation (see C5). If Lender prefers your formulation, the Term Sheet must be amended and the change priced. @H2:J. South Carolina Enforcement and Recording
@H3:J1. Judicial foreclosure; no power of sale
@P:**Analysis.** South Carolina is a judicial foreclosure jurisdiction. The draft disclaims any power of sale in terms (Sections 2.9 and 16.2), provides for a foreclosure action in the Charleston County Court of Common Pleas, preserves suit on the Note, and precludes any provision being construed to confer a non-judicial power. The granting clause uses both title-theory and lien-theory language ("mortgage, grant, bargain, sell, convey, assign") together with a defeasance clause (Section 2.7), so that the instrument is effective regardless of how South Carolina characterizes the mortgage estate. The Term Sheet specifies a mortgage rather than a deed of trust; confirm that Lender does not prefer a deed of trust, since South Carolina permits both and the remedies are the same.
@H3:J2. Redemption and upset bids cannot be waived
@P:**Analysis.** Your note directs that the Mortgage waive all rights of redemption. South Carolina's statutory redemption and upset-bid procedures in a judicial foreclosure cannot be waived by contract, and an unqualified waiver in a recorded instrument invites an argument that the lender is overreaching. The draft includes a waiver "to the fullest extent permitted by Applicable Law," with an express acknowledgment that statutory redemption and upset-bid rights are not waivable and that nothing in the Section is an attempt to waive a non-waivable right (Section 16.13). That preserves the persuasive value of the waiver where it does operate while avoiding an unenforceable overreach.
@H3:J3. Deficiency judgments
@P:Section 16.8 preserves Lender's right to a deficiency judgment "to the extent permitted by Applicable Law" and acknowledges that South Carolina law may limit or condition its availability. The Article 17 non-recourse structure limits Borrower's personal liability in any event, so the deficiency right will ordinarily be exercised against Guarantor under the Guaranty or for carve-out liability. Confirm the point with our litigation group if Lender intends to seek deficiencies.
@H3:J4. Execution formalities
@P:**Analysis.** The signature page includes two subscribing witnesses and a South Carolina notarial acknowledgment in the standard form, and a note that the acknowledgment is to be completed at closing. South Carolina conveyancing practice requires witnesses in addition to acknowledgment for instruments recorded in the RMC Office; the Title Insurer should confirm its own execution requirements before the instrument is sent out for signature. Lender's signature does not require acknowledgment, and the draft leaves the Lender block unacknowledged.
@P:**Action.** Confirm execution and acknowledgment requirements with Prestige Title before circulating; verify the current statutory citation for the witness requirement.
@H3:J5. Recording tax fixed by the instrument
@P:**Analysis.** The documentary stamp tax is $54,575 (0.37% of $14,750,000), verified at 4.6. Because the Mortgage recites the maximum principal amount secured and states that Holdback disbursements are obligatory advances, the tax is fixed at recording and no supplemental tax should arise on each draw. Confirm with the closing agent and with the Charleston County RMC Office; if the RMC Office takes the position that the tax must be paid on the obligated amount only, the recital supports the calculation.
@H3:J6. Receivership and mortgagee-in-possession provisions
@P:Section 16.3 provides for a receiver as a matter of right upon default without regard to the adequacy of the security, and Section 16.4 provides for entry and possession. These are standard and enforceable in South Carolina equity practice, but a consent to receivership that is expressed as an absolute right should be reviewed by litigation counsel for consistency with current practice in the Charleston County Court of Common Pleas.
