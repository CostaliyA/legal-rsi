## Principal findings

1. **The Title Commitment's legal description is defective.** Exhibit A recites 139,392 SF (3.2 acres) but its courses and distances compute to **279,535 SF (6.417 acres)** — roughly double — and describe a different parcel, with different bearings and a different surveyor from the survey of record. The lease adopts the surveyed description (verified: closure error 0.00′, 139,474.95 SF) and makes it controlling.
2. **The milestone reverter is a penalty, not a remedy.** At 90% completion it transfers ~$317M of improvements against at most ~$2.85M per year of harm — and the harm is *smallest* at Milestone 3, when the forfeiture is *largest*. The draft requires a Purchase Price (greater of appraised FMV and Unreimbursed Hard Costs), payment as a condition of effectiveness, no LOC forfeiture, and a 90%-complete safe harbor.
3. **The reverter is unbankable as written.** It is not subordinate to the leasehold mortgage, there is no independent lender cure for milestone defaults, and the New Lease right is materially narrower than Bayside's Section 5.3. The draft adds a 180-day lender cure with a replacement-developer right, a New Lease on any termination, and lender-driven estoppel/SNDA mechanics with self-help.
4. **The recorded 2019 right of first refusal has no mortgagee carve-out** and conflicts head-on with Bayside's requirement that the lender may foreclose and assign without consent. Amendment and subordination are made a condition precedent.
5. **The tax abatement starts three years too early and binds the wrong party.** Commencing it on the Rent Commencement Date is worth ~**$18.1M** in nominal taxes through 2043 (and avoids ~$4.2M of *over*payment during construction, since the 25% phase applied to a $178M post-development value exceeds the land-only tax by ~$1.4M/yr). Separately, the Authority cannot bind the City to the abatement, so the draft makes a City agreement a condition precedent and adds a self-executing rent offset.
6. **The $28.5M percentage-rent breakpoint fixed for 99 years** turns a participation into an escalating gross-receipts tax (at 3% revenue growth: $30,000/yr in Rent Year 6 rising to $8.0M/yr in Rent Year 96). The draft indexes it to Base Rent.
7. **"Lease Year" means two different things** in the Term Sheet (rent runs from the Rent Commencement Date; milestones from the Lease Commencement Date), a three-year difference in when escalations and percentage rent begin. The draft separates "Lease Year" from "Rent Year."
8. The remediation credit is capped while Tenant's exposure is uncapped (the Term Sheet's own arithmetic implies a 75/25 split); the ELUR ground-floor residential prohibition collides with the Term Sheet's own ground-floor lobby/amenity program; the mechanics' lien cannot be a Permitted Exception (the Title Company will not issue while it is of record); and the construction loan matures before Phase 2 is due while the completion guaranty runs to the maturity date.

## Design choices

- The lease is a clean instrument, not an annotated one: nine short "Drafting Note" annotations explain each deliberate departure from the Term Sheet, with the full reasoning, quantification, and fallback positions in the memo (Appendix B lists all 29 departures).
- All arithmetic was recomputed rather than carried forward. The memo records each variance; the only material one is the legal description. Rent rounding differences ($13 and $41) are resolved in favor of the stated formula.
- Items requiring data absent from the record (unit count, GFA, parking, revenue pro forma, several recorded instruments) are marked `[●]` in Exhibit E or listed as open items — not invented.
- The Connecticut forfeiture/penalty analysis rests on Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 227(1), 229 and 356(1). The client's recollection of a "Stamford" decision could not be confirmed from the record; the memo says so and recommends a citator-confirmed search before the point is used in negotiation.
- Settlement-conscious framing: the draft keeps a reverter in form (per Resolution 2025-047 §2(a)) but makes it compensatory, and flags that most departures are arguably "material" and so require a supplemental Board resolution.
