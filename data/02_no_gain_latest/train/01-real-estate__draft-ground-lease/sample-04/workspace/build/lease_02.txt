@H1 ARTICLE 3 — PROJECT; PERMITTED USES; DEVELOPMENT OBLIGATIONS
@H2 3.1 Development of the Project
@P Tenant shall design, finance, construct, and operate the Project on the Premises substantially in accordance with the Project Program and in compliance with Applicable Law. Subject to Force Majeure Events, Tenant shall proceed with the development of the Project with reasonable diligence and shall achieve each Milestone by the applicable Milestone Date, subject to Article 9.
@H2 3.2 Project Program and Changes
@P The Project Program attached as **Exhibit E** describes the minimum development program for the Project, including the minimum residential unit count, the minimum Rentable Area of the Office Building, the minimum retail square footage, the minimum parking ratios, the Public Open Space, and the schedule of affordable dwelling units. Tenant may from time to time modify the design, phasing, unit mix, and specifications of the Project in the ordinary course, and no such modification shall require Landlord's consent, provided that no such modification shall (a) reduce the residential unit count below the minimum set forth in the Project Program, (b) reduce the Affordable Housing Units below the number required by Section 3.6, (c) reduce the Public Open Space below 8,000 square feet, or (d) expand the Permitted Uses. Any modification that would do any of the foregoing shall require Landlord's prior written consent in accordance with the Landlord Consent Standards.
@H2 3.3 Permitted Uses
@P The Premises and the Improvements may be used for the following purposes and for no other purpose: (a) residential apartments, for rent only, together with customary ancillary residential amenities and services; (b) Class A office; (c) ground-floor retail; (d) restaurants, including restaurants with liquor service licensed by the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection; (e) accessory structured, surface, and below-grade parking; and (f) any other lawful use that is accessory and incidental to the foregoing, all subject to Applicable Law and the ELUR.
@H2 3.4 Prohibited Uses
@P Notwithstanding Section 3.3, the Premises shall not be used for: adult entertainment establishments; the sale of firearms; pawnshops; gasoline service stations or other motor fuel dispensing; industrial, manufacturing, warehousing, or distribution uses; any use that would violate the ELUR or any recorded instrument of record against the Premises; or any use that is unlawful. Tenant shall not create a nuisance or commit waste, and shall not use the Premises in any manner that materially and unreasonably interferes with the use and enjoyment of adjoining property, taking into account the urban, mixed-use, waterfront character of the Project.
@H2 3.5 Restrictions on Condominium and Cooperative Conversion
@P Tenant shall not submit the Premises or the Improvements to the provisions of the Connecticut Common Interest Ownership Act (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47-200 *et seq.*) or create any condominium or cooperative form of ownership of the Improvements, or sell, convey, or transfer any dwelling unit or portion of the Improvements separately from the leasehold estate created by this Lease, without Landlord's prior written consent, which consent shall not be unreasonably withheld if (a) the affordable housing obligations under Section 3.6 are preserved for the balance of the Term in form reasonably satisfactory to Landlord, (b) each Leasehold Mortgagee consents, and (c) Landlord's fee interest and reversionary interest are not impaired. Nothing in this Section shall restrict Tenant from transferring direct or indirect interests in Tenant or in a subtenant in accordance with Article 11.
@H2 3.6 Affordable Housing Covenant
@C1 (a) **Covenant.** Not less than fifteen percent (15%) of the residential dwelling units in the Project (the "**Affordable Housing Units**") shall be designated as affordable housing units available to households earning at or below eighty percent (80%) of Area Median Income ("**AMI**"), at rents (including utilities) not exceeding the applicable HUD maximum rent, for the duration of the Term. The Affordable Housing Units shall be distributed proportionately among unit types and shall be comparably finished to market-rate units. The number of Affordable Housing Units shall be calculated as fifteen percent (15%) of the total number of residential dwelling units in the Project as set forth in the Project Program and, upon any change in the number of residential dwelling units in the Improvements (whether by renovation, conversion, partial demolition, alteration, or otherwise), shall be recalculated so that not less than fifteen percent (15%) of the then-existing residential dwelling units are Affordable Housing Units.
@C1 (b) **AMI Standard.** References in this Lease to AMI shall mean the median income for the New Haven-Milford, Connecticut Metropolitan Statistical Area as published annually by the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development for the Section 8 program, or such other HUD-defined area as may then be applicable to the City of Port Calloway.
@C1 (c) **Location; ELUR.** The parties acknowledge that the ELUR restricts residential use on the ground floor of any structure on the Premises. Accordingly, all Affordable Housing Units (and all other dwelling units) shall be located on floors above the ground floor of the Residential Tower. Tenant shall not be in default of this Section 3.6 by reason of the placement of Affordable Housing Units if such placement complies with Section 3.9.
@C1 (d) **Compliance and Monitoring.** Within ninety (90) days after the Rent Commencement Date and annually thereafter, Tenant shall deliver to Landlord a rent roll identifying the Affordable Housing Units, the income qualifications of the occupying households, and the rents charged. Tenant shall implement and maintain reasonable income verification procedures consistent with HUD guidance. Landlord may inspect Tenant's books and records relating to the Affordable Housing Units upon reasonable prior notice, not more than once in any twelve (12) month period, and may require Tenant to engage an independent monitor reasonably acceptable to both parties if Landlord reasonably determines that a material non-compliance has occurred.
@C1 (e) **Enforcement and Cure.** If Tenant fails to comply with this Section 3.6 in any material respect, Landlord shall give written notice specifying the failure, and Tenant shall have ninety (90) days to cure (or, if cure cannot reasonably be completed within ninety (90) days, such longer period as is reasonably necessary, not to exceed one hundred eighty (180) days, provided Tenant commences cure within such ninety (90) day period and diligently prosecutes it to completion). Landlord's sole remedies for a breach of this Section 3.6 after the expiration of such cure period shall be specific performance and actual damages; a breach of this Section 3.6 shall not constitute a basis for exercising the Reverter Right, for terminating this Lease, or for drawing on the Security Deposit, except to the extent of damages actually incurred.
@C1 (f) **Prior Recorded Declaration.** The parties acknowledge the Declaration of Restrictive Covenants dated November 15, 2019 and recorded at Volume 408, Page 230 of the Port Calloway Land Records. Landlord represents that (i) such declaration is the only recorded instrument imposing affordability requirements on the Premises, and (ii) the covenant set forth in this Section 3.6 satisfies such declaration. Tenant's obligations under this Section 3.6 are undertaken in satisfaction of both this Lease and such declaration, and Landlord shall not enforce such declaration separately in a manner inconsistent with this Section 3.6.
@NOTE The Term Sheet's affordable-housing provision (Section 4) does not address recalculation of the 15% requirement, income verification, monitoring, enforcement, unit distribution, or floor location, and refers to an undefined "Port Calloway Metropolitan Statistical Area." The recorded restrictive covenant is similarly incomplete, and the survey narrative refers to a third AMI area. See Drafting Issues Memo, Items 26–28.
@H2 3.7 Public Open Space

The Public Open Space shall not be located within the area of the 15-foot public pedestrian access easement in an amount exceeding the area of such easement. The design, materials, and programming of the Public Open Space shall be subject to Landlord's approval in accordance with the Landlord Consent Standards, provided that no such approval shall unreasonably reduce the developable area of the Premises or the economic return of the Project. Tenant shall maintain the Public Open Space in good condition and repair and in a clean and safe condition for public use throughout the Term, subject to Section 3.7(b).
@C1 (b) **Cost Sharing After Year 30.** From and after the thirtieth (30th) Rent Year, the cost of maintaining the Public Open Space shall be shared equally by Landlord and Tenant, and Landlord shall reimburse Tenant for Landlord's share within thirty (30) days after receipt of a reasonably detailed invoice.
@C1 (c) **Indemnity and Risk Allocation.** Landlord acknowledges that the public pedestrian access easement and the Public Open Space are for the benefit of the public and that Landlord, as the fee owner, retains the reversionary interest therein. Landlord shall indemnify, defend, and hold Tenant harmless from and against all claims arising out of the condition of the Premises existing as of the Commencement Date, or out of the acts or omissions of Landlord, the City, or their contractors, agents, or employees, in the Public Open Space or the pedestrian easement area. Tenant shall maintain the insurance required by Article 12 and shall name Landlord as an additional insured with respect to the Public Open Space.
@H2 3.8 Zoning, Permits, and Approvals
@P Tenant shall obtain, at its sole cost, all permits, approvals, and entitlements required for the development and operation of the Project, and Landlord shall reasonably cooperate (at no material cost to Landlord) in obtaining the same, including by executing consents, applications, and waivers reasonably requested by Tenant, and by supporting Tenant's applications before the Port Calloway Planning and Zoning Commission, the Inland Wetlands Commission, and DEEP. Landlord shall not take any action, and shall not support any third-party action, that would frustrate Tenant's ability to obtain any such approval.
@H2 3.9 ELUR Compliance; Ground-Floor Uses
@C1 (a) **Compliance.** Tenant shall comply, and shall cause the Project to comply, with the ELUR and with all Environmental Laws. Tenant acknowledges that the ELUR prohibits residential use on the ground floor of any structure erected on the Premises and requires an engineered barrier over areas of ground disturbance.
@C1 (b) **Ground-Floor Program.** The ground floor of the Residential Tower shall contain only Permitted Uses that are not "residential use" within the meaning of the ELUR, such as retail space, building lobbies, mechanical and utility rooms, parking, and non-habitable amenity space, unless and to the extent DEEP has confirmed in writing that additional ground-floor uses are permitted.
@C1 (c) **DEEP Interpretation; Modification.** Landlord and Tenant shall jointly seek a written interpretation from DEEP, prior to the Commencement Date, confirming whether residential lobbies, leasing offices, fitness centers, package rooms, resident lounges, and similar non-habitable ancillary spaces may be located on the ground floor of the Residential Tower without violating the ELUR. If DEEP determines that a modification of the ELUR is required, or if DEEP objects to any element of the Project, then Landlord shall, at its sole cost and expense, promptly apply for and diligently pursue such modification of the ELUR, and the parties shall cooperate in such application. The Milestone Dates and the Rent Commencement Date shall be extended day for day for any delay caused by any DEEP review, interpretation, or modification, and no such delay shall constitute a Tenant default or a basis for the Reverter Right.
@C1 (d) **Cost Recovery.** If Tenant incurs costs to obtain any ELUR modification, any permit condition, or any DEEP approval relating to pre-existing contamination or to the pre-existing terms of the ELUR, such costs shall be added to the Remediation Costs and allocated in accordance with Section 8.4.

@H1 ARTICLE 4 — BASE RENT
@H2 4.1 Initial Base Rent
@P Commencing on the Rent Commencement Date, Tenant shall pay annual base rent in the amount of Two Million Eight Hundred Fifty Thousand Dollars ($2,850,000) (the "**Base Rent**"), payable in equal monthly installments of Two Hundred Thirty-Seven Thousand Five Hundred Dollars ($237,500) in advance on the first day of each calendar month during the Term. Base Rent is a flat dollar amount and shall not be recalculated, increased, or reduced by reference to the actual area of the Premises, whether as surveyed, as assessed, or otherwise.
@H2 4.2 Fixed Escalation (Rent Years 1 through 15)
@P Base Rent shall be adjusted on the first day of Rent Year 6 and on the first day of Rent Year 11 by the amounts set forth in the following schedule, representing an annual compounding escalation of two and one-half percent (2.5%) applied at five (5) year intervals:
@TBL 1.6,1.6,1.6|Rent Years~Annual Base Rent~Monthly Installment
@ROW Rent Years 1 – 5~$2,850,000~$237,500
@ROW Rent Years 6 – 10~$3,224,513~$268,709
@ROW Rent Years 11 – 15~$3,648,241~$304,020
@P The amounts in the foregoing table are calculated as $2,850,000 × (1.025)^5 and $2,850,000 × (1.025)^10, respectively. In the event of any conflict between the table and the formula, the formula shall control.
@NOTE The Term Sheet (Section 5) states $3,224,500 and $3,648,200 (with monthly installments of $268,708 and $304,017). Recalculation produces $3,224,513 and $3,648,241 (with monthly installments of $268,709 and $304,020). The differences are rounding artifacts of the Term Sheet but should be eliminated in the definitive documents. See Drafting Issues Memo, Item 22.
@H2 4.3 CPI Resets (Rent Year 16 and Thereafter)
@P Commencing on the first day of Rent Year 16, and on the first day of each tenth (10th) Rent Year thereafter (that is, Rent Years 26, 36, 46, 56, 66, 76, 86, and 96), Base Rent shall be reset to the greater of:
@C1 (a) the Initial Base Rent adjusted by an annual compounding escalation of two and one-half percent (2.5%) from the Rent Commencement Date through the first day of the applicable reset Rent Year, calculated as $2,850,000 × (1.025)^n, where n equals the number of full Rent Years elapsed since the Rent Commencement Date; and
@C1 (b) the Initial Base Rent adjusted by the cumulative percentage increase in the CPI from the calendar month in which the Rent Commencement Date occurs through the calendar month immediately preceding the first day of the applicable reset Rent Year;
**provided, however**, that in no event shall Base Rent for any Rent Year exceed an amount equal to the Initial Base Rent adjusted by an annual compounding escalation of three percent (3.0%) from the Rent Commencement Date (the "**Rent Cap**"), and in no event shall Base Rent for any Rent Year be less than the Base Rent payable in the immediately preceding Rent Year (the "**Rent Floor**"). The Rent Cap and the Rent Floor are cumulative and shall be applied to each reset.
@H2 4.4 CPI Mechanics
@P The CPI adjustment shall be calculated by reference to the CPI index value for the calendar month in which the Rent Commencement Date occurs as the base index, and the CPI index value for the calendar month immediately preceding the applicable reset date as the then-current index. The resulting Base Rent shall be rounded to the nearest one hundred dollars ($100). If the CPI is not published for any required month, the parties shall use the CPI for the next preceding month in which it was published. If the CPI ceases to be published, the parties shall substitute a comparable index measuring the cost of living in the Northeast Region published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics or, if none, by a nationally recognized publisher selected by mutual agreement, and the aggregate change in the substitute index shall be substituted for the CPI. If the parties cannot agree upon a substitute index within thirty (30) days, the matter shall be resolved under Article 18.3.
@H2 4.5 Payment; Net Lease
@P Base Rent, Percentage Rent, Taxes, and all other sums payable by Tenant under this Lease (collectively, "**Rent**") shall be paid without notice or demand and without abatement, deduction, or setoff except as expressly provided in this Lease. This is a triple-net lease. Tenant shall pay its Rent in lawful money of the United States by wire transfer or ACH to an account designated by Landlord in writing. Rent for any partial month shall be prorated on a per diem basis.
@H2 4.6 Late Payments
@P If any installment of Rent is not paid within ten (10) days after its due date, Tenant shall pay a late charge equal to three percent (3%) of the overdue amount, and interest on the overdue amount at the lesser of (a) the Wall Street Journal prime rate plus two percent (2%) per annum and (b) the maximum rate permitted by Applicable Law, from the due date until paid. No late charge shall be imposed on any amount disputed in good faith by Tenant and paid within ten (10) Business Days after resolution of the dispute. Amounts overdue shall not bear interest at a rate that would constitute a penalty or usurious rate under Applicable Law.
@H2 4.7 Rent Abatement
@P Base Rent and Percentage Rent shall abate in accordance with Sections 13.3 and 13.5 (Casualty and Condemnation) and as otherwise expressly provided in this Lease. Base Rent shall also abate if Landlord, by its acts or omissions, prevents Tenant from using or occupying all or a material portion of the Premises for the Permitted Uses, in which event Base Rent shall abate equitably for so long as such interference continues. Tenant's rights under this Section 4.7 are in addition to, and not in limitation of, Tenant's remedies under Section 16.4.

@H1 ARTICLE 5 — PERCENTAGE RENT
@H2 5.1 Percentage Rent Obligation
@P Commencing with Rent Year 6 and continuing for the balance of the Term, Tenant shall pay to Landlord additional rent ("**Percentage Rent**") equal to two percent (2.0%) of the amount by which Gross Revenues for such Rent Year exceed the Breakpoint for such Rent Year.
@H2 5.2 Gross Revenues
@P "**Gross Revenues**" means all rents, percentage rents, license fees, and other income of every kind actually received by Tenant or its Affiliates from the operation of the Project during the applicable Rent Year (determined on the accrual basis in accordance with sound accounting practice consistently applied), including base rent and additional rent from residential, office, and retail subtenants, parking revenues, amenity and service fees, and other ancillary income derived from the Premises or the Improvements, **less** the following exclusions:
@C2 (1) security deposits, and any other deposits, until and only to the extent applied to rent or otherwise recognized as income;
@C2 (2) utility reimbursements and recoveries, common area maintenance recoveries, real estate tax and insurance recoveries and reimbursements, and any other pass-through, escalation, or reimbursement payment from any subtenant that does not constitute true revenue to Tenant;
@C2 (3) proceeds of insurance (other than business interruption or rental loss proceeds, which shall be included to the extent allocable to periods during the Term and not previously included);
@C2 (4) condemnation awards and settlement payments;
@C2 (5) capital contributions, loans, and other financing proceeds, and proceeds from the sale or other disposition of assets (other than sales of inventory or scrap in the ordinary course);
@C2 (6) interest and other investment income on reserves, escrows, and deposits;
@C2 (7) refunds, rebates, credits, and retroactive adjustments, and any amounts received from contractors, manufacturers, or suppliers as warranties, allowances, or damages;
@C2 (8) proceeds of any business interruption insurance, rent guaranty payment, or liquidated damages payment received from a contractor (to the extent allocable to a period outside the Term or attributable to a period for which Base Rent was abated);
@C2 (9) amounts recovered from subtenants for the cost of repairs, restoration, or capital work, and amounts recovered from third parties as indemnity or contribution;
@C2 (10) any revenue attributable to the Public Open Space or to public parking operations that are, by Applicable Law, required to be operated free of charge or that are operated without revenue;
@C2 (11) gross rents in respect of any period prior to the Rent Commencement Date;
@C2 (12) federal, state, and local grants, subsidies, tax credits, and abatement payments, including any proceeds of any tax credit program and any payments received under the Abatement Program;
@C2 (13) sales, use, and occupancy taxes and any other taxes collected from subtenants and remitted to a governmental authority;
@C2 (14) the amount of any bad debt or uncollected rent, and any rent that is thereafter collected shall be included in Gross Revenues in the Rent Year of collection; and
@C2 (15) any other income that is not derived from the ordinary operation of the Project for the Permitted Uses.
@P Gross Revenues shall be reduced by the amount of any Rent concession, free rent, or landlord contribution granted to a subtenant to the extent not already excluded, and shall be determined without duplication. Gross Revenues shall in no event include revenue from any business or activity of Tenant or its Affiliates that is not conducted on the Premises.
@H2 5.3 Breakpoint
@P The "**Breakpoint**" for Rent Year 6 shall be Twenty-Eight Million Five Hundred Thousand Dollars ($28,500,000). For each Rent Year thereafter, the Breakpoint shall equal the product of (a) $28,500,000 and (b) the quotient obtained by dividing the Base Rent payable for such Rent Year by the Initial Base Rent ($2,850,000), such that the Breakpoint bears a constant relationship to Base Rent throughout the Term. Notwithstanding the foregoing, in no event shall the Breakpoint for any Rent Year be less than $28,500,000.
@NOTE Tenant's position. The Term Sheet (Section 6) fixes the Breakpoint at $28,500,000 "for the duration of the Lease Term," which converts a negotiated participation into a permanent share of gross receipts that grows without limit as a share of Project revenue. The formula above preserves the negotiated 10:1 relationship between the Breakpoint and Base Rent. Fallback positions, and the quantified effect of a fixed Breakpoint over the Term, are described in the Drafting Issues Memo, Item 23.
@H2 5.4 Statement and Payment
@P Percentage Rent shall be calculated and paid annually, within ninety (90) days after the end of each Rent Year (or, if this Lease terminates before the end of a Rent Year, within ninety (90) days after such termination). Each payment shall be accompanied by a certified statement of Gross Revenues for the applicable Rent Year, signed by an authorized officer of Tenant, together with a schedule reconciling the statement to the Project's audited or reviewed financial statements. Tenant may deliver the statement on a consolidated basis for the Project, provided that Gross Revenues are separately identified for each component of the Project.
@H2 5.5 Audit Rights
@P Landlord may, at its sole cost, audit or cause to be audited Tenant's books and records relating to Gross Revenues for any Rent Year, upon not less than thirty (30) days' prior written notice. Audits (a) may be conducted not more than once in any twelve (12) month period, (b) shall be limited to the three (3) most recently completed Rent Years, (c) shall be conducted during normal business hours at Tenant's office or by electronic means, and (d) shall be conducted by an independent certified public accountant reasonably acceptable to Tenant who is compensated on an hourly basis and not on a contingency basis. Tenant shall cooperate reasonably and shall make its books and records available. If an audit reveals an underpayment of Percentage Rent of five percent (5%) or more for the audited Rent Year, Tenant shall reimburse Landlord for the reasonable documented cost of the audit (not to exceed $25,000); otherwise Landlord shall bear the cost. If the audit reveals an overpayment, Landlord shall credit the overpayment against Rent next coming due or, if the Term has ended, refund the overpayment within thirty (30) days. All information obtained in any audit shall be maintained in confidence by Landlord and its auditor, subject to Article 18.6 and Applicable Law. Pending resolution of any good-faith dispute, Tenant shall pay the undisputed portion of Percentage Rent.
@H2 5.6 Books and Records
@P Tenant shall maintain complete and accurate books and records relating to Gross Revenues for not less than three (3) Rent Years after the end of each Rent Year, or such longer period as may be required by Applicable Law.
@H2 5.7 No Double Counting; Mortgagee Protection
@P Percentage Rent shall be subordinate to the lien of any Leasehold Mortgage and shall not be secured by any lien on the Improvements. Percentage Rent shall not be payable to the extent it would cause the total Rent payable in any Rent Year to exceed the Base Rent plus two percent (2.0%) of Gross Revenues for such Rent Year.
